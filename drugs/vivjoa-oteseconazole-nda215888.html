13.2 Animal Toxicology and/or Pharmacology Data regarding the increased incidences of hemorrhage were added to highlight the possible effects of long-term exposure to high doses of oteseconazole. This information was added to this subsection because it was considered to be significant animal data necessary for the safe and effective use of VIVJOA in humans that is not incorporated in other sections of the PI as delineated in 21 CFR 201.57(14)(ii). Refer to Section I.2.2 of this review for additional details.

14 CLINICAL STUDIES The clinical studies discussed in Section 14 of the PI included effectiveness and safety data for females of reproductive potential. A disclaimer statement was added to note that VIVJOA is contraindicated in females of reproductive potential to avoid implying the use of the VIVJOA in this population as delineated in 21 CFR 201.57(c)(15)(i) that emphasizes that any clinical study discussed in Section 14 must not imply or suggest indications or uses not stated in the Indications and Usage section of the PI. The addition of the disclaimer statement was based on the recommendations noted in section II.B of the guidance for industry: Clinical Studies Section of Labeling for Human Prescription Drug and Biological Products – Content and Format (January 2006) that indicates that if such studies are included in Section 14 of the PI, “the limitations of the study and the reasons for inclusion should be stated.” The disclaimer statement added to Section 14 was as follows: Although females of reproductive potential were included in the clinical efficacy data, VIVJOA is contraindicated in females of reproductive potential because of a risk of embryo-fetal toxicity [see Contraindications (4), Warnings and Precautions (5.1), and Use in Specific Populations (8.1, 8.3)]. The use of rescue VVC medication is an intercurrent event that confounds the assessment of a possible culture-verified recurrence at a later timepoint. Furthermore, the use of additional VVC medication was noted to be higher on the oteseconazole arm compared to placebo in subjects for who a culture verified episode was not reported. Ignoring the use of rescue medication overestimates the effect of treatment with oteseconazole. Therefore, the results of the sensitivity analysis that treats VVC medication use as an event were added for each trial. Refer to Section 6.3.1 for additional details. (b) (4)

For Trial 017, it was expected that subjects who failed to resolve their infection at Day 14 would be considered events in the primary analysis. The results presented were revised based on this definition. Refer to Section 6.2.3.3 for additional details. (b) (4)

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Study 2139-002: A 26-Week Oral Toxicity Study in Rats With a 17-Week Recovery Period. Oteseconazole dosing for 26 weeks in rats resulted in changes in the liver, adrenals, thyroid, kidneys, and ovaries with no significant effects at 0.15 mg/kg and minimal, nonadverse findings representing an extension of the drug’s pharmacology or of questionable human relevance (thyroid follicular cell hypertrophy/hyperplasia, hepatocellular vacuolation, hepatocellular hypertrophy and renal tubular pigment) at 0.5 mg/kg day, about 0.2 times the maximum recommended human dosage (MRHD) based on area under the concentration-time curve (AUC) comparisons.

At doses ≥1.5 mg/kg/day (about 1.3 times the MRHD based on AUC comparisons, additional findings in rats included increased cholesterol, globulin, platelet counts, mild, reversible prolongations in APTT, increased organ weights (liver, thyroid, adrenal), adrenal gland vacuolation, chronic progressive nephropathy, hepatocellular hypertrophy/hyperplasia, multinucleated hepatocytes, increased Kupffer cell pigment, sex cord /stromal hyperplasia with (at 5 mg/kg) decreased prostate weight, increased lung histiocytosis, hepatocellular necrosis, increases cytochrome P450 content (+100%), Thyroxine (+525%) and Triiodothyroxine glucuronidation (+265 %). At the end of the 17-week recovery period, increased platelet count, decreased reticulocytes and thyroid follicular hyperplasia were observed at doses ≥1.5 mg/kg/day while tubular pigment, hepatocellular hypertrophy, hepatocellular necrosis, multinucleated hepatocytes and pigmented Kupffer cells persisted only at 5 mg/kg (about 6 times the MRHD based on AUC comparisons). The no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) was considered to be 0.5 mg/kg which was associated with a maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) of 741 ng/mL and an AUC of 14,295 ngꞏhr/mL (about 0.2 times the MRHD based on AUC comparisons).
