Executive Summary

Selumetinib (AZD6244) is an orally bioavailable, non-ATP competitive kinase inhibitor that targets mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK)/extracellular signal regulated kinases 1 and 2 (MEK1/2); the established pharmacological class is kinase inhibitor. MEK1/2 are ubiquitously expressed proteins that are part of the Ras-Raf-MAPK-ERK signaling transduction cascade. Activation of the Ras-MAPK signaling pathway leads to proliferation in a large number of cancer types. Neurofibromatosis type I (NF1) is a syndrome caused by germline mutations in the NF1 gene that result in widespread phenotypic outcomes, including plexiform neurofibromas. NF1 codes for the protein neurofribromin, a widely expressed Ras GTPase activating protein (GAP). Ras GAPs catalyze the conversion of Ras from the GTP-bound active conformation to the GDP inactive conformation, thereby limiting downstream Ras signaling. Mutations in NF1 commonly lead to increased Ras pathway signaling and dysregulated growth. Blocking downstream Ras signaling by targeting MEK1/2 may help circumvent the effects of losing neurofibromin GAP activity Kiuru, M and Busam, KJ (2017), including slowing or reducing plexiform neurofibroma growth.
