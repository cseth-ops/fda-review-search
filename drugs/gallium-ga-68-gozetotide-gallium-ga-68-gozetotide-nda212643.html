Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

This NDA should be approved from a nonclinical perspective.

Prostate-specific membrane antigen (PSMA) is a target of interest for development of low molecular weight radiopharmaceuticals (Kratochwil et al. 2016; Lenzo et al. 2018; von Eyben et al. 2018). The submission supports the marketing authorization of 68Ga-PSMA-11 for PET imaging of prostate cancer. The Applicant did not conduct any nonclinical studies to support the application. However, the Applicant is relying on published reports of the safety and efficacy of 68Ga-PSMA-11 in addition to available clinical experience to support the NDA application.

Based on agreement reached with the Agency, the Applicant provided peer-reviewed scientific literature on the nonclinical pharmacology of 68Ga-PSMA-11 to support the safety and efficacy of the product. The Applicant cross-referenced an extended single dose acute toxicity study of PSMA-11. Pharmacology data (see Section 5.3) demonstrate PSMA-specific surface binding and internalization in human prostate cancer cell lines and 68Ga-PSMA-11 uptake in tumor animal models on micro-PET. No safety pharmacology data are required for a microdose product. Moreover, no safety concerns have been reported during the extensive clinical experience with 68Ga-PSMA-11. The pharmacokinetics data indicate rapid clearance of 68Ga-PSMA-11 from the

circulation. The use of 68Ga-PSMA-11 as a microdose does not necessitate a genotoxicity study, and no reproductive and developmental toxicity studies are required for the intended male population. Therefore, no other nonclinical studies are necessary to support the safety of 68Ga­ PSMA-11 for the indication in the intended populations.

Safety pharmacology

According to the Applicant, no safety pharmacology data on 68Ga-PSMA-11 were identified in the scientific literature. The Applicant cited a cardiovascular safety study of N,N′-bis (2­ hydroxybenzyl) ethylenediamine-N,N′-diacetic acid monosodium salt (NaHBED) conducted in normotensive rats (Bergeron et al. 2002). In this study, 300 mmol/kg of NaHBED in 0.5-mL distilled water was administered via a jugular vein catheter (n=5 for both saline control and NaHBED). NaHBED produced no effects on either blood pressure or heart rate when given as a bolus injection or 20-minute infusion. The no observed effect level (NOEL) for the cardiovascular safety of NaHBED was 300 mmol/kg (121.3 mg/kg) with an estimated human equivalent dose (HED) of 19.6 mg/kg or 1.2 g in a 60 kg adult.

Drug Interactions: The Applicant reported that no concerning pharmacodynamic drug interactions were identified in the scientific literature.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Single-Dose Toxicity Study: The Applicant provided right of reference to the extended single-dose toxicity study in Wistar rats conducted at The rats were intravenously administered (b) (4)

0 or 86 μg/kg PSMA-11 and evaluated for clinical signs, with the main study animals euthanized and necropsied on Day 2 while the recovery group was euthanized and necropsied on Day 15. There were no adverse effects observed in rats administered 86 μg/kg PSMA-11. The maximum proposed clinical mass dose is 5 μg (0.08 μg/kg assuming a 60 kg body weight; over 1000-fold safety factor). The safety margin obtained from this study suggests that 68Ga-PSMA-11 is safe at the clinical mass dose of 5 μg in the intended population.

Repeated-Dose Toxicity Study: The Applicant did not provide any data on repeated dose toxicity of PSMA-11. However, the Applicant cited a published study (Bergeron et al. 2002) on the safety evaluation of NaHBED. In the study, a single dose of NaHBED (75 μmol/kg) was intravenously administered as a 20-minute infusion once daily for 14 days to iron overloaded Beagle dogs. Necropsy conducted following euthanasia of the animals 24 and 48 hours after the last dose demonstrated no systemic toxicity at the employed dose. A NOEL of 75 μmol/kg (0.303 mg/kg) NaHBED was obtained in this dog study. The safety margin could not be estimated because the no observed adverse effect level could not be determined in this study.

General toxicology; additional studies: Not conducted and not needed.

Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology Summary:

The Applicant did not conduct any nonclinical studies to support this submission. In line with an agreement reached with the Agency during the pre-NDA meeting held on August 29, 2018, the Applicant provided scientific literature on the nonclinical pharmacology of 68Ga-PSMA-11 to support the safety and activity of the product.

The available in vitro data demonstrate that PSMA-11 exhibits satisfactory levels of specific surface binding and internalization in human prostate cancer cell lines with affinity constant values in the nanomolar range. Proof of concept data from mice show that 68Ga-PSMA-11 uptake in tumor on micro-PET correlates with PSMA expression determined by flow cytometry. In line with FDA guidance on microdose products, the Applicant did not need to provide any safety pharmacology data on 68Ga-PSMA-11. The available favorable clinical experience with the tracer provided additional justification.

The pharmacokinetic data suggest that 68Ga-PSMA-11 is cleared from the circulation within 1­ hour post-injection with highest uptake found in kidney and spleen. The Applicant has the right of reference to data on the single dose acute and extended toxicity (b) (4)

study on PSMA-11 and provided the data to support the safety of the tracer. An adequate safety margin of 1000-fold the clinical dose was established by the toxicity study suggesting no safety concerns.

The available nonclinical data on 68Ga-PSMA-11 support the safety and activity of the product. The Applicant did not provide any genotoxicity data on 68Ga-PSMA-11. Genotoxicity data is however not required for such microdose and single-dose products. The Applicant requested and received a waiver for conducting reproductive and developmental toxicity studies.

Therefore, based on the available nonclinical data on 68Ga-PSMA-11 and the safe clinical experience with the tracer, there are no safety concerns with the product for the proposed indication.

Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology Recommendation:

Based on the available nonclinical data and clinical experience with 68Ga-PSMA-11, it is recommended that 68Ga-PSMA-11 be approved from a nonclinical perspective.

Clinical efficacy

PSMA-PreRP

Trial Design

PSMA-PreRP was a prospective, single-arm, open-label study of 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET for imaging of PSMA expressing prostate carcinoma performed at two sites, UCLA and UCSF. It enrolled adult males with biopsy-proven intermediate or high risk prostate carcinoma. Patients had not yet received definitive therapy (including prostatectomy, radiation therapy, or focal gland therapy), and were planned to undergo prostatectomy with pelvic lymph node dissection. Patients were required to have a Karnofsky performance status of at least 50. Patients with lower performance status could certainly tolerate the PET scan, but they would be less likely to tolerate the prostatectomy.

After enrollment, each patient received 3 to 5 mCi (UCLA) or 3 to 7 mCi (UCSF) of 68Ga-PSMA-11 intravenously followed 45 to 60 min (UCLA) or 50 to 100 min (UCSF) later by PET/CT or PET/MR from mid-thigh to skull base. Unless advanced scatter correction algorithms were available on

the scanner, patients received 20 mg of furosemide intravenously with the radiotracer to reduce scatter and scatter correction artifact related to urinary excretion of radiotracer. As discussed in Section 6 of this review, the selection of 68Ga-PSMA-11 dose was supported by reports of successful imaging at similar doses that were published prior to study initiation.

Three to five readers from a centralized pool of six nuclear medicine physicians and radiologists independently interpreted each 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET scan. These central readers also had access to the anatomic images (CT or MR) obtained with the PET scan for attenuation correction. The readers were blinded to all other information except the history of pre-prostatectomy status. Training was required for the readers prior to participation in the study. Each reader rated multiple predefined regions as positive or negative based on the presence or absence of focal radiotracer uptake greater than physiologic background activity. A single result for each scan was generated by assigning the majority read result in each region, as further described in the Study Endpoints section below. No contract research organization was utilized.

A local read of each PET scan was performed by a single unblinded site reader promptly after the scan, and the result was made available to the physicians caring for the patient, including the urologist performing the prostatectomy. If the local read demonstrated PET positive pelvic lymph nodes, the prostatectomy and pelvic lymph node dissection was to proceed as planned. If the local read demonstrated PET positive bone or extrapelvic soft tissue lesions, correlative imaging or biopsy was to be performed and the treating urologist would determine whether prostatectomy and pelvic lymph node dissection should be performed.

Urologists performed the pelvic lymph node dissections using standard of care template technique guided by surgical anatomy rather than imaging. The reference standard for the PET result was the pathology report from this template pelvic lymph node dissection. Clinical safety

Safety data were collected in the PSMA-PreRP and PSMA-BCR studies listed in section 7.1. In addition, due to the extensive investigational use of 68Ga-PSMA-11 prior to initiation of the Applicant’s development plan, available safety results from the published literature were collected in the PSMA-PreRP-Meta and PSMA-BCR-Meta meta-analyses. All of these sources of safety data were reviewed.

No specific safety issues were encountered during development of the drug.

Review of the Safety Database

Overall Exposure

The pooling of safety data from the two clinical trials and two meta-analyses was discussed at the pre-NDA meeting and agreement between the Applicant and FDA was reached. However, the Applicant reported that only 3 of the 40 publications included in the meta-analyses mentioned adverse events. Each of the three articles reported that no adverse events (AEs) were identified but did not describe the procedure for AE monitoring. Therefore, the meta- analyses will be excluded from further safety discussion.

Safety data were available for 960 patients in the PSMA-PreRP and PSMA-BCR studies, with the safety population in each study defined as all enrolled patients who received a 68Ga-PSMA-11 injection. The PSMA-PreRP and PSMA-BCR studies were pooled for safety analysis because they had identical dosing, administration, and follow up procedures, and apart from the difference in primary versus recurrent prostate cancer, the enrollment criteria were very similar.

Each patient received a single dose of 68Ga-PSMA-11, which is appropriate for the population of intended use, where PSMA PET imaging will performed once or at widely spaced intervals. The dose range in the draft prescribing information is 3 to 7 mCi, which matches the administered dose in the pooled safety population of 5.1 ± 1.1 mCi (mean ± SD).

Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment

In American men, prostate cancer is the most common malignancy and the second-most common cause of cancer death. While many imaging techniques have been applied to prostate cancer, none have optimal performance. The need for improved prostate cancer imaging is reflected in the recurrence rate of up to 30% after definitive therapy of what was thought to be localized disease. Similarly, many patients with biochemical recurrence fail to have their disease localized by imaging. Diagnostic challenges persist despite recent imaging advances, such as multiparametric MRI and PET drugs approved for prostate cancer, such as 18F-fluciclovine.

68Ga-PSMA-11 injection is a positron emitting radiopharmaceutical proposed by the Applicant (b) (4)

The data submitted in this application from two adequate and well-controlled trials prospectively conducted by the Applicant support approval for imaging evaluation of suspected prostate cancer metastasis in two patient populations, men who are candidates for initial definitive therapy and men with biochemical evidence (serum PSA level) of prostate cancer recurrence (hereafter referred to as biochemical recurrence).

In patients with intermediate- to high-risk prostate cancer who are candidates for prostatectomy, the data submitted show low sensitivity but high specificity of 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET for detection of pelvic lymph node metastases. Of note, there is reason to suspect that the diagnostic performance was underestimated due to trial design and conduct. The data also suggest that 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET performs better in patients with higher grade prostate cancer in this setting. Regardless, it is anticipated that many patients with pelvic lymph node metastases will not be detected by 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET. However, given similar limitations in available imaging techniques, such false negative results are expected to have no impact on the treatment plan of definitive therapy in affected patients. The potential value of 68Ga-PSMA-11 PET lies in the demonstration that PPV exceeds the expected prevalence of lymph node metastasis in the population of intended use. While a positive PET scan may still need to be confirmed by other means, the results would allow a patient with metastasis to avoid the morbidity of surgery for more appropriate treatment. A positive PET scan might also help target therapy as part of the emerging paradigm of treatment of patients with limited metastases, also referred to as oligometastatic disease.
