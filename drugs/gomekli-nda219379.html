Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

Mirdametinib is an orally bioavailable small molecule non-ATP competitive inhibitor of mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK)/extracellular signal regulated kinases 1 and 2 (MEK1/2). MEK1/2 proteins are upstream regulators of the extracellular signal-related kinase (ERK) pathway. Both MEK and ERK are critical components of the RAS-regulated RAF-MEK-ERK pathway, which is often activated in different types of cancers. The proposed indication for mirdametinib is the treatment of adult and pediatric patients with neurofibromatosis type 1 (NF1)-related tumors. Neurofibromin (encoded by NF1) contains a RAS-GTPase activating domain. Mutations in the NF1 gene result in loss of production or reduced function of neurofibromin, causing hyperactive Ras signaling, that results in a wide spectrum of clinical findings, including benign or malignant NF1-associated tumors.

The established pharmacological class of mirdametinib is kinase inhibitor. In biochemical assays, mirdametinib inhibited the kinase activity of MEK1 and MEK2 with inhibitory constant (Ki) values of 1.1 nM and 0.79 nM, respectively. In a cell-free signaling cascade assay, mirdametinib inhibition of MEK reduced the level of phosphorylated ERK1 (pERK1) with a Ki value of 0.9 nM. Mirdametinib and its pharmacologically active unique human metabolite, M22, reduced the levels of pERK in cells in vitro, but M22 demonstrated considerably lower ability to reduce the levels of pERK (>91-times lower). In addition, the carboxylic acid metabolite of mirdametinib, PD-0315209, demonstrated inhibitory activity against MEK1 phosphorylation in a cell-based assay (IC50=1.3 μM) but was less active compared to mirdametinib (IC50=0.59 nM). The Applicant further demonstrated the antitumor activity of mirdametinib in a transgenic mouse model of NF1 neurofibroma (Nf1fl/fl;Dhh-Cre ). The Applicant cites literature [Jessen (2013), Jousma (2015)] to support the claim that in this model, deletion of Nf1 gene in Schwann cells exhibits similar neurofibroma growth and histology to human neurofibromas. In this model, spontaneous neurofibromas are detected by magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) at approximately 4 months of age. Daily treatment of animals with mirdametinib for two months (from 7 to 9 months of age) resulted in reduced tumor growth compared to untreated animals. Protein lysates of tumor tissue harvested from mice treated with mirdametinib showed reduced levels of pERK, a downstream target of MEK signaling. The results of the pharmacology studies support the proposed mechanism of action of mirdametinib and proof-of-concept for the proposed indication.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The Applicant’s Position: Mirdametinib was assessed in a series of nonclinical toxicology studies in alignment with applicable International Conference on Harmonization (ICH) guidance documents. The oral route of exposure was selected for these studies as the oral route is the intended clinical route of administration. The rat and dog were selected as the general toxicology species based on mirdametinib PK and metabolism properties compared across species. The definitive studies were performed in the US in accordance with the US Department of Health and Human Services, FDA, US Code of Federal Regulations, Title 21, Part 58: GLP for Nonclinical Laboratory Studies. Exploratory studies were conducted in mice, rats, dogs, and monkeys, while definitive toxicity studies were conducted in rats and dogs. Studies were based on daily oral (gavage) dosing with a limited number of studies involving IV administration. Mirdametinib was administered to rats and dogs in repeat-dose general toxicology studies up to 3 months. The 1-month studies in rats and dogs were followed by a 1-month recovery period. Mirdametinib is rapidly absorbed, with Tmax typically being observed within 1 to 2 hours of oral administration. Exposure increases with dose in a generally dose proportional manner and minimal accumulation is observed following repeat dosing for either mirdametinib or its primary metabolite, PD-0315209. Absolute bioavailability of mirdametinib ranged from moderate (rat and monkey) to high (dog). Mirdametinib showed high protein binding in all evaluated species and was also found to be widely distributed into tissues, including exposure in the brain. Mirdametinib is extensively metabolized and primarily undergoes glucuronidation and oxidation of the parent compound. UGT1A6 and UGT2B7 are heavily involved in the metabolism of mirdametinib and its most abundant metabolite, PD-0315209, is produced by carboxylesterase variants CES1b and CES1c. Clinical safety

Data: See the Applicant’s Position below.

The Applicant’s Position: The pivotal safety data supporting the safety evaluation of mirdametinib is based on Study MEK-NF-201. The studies used to support the integrated summary of safety are described in Table 26. Data from 8 additional non pooled interventional studies and the compassionate use program were used as supportive safety information, for a total of 577 patients exposed to mirdametinib.

As agreed with the agency during the Pre-NDA meeting, the integrated safety data presents 4 clinical studies that were grouped into the following study pools based on the targeted underlying condition (NF1 PN or advanced cancers), dosing regimen (body surface area vs unit dosing), participants, and study design: • Pool 1: Study MEK-NF-201 and Study NF-106 in adult and pediatric participants with NF1 PN. The data from these studies were analyzed together as a single pool and are the primary safety data used to assess the safety profile of mirdametinib in adult and pediatric participants with NF1 PN. • Pool 2: Study A4581001 and Study A4581002 in adult participants with advanced cancers. The data from these studies were analyzed together as a single pool and are supportive safety data used to assess the safety profile of mirdametinib. This Assessment Aid will provide the safety data from Study MEK-NF-201, followed by Pool 1 and Pool 2, and supported by relevant data from healthy volunteers and the Global Pharmacovigilance database. Data will be compared to the MEK-NF-201 data as it serves as the primary basis for establishing the efficacy of mirdametinib in the treatment of participants with NF1 PN.

The FDA’s Assessment: FDA agrees with the Applicant’s description of the pivotal and supportive datasets included in the NDA to support the safety evaluation of mirdametinib. FDA independently reviewed exposure and safety data for the pivotal ReNeu study and supportive Study NF-106 in which safety data was analyzed together as Pool 1 to support the safety profile for adult and pediatric patients with NF1 PN treated with mirdametinib 2 mg/m2 BID (maximum 4 mg BID); 3 weeks on/1 week off.

A 90-day updated safety analysis with corresponding datasets for Pool 1 (data cut-off date of September 19, 2024) was submitted to supplement the summary of clinical safety. The 90-day safety update included an additional 9 months of safety data. There were no new patients enrolled into Pool 1 since the initial data cut (Primary Analysis). Study NF-106 was completed prior to the primary analysis and there was no additional data in the 90-day safety update. The 90-day safety update did not identify new safety signals not already discussed in this review.

FDA reviewed but did not independently verify the data from Pool 2 which included Study A4581001 and Study A4581002 in adult patients with advanced cancers.
