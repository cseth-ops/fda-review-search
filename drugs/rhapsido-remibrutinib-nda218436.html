Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application The Applicant has conducted a comprehensive program of pharmacology, PK, and toxicology studies to support clinical development and the NDA submission of remibrutinib (referred to during development as LOU064). All pivotal toxicity studies have been submitted and reviewed previously under IND 131325. The impurities as well as some pharmacology studies have been reviewed herein under NDA 218436. The key findings from the nonclinical studies are cited and summarized in this review to support the approval of NDA 218436. Pharmacological studies demonstrated that remibrutinib inhibited both unphosphorylated and phosphorylated BTK with a >200-fold selectivity for BTK over BTK related enzymes TEC and BMX based on IC50 evaluation in enzymatic kinase assays. In cell-based assays, the inhibition of BTK was associated with reduced lymphocyte activation, inhibition of FcγR and FcεR, and reduced secretion of IL-6. Studies also demonstrated remibrutinib inhibition of collagen-induced platelet aggregation. In vivo, remibrutinib showed a response in two models of skin mast cell degranulation induced by FcγR and FcεR mediated hypersensitivity, a mouse reverse passive Arthus model, and a mouse passive cutaneous anaphylaxis model. These models are relevant for CSU. The Applicant has conducted subchronic and chronic toxicology studies with remibrutinib in both rats and dogs. Target organs of toxicity were identified as the lymph nodes (underdeveloped germinal centers, sinusoidal red blood cells), thyroid gland (follicular hypertrophy), liver (hepatocellular hypertrophy, multinucleated hepatocytes, pigment), pancreas (islet fibrosis, pigment), and blood (prolongation of bleeding, inhibition of platelet aggregation). The nonclinical findings were not considered clinically dose-limiting. The nonclinical general toxicology studies provided sufficient support for the clinical development program. In animal reproduction studies, remibrutinib was nonteratogenic and did not affect fertility in rats. Pregnant rats dosed with remibrutinib during the period of organogenesis did not show fetal malformations at doses up to 1000 mg/kg/day (approximately 126x the MRHD). In pregnant rabbits, remibrutinib treatment resulted in signs of maternal toxicity (clinical signs of toxicity, weight loss, and decreased food consumption) and adverse developmental outcomes (open/opaque eyes, small jaws, hyperflexion of forelimbs) at doses ≥300 mg/kg/day (approximately 141x the MRHD). In a pre- and postnatal development study in rats, potentially remibrutinib-related findings were observed at the highest dose of 1000 mg/kg/day dose (approximately 194x the MRHD). Toxicity in two maternal rats resulted in early euthanasia of these animals with one animal exhibiting reduced food consumption, reduced body weight, and poor clinical signs followed by delivery of five stillborn pups and five small, cold, weak pups that were terminated on Postnatal Day 0 to 1. Safety pharmacology

A complete battery of safety pharmacology studies has been conducted with remibrutinib. The remibrutinib IC50 at human ether-à-go-go-related gene channels expressed in HEK293 cells was calculated to be 1.4μM. The effects of remibrutinib on central nervous system function and the respiratory system were examined using a functional observation battery in a single-dose oral gavage study in Wistar rats. There were no effects on autonomic, neurologic, behavioral observations or for respiratory rate, tidal volume, minute volume, or peak inspiratory or expiratory flow observed at up to 1000 mg/kg. The effects of remibrutinib on cardiovascular system function were examined in a single-dose oral gavage telemetry study in Beagle dogs. No effects on body temperatures, heart rates, arterial pressures, or ECG parameters (including PQ, RR, QRS, QT, and QTv) were observed at doses up to 450 mg/kg.

Nonclinical ADME / toxicokinetics

In rats, remibrutinib was rapidly absorbed, with a bioavailability of 7 to 29% after oral administration. A single oral dose of remibrutinib resulted in broad distribution in all tissues except for the brain, lens of the eye, spinal cord, and white fat, with a Tmax of 0.5 to 8 hours. Similar distribution was noted in both sexes. Specific retention of total radiolabeled components in the pigmented uveal tract/retina was observed. The highest exposures (based on AUClast) were observed for the kidney medulla, small intestine mucosa, skin, cecum mucosa, kidney cortex, liver, and uveal tract/retina. Following IV or oral administration in rats, remibrutinib appeared to be eliminated via metabolism with <4% of the parent compound detected in excreta and a pattern of metabolites that was qualitatively and quantitatively similar. In dogs, a single oral dose of remibrutinib resulted in moderate absorption (41.9%) with a bioavailability of 13.5% and a median Tmax of 2 hours. Remibrutinib was distributed into tissues (volume of distribution at steady state [Vss] =1.26 L/kg) and exhibited high clearance ([CL] =187 L/h/kg). Excretion occurred mainly in the feces, with excretion by urine representing a minor fraction. Remibrutinib was associated with rapid metabolism in the mouse, rat, dog, and human, with 31 metabolites identified across all species. The major metabolic pathways consisted of N- demethylation, followed by glutathione or cysteine adducts and hydrogenation leading to M11 and M17 formation observed in the mouse, rat, and human, or O-dealkylation leading to M22 observed in all species, and glucuronidation leading to M7 observed in all species. Two metabolites formed by C-hydroxylation (M25) and C-hydroxylation and N-hydroxylation (M27) were only detected in in vitro human hepatocyte incubation with only M25 also observed in vivo at levels of 1.12% of the exposure based on AUC0-24h. The were no major human metabolites identified. Very low levels of degradation were observed for remibrutinib following incubation in blood or plasma from the rat, dog, or human for up to 2 hours with preferential distribution to the plasma in all tested species and blood distribution appearing to be concentration independent. Remibrutinib highly bound to plasma proteins with no concentration dependence: 89.8%, 92.8%, and 95.4% in the rat, dog, and human, respectively. Formation of covalent drug-protein adducts was observed in human liver microsomes and human hepatocytes. Coincubation with glutathione in human liver microsomes resulted in a 55% decrease in covalent drug-protein adducts, suggesting that glutathione-mediated activation of remibrutinib contributes to the formation of these adducts. Formation of covalent drug-protein adducts was time-dependent in human, dog, and rat blood and plasma, with adduct formation higher in plasma than blood, and with adduct formation in rat and dog plasma higher than that observed in human plasma. Remibrutinib covalently bound plasma proteins without metabolic activation at high levels. A 2-Week Rat Study (1470733; 42325 TSR) In a 2-week oral repeat-dose toxicity study in Wistar rats (10/sex/group), animals were administered doses of 0 (vehicle), 30, 100, 300, and 1000 mg/kg/day of remibrutinib. Recovery groups (5/sex for the control and 1000 mg/kg/day dose groups) were included to assess reversibility of the findings. Mortality was observed in one female administered 100 mg/kg/day of remibrutinib which was considered potentially related to test article induced prolongation of bleeding during a tail-tip bleeding test. Target tissues were identified as the blood, lymph nodes (decreased paracortex in mesenteric, sinusoidal red blood cells), spleen (decreased periarteriolar lymphoid sheaths and decreases in marginal zone), thyroid (minimal follicular hypertrophy), and liver (increased mitosis, periportal vacuolation, focal necrosis, and inflammatory cell foci). Mild, reversible increases in WBC, lymphocytes (including B-cells and T-cells) were observed in both sexes administered ≥100 mg/kg/day of remibrutinib. Due to changes in hemostasis at ≥30 mg/kg/day, the Applicant did not establish any no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) in this study; however, given the hemostatic effects are related to the pharmacology of remibrutinib and are monitorable, a NOAEL was considered to be 30 mg/kg/day with an associated Day 14 AUC0-24h of 13000 ng*h/mL in males and 31900 ng*h/mL in females.

Carcinogenicity

Mouse Carcinogenicity Study (1870307; 8411537) A 26-week oral carcinogenicity study of remibrutinib in Tg.rasH2 mice showed no evidence of carcinogenicity in mice (25/sex/dose) dosed by oral gavage with 0 (water), 0 (vehicle), 150, 500, and 1500-mg/kg/day remibrutinib once daily. The Agency concurred with the dose selection for the study on December 2, 2019. Remibrutinib had no effects on survival rates. The remibrutinib treatment groups did not exhibit increases in the incidence of tumor findings. Non-neoplastic findings included decreases in germinal center cellularity in the spleen and mesenteric lymph nodes and findings of hepatocyte cytoplasmic eosinophilic inclusions, karyocytomegaly, and/or multinucleated hepatocytes in the liver. A positive control group (N-methyl-N-nitrosourea; 75 mg/kg/day) showed expected increases in tumor incidences in the hemolymphoreticular tissue, stomach, and skin. Remibrutinib was negative for carcinogenicity under these study conditions.

Rat Carcinogenicity Study (1870308; 8425081) A 104-week oral carcinogenicity study of remibrutinib in Wistar rats (50/sex/dose) showed no evidence of carcinogenicity at oral doses of 0 (water), 0 (vehicle), 30, 100, and 300 mg/kg/day remibrutinib once daily. The Agency concurred with the dose selection for the study on September 23, 2019. Remibrutinib had no effects on survival rates. No remibrutinib-related neoplastic changes were noted up to the highest tested dose of 300 mg/kg/day. Non-neoplastic microscopic findings included findings in the lymphoid tissues (sinusoidal red blood cells, sinusoidal dilatation, focal angiomatous hyperplasia, diffuse reticuloendothelial hypertrophy/hyperplasia, and/or pigmented macrophages), thyroid (follicular cell hyperplasia), and pancreas (islet fibrosis). Remibrutinib was negative for carcinogenicity under these study conditions.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Remibrutinib did not affect fertility and was nonteratogenic in rats. In rabbits, remibrutinib treatment resulted in maternal toxicity and adverse developmental outcomes at doses ≥300 mg/kg/day. In the rat pre- and postnatal development study, remibrutinib treatment resulted in increased number of stillborn pups, dead pups, and a smaller litter size at 1000 mg/kg/day.

Fertility Study (1770589) In a fertility study, male and female rats (Wistar, 24/sex/dose) were dosed by oral gavage with 0, 100, 300, or 1000 mg/kg/day remibrutinib. Males were dosed for 9 weeks starting from 28 days

prior to mating and through mating. Females were dosed for 14 days prior to mating, through mating, and until Gestation Day (GD) 6. Females were sacrificed on GD 13 for uterine content examinations. No treatment-related findings were observed in any of the fertility parameters evaluated. Therefore, the NOAEL was 1000 mg/kg/day.

No treatment-related effects were observed in any of the key parameters. The NOAEL was 1000 mg/kg/day, corresponding to a Cmax of 4370 ng/mL and AUC0-24h of 49200 ng*h/mL. In another embryo-fetal developmental study (1670382), pregnant rabbits (New Zealand White, 20/dose) were dosed by oral gavage with 0, 100, 300, or 450 mg/kg/day remibrutinib during GDs 7 to 20, sacrificed on GD 29, and examined for fetal malformations and variations. Mortality (euthanasia) occurred at ≥300 mg/kg/day. One doe administered 300 mg/kg/day and the entire group of does administered 450 mg/kg/day were terminated during the period of GD 14 to 22 due to severe toxicities including abortion, clinical signs, weight loss, and decreases in feed consumption. The clinical signs included subdued/decreased activity, cold to touch, irregular respiration, decreased fecal output, pale pinnae, partially closed eyes, and red liquid material. The 450-mg/kg/day dose group was excluded from the analysis of fetal malformations and variations due to severe maternal toxicity. The 300-mg/kg/day dose group showed slight but statistically nonsignificant increases in a few external malformations such as eye opening, small jaws, and hyperflexion of forelimbs. No increases in the incidence of malformations or variations were observed in the 100-mg/kg/day dose group. The NOAEL was 100 mg/kg/day, corresponding to a Cmax of 2360 ng/mL and AUC0-24h of 8990 ng*h/mL.
