Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

From a nonclinical perspective, no approvability issues have been identified for etrasimod at the proposed clinical dose of 2 mg/day.

Nonclinical studies conducted with etrasimod include pharmacology studies, safety pharmacology studies, pharmacokinetics, repeat-dose toxicology studies of up to six months duration in rats and nine months duration in dogs, a standard genotoxicity battery, two-year carcinogenicity studies in mice and rats, a standard battery of developmental and reproductive toxicity studies in rats and rabbits, and an in vitro phototoxicity study.

Etrasimod is a sphingosine-1-phosphate receptor subtype 1, 4, and 5 (S1P1,4,5) agonist with no significant pharmacological activity at S1P2 or S1P3 receptors. Etrasimod agonism of S1P1 reduces lymphocyte egress from lymph nodes, resulting in decreased circulating lymphocytes.

In a standard battery of nonclinical safety pharmacology studies, etrasimod had no significant effects on hERG channel current in vitro, cardiovascular function in dogs, CNS parameters in rats, or respiratory function in rats. In an in vitro assay using human atrial cardiomyocytes, etrasimod activated IKAch channels at concentrations less than the predicted steady state Cmax and Ctrough values at the clinical etrasimod dose of 2 mg/day; this finding may provide an underlying mechanism for the transient etrasimod-induced decrease in heart rate observed in clinical trials.

In repeat-dose toxicity studies, mortalities, decreased body weight gain, and histopathological findings in the liver and thymus were observed in rats at exposures > 1000-fold the exposure at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 2 mg/day (based on AUC comparison). No adverse effects were observed in rats or dogs at exposures up to 738- and 446-fold, respectively, the exposure at the MRHD (based on AUC comparison). Dose-dependent decreases in circulating lymphocytes were observed in both rats and dogs and correlated with lymphoid depletion in the lymph nodes and spleen of rats only; these findings were attributed to the pharmacological activity of etrasimod and not considered adverse.

Nonclinical ADME / toxicokinetics

Following the first dose of etrasimod on GD 6, plasma concentrations for all post-dose samples (i.e., 1, 4, and 24 hours post-dose) were below the lower limit of quantification (< 5.00 ng/mL) for all control animals as well as for one animal at 0.4 mg/kg/day and one animal at 4 mg/kg/day. All other treated animals had measurable concentrations of drug. There were no deviations or documented events noted in the dosing records; thus, no explanation was provided for the observed lack of drug exposure in these animals. Toxicokinetic analyses were reported both with and without the apparently anomalous data from these animals, but only the analysis excluding the anomalous values is presented below. No unexplained abnormalities were observed in the toxicokinetic data collected on GD 17 or PND 20.

Following a single dose (GD 6), Cmax and AUC of etrasimod increased approximately proportionally with increasing dose. Tmax of etrasimod occurred between 1-4 hours and tended to occur later with increasing dose.

On GD 17, Cmax and AUC of etrasimod increased approximately proportionally with increasing dose. Tmax of etrasimod occurred at 4 hours for all dose levels. No significant accumulation of etrasimod was observed between GD 6 and 20.

On PND 20, Cmax and AUC of etrasimod increased approximately proportionally with increasing dose. Tmax of etrasimod occurred between 1-2 hours with no clear effect of dose. A slight loss of exposure to etrasimod was observed at 0.4 and 2 mg/kg/day (PND 20 AUC values 64% and 69% those observed on GD 6, respectively) but not at 4 mg/kg/day.

Embryo-fetal development

Dose-Range Finding Studies in Rats and Rabbits

Two dose-range finding studies were conducted with etrasimod to determine the doses for the definitive embryo-fetal development (EFD) study in rats. In the first study using doses of 25, 75, 150, and 250 mg/kg/day (Study TX14016), severe maternal toxicity and decreased intrauterine survival were observed such that, among all treated groups, there was only a single viable fetus from the 25 mg/kg/day group and no others. The single fetus in the 25 mg/kg/day group (121 times the clinical dose, based on HED) was observed with a meningocele.

In the second study using doses of 2, 5, 10, and 20 mg/kg/day (Study TX14025), increased post- implantation loss with corresponding lower numbers/proportions of viable fetuses was observed at ≥ 5 mg/kg/day. A meningocele was observed in a single fetus each in the 5 and 10 mg/kg/day groups, and spina bifida was observed in one fetus at 10 mg/kg/day. These findings occurred at > 21 times the exposure at the clinical dose, based on AUC (based on the available AUC data at 4 mg/kg/day) or 24 times the clinical dose, based on HED.

While these findings suggest the potential of etrasimod exposure during organogenesis to result in neural defects, the toxicological significance of these findings is unclear due to the sporadic nature of the findings, lack of clear dose effect, and overall developmental failure at higher doses. However, the presence of these findings is unsurprising, as S1P receptors play an essential role in embryogenesis. Based on the totality of findings in these studies, the doses in the definitive EFD study in rats were set to 1, 2, and 4 mg/kg/day.

In the dose-range finding study in rabbits using doses of 2, 25, 75, and 150 mg/kg/day (Study TX14018), moribundity or abortion were observed in 5/6 females at 75 mg/kg/day and all

females at 150 mg/kg/day (726 and 1452 times the clinical dose, respectively, based on HED). The single surviving female at 75 mg/kg/day was observed to have increased post-implantation loss. At 25 mg/kg/day (242 times the clinical dose, based on HED), increased post-implantation loss and a lower number of viable fetuses were observed. No adverse effects were observed at 2 mg/kg/day (19 times the clinical dose, based on HED, or less than the clinical dose, based on AUC). Based on these findings, the doses in the definitive EFD study in rabbits were set to 2, 10, and 20 mg/kg/day.

Clinical efficacy

APD334-301

Trial Design

APD334-301 was a multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of etrasimod 2 mg over 52 weeks. The trial population included subjects with a modified Mayo Score (mMS) of 4 to 9 who had an inadequate response to, loss of response to, or intolerance to at least 1 of the following: oral 5-amniosalicylic acid (5-ASA) compounds, corticosteroids, thiopurines, biologic therapy (TNF blocker, vedolizumab, anti- interleukin [anti-IL] 12/23), or Janus kinase (JAK) inhibitors. A total of 433 subjects, including 1 adolescent, from 37 countries were randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either etrasimod 2 mg once daily or matching placebo once daily for up to 52 weeks. The trial included an initial 12- week treatment period, after which, subjects who met the predefined definition for UC worsening2 were eligible to enter into the open-label extension trial (APD334-303). All other subjects were to continue on their randomized treatment for an additional 40 weeks. Subjects that met the predefined definition for UC worsening during this 40-week treatment period could switch to the open-label extension trial. Randomization was stratified by prior treatment status (i.e., naïve to biologic or JAK inhibitor therapy at trial entry [yes/no]), baseline corticosteroid use (yes/no), and baseline disease activity (mMS: [4-6] or [7-9]). Clinical safety

The safety evaluation of etrasimod for the treatment of moderately to severely active UC focused on data from the randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials APD334-301, APD334-302, and APD334-003, and is supplemented by additional data from an integrated summary of safety (ISS). The ISS was a pool of controlled data from studies in subjects with UC including APD334-301, APD334-302, and APD334-003 as well as uncontrolled data from the open-label extension studies APD334-303, APD334-005, and ES101002.

APD334-301 was a phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of etrasimod 2 mg for the treatment of moderately to severely active UC. Eligible subjects were randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either etrasimod 2 mg once daily or matching placebo once daily for up to 52 weeks. After Week 12, subjects who met the prespecified definition of UC worsening were eligible to enter the open-label extension study (APD334-303).

Safety data from APD334-302 and APD334-003 were pooled (“12-Week Safety Pool”) because the two study designs and populations were sufficiently similar, to better assess the adverse event profile for the 12-week trials. APD334-302 was a phase 3, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of etrasimod 2 mg for the treatment of moderately to severely active UC. Eligible subjects were randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either etrasimod 2 mg once daily or matching placebo once daily for up to 12 weeks. APD334-003 was a phase 2, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of etrasimod 2 mg and 1 mg for the treatment of moderately to severely active UC. Eligible subjects were randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio to receive either etrasimod 2 mg once daily, etrasimod 1 mg once daily, or matching placebo for up to 12 weeks. The 1 mg etrasimod arm of APD334-003 was not pooled because this dose was not evaluated in APD334-301 or APD334-302. Due to the differences in randomization ratios, pooled analysis results were adjusted for the study size.

The ISS consisted of controlled and uncontrolled data from studies in subjects with UC, as described previously. Since much of the data in the ISS was uncontrolled, the ISS was not considered to provide substantial evidence of effectiveness or safety. However, safety data were reviewed to assess for rare events given the extended duration of treatment for the open- label studies.

Clinical trial data were analyzed using JMP and JMP Clinical software. All safety assessments and conclusions are those of the clinical review team unless otherwise specified. The clinical review team recoded several adverse event terms using logical groupings in order to better ascertain the safety profile of etrasimod; refer to Table 107 in Section 16.5 of the Appendix. The clinical review team did not identify any major data quality or integrity issues that precluded performing a thorough safety review.

Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment

Moderately to severely active ulcerative colitis (UC) is a serious chronic disease associated with morbidity and mortality when inadequately treated. Multiple therapies are approved for the treatment of moderately to severely active UC; however, there remains a need for novel therapies as not all patients will respond or have continued response to any given treatment.

Etrasimod demonstrated efficacy in inducing clinical remission after 12 weeks of treatment with 2 mg daily, and in maintaining clinical remission when continued over 40 weeks of treatment. Efficacy was demonstrated in two adequate and well-controlled investigations: • APD334-301: A 52-week, randomized, double-blinded, placebo-controlled “treat-through” trial • APD334-302: A 12-week, randomized, double-blinded, placebo-controlled induction trial

In the 52-week, “treat-through” trial, etrasimod demonstrated efficacy in achieving the coprimary endpoints of clinical remission at Week 12 and Week 52, compared to placebo. In the 12-week induction trial, etrasimod demonstrated efficacy in achieving the primary endpoint of clinical remission at Week 12, compared to placebo. In both trials, efficacy was demonstrated on multiple clinically relevant secondary endpoints at Week 12 (APD334-301 and APD334-302) and Week 52 (APD334-301), compared to placebo. Overall, the results provided substantial evidence of effectiveness for etrasimod in the treatment of moderately to severely active UC in adults. Therefore, the Applicant has met the evidentiary standard for efficacy to support approval of etrasimod for the proposed indication.

The safety profile of etrasimod 2 mg once daily for up to 52 weeks was acceptable and supports approval of the dosing regimen. The safety database included data from the 52-week phase 3 trial APD334-301, the 12-week phase 3 trial APD334-302, and 12-week phase 2 trial APD334- 003. The size of the safety database was adequate to support approval of the proposed dosing regimen. However, the size of the controlled 52- week trial does not allow for a definitive characterization of the potential for rare, serious safety concerns (e.g., mortality, malignancy, progressive multifocal leukoencephalopathy [PML], cryptococcal meningitis) that have been observed in S1P receptor modulators, including ozanimod (ZEPOSIA) that is approved for the treatment of moderately to severely active UC. There are several known potential risks related to the S1P receptor modulator class which were observed in the etrasimod development program (i.e., infections, bradyarrhythmia and atrioventricular conduction delays, liver injury, macular edema, increased blood pressure, and respiratory effects), which were consistent with other S1P receptor modulators.
