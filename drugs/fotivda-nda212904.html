Trial Design

Study TIVO-3 was an open-label, randomized, controlled, multi-national, parallel-arm study to compare treatment with tivozanib to treatment with sorafenib in adult patients with histologically or cytologically confirmed RCC with a clear cell component who failed 2 or 3 prior systemic regimens, including a TKI other than sorafenib or tivozanib.

Patients were randomized 1:1 to receive either tivozanib or sorafenib using the Interactive Web Response System (IWRS). Randomization was stratified by the following factors: • IMDC prognostic category (favorable vs. intermediate vs. poor) • Prior therapy (2 prior TKIs vs. a prior checkpoint inhibitor plus a TKI vs. a prior TKI plus any other systemic agent)

Treatment began within 14 days of randomization. Tivozanib was administered 1.5 mg orally daily 3 weeks on/ 1 week off in each 4-week cycle and sorafenib 400 mg BID was administered orally continuously throughout each cycle. Note that 1.5 mg in the protocol refers to the dose of tivozanib hydrochloride, which is equivalent to the recommended dosage of 1.34 mg, referring to tivozanib free base.

Eligible patients were required to have recurrent metastatic RCC with a clear cell component that had progressed after 2 or 3 prior systemic regimens, one of which included a VEGFR TKI other than sorafenib or tivozanib. Postoperative or adjuvant systemic therapy was not counted as a prior therapy unless a recurrence was detected within 6 months of completion of adjuvant treatment. Patients were also required to have measurable disease per RECIST criteria and ECOG performance status of 0 or 1.

Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment Patients with relapsed or refractory advanced renal cell carcinoma following two or more prior systemic therapies may receive a TKI (different than one received previously) or an mTOR inhibitor on progression; however there is no therapy specifically approved in this line of treatment. Thus patients with advanced RCC who progressed on two prior lines of therapy, including a TKI, were evaluated in TIVO-3, which randomized 350 patients to tivozanib or sorafenib with a primary endpoint of PFS per BIRC assessmant.

The primary efficacy analysis for TIVO-3 demonstrated a PFS benefit per BIRC assessment for treatment with tivozanib (n=175) vs. sorafenib (n=175). The estimate of the hazard ratio for PFS was 0.73 (95% CI: 0.56 to 0.95, p-value of 0.016). Median PFS estimate was 5.59 months (95% CI: 4.83, 7.33) in the tivozanib arm vs. 3.88 months (95% CI: 3.71 to 5.55) in the sorafenib arm. Subgroup analyses were consistent across the prespecified stratification factors of IMDC risk category and prior therapy (2 prior TKIs vs. 1 prior TKI + prior CPI vs. 1 prior TKI + any other agent). The final median OS estimate was 16.39 months for the tivozanib arm vs. 19.15 months for the sorafenib arm, with a HR of 0.97 (95% CI: 0.75 to 1.24) for tivozinib. The ORR was 18% (95% CI: 12%, 24%) in the tivozanib arm vs. 8% (95% CI: 4%, 13%) in the sorafenib arm; these results were supportive of the overall efficacy of tivozanib vs. sorafenib.

The safety profile of tivozanib for patients on TIVO-3 was in line with the known safety profile described with other TKIs. Deaths due to an adverse event (excluding disease progression) occurred in 7.5% of tivozanib-treated patients on TIVO-3 and included pneumonia, hepatobiliary disorders, respiratory failure, myocardial infarction, cerebrovascular accident, and subdural hematoma. Permanent treatment discontinuation due to adverse events occurred in 21%. Grade 3-4 adverse events occurred in 67% of patients and the most common (>20%) all-grade adverse events were fatigue, hypertension, diarrhea, decreased appetite, nausea, dysphonia, hypothyroidism, cough, and stomatitis. Warnings and Precautions for tivozanib include hypertension and hypertensive crisis, cardiac failure, cardiac ischemia and arterial thromboembolic events, venous thromboembolic events, hemorrhagic events, proteinuria, thyroid dysfunction, risk of impaired wound healing, reversible posterior leukoencephalopathy syndrome, embryo-fetal toxicity, and allergic reactions to tartrazine.

According to 21 CFR, Part 314.50, Subpart B a regular approval for tivozanib is recommended. All review disciplines are in agreement.
