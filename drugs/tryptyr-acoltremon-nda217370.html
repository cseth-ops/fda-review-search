Pharmacology The published literature establishes that acoltremon (as WS-12) is a TRPM8 agonist like menthol. TRPM8, a calcium-permeable cation channel, is a cold sensor in humans. The activation of TRPM8 opens the channel and allows influx of calcium ions into the cell, resulting in cold thermal transduction and pain detection, among other effects (Izquierdo et al. 2021). The Applicant verified this activity in vitro (using cells transfected with rat TRPM8) and ex vivo

(using guinea pig corneas). In vivo, topical ocular acoltremon increased blinking immediately after administration.

Carcinogenicity

Carcinogenicity studies were not conducted with acoltremon. ICH S1A indicates that pharmaceuticals administered by the ocular route may not require carcinogenicity studies unless there is cause for concern or unless there is significant systemic exposure (March 1996). Alcon consulted with the Agency at the pre-NDA meeting held on March 4, 2024, on supporting data required for rodent carcinogenicity study waiver requests. Based on the Agency feedback, the Applicant used the following integrated approach to assess potential carcinogenicity risk of acoltremon to request a waiver of the studies. This rationale was presented to the Executive Carcinogenicity Assessment Committee for review of the waiver request. The committee agreed with the Division that in the absence of systemic exposure for acoltremon ophthalmic solution, 0.003%, there is no need to conduct carcinogenicity studies.

Carcinogenicity Data for Another Compound in the Same Class Acoltremon is also widely known as WS-12, a menthol derivative, and Flavor and Extract Manufacturers Association 4681. WS-12 is widely used as a food flavoring agent and is the most common agent used to create the “cool mint” flavor. WS-12 is GRAS for oral food exposure (refer to Pharmacology and Toxicology review of IND 147005 by Dr. McDougal). The Applicant indicated the lack of test-article-related carcinogenicity findings in mouse and rat 2-year feeding studies conducted with the cooling agent dl-menthol by the National Toxicology Program (National Toxicology Program 1979). In these studies, B6C3F1 mice (50/sex/group) were administered vehicle control (basal diet containing 2% corn oil) or 2000 or 4000 ppm mg/kg/day dl-menthol in the diet ad libitum 7 days per week for 104 weeks. Assuming a 0.03-kg mouse consumes 5 grams of food per day, these doses corresponded to 0, 334 and 667 mg/kg/day, respectively. Fischer 344 rats (50/sex/group) were administered vehicle control (basal diet containing 2% corn oil) or 3750 or 7500 ppm mg/kg/day dl-menthol in the diet ad libitum 7 days per week for 104 weeks; assuming a 0.4-kg rat consumes 20 grams of food per day, these doses corresponded to 0, 188 and 375 mg/kg/day, respectively. Animals were evaluated for mortality, clinical observations, masses, body weight, and food consumption, gross and microscopic pathology. All animals survived to the scheduled necropsy and there were no test-article-related effects on clinical observations, body weight, food consumption, or gross pathology findings. There were no test-article-related preneoplastic or neoplastic findings up to and including the highest doses tested in mice (667 mg/kg/day) and rats (375 mg/kg/day). Based on the available data, dl-menthol did not produce any evidence of a carcinogenic effect in B6C3F1 mice and Fischer 344 rats up to the highest tested doses (mice: 667 mg/kg/day; rats: 375 mg/kg/day).

Prenatal and postnatal development

Not performed based on justified waiver request.

Reproductive Study Waiver Requests Alcon consulted with the Agency at the End of Phase 2 meeting held on January 28, 2022, about embryo-fetal toxicity studies, as per ICH M3(R2) (January 2010) and ICH S5(R3) (May 2021) guidance and what supporting data would be required for waiver requests for fertility and early embryonic development and pre- and postnatal development studies. The Agency indicated that waivers should include a thorough discussion of the risk based on the drug class, pharmacological activity, pharmacokinetics in humans and animals, genotoxicity and toxicity results, clinical safety findings, and supportive information from the literature as appropriate. The Applicant’s justification for waiving the studies (submitted in Module 1.12.5 [Other Correspondence]) was that the nonclinical safety assessment program for Tryptyr (acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003%) did not suggest a risk for the male and/or the female reproductive system. (1) Preclinical findings in repeated-dose toxicity studies were negative for reproductive toxicity in rats (systemic up to 28 days), rabbits (ocular up to 6 months) and dogs (ocular up to 6 months) based on anatomic pathology findings. Reviewer’s comment: Note that at the start of the 6-month studies, neither the rabbit (age of 4 to 5 months old while sexually mature at 6 months (Macari and Machado 1978) nor the dog

(age of 5 to 7 months old while sexually mature at 6 to 12 months (Shayne C. Gad and Chengelis 1998) were fully sexually mature. (2) There were no findings of maternal toxicity, embryo-fetal survival, intrauterine growth, or morphological development in embryo-fetal development toxicology studies in rats and rabbits. (3) Following topical ocular application, the systemic exposure to the test article was considered negligible due to lack of pharmacological activity and systemic toxicity. Reviewer’s comment: This reviewer agrees with the negligible systemic exposure. (4) The Applicant indicated that they have reviewed the published literature related to acoltremon ophthalmic solution use in pregnant and lactating women and the effects of acoltremon ophthalmic solution on male and female fertility with no results. So far, in the clinical development program for acoltremon, 70 female subjects (aged 15 to 44 years) have been exposed to acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.003% and 3 female subjects (aged 15 to 44 years) were exposed to acoltremon ophthalmic solution 0.00014%. Reviewer’s comment: Based on the absence of systemic findings and negligible systemic exposure observed during preclinical development, this reviewer judged that the requirements for fertility and pre- and postnatal development studies may be waived.
