Treosulfan (10 μg/mL) had no effect on contractions of isolated guinea pig ileum produced by acetylcholine or histamine (Leo, 1978b). It also had no effect on isoprenaline-induced dilation of isolated cat tracheal muscle following exposure to concentrations ranging from 3 to 20 μg/mL.

In vivo evaluation of cardiovascular effects of treosulfan in female fasting adult mongrel dogs showed that i.v. dosing of 5-200 mg/kg of treosulfan had no effect on arterial or venous blood pressure, or the carotid occlusion reflex (Leo, 1978b). Moreover, 4-hour ECG evaluations in these treosulfan-exposed dogs were unremarkable. Female adult rats exposed to i.v. doses of treosulfan at 20 mg/kg, then 40 mg/kg 30 minutes later exhibited no changes in blood pressure.

Respiratory and CNS Function Mice that received 100 mg/kg of treosulfan i.p. were not protected from convulsions induced by i.v. injection of pentetrazole or from the convulsant effects induced by electroshock (Leo, 1978b). Treosulfan (50 mg/kg p.o.) offered no analgesic effect to mice following i.p. injection of 50 mg/kg of acetic acid. It also offered no conduction anesthesia to electric stimulation in tail nerves following i.v. injection of 2% treosulfan. Ham/ICR mice administered 150 or 300 mg/kg of treosulfan i.p. showed no signs of abnormal behavior. A fasted female dog administered 200 mg/kg of treosulfan p.o. exhibited no change in respiration, heart rate, or pupil size for 4 hours post-dose.

” The Office of Clinical Pharmacology did not recommend approval then because of issues regarding the veracity of the primary study endpoint. A summary of key review issues and recommendations from the original clinical pharmacology review of NDA 214759 is provided below:

“The only new data submitted in this re-submission were in vitro metabolism study results which determined that treosulfan is metabolized by CYP2D6 and its monoepoxide intermediate is metabolized by CYP2C8. “The previous review indicated the need for the following PMR: Conduct an in vitro study to evaluate treosulfan and epoxide metabolites as substrates of major cytochrome P450 enzymes consistent with FDA guidance.

The in vitro assessment of treosulfan metabolism showed involvement of CYP2D6 (t1/2 with rCYP2D6 = 108 min) and was noted in the previous review. Based on an updated communication with the Applicant …, we have determined that the above PMR is no longer needed.”

“Based on a review of the submitted reports of the in vitro studies, the monoepoxide … and diepoxide metabolites … are not meaningfully metabolized by CYP enzymes. Of note, the t1/2 values for positive control compounds for the respective rCYPs were very short. No labeling update is needed based on these results.”

6 Clinical Pharmacology

6.1 Executive Summary

The Applicant is seeking approval of treosulfan as part of conditioning treatment prior to allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (alloHSCT) in adult patients with acute myeloid leukemia (AML) and myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS). The proposed dosing regimen is treosulfan 10 g/m2/day for 3 consecutive days (Days -4, -3, and -2) in combination with fludarabine 30 mg/m2/day for 5 consecutive days (Days -6 through -2) prior to allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (alloHSCT; Day 0).

Pivotal evidence to support efficacy in the proposed population is based on the results of Study MC-FLudT.14/L Trial II. Key review issues include the adequacy of the proposed treosulfan dosing regimen in adult patients, pediatric extrapolation to identify a treosulfan dosing regimen appropriate for pediatric patients >1 year of age, and evaluation of the Applicant’s proposals for patients with organ impairment. Clinical efficacy

Study MC-FludT.14/L Trial II

Trial Design

Study MC-FludT.14/L Trial II was a phase III open-label, randomized clinical trial to compare treosulfan-based conditioning therapy with busulfan-based reduced-intensity conditioning (RIC) prior to allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation in patients with AML or MDS considered ineligible for myeloablative conditioning regimens. The first patient was enrolled on 13-Jun-2014 and the last patient completed 25-Jan-2018.

Per statistical analysis plan, “MC-FludT.14/L was designed as a multicentre, multinational, randomised, active-controlled, group-sequential phase III trial to show non-inferiority of Treosulfan-based conditioning therapy compared to Busulfan-based reduced-intensity conditioning (RIC) prior to allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation in patients with Acute Myeloid Leukaemia (AML) or Myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) considered ineligible to standard conditioning regimens.”

The study was designed as a non-inferiority trial with a primary endpoint of event-free survival (EFS) within 2 years. The study population consisted of adult patients with AML or MDS who were not eligible for myeloablative conditioning regimens. Patients were randomized to receive either reduced-intensity conditioning with busulfan and fludarabine, or the treosulfan and fludarabine.

Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment AML and MDS have high rates of morbidity and mortality, and allogeneic hematopoietic stem cell transplantation (HSCT) is required for curative therapy in patients with high-risk disease. Currently, there are no drugs approved for use as a preparative regimen prior to allogeneic HSCT for AML or MDS, and regimens are not standardized. There is a need for additional conditioning regimen options for patients with AML and MDS proceeding to allogeneic HSCT.

MC-FludT.14/L Trial II (NCT00822393) was a multicenter, randomized, active-controlled trial comparing treosulfan to busulfan in combination with fludarabine as a preparative regimen for allogeneic HSCT. Eligible patients included adults 18 to 70 years old with AML in complete remission or MDS, Karnofsky performance status > 60%, and hematopoietic cell transplantation comorbidity index [HCT CI] score > 2 for patients < 50 years of age. Patients were randomized to receive treosulfan 10 g/m² daily on days -4, -3 and -2 or to busulfan 0.8 mg/kg every 6 hours on days -4 and -3 in combination with fludarabine 30 mg/m2 daily on days -6, -5, -4, -3 and -2, and hematopoietic stem cell transplantation on day 0. Antithymocyte globulin was administered as well to patients with unrelated donors.

The study was designed as a noninferiority trial with a primary endpoint of event-free survival (EFS) with 2 years of follow-up after transplantation. Events were defined as disease relapse, graft failure, or death. The noninferiority margin on the hazard ratio scale was 1.3. If noninferiority was demonstrated, then the primary endpoint would be tested for superiority. Overall survival (OS) was a secondary endpoint, but there was no alpha allocated to testing OS.

Study MC FludT.14/L Trial II was considered a positive study based on the primary endpoint of EFS (non-inferiority p-value = 0.0000164 as compared to an efficacy boundary of 0.000149). Based on FDA-adjudication, EFS showed a hazard ratio (HR) of 0.65 (95% CI: 0.50, 0.85) in the ITT population. However, EFS as defined by the Applicant reflects a composite of efficacy (relapse and death) and safety (graft failure) measures, and the clinical meaningfulness of this definition is unclear for both the AML and MDS populations. Additional analyses of EFS and related endpoints (including RFS) were conducted to explore the robustness of the result to definition and population. Therefore, FDA relied on OS, defined as the time from randomization until death from any cause, to establish the efficacy of treosulfan.
