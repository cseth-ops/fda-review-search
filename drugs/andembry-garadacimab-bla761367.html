Clinical Pharmacology No new clinical pharmacology data were provided in this CR resubmission. On resubmission, the Applicant addressed clinical pharmacology comments in the proposed PI. These included changes to Sections 7 (Drug Interactions), 12.2 (Pharmacodynamics), and 12.3 (Pharmacokinetics). The updated proposed PI is acceptable from a clinical pharmacology perspective. As concluded during the initial review cycle, no issues preclude approval from a APPEARS THIS clinical pharmacology perspective. WAY ON ORIGINAL Clinical/Statistics The initial BLA submission included results from a single, adequate and well-controlled phase 3 trial (Trial 3001). This trial was a 6-month, randomized, double-blind, placebo- controlled trial evaluating 64 adult and adolescent subjects with HAE Type I and II who received either garadacimab 200mg or placebo monthly.

Trial 3001 met its primary endpoint, demonstrating a statistically significant reduction in the time-normalized number of investigator-confirmed HAE attacks per month. This endpoint is an established and clinically meaningful outcome for HAE prophylaxis development programs. The garadacimab group showed a mean of 0.27 attacks per month (95% CI: 0.05, 0.49) compared to 2.01 attacks per month (95% CI: 1.44, 2.57) in the placebo group. Substantial evidence of effectiveness of garadacimab in patients with HAE was established using data from Trial 3001 and confirmatory evidence based on mechanistic data. The available safety data demonstrated that garadacimab is safe for its intended use.

In the BLA resubmission, no new efficacy data were required or submitted. The Applicant provided updated exposure and safety datasets through June 15, 2024 (previous data cutoff was May 22, 2023). The clinical review team analyzed the updated datasets and modified the drug exposure and frequency of injection site reactions in Section 6 of the PI, accordingly. Safety pharmacology

Safety pharmacology parameters were incorporated into the pivotal repeat-dose 26-week monkey toxicology study. With intravenous (IV) doses up to 100 mg/kg/week and subcutaneous (SC) doses up to 200 mg/kg/week, no treatment-related effects on electrocardiogram, blood pressure, respiration rate, or pulse rate were observed during the 26-week Treatment Period. In a mouse study evaluating respiratory parameters, garadacimab at IV doses of 3, 10, 30 and 100 mg/kg or SC doses of 60 and 200 mg/kg produced no statistically significant or biologically relevant effects on respiratory rate, tidal volume, or minute volume at any postdose time point. The no observed adverse effect levels (NOAELs) were identified as 100 mg/kg (IV) and 200 mg/kg (SC).

13.1.3. Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion/Pharmacokinetics A single-dose pharmacokinetic (PK) study using the IV and SC routes was conducted in cynomolgus monkeys. The SC bioavailability of garadacimab (6 mg/kg) was ~66% in monkeys. Mean estimated t1/2 values for garadacimab in the single-dose PK study with monkeys ranged from 147 to 581 h (IV) and 206 to 424 h (SC).

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Repeat-Dose Toxicity The Applicant characterized the toxicity profile of garadacimab by conducting 4-week mouse and 26-week monkey toxicology studies with garadacimab using the IV and SC routes. Immunogenicity in mice precluded conducting longer toxicology studies in this species.

CSL312: Repeat-Dose Toxicity Study by Intravenous and Subcutaneous Administration to CD-1 Mice for 4 Weeks Followed by an 8-Week Recovery Period (Study # (b) (4) )

Key Study Findings • In a 4-week toxicity and toxicokinetic (TK) study, CD-1 mice (12/sex/group) received garadacimab at IV doses of 0, 3, 10, 30, and 100 mg/kg and SC doses of 0, 60, and 200 mg/kg (SC). • There were 23 unscheduled deaths during the study (16 males and 7 females). The deaths of one HD IV male, one low dose (LD) SC male, and two LD SC females were considered to be due to the blood sampling procedure. The deaths and/or premature euthanasia of the remaining animals, primarily in the LD IV group, were attributed to immunogenicity based upon observed adverse clinical signs, although there were no histopathological findings.

• Significant prolongations of the aPTT were observed for males (up to 127%) and females (up to 123%) in all garadacimab treatment groups (IV and SC) relative to control animals at the end of the 4-week Treatment Period. • The target organs of toxicity were identified as the lymph nodes (axillary and inguinal) and SC injection sites. An increase in incidence and severity of minimal to slight inflammatory cell infiltrates at the SC injection sites (dorsal 1 and 2) were observed for females in the 60 and 200 mg/kg SC treatment groups at the end of the Treatment Period. • An increased incidence and/or severity of minimal to slight lymphocyte cellularity in the axillary and/or inguinal lymph nodes was observed for males and/or females in the 60 and 200 mg/kg SC treatment groups at the end of the 4-week Treatment Period. • Systemic exposure (maximum plasma concentration (Cmax)) and area under the concentration-time curve (AUC) from 0 to 96 h) to garadacimab increased in a dose- proportional manner for males and females from 3 to 100 mg/kg (Day 1) and 10 to 100 mg/kg (Day 29) following IV administration. Moderate accumulation of garadacimab was observed in the IV dosing groups (1.56 to 3.08) and SC groups (1.38 to 2.33) during the study period. No clear differences in systemic exposure between males and females were observed. • The determined NOAEL was the high SC dose of 200 mg/kg.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

The potential reproductive toxicity of garadacimab was evaluated in fertility and early embryonic development studies with male and female rabbits, an embryofetal development study with female rabbits, and a pre- and postnatal development study with female rabbits. Rabbits were determined to be a pharmacologically relevant species. • Garadacimab binds with high affinity to rabbit FXIIa and βFXIIa as well as FXII zymogen. Data demonstrated that rabbits are a pharmacologically relevant species for toxicological investigation (REP-21280).

• Garadacimab inhibited dextran sulfate-mediated FXIIa activity in diluted plasma from rabbit, human, and other animal species confirming rabbits as a pharmacologically relevant species and supporting their use in toxicological investigations (REP-21282). In correspondence dated March 29, 2019, the Division agreed with the Applicant’s reproductive toxicity program. Rats were not a pharmacologically relevant species. Rabbits are not commonly used in male and female fertility studies. • The duration of the spermatogenic cycle is 48 days in the rabbit as compared to 52 days in rats, which are more commonly used in fertility studies. Treatment of rabbits with garadacimab once every 3 days for a total of 15 doses should cover the majority of the duration of the spermatogenic cycle in rabbits. Rabbits were not used in general toxicology studies with garadacimab, so there were no microscopic evaluations of the testes in longer studies. The assessment of fertility using male rabbits appears to be generally acceptable as the fertility study covered the majority of the spermatogenic cycle. • Female rabbits differ from female humans in that they do not have an estrus cycle. Females ovulate once they are mounted by a male (induced ovulation). The female rabbit can become impregnated any time, although it is not recommended that females be bred before 4 weeks of age. The assessment of fertility using female rabbits appears to be generally acceptable, although potential effects of estrus cycle could not be determined. Rabbits are commonly used in embryofetal development studies. Pre-and postnatal development studies are not commonly conducted with female rabbits. The Applicant appropriately adjusted dosing to cover the duration of the gestation and lactation periods in rabbits. Monitoring of the growth and development of the F1 generation was also appropriately adjusted for the use of rabbits.

Fertility and Early Embryonic Development Studies Conducted With Male and Female Rabbits The 26-week repeat-dose monkey study was conducted in sexually immature male and female animals, and estrous cycling was not monitored in females. Therefore, an assessment of the effects of garadacimab on male and female fertility was not possible from this study. The Applicant conducted fertility and early embryonic development studies with male and female rabbits.

Embryo-fetal development

In the embryo-fetal development study, pregnant rabbits were administered IV doses of 10, 30, and 100 mg/kg q3 days on Gestation Days 6, 9, 12, 15 and 18. Necropsies were conducted on Gestation Day 29. There was no effect on treated females observed related to garadacimab treatment. There were no treatment-related external, visceral, or skeletal major or minor abnormalities. The NOAEL for maternal and fetal toxicity was concluded to be 100 mg/kg/occasion (HD).

Prenatal and postnatal development

In the rabbit pre-and postnatal development study, pregnant rabbits (F0) were treated with garadacimab from Gestation Day 7 through Lactation Day 38. SC doses of 0, 10 (low dose), 30 (mid dose), and 100 (HD) mg/kg/dose and an IV dose of 100 (HD) mg/kg were administered once every 5 days on Gestation Day 7, 12, 17, 22, and 27 and Lactation Days 3, 8, 13, 18, 23, 28, 33, and 38. The F1 generation was not directly administered the test article. Prolongation of aPTT values and toxicokinetic indicated that drug exposure was continuous during the gestation and lactation periods in F0 maternal animals. On Gestation Day 29, fetal parturition plasma garadacimab concentrations were 76.6%, 136.6%, and 40.8% of the maternal concentrations, at SC low dose, SC mid dose, and SC HD, respectively. At IV HD, maternal and fetal concentrations were approximately equivalent. Garadacimab was able to cross the placenta. For F0 dams, no garadacimab-related effects were observed on mortality, clinical signs, body weight, natural delivery, litter data, or maternal macroscopic observations. The maternal NOAEL was 100 mg/kg/dose (HD) SC/IV. For the F1 generation, no garadacimab-related effects were observed on mortality, clinical signs, body weight, developmental signs, functional observation battery, eye-blink response, presence of motile sperm, reproductive performance, cesarean section parameters, or preweaning or postweaning macroscopic observations. In the F1 generation, the NOAEL for fetal development was 100 mg/kg/dose (HD) SC/IV. The NOAEL for developmental, neurobehavioral, and reproductive performance in the F1 generation was 100 mg/kg/dose (HD) SC/IV. Prolongation of aPTT was observed in all four studies with F0 male and female rabbits, but not associated with any bleeding events. aPTT was not measured in F1 rabbits in the pre-and postnatal development study.
