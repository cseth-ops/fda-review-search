Long-term safety — 4791-2

Safety requirement, 505(o)(3) · approved 2025 · Draft Protocol Submission 06/2025 · Final Protocol Submission 10/2025

Conduct a prospective clinical registry to evaluate the potential risk of serious hepatotoxicity in patients receiving vimseltinib for the treatment of tenosynovial giant cell tumor. Analyses should include evaluation of alanine aminotransferase, aspartate aminotransferase, bilirubin, symptoms suggestive of hepatotoxicity, and dose reduction and treatment discontinuation related to hepatotoxicity. Include investigations of time to event and severity, and potential risk factors such as age, race, and comorbidities. The registry should aim to enroll at least 100 patients. Monitor patients until discontinuation of study treatment or a minimum of 5 years from start of treatment, whichever occurs first. Interim safety analyses should be performed yearly beginning one year after the first patient has received vimseltinib to characterize the long-term risk of hepatotoxicity with vimseltinib.

Serious risk named in the letter: hepatotoxicity

What the review says about it

To further assess the potential risk for hepatotoxicity, a PMR (see section 13) was issued that will include a patient registry to provide additional information to further characterize the risk of hepatotoxicity in the indicated population outside of the clinical trial setting and to elucidate the potential long-term risk of liver injury associated with vimseltinib treatment.

review page 218

Additionally, a post-marketing requirement (PMR) registry study collecting data on liver function for an additional at least 100 patients dosed with vimseltinib in the postmarketing setting was agreed upon for this application.

Cohort A +

FDA disagreed · Clinical safety · review page 198
The applicant's position
Participants in the vimseltinib arm experienced more SAEs than those in the placebo arm in the (b) (4) double-blind period of MOTION; Serious AEs including drug-related SAEs were generally consistent across the different pooled populations. No individual drug-related SAE was reported in more than participant.
FDA's assessment
FDA does not agree with Applicant’s assessment, as all SAEs are considered attributable regardless of causality. Patients who received vimseltinib experienced more SAEs than those in the placebo arm (7% vs. 2.6%, respectively) with each SAE occurring in one patient only. FDA reviewed the narratives for all patients who experienced an SAE. Based on the reviewer assessment, only cellulitis (1 patient) and sub-cutaneous abscess (1 patient) were considered SAEs (2.4%). The original proposed labeling from the Applicant did not include SAEs but through negotiations, an incidence of 2.4% was included in product labeling. Executive Summary

Vimseltinib is a small molecule kinase inhibitor of colony-stimulating factor 1 receptor (CSF1R). The established pharmacologic class is kinase inhibitor. Figure 1 Chemical Structure of Vimseltinib

(Excerpted from Applicant’s Submission) Target Background Colony stimulating factor 1 receptor (CSF1R), also known as CD115 (Cluster of Differentiation 115) or macrophage colony-stimulating factor receptor (M-CSFR), is a tyrosine kinase transmembrane receptor encoded by the human CSF1R gene (also known as c-FMS). CSF1R signaling is involved in macrophage and osteoclast development and differentiation. In tenosynovial giant cell tumors, colony stimulating factor 1 (CSF-1) is overexpressed. Overexpression of CSF1 and excess signaling through CSF1R may enhance tumor-associated macrophage (TAM)-mediated tumor growth, angiogenesis, and immunosuppression.

Pharmacology Vimseltinib inhibited CSF1R with an IC50 of 2.8 nM. In vitro, vimseltinib inhibited autophosphorylation and phosphorylation of CSF1R in THP-1 cells (a cell line used to study monocyte and macrophage function) and ERK signaling in whole blood monocytes. Vimseltinib had anti-tumor activity, inhibiting CSF1R-dependent proliferation in vitro in M-NFS-60 myelogenous leukemia cells. In an in vivo mouse model, administration of vimseltinib reduced levels of CSF1R-dependent cFOS transcription.

ADME/PK A single IV dose of 1 mg/kg vimseltinib had 73% CNS permeability in Sprague Dawley rats.

Absorption Vimseltinib was rapidly absorbed across the dose range studied in the clinical pharmacology studies, with single doses that ranged from 6 mg to 30 mg and repeat doses that ranged from 40 mg to 50 mg once daily. After oral administration of a single 30 mg dose of vimseltinib (given as 3 capsules each containing 10 mg) under fasted conditions in Study DCC-3014-01-002, median time to maximum drug concentration (Tmax) ranged from 1 to 2 hours. Due to its pH dependent solubility, vimseltinib has the potential to be subject to a drug interaction with acid-reducing agents. Study DCC-3014-01-006 evaluated the drug interaction potential of a single dose of vimseltinib as a victim drug when co-administered with the proton pump inhibitor rabeprazole in healthy participants. The results suggested concomitant administration of proton pump inhibitors has a weak effect on vimseltinib absorption. Administering vimseltinib with a high-fat meal decreased the rate of vimseltinib absorption, possibly due to delayed gastric emptying, but there was no food effect on the extent of absorption. General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Repeat-dose toxicology studies were conducted in rats (4-weeks, 13-weeks, and 26-weeks) and dogs (4-weeks, 13-weeks, and 39-weeks). The indication did not warrant repeat-dose toxicology studies of more than 3 months duration.

Rats exhibited similar findings across studies:

x swollen head and limbs x decreases in RBC-related parameters (hemocrit, hemoglobin, erythrocytes), lymphocytes, calcium, and phosphate x increases in AST, ALT, glutamate dehydrogenase (GLDH), fibrinogen, reticulocytes, platelets, and neutrophils x notable adverse histopathology in the bone, teeth, kidneys (chronic progressive nephropathy), and blood vessel degeneration (fibrinoid necrosis with presence of inflammatory cells) in multiple tissues. x additional findings in rats relevant to reproductive effects are described below under Reproductive and Developmental Toxicity

Rats were more sensitive to the effects of vimseltinib compared to dogs.

Dogs were dosed higher in the 4-week study (up to 25 mg/kg/day) vs. the 13-week and 39-week studies (up to 8 mg/kg/day) and exhibited more severe toxicities at higher doses. Considering all three studies, findings included:

x decreases in phosphate, calcium, and APTT x increases in AST, ALT, GLDH, creatine kinase, fibrinogen, reticulocytes, and platelets x edema in the skin, skin de-pigmentation (females only), excessive lacrimation, and epididymal mineralization

The vimseltinib toxicity profile is characterized by inflammation, hematologic, hepatic, renal, bone, and gastrointestinal toxicities, and is generally consistent with CSF1R inhibition. Findings in animals indicate vimseltinib has the potential to cause hepatotoxicity, which may be secondary to inflammation. Another small molecule kinase inhibitor of CSF1R is pexidartinib. Literature (Qin et al., 2023) suggests that the mechanism of action for pexidartinib-mediated liver injury may be due to its unique biomolecular characteristics, including metabolites, rather than due to a class effect alone. While plausible, the totality of the nonclinical data for vimseltinib do not fully rule out the risk for hepatotoxicity in humans.

General toxicology findings reflected in USPI sections 8.4 and 13.2 In a 26-week repeat-dose general toxicology study in rats, chronic progressive nephropathy occurred at vimseltinib doses of 2.5 mg/kg/day (approximately 6 times the exposure at the recommended dose based on AUC). Degeneration of blood vessels (perivascular inflammation and necrosis of arteries and arteriole walls) occurred in multiple tissues at 5 mg/kg/day vimseltinib (approximately 12 times the exposure at the recommended dose based on AUC).

Rats administered vimseltinib at 2.5 mg/kg had physeal thickening and decay of the incisors and molars. Bone and tooth toxicities occurred at exposures approximately 8 times the

recommended dose based on AUC. These findings are relevant to pediatric use.

In a 39-week repeat-dose general toxicology study in dogs, skin depigmentation in the head and legs occurred at vimseltinib doses of 4 mg/kg/day corresponding to exposures lower than the exposure at the recommended dose based on AUC.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

In a rat embryo-fetal development study, pregnant female rats were dosed once daily during the period of organogenesis (gestational days 6 to 17) at doses of 2.5, 5, or 15 mg/kg/day. Structural abnormalities (skeletal variations) occurred at 2.5 mg/kg/day (approximately 3 times the exposure at the recommended dose based on AUC). Additional structural abnormalities (cardiac malformations) were observed at the highest dose of 15 mg/kg/day (approximately 23 times the exposure at the recommended dose based on AUC).

Based on data from animal studies and its mechanism of action, vimseltinib can cause fetal harm. Females of reproductive potential and males with female partners of reproductive potential are recommended to use effective contraception during treatment with vimseltinib and for 1 month after the last dose. The duration of contraception is based on 5 times the clinical elimination half- life of 6 days.

There are no data on the presence of vimseltinib or its metabolites in either human or animal milk or its effects on a breastfed child or on milk production. Because of the potential for serious adverse reactions in the breastfed child, women are recommended not to breastfeed during treatment with vimseltinib and for 1 month after the last dose.

Based on findings from animal studies, vimseltinib may impair fertility.

Trial Design

The Applicant’s Description: This is a pivotal Phase 3, placebo-controlled study of vimseltinib in patients with TGCT, consisting of 2 parts: Part 1 was double blind and Part 2 was open label. The double-blind and randomized design of this study allowed for adequate assessment of vimseltinib compared to a control group. While pexidartinib is approved in the United States, it is associated with a risk of potentially fatal liver injury and available under restricted access (REMS program) (TURALIO, USPI 2023). The study design is outlined in Figure 8Figure 8. The study consisted of a 42-day screening period prior to the first dose of study drug, a Part 1 double-blind treatment period of 24 weeks (referred to in 28-day cycles) and a Part 2 open label period until Week 49. Participants were randomized in a 2:1 ratio to receive either vimseltinib at the dose of 30 mg twice weekly or placebo for 24 weeks. Interactive response technology was used to randomize and assign study drug. Randomization was stratified for tumor location (lower limb/all other) and region (U.S./non-U.S.). After completion of the End of Part 1 assessments, participants who wished to continue to the open-label part of this study (Part 2) were unblinded. Participants randomized to placebo crossed over to receive vimseltinib and participants initially randomized to vimseltinib continued to receive vimseltinib in Part 2.

FDA did not identify any cases of Hy’s law or drug-induced liver injury (DILI). Due to this known risk of in class hepatotoxicity, sections 2.2 and 5.1 of product labeling addresses specific guidelines for managing potential hepatotoxicity in patients, including guidelines for routine laboratory surveillance.

After multidisciplinary review of clinical and nonclinical data, FDA determined that a REMS program was not required for vimseltinib. However, FDA will continue to collect further data on hepatotoxicity through a PMR registry study enrolling at least 100 patients in the postmarketing setting. There is nonclinical evidence to suggest small molecule inhibitors of CSFR1 lead to a higher incidence of elevated transaminases, but also may cause hepatic damage. FDA will continue to reevaluate the safety data for hepatotoxicity.

Additional warnings and precautions included in section 5.1 of product labeling are for potential embryo-fetal toxicity, allergic reactions to FD&C Yellow No.5 (Tartazine) and No. 6 (Sunset Yellow FCF) and increased creatinine without affecting renal function.
