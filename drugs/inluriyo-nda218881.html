Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

5.1 Executive Summary

Imlunestrant (Inluriyo) is an estrogen receptor (ER) antagonist indicated for the treatment of adult patients with ER-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, ESR1-mutated advanced or metastatic breast cancer, with disease progression following at least one line of endocrine therapy. The recommended dose of Inluriyo is 400 mg orally once daily. The nonclinical development program for imlunestrant consisted of studies in mice, rats, and monkeys to evaluate the pharmacology (primary, secondary, safety), metabolism, general toxicity, embryo-fetal development, and genotoxicity. During the nonclinical development, imlunestrant was referred to as LY3484356 tosylate, LSN3484356, and LSN3513322.

Estrogen receptors (ERs) consist of nuclear ERs, extra-nuclear ERs, and G protein-coupled ERs. Estrogen receptor-alpha (ERα) and estrogen receptor-beta (ERβ) are located in the nucleus and are encoded by the ESR1 and ESR2 genes, respectively. ERα is expressed in breast, prostate, uterus, liver, and bone tissues. Mutations in the ESR1 gene, such as Y537S/N and D538G, contribute to ER+HER2- breast cancer resistance to endocrine therapy.

In primary pharmacology studies, imlunestrant bound to wild-type ERα, mutant (Y537S) ERα, and wild-type ERβ with Ki values of 0.6 nM, 2.8 nM, and 0.1 nM, respectively. Imlunestrant induced degradation of ERα in breast cancer cell lines with wild-type and ESR1-mutated (Y537S/N) ERα and led to inhibition of ER-dependent gene transcription. In vitro, imlunestrant inhibited the proliferation of both ERα wild-type and ESR1-mutated (Y537S/N) breast cancer cell lines (IC50s 1.67 to 7 nM). In vivo, daily oral administration of imlunestrant (3 to 30 mg/kg, 28 days) to mice bearing ERα wild-type and ESR1-mutated (Y537S) breast cancer xenografts led to ≤ 83% tumor growth inhibition (TGI) compared to control. In secondary pharmacology studies, the binding affinity of imlunestrant to ER was at least 300- fold higher than other nuclear hormone receptors such as human glucocorticoid receptor, androgen receptor, mineralocorticoid receptor, or progesterone receptor (PR) suggesting unlikely off-target activity at clinical concentrations. The Applicant conducted a non-GLP study in immature female rats to evaluate the potential agonistic effects of imlunestrant on ERα. Oral administration of imlunestrant at doses up to 200 mg/kg for 4 days did not result in remarkable changes to uterine weight or the histopathology of uterus, cervix, and vagina when compared to control. On the other hand, positive controls estradiol and tamoxifen led to severely increased uterine weights and histopathology findings in female reproductive organs. The data suggests that imlunestrant does not have agonistic effects on ERα. Imlunestrant had no remarkable effects on the central nervous and respiratory systems when administered orally once daily to rats up to 150 mg/kg/day for up to 6 months or on the

cardiovascular, central nervous, and respiratory systems when administered orally once daily to cynomolgus monkeys up to 600 mg/kg/day for up to 3 months.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The Applicant’s Position:

The general toxicology profile across repeat-dose toxicity studies in rats and monkeys demonstrates that imlunestrant was well tolerated at doses and exposures that exceeded the anticipated therapeutic levels in humans.

Single-dose toxicity studies No single-dose toxicity studies of imlunestrant were conducted. Single-dose studies are typically not conducted for modern drug development, as short pilot and range-finding studies generally provide equivalent or more information. In pilot and range-finding studies, single doses were well tolerated with no mortality or clinical observations indicating acute toxicity from Day 1 to Day 2. Repeat-dose toxicity studies Imlunestrant has been administered by QD oral gavage to rats for up to 6 months and to monkeys for up to 3 months. Consistent with imlunestrant pharmacology, the primary target organs in rat and monkey repeat-dose studies were female reproductive tissues. Microscopic findings included • cysts in the ovaries of rats and monkeys • hyperplasia of ovarian granulosa cells in rats • atrophy of the epithelium of the uterus, cervix, and vagina of rats, as well as • hypertrophy/hyperplasia of the female mammary glands. Except for the mammary gland effects, these findings were generally correlated with organ weight changes, macroscopic changes, or both. Effects on male reproductive tissues were also apparent in longer-term rat studies. Reproductive tissue effects were collectively considered adverse in both sexes but their relevance for the intended patient population of imlunestrant is minimal because men and pre/perimenopausal women treated with imlunestrant will receive GnRH agonist and because the effects in animals were generally reversible.

The FDA generally agrees with the Applicant’s position. Additional target organs of toxicity included kidney and urinary bladder.

Imlunestrant does not pose a biologically relevant genotoxic risk. Imlunestrant was not mutagenic in the Ames bacterial reverse mutation assay. Imlunestrant produced chromosomal damage in the in vitro micronucleus assay but did not cause chromosomal damage or DNA strand breaks in vivo when tested up to 2000 mg/kg in the rat comet and bone marrow micronucleus assays. The FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position that imlunestrant is not considered genotoxic.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

The Applicant’s Position: Based on its intended pharmacologic mechanism of action as a SERD and findings in animal studies, imlunestrant could cause harm to a fetus and may impair fertility. In repeat-dose toxicity studies, imlunestrant was associated with reversible cessation of estrous cycling and generally reversible anatomic effects on the female reproductive tract. Anatomic effects on the male reproductive tract were observed in rats and were reversible. In an embryo-fetal development study of pregnant rats administered 0, 0.3, 3, or 30 mg/kg/day oral imlunestrant during the period of organogenesis from Gestation Days 6 to 17, imlunestrant caused gross pathological changes in

• maternal reproductive tissues • embryo lethality • fetal malformations • variations, and • effects on fetal growth.

Phototoxicity

Imlunestrant was positive for phototoxic potential in an in vitro study using BALB/c 3T3 mouse fibroblasts. In a rat phototoxicity study, imlunestrant was mildly phototoxic to the skin, producing barely perceptible dermal redness following UV radiation exposure, with no evidence of ocular phototoxicity. The risk of clinically relevant phototoxicity is low because the dermal effects observed in rats were minimal at Cmax exposures exceeding those at the human therapeutic dose by 36-fold.

The FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position that imlunestrant showed phototoxic potential in vitro and was phototoxic to the skin in vivo. Clinical safety

The Applicant’s Position: The safety population of EMBER-3 included all participants randomly assigned to study treatment who took at least 1 dose of study treatment (N = 859) with 327 patients assigned to Arm A and 324 patients assigned to Arm B. The Safety Population for Arms A and B is henceforth called the Overall Safety Population. The ESR1m-detected safety population in Arm A and Arm B (N=254) was a subset of the overall safety population. The safety profile of imlunestrant monotherapy was generally favorable and similar to SOC. The imlunestrant safety profile was manageable through routine monitoring as evidenced by low rates of dose reduction or treatment discontinuation due to AEs. The safety profile of the ESR1m-detected safety population was similar to that of the overall safety population. The safety profile of imlunestrant monotherapy was consistent with the mechanism of action of SERDs, and events commonly experienced by patients with ABC.

• The incidence of most TEAEs was similar between treatment arms and the majority were low grade (Grade 1 or 2). • The most frequently reported TEAEs in the imlunestrant arm were fatigue, diarrhea, and nausea, which were easily detectable based on clinical signs and symptoms. The majority of these events were observed early in treatment. A higher incidence of low grade diarrhea and fatigue in the imlunestrant arm was not considered clinically relevant.

• The incidence of SAEs and deaths due to AEs were similar across treatment arms. The incidence of Grade ≥3 TEAEs showed a slightly lower incidence in imlunestrant arm (17.1%) compared with SOC (20.7%). • Overall, TEAEs were manageable with supportive therapies and/or dose adjustments (withheld or reductions). • The frequency of discontinuations or dose adjustments due to AEs was low across both arms.

The FDA safety analyses were based on the FDA’s independent review of data from the EMBER-3 (J2J-OX-JZLC) pivotal trial. The FDA independent analyses were based on 651 patients who received at least one dose of study treatment in Arm A (imlunestrant) or Arm B (treatment of physician’s choice, TPC) of the trial, including 327 patients who received imlunestrant and 324 patients who received TPC (fulvestrant-292 patients, or exemestane-32 patients). The FDA assessed safety in all patients in Arm A and Arm B, as well as in patients with ESR1m-detected status in these two arms. ESR1m-detected status for the FDA review of safety was defined as in the review of efficacy (section 8.1.1). The FDA agrees that the safety profile of the ESR1m-detected safety population was similar to the safety profile of the overall safety population.
