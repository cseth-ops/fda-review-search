Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

The following information is taken from the Nonclinical Pharmacology/Toxicology Review.

4.1 Primary Pharmacology Tagraxofusp-erzs was shown to bind human IL-3RA with a Kd of 470 nM, which is comparable to human IL-3 binding to IL-3RA (hIL-3 Kd 670 nM). Submitted and published data support the mechanism of action of tagraxofusp, which upon binding to IL-3RA, is internalized and increases ADP ribosylation of elongation factor-2 (EF-2). It is widely accepted that ADP- ribosylation of EF-2 by DT blocks the ability of EF-2 to facilitate protein synthesis.

In vitro, tagraxofusp-erzs reduced cell proliferation and increased expression of apoptotic markers in BPDCN cell lines and in blasts from patients with BPDCN. The correlation between sensitivity to tagraxofusp-erzs and CD123 receptor density was mixed. In mouse xenograft models of BPDCN, tagraxofusp-erzs increased overall survival compared to controls.

4 Pharmacology Tagraxofusp functions by binding to cell surface IL-3 receptor alpha via its IL-3 domain. IL-3 binding has been shown to result in internalization of the IL-3-IL-3R complex. 1,2 Once in the cytosol, the DT catalytic domain of tagraxofusp catalyzes the transfer of ADP-ribose from nicotinamide adenine dinucleotide (NAD) to a diphthamide residue on elongation factor 2 (EF-2), leading to inhibition of protein synthesis and ultimately apoptosis.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

No reproductive and development toxicology studies were conducted for tagraxofusp. Based on the cytotoxic mechanism of action of tagraxofusp, it is expected to cause maternal and embryo-fetal toxicity when administered to a pregnant woman.

Since tagraxofusp is a biologic, the only relevant animal model is the nonhuman primate, it will be administered to a patient population with severe and life-threatening disease (BPDCN), and neutralizing ADA many limit identification of doses to provide relevant animal:human safety margins in reproductive and developmental toxicology studies, a literature assessment was provided by the Applicant,

In the literature assessment, the Applicant noted that specific spatial and temporal expression of IL-3 and CD123 in the embryo and fetus is unknown.

Mice lacking the βc subunit of IL-3R showed lung abnormalities, including lymphocytic infiltration and some areas of alveolar proteinosis, as well as a reduced number of eosinophils. βc-/-IL-3-/- double knockout mice have a similar phenotype. 29 IL-3- deficient mice are also phenotypically normal with compromised hypersensitivity reactions, increased numbers of tissue mast cells, and enhanced basophil production. 30,31 Assessment of 8 patients with pulmonary alveolar proteinosis demonstrated a βc deficiency at the genetic level in 4 of 8 patients and no apparent hematopoietic abnormalities, which is consistent with findings in knockout mice. 32 Another study also suggests an embryotoxic effect of exogenous recombinant IL-3 in rodents. 33 In addition, it has been shown that drugs targeting the IL-3R may affect the development of fetal hematopoietic stem cells, because blocking IL-3 with an antibody in an explant culture before transplantation in mice or transplanting IL-3-/- cells resulted in hematopoietic stem cells that were functionally normal but significantly decreased in number. 34 Together, these data suggest possible embryotoxic effects on fetal hematopoiesis and embryo-fetal development through exposure to exogenous IL-3 or blockade of IL-3 signaling.

Integrated nonclinical assessment

Tagraxofusp is a CD123-directed cytotoxin indicated for the treatment of patients with BPDCN. Nonclinical pharmacology, pharmacokinetic, and toxicology studies have been submitted to support the approval of tagraxofusp for this indication.

Pharmacology Tagraxofusp, also known by laboratory codes SL-401, DT388IL3, and Molecule 129, is a recombinant fusion protein of the DT Q388R variant with the DT-receptor binding domain replaced by IL-3. SL-401 was shown to bind human IL-3RA, using surface plasmon resonance (SPR), with a Kd of 470 nM. SL-401 and human IL-3 binding to IL- 3RA were comparable (hIL-3 Kd=670 nM). SL-401 did not bind to mouse IL-3RA, which based on a BLAST search had 65% DNA sequence homology and 49% identity to human IL-3RA. It is inferred that tagraxofusp does not bind to dog, rat, and rabbit CD123 since these species DNA sequence homology and identity are lower than in the mouse with no measurable binding. STML-401-0114

SL-401 in Patients with Acute Myeloid Leukemia or Blastic Plasmacytoid Dendritic Cell Neoplasm

Study Design

Overview and Objective

STML-401-0114 is a multicenter, open-label, multistage Phase 2 trial evaluating the safety and efficacy of tagraxofusp-erzs monotherapy in subjects with BPDCN or R/R AML.

• Stage 1 – dose escalation o Subjects with any BPDCN and R/R AML o Primary objective – to determine the maximum tolerated dose (MTD). • Stage 2 - dose expansion o Subjects with any BPDCN and R/R AML o Primary objective – to determine the efficacy in subjects with BPDCN as assessed by ORR and to characterize the safety profile. • Stage 3 – pivotal cohort supporting BLA o Subjects not treated previously for BPDCN o Primary objective – to determine the CR + CRc rate and characterize the safety profile in this population. • Stage 4 – expanded access cohort o All subjects with BPDCN o Only stage of the study that remains open.

Key secondary objectives included determining the duration of response, PFS, and OS in subjects with BPDCN (pooled) as well as characterizing the pharmacokinetics and immunogenicity of tagraxofusp-erzs.

Trial Design

In Stage 1, doses of 7, 9, and 12 µg/kg/day x 5 days of a 21-day cycle were studied in subjects with BPDCN and R/R AML in a standard 3+3 dose escalation design. Doses up to 16 µg/kg/day x 5 days were studied only in subjects with R/R AML. The MTD for both populations was determined to be 12 µg/kg/day, and this was the dose was used in Stages 2-4.

Clinical safety

The clinical review of safety for this BLA was based on all available safety data from studies STML-401-0114 and STML-401-0314 which enrolled subjects with myeloid malignancies (BPDCN, AML, and MPN) with active disease treated with tagraxofusp-erzs monotherapy. The 60-day safety update data set (Module 5.3.5.3) was used for the safety analyses. This population is referred to as the “Full Safety Population” in this review. Of note, the 60-day safety update included safety data from an additional 14 subjects with BPDCN treated in Stage 4 of STML-401-0114 after the data cut-off for the primary efficacy analysis; therefore, the total number of subjects with BPDCN is higher in the safety review than in Sections 6 and 7.

Beginning with Section 8.4.4, parts of the safety review focus specifically on subjects from STML-401-0114 with active disease (BPDCN or AML) treated with tagraxofusp-erzs at the 12 mcg/kg/day dose on days 1-5 of a 21-day cycle. Safety analyses in these subjects are particularly relevant to labeled dose and schedule; this population is referred to as the “Focused Safety Population” in this review.

Three children were treated outside of clinical trials at the 12 mcg/kg/day dose on days 1-5 of a 21-day cycle, and the safety experience for these subjects is described in Section 8.8.3.

Benefit-risk

BLA 761116 was submitted under 351(a) for the new molecular entity tagraxofusp-erzs, a CD123-directed cytotoxin. The indication proposed by the Applicant was “For the treatment of patients with blastic plasmacytoid dendritic cell neoplasm (BPDCN).”

The review team recommended regular approval for the indication “For the treatment of blastic plasmacytoid dendritic cell neoplasm (BPDCN) in adults and in pediatric patients 2 years and older” using tagraxofusp-erzs 12 mcg/kg/day on days 1-5 of a 21-day cycle until disease progression.

Evidence of clinical benefit came from Study STML-401-0114 a multicenter, multicohort, single-arm trial of tagraxofusp-erzs for adults with BPDCN or acute myeloid leukemia (AML). The primary efficacy endpoint of the analyses was complete response (CR) and clinical CR (CRc), the latter of which included minimal residual skin abnormalities. In the absence of established response criteria for this rare disease, the clinical meaningfulness of this novel endpoint was assessed by qualification in the patients in the initial cohorts of the study and affirmed by FDA prior to analysis of the pivotal cohort.

The Stage 3 cohort of Study STML-401-0114 was designed to exclude a 10% CR+CRc rate in patients with untreated BPDCN. The CR+CRc rate in this pivotal cohort of 13 patients was 54% (95% CI: 25, 81), and the median duration of CR+CRc was not reached with a median follow-up of 11.5 months (95% CI: 8.8, 11.9). The lower 95% bound of the CR+CRc rate exceeded 10%, so the study is considered positive. The relatively high point estimate of the CR+CRc rate (54%) and the durability of the response was considered clinically meaningful.

An additional response analysis was conducted in 15 patients with relapsed or refractory BPDCN pooled from the other stages of Study STML-401-0114. This exploratory analysis showed a CR+CRc rate of 13% (95% CI: 2, 41); the 2 responses seen lasted 3.6 and 13.9 months.

Safety of the recommended dose and schedule of tagraxofusp-erzs was assessed in 94 patients with active BPDCN or AML. Capillary leak syndrome (CLS) occurred in 55% of patients, and it was fatal in 2%. Other major safety issues included hypersensitivity reactions and hepato- toxicity. The risk of these serious events was controlled in the clinical trial by close monitoring and guidelines for dose modification and supportive care which will need to be included in labeling. These events also warrant warnings, including a boxed warning for CLS. The most common (incidence ≥30%) adverse reactions were CLS, nausea, fatigue, peripheral edema, pyrexia and weight increase. The most common (incidence ≥ 10%) Grade ≥ 3 laboratory abnormalities were platelets decreased, hemoglobin decreased, neutrophils decreased, glucose increased, ALT increased and AST increased.
