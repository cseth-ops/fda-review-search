Safety pharmacology

1. A pulmonary and cardiovascular safety evaluation of ISIS 396443 administered as a continuous intrathecal infusion in albino rats (Study #396443-AS04 (727-038), conducted by (b) (4) , report dated 12/14/12, GLP)

a. Methods

Male S-D rats (8/gr) were administered ISIS 396443 (0, 0.02, 0.06, or 0.2 mg/day; Lot #RP396443-001) by continuous intrathecal infusion (0.25ul/hour) via implanted minipumps for 28 days.

b. Results

i. Mortality, clinical signs, body weights

One MD (#318) and 3 HD (#s 327, 331, and 332) animals died or were sacrificed, but these deaths were attributed to surgical procedure or accident and not drug. No drug-related signs or effects on body weight were observed.

ii. Pulmonary monitoring (monitoring in plethysmograph chamber on Day 11, 12, or 22, for 60 seconds at 15 minute intervals for at least 4 hours)

No drug-related effects on pulmonary function were observed.

iii. Cardiovascular monitoring (systolic, diastolic, MAP, and HR monitored on SD19, 22, 23, or 25)

No significant drug-related effects on heart rate or blood pressure were observed.

iv. Postmortem evaluations (lumbar spinal cord samples collected after sacrifice following completion of cardiovascular monitoring)

ISIS 396443 concentrations in the lumber spinal cord were 70, 170, and 389 μg/g at the end of the infusion at the LD, MD, and HD, respectively.

PK in monkeys was evaluated in a dedicated PK study in adults (Study 396443-APK01) and in the 3 toxicology studies: 1) single bolus IT study in adult monkeys (Study 396443-AS01), 2) a 14- week IT study in juvenile monkeys (Study 396443-AS03), and 3) a 53-week IT study in juvenile monkeys (Study 396443-AS06). A. Absorption

In the single dose adult monkey study in which animals received either the vehicle (artificial CSF) or ISIS 396443 (1, 3, or 7 mg given as 3 min IT bolus injection via lumbar catheter), peak CSF concentrations were observed within 15 minutes of dosing and then rapidly declined (up to 24–48 hours post injection). Following the initial rapid distribution phase, ISIS 396443 elimination from the CSF was much slower with an observed elimination half-life of approximately 11 days. Plasma concentrations peaked at 2-5 hrs and CSF-to-plasma ratios ranged from 95-524 within 2 to 48 hr (Table III.2).

Nonclinical ADME / toxicokinetics

PK/ADME data were collected in a dedicated study in adult monkeys and in the toxicity studies. Following IT administration to monkeys, the estimated CSF terminal elimination half-life (t1/2) was consistent between adult and juvenile monkeys, with observed values of 102-111 days. Concentrations in plasma peaked 2-5 hours after the IT bolus injection and then rapidly declined during the first 24-48 hours post-dose, followed by a slower elimination phase. The estimated plasma terminal elimination half-life (35-56 days) was shorter than that in CSF (102 to 111 days) but similar to that in systemic tissues (12-23 days in liver and 35 days in kidney).

Concentrations in systemic tissues like liver and kidney following IT administration were similar to those seen in the spinal cord indicating that a substantial proportion of the administered dose was transferred from CSF to the systemic circulation. In both adult and juvenile monkeys, ISIS 396443 appeared to be cleared slowly from CNS and systemic tissues. The estimated median CNS tissue terminal elimination half-life (t1/2) was consistent between adult and juvenile monkeys, with observed values of 116 days (range 74-275 days) and 174 days (range 117-195 days), respectively, which are similar to the observed terminal elimination half-life in CSF.

Comparison of plasma, kidney cortex, and liver exposure after IT and IV administration suggested minimal metabolic clearance of ISIS 396443 in the CNS prior to eventual transfer to plasma. Drug exposure in liver and kidney appeared to be independent of the route of administration. ISIS 396443 is highly bound to plasma proteins in mice, monkeys, and humans (>94%), which reduces glomerular filtration (limiting urinary excretion) and therefore facilitates distribution to systemic tissues. Binding to CSF proteins (monkeys and humans) was lower (<25% at 5 μg/mL and none bound at 150 μg/mL), presumably due to lower CSF protein concentrations. Following SC administration in mice and rabbits, a lack of measurable fetal liver exposure indicated limited placental transfer.

Metabolite profiling in monkeys following IT administration indicated that intact ISIS 396443 was the primary oligonucleotide present, accounting for 100% of the total oligonucleotides in CSF, >95% in plasma and 79% to 81% in CNS and systemic tissues. Genetic toxicology

1. Bacterial Reverse Mutation Assay (Study No. 396443-IS02, (b) (4) AD25FG.503.BTL, conducted by (b) (4) , report dated 9/9/11, GLP)

ISIS 396443 (Lot #RP396443-001, PBS solvent) was tested in the Ames assay using tester Salmonella strains TA98, TA100, TA1535 and TA1537, and E. coli strain WP2 uvrA in the presence and absence of rat liver S9 using the plate incorporation method. In the initial cytotoxicity-mutation assay, the concentrations tested were 1.5, 5, 15, 50, 150, 500, 1500, and 5000 μg per plate. No positive mutagenic responses were observed with any of the tester strains in either the presence or absence of S9 activation. Neither precipitate nor appreciable toxicity was observed. Based on the findings of the initial assay, concentrations of 50, 150, 500, 1500, and 5000 μg per plate were tested in the confirmatory mutagenicity assay. No positive mutagenic responses were observed with any of the tester strains in either the presence or absence of S9 activation. Neither precipitate nor appreciable cytotoxicity was observed. Under the conditions of this study, ISIS 396443 was concluded to be negative in the Ames assay.

2. In Vitro Mammalian Chromosome Aberration Test (Study No. 396443-IS03, (b) (4)

AD25FG.331.BTL, conducted by (b) (4) , report dated 9/6/11, GLP)

ISIS 396443 (Lot #RP396443-001) was tested in the CHO chromosome aberration assay in the absence and presence of rat liver S9. In the preliminary cytotoxicity assay, the maximum concentration tested was 5000 μg/mL. CHO cells were treated for 4 and 20 hours in the non- activated test system and for 4 hours in the S9-activated test system. All cells were harvested 20 hours after treatment initiation. Substantial cytotoxicity (i.e., at least 50% cell growth inhibition, relative to the solvent control) was not observed at any concentration in all three exposure groups. Based on these findings, the concentrations chosen for the chromosome aberration assay ranged from 750 to 5000 μg/mL for all three treatment groups. In the chromosome aberration assay, the cells were incubated with ISIS 396443 for 4 and 20 hours in the non- activated test system and for 4 hours in the S9-activated test system. All cells were harvested 20 hours after treatment initiation. In the absence of precipitation in the treatment medium and at least 50% toxicity, the highest concentration evaluated was 5000 μg/mL. Two additional lower concentrations (2000 and 3500 μg/mL) were included. The percentage of cells with structural or numerical aberrations in the drug-exposed cultures was not significantly increased relative to solvent control at any dose. The positive and solvent controls fulfilled the requirements for a valid test. Based on the findings of this study, ISIS 396443 was concluded to be negative for the induction of structural and numerical chromosome aberrations in CHO cells in non-activated and S9-activated test systems.

Carcinogenicity

No carcinogenicity studies were conducted to support the NDA, which is consistent with the Division’s decision to allow the assessment of carcinogenicity to be conducted post- approval. However, the sponsor has submitted a request for waiver of carcinogenicity studies for reasons discussed by Dr. Fisher. I agree with Dr. Fisher’s recommendation that, because of the substantial systemic exposure to ISIS 396443 in humans after IT administration, the sponsor should conduct a 2-year carcinogenicity study as a post- marketing requirement.

Conclusions and Recommendations

A less-than standard battery of nonclinical studies (no chronic toxicity study in rodent [to assess systemic exposure], pre- and postnatal development (PPND) study in rodent, or carcinogenicity studies in mouse and rat) was conducted to support approval of nusinersen. Under the IND, the Division agreed that the lack of carcinogenicity studies would not preclude filing or approval of the NDA. The lack of the other studies was not considered a refuse-to-file issue.

The finding of greatest concern is the neurotoxicity observed in the toxicity studies in monkey, which may be associated with adverse effects on learning. The no-effect dose (0.3 mg) for neurotoxicity in the 53-week monkey study was associated with csf levels (12 ng/mL) generally less than that in SMA patients (7-35 ng/mL); therefore, there is no safety margin based on csf levels. However, considering the seriousness of the indication and the apparently substantial evidence of efficacy in the clinical trials in SMA patients, there is no objection to approval of the NDA from a pharmacology/toxicology standpoint. Completion of the ongoing PPND study and a carcinogenicity study in one species (which will provide chronic toxicity data) should be post-marketing requirements. The neurotoxicity findings should be described in labeling.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

1. A Combined Fertility and Developmental Toxicity Study of ISIS 396443 Administered by Subcutaneous Injection in CD-1 Mice (Report No. 396443-AS08; dated 4/01/15; conducted by (b) (4) GLP)

a. Methods

Male and female CD-1 mice (25/sex/grp + 6/sex/grp TK) were administered ISIS 396443 (lot number: CA396443-001) subcutaneously (10 mL/kg) at doses of 0 (0.9% sterile saline), 3, 10, or 25 mg/kg every other day to deliver weekly doses of 10.5, 35, and 87.5 mg/kg/week. Males were dosed for 4 weeks prior to the mating and during and after the 2-week mating period for a total of 10 weeks. Females were dosed for 2 weeks prior to mating, during the mating period, and until GD15 (i.e., throughout organogenesis).

Clinical observations (daily), body weight and food consumption (weekly), and estrous cyclicity were assessed and animals were mated (1:1) starting on Day 15 (females) or Day 29 (males) until confirmation of mating or for 14 days. Terminal necropsy for males was on Day 71 and for pregnant dams on GD18. For males, gross observations were recorded and selected organs were weighed and tissues collected. For pregnant females, typical litter parameters were assessed, external fetal examinations were conducted, and half the fetuses in each litter were assigned to skeletal evaluations (alizarin red staining) and the other half to visceral evaluations (Wilson’s [head] and Staples [bodies] methods). Blood samples for hematology or serum chemistry were taken from animals at necropsy, with half the animals in each group assigned for each. Liver and kidney samples were harvested from adult TK males and females and the placenta and fetal kidneys and livers were harvested from up to 10 fetuses per litter. Histopathology was performed on the kidney, liver, and spleen of 4/sex/grp. Histopathology of additional tissues was performed only on C and HD animals. Sperm was collected from the left cauda epididymis of TK males for total sperm count, motility, concentration, and morphology analysis.
