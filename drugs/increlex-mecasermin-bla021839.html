Review versus label

Where the approved label's nonclinical text does not match what the reviewer reported: a margin stated differently at the same dose, or a tumour finding the review flags that the label does not name. These are leads to check, not errors: labeling can deliberately recompute margins, and some flagged tumours were judged not drug-related elsewhere in the review.

Safety pharmacology

Formal safety pharmacology studies were not performed regarding possible activity of mecasermin or formulation components on known pharmacologic receptors (e.g., beta adrenergic, serotonin). In addition, no formal in vivo safety pharmacology studies were performed. Specific evaluations of cardiovascular and respiratory parameters (electrocardiography, blood pressure, respiration rate) were performed in Beagle dogs. The summary of this data is provided in the toxicology section of this review.

2.6.2.5 Pharmacodynamic drug interactions

Pharmacodynamic drug interaction studies were performed with mecasermin and rhGH in mice, and normal, aged, hypophysectomized, and dwarf rats. Effects on bone growth, changes in blood glucose, changes in body weight, and immune function were evaluated when these two hormones were co-administered. In studies investigating the use of mecasermin to treat AIDS related wasting, pharmacodynamic drug interactions were studied in mice treated with mecasermin following pretreatment with AZT for 10 days. The pharmacodynamic drug interaction studies addressed multiple endpoints, including primary and secondary pharmacodynamic endpoints. The pharmacodynamic drug interaction studies were not conducted under GLP conditions.

The effect of co-treatment with mecasermin and recombinant rhGH on bone growth was studied in female hypophysectomized rats. Mecasermin was administered at a dose of approximately 1.2 mg/kg/day for 7 days by continuous SC infusion using osmotic minipumps (n =7 to 9 rats/group). The rhGH was delivered by a daily SC bolus injection of 0.06 mg/kg/day. The mean tibia epiphyseal plate width increased from 199 + 15 wm in the excipient group to 275 + 13 um in the mecasermin treated animals and to 345 + 33 in the mecasermin plus rhGH treated animals. The bone growth increase in the mecasermin treated animals was significantly greater than excipient (P< 0.001) and the combination of mecasermin and rhGH resulted in an increase of bone growth greater than either hormone given alone (P< 0.05). The longitudinal tibia growth increased from 20 + 4 pm/day in the excipient group to 44 + 1] wm in the mecasermin treated animals and to 70 + 7 um in the animals treated with the combination of mecasermin and rhGH. The longitudinal bone growth increase in the mecasermin treated animals was significantly gréater than excipient (P< 0.01) and the combination of mecasermin and rhGH resulted in an increase of bone growth greater than either hormone given alone (P< 0.05).

Study title: Pilot irritancy of various IGF-1 formulation buffers injected subcutaneously in rabbits

Key study findings:

1. Subcutaneous injection of acetate-containing, buffers resulted in mild to moderately severe inflammatory changes in the muscular and hypodermal layers in the rabbit.

2. The acetate plus mannitol preparation induced the mildest reaction

3. Subcutaneous injections of citrate buffers did not induce similar inflammatory changes.

4. Citrate-based buffer or the acetate/mannito]l buffer caused less local irritation than the acetate buffer formulations.

Results: The most significant lesions occurred in the subdermal muscle and hypodermis. Muscular lesions were generally multifocal except at one injection site that received 2ml of 10mM acetate not containing mannitol or glycine. At this site, the lesion was severe and diffuse. Lesions in the muscle consisted of individual cell necrosis and degeneration accompanied by mixed inflammatory cell accumulation. Hypodermal lesions were typically composed of scattered mononuclear inflammatory cell accumulations and diffuse edema.
