General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Acute toxicity studies were conducted in mice and rats after oral (50, 250, 500 and 1000 mg/kg in CD-1 mice; 50, 250, 500 and 1000 mg/kg in Wistar rats) and i.v. (5, 25, 50 and 150 mg/kg in CD-1 mice; 5, 25, 50 and 75 mg/kg in Wistar rats) administrations. In mice, the minimum lethal doses after oral and intravenous administration were 1000 and 150 mg/kg, respectively, in both sexes. In rats, the minimum lethal dose after oral administration was 500 mg/kg in both sexes. Following i.v. administration to rats, ICL670 was non-lethal up to 75 mg/kg in both sexes. Clinical signs in mice following oral or intravenous administrations included: discolored urine, lacrimation, ataxia, cold to touch, decreased defecation, decreased motor activity, dehydration, hunched posture, ptosis, distended abdomen, and labored breathing. Clinical signs in rats following oral or intravenous administration included decreased defecation and discolored urine.

Repeated dose toxicity studies were conducted in mice (up to 4 weeks at 10, 30, 100 and 200 mg/kg/day in iron-supplemented animals or at 100, 300 and 600->450 mg/kg/day in iron-supplemented animals and at 100 and 600 mg/kg/day in non-supplemented animals), rats (up to 26 weeks at 30, 80 and 180 mg/kg/day in iron-supplemented animals) and marmoset monkeys (up to 39 weeks at 20, 40 and 80 mg/kg/day in non-supplemented animals and at 80 mg/kg/day in iron-supplemented animals). The no-adverse-effect- levels (NOAEL) in mice, rats and marmoset monkeys were considered as 100 (Study No. 981060), 30 (Study No. 987037) and 40 mg/kg/day (Study No. 982027), respectively.

In mice, the target organs of toxicity appeared to be the kidney (tubular degeneration, tubular necrosis, regenerative changes, increased mitosis, basophilia, tubular vacuolation at 100 mg/kg/day in non-supplemented males and at 450 mg/kg/day in iron-supplemented animals), stomach (gastric erosions/ulcers, inflammation at 100 and 600 mg/kg/day in non-supplemented males and females, respectively, and at 450 mg/kg/day in iron- supplemented males and females), liver (necrosis and inflammation at 100 mg/kg/day in non-supplemented animals and at 450 mg/kg/day in iron-supplemented males), heart (multifocal myocytolysis in the ventricle at 600 mg/kg/day in both sexes with standard diet) and lymph node (lymphoid depletion at 100 mg/kg/day in non-supplemented mice and at 450 mg/kg/day in iron-supplemented males). In an oral toxicity study in juvenile mice at 5, 15 and 35 mg/kg/day in iron-supplemented animals and at 35 mg/kg/day in non-supplemented animals for up to 5 consecutive weeks from Day 7 post partum, the major target organ of toxicity appeared to be the thymus (lymphoid atrophy/necrosis at 35 mg/kg/day at Day 21 predominantly in males with or without iron supplementation). Carcinogenicity

Study Title: 2-Year Oral (Gavage) Carcinogenicity Study with ICL670 in Wistar Hannover Rats

Key Study Findings: In this study, no test article-related mortality or moribundity was evident upon histopathological assessment. At study termination, 72-84% survival was observed in the males and 58-68% survival was observed in the females. There were no statistical differences in survival between the control groups and ICL670-treated dose groups. There were no test article- related effects noted during palpable mass examination. There were no apparent significant treatment-related changes between the incidence and severity of non-neoplastic lesions when compared to control. There was no treatment-related increase in tumor incidence for either male or female and there was no statistically significant dose-response trend in tumor rates. Based on the results of this study, the oral administration of ICL670 via gavage to rats fed an iron supplemented diet for at least 104 weeks did not appear to be carcinogenic at doses up to 100-60 mg/kg/day.

Study Title: An Oral Pre- and Postnatal Development Study in Rats (Segment IIT)

Key Study Findings:

In an oral (gavage) Segment III study in rats, pregnant females were administered with ICL670 at 0, 10, 30 and 90 mg/kg/day. Females were treated once daily on gestation day (GD) 6 through lactation day (LD) 20. The 90 mg/kg/day dose group was terminated on LD 1 to 3 due to excessive dam/litter losses. There was a statistically significant increase in the number of stillborn pups at 90 mg/kg/day group. There was a statistically significant increase in the number of stillborn pups at 90 mg/kg/day group. There appears to be no treatment-related adverse effects on behavior in F1 pups. There was an increased incidence of renal anomalies (short/ absent renal papilla, fluid- filled kidney) in F1 male pups sacrificed at the end of the post-weaning phase of the study at 30 mg/kg/day. However, theses effects were not considered as teratogenic effects, as kidney toxicity was also observed in the neonatal rat toxicity study and were considered as nephrotoxicity associated with ICL670. Overall, the NOAEL for Fo maternal toxicity appears to be 30 mg/kg/day and the NOAEL for fetal to toxicity appears to be 10 mg/kg/day.
