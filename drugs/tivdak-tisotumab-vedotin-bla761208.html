Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

5.1 Executive Summary The FDA’s Assessment: Tisotumab vedotin (Tivdak) belongs to the pharmacological class of tissue factor (TF)-directed antibody and microtubule inhibitor conjugate, composed of a TF specific human IgG1κ antibody linked to the microtubule disrupting agent monomethyl auristatin E (MMAE) via a protease- cleavable maleimidocaproyl-valyl-citrullinyl-p-aminobenzyloxycarbonyl (mc-val-cit-PABC) linker. The average drug to antibody ratio is approximately 4. Tisotumab vedotin is administered by intravenous infusion once every 3 weeks and is proposed for the treatment of adult patients with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer with disease progression on or after chemotherapy at a recommended dose of 2 mg/kg. The nonclinical development program for tisotumab vedotin and the cytotoxic payload, MMAE, consisted of studies in mice, rats, and monkeys to evaluate the primary and safety pharmacology, general and developmental toxicology, and genotoxicity. During the nonclinical development, tisotumab vedotin was referred to as IgG1- 1015-011-vcMMAE or HuMax-TF-ADC (IgG1-1015-011 or HuMax-TF for the unconjugated antibody, tisotumab) and MMAE as SGD-1010.

Human tissue factor is a 43-47 kDa plasma membrane glycoprotein, consisting of an extracellular domain (aa 1-218), a hydrophobic transmembrane domain (aa 220-242), and a cytoplasmic domain (aa 243-263). Tissue factor is the main physiological initiator of the blood coagulation cascade. The extracellular domain binds to serine protease factor VII (FVII) to activate coagulation. The TF:FVIIa complex initiates the extrinsic pathway of blood coagulation by producing activated FX (FXa) and FIX (FIXa) resulting in production of thrombin and clot formation composed of insoluble fibrin and activated platelets. In addition to its role as a pro- coagulant activator, TF plays a role in both vasculogenesis in the developing animal and angiogenesis in normal and malignant adult tissues.

In vitro, the unconjugated TF-directed antibody, tisotumab, bound TF with a KD of 3.15 nM;

tisotumab and tisotumab vedotin bound to the recombinant extracellular domain of TF in a dose dependent manner with similar EC50 values of 2.5 nM (373 ng/mL) and 3.1 nM (469 ng/mL), respectively. Additionally, they bound similarly to TF expressing human cancer cells with EC50 values ranging from 0.19 nM (30 ng/mL) to 2.30 nM (346 ng/mL) depending on the cell line. The data suggests that conjugation of tisotumab does not affect the binding affinity to TF. Upon binding to TF on the cell membrane, tisotumab vedotin is internalized into the cell and localized to the lysosomes. In vitro, the extent of cytotoxicity depends on the level of TF cell surface expression; tisotumab vedotin IC50 values ranged from approximately 1.63 ng/mL to 575 ng/mL amongst the cell lines tested. Tisotumab and tisotumab vedotin induced antibody- dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) in a similar manner with EC50 values ranging from 0.031 nM (4.7 ng/mL) to 0.103 nM (15.4 ng/mL) at the conditions tested. Additionally, data suggests that tisotumab and tisotumab vedotin may have antibody-dependent cellular phagocytosis (ADCP) activity.

Embryo-fetal development

Study title/ number: Intravenous Injection Study for Effects on Embryofetal Developmental and Toxicokinetics with SGN-35 and SGD-1010 in Rats/8204397 Key findings in rats given 0.2 mg/kg MMAE: • No viable fetuses due to post-implantation loss in 1/24 dams.

A small fraction of MMAE released from tisotumab vedotin is metabolized. In vitro data indicates that metabolism of MMAE occurs primarily by CYP3A4. In vitro, the binding of MMAE to human plasma proteins ranged from 67.9% to 82.2%. MMAE is not likely to displace or to be displaced by highly protein-bound drugs. After a single dose of brentuximab vedotin, another ADC that contains MMAE, ~24% of the total MMAE administered was recovered in feces and urine over a 1-week period (Han 2013), ~72% of which was recovered unchanged in the feces. A similar excretion profile for MMAE is expected after tisotumab vedotin administration. The Applicant’s Position: Population PK indicated that distribution of tisotumab vedotin was limited to the central vascular compartment, with a median elimination half-life of ~4.04 days and no accumulation upon repeat dosing. MMAE has a mean elimination half-life of ~2.56 days with no accumulation on repeat dosing. MMAE exhibited moderate binding to plasma proteins. MMAE is metabolized predominantly by CYP3A4 and excreted mainly by hepatobiliary routes. The FDA’s Assessment: FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position. 6.2.2 General Dosing and Therapeutic Individualization 6.2.2.1 General Dosing Data: The dose escalation part in GEN701 evaluated tisotumab vedotin at doses ranging from 0.3 to 2.6 mg/kg. At the 2.2 mg/kg dose level, 3 subjects experienced 1 dose-limiting toxicity (DLT) each. Because the protocol specified that intermediate dose levels could be evaluated, the DMC recommended to include 3 subjects on 2.0 mg/kg, which was not a preplanned dose level in the protocol. None of the 3 subjects on the 2.0 mg/kg dose level experienced DLTs and, therefore, 2.0 mg/kg was identified as the recommended phase 2 dose (RP2D) for the expansion part. In the pivotal trial GCT1015-04, in subjects treated with 2.0 mg/kg 1Q3W, the probability of response significantly increased with increase of tisotumab vedotin Cycle 1 Cmax. The exposure- efficacy relationships explored in the analyses indicate an increase of efficacy with increase in tisotumab vedotin Cycle 1 exposure, however, responses were observed across the entire exposure range. Lower risk of death is associated with increase of tisotumab vedotin exposure. DOR and PFS were not correlated with ADC exposure (full covariate model). Weight-based dosing at 2.0 mg/kg 1Q3W up to 200 mg for body weight ≥100 kg ensured exposure was similar across tertiles of weight for both ADC and MMAE and is therefore an appropriate dosing paradigm for tisotumab vedotin. Tisotumab vedotin and MMAE exposure and safety endpoint relationships suggest increased exposure was associated with increased incidence of AEs, but that 2.0 mg/kg 1Q3W (up to 200 mg for body weight ≥100 kg) was tolerated with a high relative dose intensity of 96% and overall manageable safety profile.

8.1.1 Pivotal Trial GCT1015-04 (innovaTV 204) Trial Design The Applicant’s Description: Basic Study Design This is a single arm, international, multicenter trial of tisotumab vedotin in subjects with recurrent or metastatic cervical cancer. Eligible subjects had experienced disease progression during or after treatment with a chemotherapy doublet in combination with bevacizumab (if eligible to receive bevacizumab) and had received no more than 2 prior systemic treatment regimens for their recurrent or metastatic disease. Subjects were treated with IV tisotumab vedotin 2.0 mg/kg 1Q3W. Imaging was obtained every 6 weeks for the first 30 weeks and then every 12 weeks thereafter, continued until the subject experiences IRC verified disease progression, began a new anti-cancer therapy, withdrew from the trial, or had died, whichever came first. For subjects who achieved a response, a repeat scan was obtained no earlier than 4 weeks (28 days) after the first scan to confirm the response. Survival status was assessed every 60 days (±7 days), beginning from the day of last IMP dose, or more frequently around the time of a database lock. The study schema is shown in Figure 7.

Clinical safety

Data: The safety profile summarized in this application consists of data from 4 clinical trials of tisotumab vedotin monotherapy including 272 unique subjects. All subjects received the recommended starting dose of 2.0 mg/kg of tisotumab vedotin 1Q3W. The number of subjects in each of the 4 safety analysis groups is described below. The data from these trials are grouped into 4 safety analysis sets: • The GCT1015-04 group includes the 101 subjects with cervical cancer from the pivotal trial. • Pool 1 (Supportive Cervical) includes 57 subjects with cervical cancer tumor types from the GEN701 Cohort Expansion part, GEN702 Cohort Expansion part, and GCT1015-03 trials. This pool evaluates safety in subjects with cervical cancer included in trials of tisotumab vedotin other than GCT1015-04. • Pool 2 (All Cervical) combines GCT1015-04 with Pool 1 for a total of 158 subjects with cervical cancer. • Pool 3 (All Tumor Types) adds the 114 subjects with non-cervical tumor types from GEN701 and GEN702 to the All Cervical cohort for a total of 272 subjects. This population is intended to represent the safety profile of tisotumab vedotin in the largest possible group of subjects who received the RP2D. Inclusion of Pool 3 allows assessments of safety in subjects with a diverse set of tumor types and increases the total number of subjects. The primary evidence of safety is derived from the GCT1015-04 data as the subjects represent a more homogeneous patient population than the pooled data. In addition, all subjects in GCT1015-04 received the same protocol-specified mitigation measures and dose modification

assess very infrequent safety events, FDA evaluated data from the Pool 3 population which included patients with other tumor types treated at the recommended dose of tisotumab vedotin across trials (n=272).
