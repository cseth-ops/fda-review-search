Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

5.1. Executive Summary This NDA can be approved from a nonclinical perspective. Florquinitau F 18 ([18F]MK-6240) is a radioactive diagnostic drug intended for PET imaging of the brain in adults with cognitive impairment who are being evaluated for AD to identify patients with tau NFT pathology. The Applicant conducted a comprehensive battery of nonclinical studies that support the marketing authorization of florquinitau F 18 at the recommended administered activity of 5 mCi and not more than (b) (4) µg mass dose for adult patients with cognitive impairment who are being evaluated for AD. The decision to approve is supported by the nonclinical findings from pharmacology and toxicology studies that demonstrated that florquinitau bound with high affinity and specificity to AD brain homogenates, demonstrated rapid brain penetration with rapid clearance, and showed an adequate safety margin by general toxicology (7- to 8-day repeat dose toxicity study in rats) and in vitro genotoxicity studies (non- GLP in vitro bacterial reverse mutation assay and in vitro micronucleus assay). All nonclinical safety pharmacology, pharmacokinetic, and toxicology studies were conducted by or for the Applicant in accordance with Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) regulations except where stated otherwise.

In Vitro and In Vivo Pharmacodynamics Studies The Applicant conducted in vitro and in vivo pharmacology studies to evaluate binding affinity and selectivity in vitro in human brain slices by saturation binding assays and autoradiography using [3H]MK-6240 (tritiated version of florquinitau) and by in vivo displacement PET studies in healthy rhesus monkeys using florquinitau F 18. The studies demonstrated high affinity and selectivity of [3H]MK-6240 for NFT target with low non-displaceable binding. In vivo data from PET studies conducted in healthy monkeys demonstrated rapid penetration of florquinitau F 18 across the blood-brain barrier (BBB), with no significant binding to CNS targets that could interfere with NFT detection. Florquinitau F 18 also showed poor binding to amyloid-β when evaluated in frontal cortex homogenates enriched for amyloid-β from AD brain tissue (41, 42).

Secondary Pharmacodynamics Studies The Applicant conducted an in vitro selectivity binding study against a panel of 118 known CNS receptors and enzymes using florquinitau to evaluate off-target potential. Florquinitau demonstrated low affinity (KD > 0.5 μM) for all the panel targets, suggesting that they are unlikely to interfere with the in vivo imaging of NFTs. Data from an in vivo PET study in three anesthetized male rhesus monkeys intravenously injected with florquinitau F 18 (approximately 185 MBq) showed rapid penetration of florquinitau F 18 across the BBB, followed by rapid clearance. Florquinitau F 18 reportedly demonstrated no high-affinity binding to CNS targets that could interfere with imaging of tau NFT pathology (41, 42).

Safety pharmacology

The Applicant conducted no stand-alone safety pharmacology studies. However, an in vitro human ether-à-go-go-related gene (hERG) safety study was conducted under GLP conditions to assess the potential effects of florquinitau at 0.3, 1, and 3 μmol/L on the cardiac potassium ion channels in HEK293 cells stably expressing the hERG potassium channel. Florquinitau induced an inhibition of current amplitude by 8.0%, 20.8%, and 51.5% (corresponding to 12.9%, 25.6%, and 55.9% inhibition for florquinitau at 0.3, 1, and 3 μmol/L respectively) in comparison to the negative control group resulting in an estimated IC50 (half-maximal inhibitory concentration) of 3.11 μmol/L with a safety margin of ~ 5,100-fold based on a maximum mass dose of (b) (4) μg per intravenous administration of 5 mCi florquinitau F 18. Inhibition of hERG channels is unlikely at this dose.

Pharmacokinetics/Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion (PK/ADME) The nonclinical pharmacokinetics/absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion (PK/ADME) program included in vitro studies of plasma protein binding (rat, non-human primate (NHP), human), in vitro metabolism by hepatocytes (rat, NHP, human), in vitro drug- drug interaction studies, and in vivo biodistribution and CNS uptake as part of a general toxicity study conducted in rats. Florquinitau is intended for administration by the intravenous route, so no absorption studies were conducted. Florquinitau displayed moderate plasma protein binding in rat, monkey, and human plasma at 87.2 ± 1.3%, 86.5 ± 3.5%, and 84.0 ± 1.8%, respectively. Florquinitau likely undergoes oxidative defluorination as determined by an in vitro metabolism study in rat, NHP, and human liver microsomes. CYP1A2 was the primary enzyme involved in the oxidative metabolism of florquinitau with defluorination by CYP1A2 in human liver microsomes and by CYP3A4 in rhesus monkey liver microsomes. No drug-drug interactions were reported through in vitro studies that evaluated florquinitau as a substrate or inhibitor to human drug transporters. Biodistribution studies of florquinitau F 18 demonstrated rapid uptake and rapid washout from cortical and cerebellar regions in PET imaging studies with normal rhesus monkeys. The urinary bladder was the critical organ with an average absorbed radiation dose of 0.176 mGy/MBq. The average effective dose was estimated as 26.1 μSv/MBq, which is in line with other 18F-labeled PET tracers. The excretion profile for florquinitau F 18 was not determined. However, florquinitau F 18 dosimetry results in NHPs suggested that clearance occurred by both the hepatobiliary and renal excretion routes.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

General toxicology of florquinitau and a related impurity, (b) (4) , by intravenous administration was evaluated in a repeat-dose toxicity study in Wistar Han Crl:WI(Han) rats for seven or eight days (for males and females, respectively) to establish the safety of florquinitau F 18. There were no significant findings for mortality, clinical signs, ophthalmoscopy, gross pathology, or histopathology reported following daily dosing of florquinitau and (b) (4) at up to 333 μg/kg/day ( (b) (4) ratio of test article and impurity). The safety margins (based on the lack of findings from the study) were (b) (4) x the clinical mass dose of (b) (4) µg based on no-

observed-adverse-effect levels (NOAEL) of ≥333 μg/kg/day and (b) (4) μg/kg/day for florquinitau and (b) (4) respectively.

Genotoxicity, Carcinogenicity, Reproductive Toxicity, and Other Nonclinical Studies Florquinitau was negative for genotoxic potential by a non-GLP Ames bacterial reverse mutation assay and a non-GLP in vitro micronucleus assay. While a complete battery of genotoxicity studies was not conducted for [19F]MK-6240, the totality of data did not indicate genotoxic potential, and these studies are not required for diagnostic radiopharmaceuticals. Moreover, carcinogenicity studies were not conducted and are not required for diagnostic radiopharmaceuticals.

Other Nonclinical Studies No other nonclinical studies are recommended to support the safety of florquinitau F 18 for the indication in the intended population.

5.3.2. Mechanism of Action and Proof-Of-Concept Studies

Mechanism of Action: The mechanism of action of florquinitau F 18 is based on data demonstrating that florquinitau F 18 binds to NFTs from AD brain homogenates with a KD of 0.3 nM. Binding specificity and

selectivity were evaluated by in vitro autoradiography studies of [3H]MK-6240 with unlabeled florquinitau or T-808 (a structurally distinct tau ligand) for determination of non-displaceable binding, conducted in human brain slices from patients with AD. Displaceable [3H]MK-6240 binding was found in the gray matter of the entorhinal cortex and hippocampus of NFT-rich human AD brain slices while no displaceable binding was found in the adjacent white matter. The in vitro data also demonstrated high binding specificity to NFTs over other targets, as revealed by a screen of 118 CNS targets, with IC50 values of greater than 1 μM for all targets and absence of binding to amyloid plaques (amyloid-β enriched brain homogenates from AD brain frontal cortex), with a Ki of 10 μM. 6. Clinical Pharmacology

6.1. Executive Summary

Florquinitau F 18 is a fluorine-18 labeled small molecule that binds to tau NFT in the brain. Florquinitau F 18 is intended for PET of the brain in adults with cognitive impairment who are being evaluated for AD to identify patients with tau NFT pathology. The Applicant’s proposed activity of florquinitau F 18 is 185 MBq (5 mCi) as a bolus IV injection. The proposed mass dose is not more than (b)(4) μg per dose. Imaging is proposed to be initiated at 90 minutes post- administration, with an imaging duration of 20 minutes. The efficacy of florquinitau F 18 was evaluated in two phase 3 studies: CE-20-01 and CE-23-01. The proposed injected activity, mass dose, and imaging acquisition window and duration were used in these pivotal studies. The co-primary efficacy endpoints of both studies were PPA and NPA of florquinitau F 18 PET images for identification of participants with tau NFT pathology as determined by five independent readers on a participant-level visual read compared to the reference standard. Both co-primary endpoints met prespecified thresholds for both studies. The safety of florquinitau F 18 was evaluated in pooled data from studies AIBL and POINTER, and a meta-analysis of other studies and a global safety database. The adverse events associated with florquinitau F 18 were generally uncommon and non-serious. See additional details on efficacy and safety in Section 8. The proposed injected radioactivity, mass dose, and imaging acquisition window are acceptable based on evaluation of phase 3 studies and early phase clinical studies. No dose modification is recommended based on intrinsic factors (age, sex, renal function, and hepatic function). Patient counseling regarding drug interactions is recommended to avoid use of CYP1A2 inducers, including tobacco smoke, at least 7 days before florquinitau F 18 administration. The Office of Clinical Pharmacology has reviewed the information contained in NDA 220496 and determined that the NDA can be approved.

6.2. Summary of Clinical Pharmacology Assessment

6.2.1. Pharmacology and Clinical Pharmacokinetics

Florquinitau F 18 is distributed mainly to gallbladder, small intestine, upper large intestine, and urinary bladder wall. Brain uptake peaks within minutes of administration. The signal intensity in brain regions increases after time zero and is stabilized by approximately 40 minutes to 60 minutes following administration.

The plasma terminal biological half-life of florquinitau F 18 is 2.3 ± 0.5 hours. F 18 decays by positron emission to stable oxygen-18 with a half-life of 1.8 hours. Refer to Section 6.3.1 for details.

8.1.1. CE-20-01

8.1.1.1. Study Design

Overview and Objectives In studies CE-20-01 and CE-23-01, florquinitau F 18 images previously acquired in other studies for purposes other than diagnostic performance evaluation were prospectively read in a blinded fashion. The stated objective of studies CE-20-01 and CE-23-01 was to evaluate the diagnostic performance of florquinitau F 18 PET for the identification of tau NFTs in the brains of patients with cognitive impairment being evaluated for AD. Both study CE-20-01 and CE-23- 01 were identical in design and are described together below. Study-specific design components are described where relevant.

Study Design

Design Features Common to Both Primary Efficacy Studies Studies CE-20-01 and CE-23-01 evaluated the PPA and NPA of florquinitau F 18 PET for the identification of participants with tau NFT pathology against a predefined clinicoradiographic reference standard for tau positive/negative binary classification in lieu of a histopathological truth standard (13). The reference standard (RS) for tau NFT pathology in both CE-20-01 and CE-23-01 was determined based on the participant’s baseline clinical evaluation of cognitive function and radiographically (amyloid PET) determined brain amyloid status as reported in the image source studies. The RS was categorized as positive or negative for tau NFT at the participant-level as follows: • Tau reference standard positive: cognitively impaired (either MCI due to AD or mild AD dementia) and amyloid PET positive (most representative of B2/B3 NFT)

• Tau reference standard negative: cognitively unimpaired and amyloid PET negative (most representative of B0 NFT) For both CE-20-01 and CE-23-01, a stratified random sampling approach was used to sample a 1:1 ratio of tau reference standard positive and negative populations. Participants who did not meet the definition of either tau reference standard positive or tau reference standard negative were not included. Within both populations, sampling was further stratified by age and sex, and within the positive reference standard population, sampling was balanced for Stage 3 vs. Stage 4 AD. Both studies CE-20-01 and CE-23-01 included images that met the following criteria for adequate technical quality: 1. Florquinitau F 18 PET was acquired between 90 and 110 minutes after an intravenous administration of 5 mCi ±20%. 2. The images did not contain artifacts such as plane nonuniformities due to PET camera normalization, reconstruction streaks, motion-induced errors, attenuation correction artifacts, bilateral asymmetries, striping in coronal/sagittal planes, detector block artifacts, star artifact (specific to certain cameras), or other artifacts arising from data corruption. 3. The images fully covered the brain including the cerebellum. 4. The images demonstrated an acceptable signal to noise ratio (clear visibility of the reference anatomy such as the brain outline and retina on visual inspection). Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment Alzheimer’s disease (AD) is a debilitating and ultimately fatal progressive neurodegenerative disorder. AD is reported to be the sixth most common cause of death in the United States with an estimated 7.4 million Americans aged 65 and older currently living with AD (1). The spread of tau pathology from the limbic regions in early AD to the neocortical areas in advanced AD is closely associated with the onset of cognitive impairment and subsequent neurodegeneration and cognitive decline (2-4). Florquinitau F 18 is a positron-emitting radiopharmaceutical intended for use with PET imaging of the brain in adults with cognitive impairment who are being evaluated for AD to identify patients with tau neurofibrillary tangle (NFT) pathology. The effectiveness of florquinitau F 18 was evaluated in two adequate and well-controlled multicenter clinical studies, CE-20-01 and CE-23-01. Both studies were conducted with participants who were either cognitively impaired and amyloid PET positive or were cognitively unimpaired and amyloid PET negative. In CE-20-01, the lower bound of the 95% CI for positive percent agreement (PPA) ranged from 72% to 82%, and from 94% to 96% for negative percent agreement (NPA), across five readers. In CE-23-01, the lower bound of the 95% CI for PPA ranged from 61% to 76%, and from 89% to 97% for NPA, across five readers. All five readers in both studies met the prespecified threshold of 60% for the co-primary endpoints of PPA and NPA. The results of the two studies demonstrate acceptable performance of florquinitau F 18 in identifying patients with tau NFT pathology. Weaknesses of the clinicoradiographic reference standard, including limited representation of the population of intended clinical use and related suboptimal evaluation of earlier stage disease, are also shared by the traditional autopsy reference standard. The potential for lower performance in earlier disease stages and general risk of misdiagnosis related to false positive and false negative results are addressed through warnings and precautions in the labeling. The performance of florquinitau F 18 for assessing the distribution of tau pathology could not be determined with the patient-level reference standard, and the labeling appropriately reflects this limitation by indicating florquinitau F 18 for the identification of patients with tau NFTs (b) (4) Such information is expected to have utility in certain clinical scenarios, such as evaluation of patients with suspected AD but atypical presentation, as reflected in current appropriate use criteria for tau PET (5). The safety of florquinitau F 18 was evaluated in two clinical studies including a total of 1,734 subjects. The most commonly reported adverse reactions were headache, nausea, injection site reactions, dizziness, and abdominal discomfort. The safety profile of florquinitau F 18 is broadly similar to that of other PET imaging drugs. The radiation effective dose of approximately 5.4
