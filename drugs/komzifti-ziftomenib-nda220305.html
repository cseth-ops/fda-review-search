Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

The FDA’s Assessment: Ziftomenib is a menin inhibitor. Menin is a nuclear scaffold protein that is part of protein complexes that regulate gene transcription via chromatin remodeling. Menin binds directly to the N-terminal of mixed lineage leukemia lysine methyltransferase 2a (MLL [KMT2A]), and this interaction is involved in nucleophosmin 1(NPM1)-driven gene regulation. Ziftomenib binds to menin at the KMT2A binding site and disrupts the interaction between menin and KMT2A. The proposed indication for ziftomenib is for the treatment of adult patients with relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukemia (AML) with a susceptible NPM1 mutation. The leukemogenic activity of mutant NPM1 is mediated by the interactions between menin and MLL[KMT2A]. Susceptible NPM1 mutations are defined as those that result in loss of the nucleolar localization signal and the insertion of a new nuclear export signal leading to the cytoplasmic accumulation of mutant NPM1 and leukemogenesis.

Ziftomenib inhibited the interaction between recombinant menin and a KMT2A peptide (IC50 = 37 nM), suggesting that ziftomenib can inhibit the interaction between menin and KMT2A fusion proteins. Ziftomenib showed anti-proliferative activity against a variety of leukemia and lymphoma cell lines with different KMT2A fusion states (IC50 = 3.9-31 nM). Alterations to menin-KMT2A target genes were also induced in these models. The in vivo anti-tumor activity of ziftomenib was evaluated in xenograft models using the human AML cell lines MV4-11 and MOLM-13 and patient-derived cells in immunocompromised rats and mice with oral administration and a variety of treatment durations. Overall, ziftomenib showed dose-dependent anti-tumor activity in these nonclinical in vivo models. These findings are supportive of the mechanism of action of ziftomenib as described in the label. In binding assays conducted to determine off-target binding, ziftomenib did not show high levels of activity against the targets evaluated under the conditions tested, suggesting a low potential for off-target activity in a physiologically relevant context.

Based on findings from non-GLP and GLP assays, ziftomenib has the potential to block the human Ether-a-go-go-Related Gene (hERG) and the human L-type voltage-dependent calcium channel, alpha 1C subunit (hCavl.2) channels. The IC50 values for hERG and hCavl.2 inhibition were 1.61 and 2.8 μM, respectively. Dose-dependent lower blood pressure and longer PR intervals were seen after single doses of 40, 120, and 200 mg/kg/day in dogs, while decreases in heart rate, and increases in RR interval were noted at 200 mg/kg/day during a 13-week repeat- dose study in dogs.

Clinical efficacy

KO-MEN-001

Trial Design

The Applicant’s Description: Basic Study Design KO-MEN-001 is an ongoing Phase 1/2, first-in-human, open-label, multiple cohort study of the menin inhibitor, ziftomenib, in adult patients with R/R AML. A graphical representation of the study is provided in Applicant Figure 1. The Phase 1 portion consisted of 2 parts: dose-escalation (Part 1a) and dose-validation/expansion (Part 1b). In the Part 1a portion of the study, 30 R/R AML patients received ziftomenib in doses ranging from 50 to 1000 mg QD and an MTD of 800 mg daily was established. Based on safety, tolerability, PK, and preliminary antileukemic activity observed in Part 1a, Part 1b was initiated for expansion at 2 dose levels (200 mg and 600 mg QD) in a total of 53 patients with KMT2A-r or NPM1-m AML, to determine the RP2D. Based on the totality of efficacy, safety, and PK data after completing Phase 1, the RP2D of ziftomenib was determined to be 600 mg QD for adult patients with R/R NPM1-m AML administered in continuous 28-day cycles. The Phase 2 portion of KO-MEN-001 was a single-cohort study evaluating ziftomenib 600 mg QD in patients with locally determined R/R NPM1-m AML. The Phase 2 study is the primary basis of efficacy for this application; the primary analysis was performed when the planned sample size of 85 patients had been followed for at least 6 cycles or discontinued earlier, as prespecified in the study protocol and statistical analysis plan. Patients could continue treatment with ziftomenib until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity, and patients were permitted to resume treatment with ziftomenib following hematopoietic stem cell transplant (HSCT).

Clinical safety

THE APPLICANT’S POSITION

The pivotal study, KO-MEN-001 (main study), was the primary source of data for the evaluation of the safety of 600 mg QD of ziftomenib as a monotherapy for the treatment of patients with R/R NPM1-m AML. The primary safety population included all patients with a confirmed NPM1 mutation who received 600 mg QD, including the 92 patients in Phase 2 and 20 patients in Phase 1b for a total of 112 patients. Additional supportive sources of safety data summarized within the application include other ongoing clinical studies of ziftomenib. The KO-MEN-001 sub-studies are evaluating drug-drug interactions in non-NPM1-m AML (sub-studies 1 and 2) or ziftomenib activity in ALL (sub- study 3), and Studies KO-MEN-007 and KO-MEN-008 evaluate ziftomenib in combination with standard-of- care regimens (eg, venetoclax, azacitidine, intensive and low-dose chemotherapy, and gilteritinib). As these data do not directly inform the proposed indication of patients with R/R NPM1-m AML due to the inherent heterogeneity of combination agents, molecular types and study populations, summary-level data are presented in the application for a comprehensive package without integration. For studies evaluating the combination of ziftomenib with standard- of-care regimens (eg, KO-MEN-007 and KO-MEN-008), safety data will be summarized for each individual cohort within each study. There were also two completed (KO-MEN-003, KO-MEN-005) and one terminated (KO-MEN- 006) PK studies in healthy volunteers. Safety data from these studies are presented individually in the respective CSRs and were not included in the ISS.
