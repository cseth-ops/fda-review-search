Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

5.1. Executive Summary Penpulimab is a human IgG1 monoclonal antibody that targets programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1); its established pharmacologic class is PD-1-blocking antibody. PD-1 is expressed on activated T and B cells, natural killer cells, and myeloid lineage cells. Binding of PD-1 to its ligands, programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) and PD-L2, inhibits T-cell activity, T-cell proliferation, and cytokine production. In the tumor microenvironment, PD-1 signaling contributes to the inhibition of T cell-mediated immune surveillance of tumors. Thus, blockade of PD-1 signaling in the tumor microenvironment is intended to enhance tumor immunosurveillance and anti-tumor immune responses.

In in vitro studies, penpulimab bound to human and cynomolgus monkey PD-1 but did not bind rodent PD-1, supporting the use of cynomolgus monkeys as the single pharmacologically relevant species for toxicity assessment. In cellular assays, penpulimab blocked the binding of human PD-1 to human PD-L1 and PD-L2 and also competitively bound to PD-1 expressed on the surface of human PD-1-transfected 293T cells. Additionally, penpulimab induced the secretion of interleukin-2 (IL-2) and interferon-gamma (IFN-γ) in a mixed culture of peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and dendritic cells (DCs) and in a mixed culture of PBMCs, DCs, and Raji Burkitt lymphoma cells. Penpulimab also enhanced IFN-γ secretion by cytomegalovirus (CMV)-responsive human PBMCs during the recall response to CMV antigen.

Given that pempulimab does not bind mouse PD-1, the Applicant investigated the in vivo antitumor activity of pempulimab in a human PD-1 knock-in genetically engineered mouse model (HuGEMM) implanted subcutaneously with MC38 mouse colon adenocarcinoma cells. Intraperitoneal administration of pempulimab at doses of 0.8 and 8 mg/kg twice a week for a total of 5 doses resulted in tumor growth inhibition (TGI) compared to the isotype control. The Applicant also evaluated the antitumor activity of penpulimab in immunodeficient mice implanted subcutaneously with Raji cells expressing PD-L1 or a Raji-PD-L1/PBMC mixture. Intravenous (IV) administration of penpulimab (10 mg/kg) on Days 7, 13, and 21 resulted in greater TGI compared to the isotype control.

The Applicant evaluated the safety of penpulimab in cynomolgus monkeys in GLP-compliant repeat-dose toxicology studies of up to 13 weeks duration. In the 6- and 13-week studies, monkeys received IV infusion of pempulimab at doses up to 60 mg/kg once every 2 weeks. There were no mortalities or penpulimab-related findings of toxicological significance. Target

organs of toxicity in the 6-week study were the kidney (increased cortical tubular pigment or pigmented interstitial macrophages), spleen (increased cellularity), adrenal gland (cortical hypertrophy), parotid salivary gland (acinar cell atrophy), thymus (increased weight); and in the 13-week study was lymph nodes (erythrophagocytosis and increased cellularity). The incidence of anti-drug antibody formation was 63.33% in the 6-week study and 93.33% in the 13-week study, which may explain the limited findings noted in the 13-week study. General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The Applicant’s Position:

In 6-week repeat-dose toxicity study, no apparent toxicity was observed in cynomolgus monkeys following intravenous infusion of penpulimab at 4, 15, or 60 mg/kg once every two weeks for 6 weeks. Treatment-related histopathological change of kidneys (increased cortical tubular pigment or pigmented interstitial macrophages) was noted in some animals but no impact on kidney functions. Pigment in the cortical tubular epithelium of the kidneys is not reported as a common background finding in cynomolgus monkeys. In this study, there were no apparent changes in clinical chemistry, urinalysis, or organ weights which would indicate an impact of treatment on kidney function. There were also no other histopathological changes associated with the pigment which were indicative of damage to the kidneys. These changes were therefore considered not to be adverse. The no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) was considered to be 60 mg/kg.

In 13-week repeat-dose toxicity study, no apparent toxicity and histopathological change of kidney was observed in cynomolgus monkeys following intravenous infusion of penpulimab at 4, 15, or 60 mg/kg once every two weeks for 13 weeks, the NOAEL was considered to be 60 mg/kg.

The FDA’s Assessment:

In general, FDA agrees with the Applicant’s assessment of the toxicology data. In the 6-week repeat-dose toxicology study, clinical chemistry findings included a dose-dependent decrease in total bilirubin and increase in creatine kinase levels at all dose levels in males, which were present at the end of the recovery period. In addition to the kidney, as noted by the Applicant, other target organs of toxicity in the 6-week study were the spleen (increased cellularity of the red and white pulp), adrenal gland (cortical hypertrophy), parotid salivary gland (acinar cell atrophy), and thymus (increased weight). Changes in the adrenal gland, spleen, and thymus (females) were present at the end of the recovery period. Of the 30 monkey serum samples tested, 19 were ADA-positive with an incidence of 63.33%.

In the 13-week toxicity study, histological findings included erythrophagocytosis and increased cellularity in the lymph nodes (inguinal, mandibular, mesenteric), which were present at the end of the recovery period. Limited findings may be reflective of ADA formation. Of the 30 monkey serum samples tested, 28 were ADA-positive with an incidence of 93.33%.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

The Applicant’s Position:

Fertility, embryonic fetal development or pre- and postnatal developmental toxicity studies were not conducted with penpulimab. As described in ICH Guidance S9, fertility and early embryonic developmental toxicity study is not warranted for marketing of pharmaceuticals for the treatment of patients with advanced cancer. Information available from general toxicology studies on the pharmaceutical’s effect on reproductive organs were used as the basis of the assessment of impairment of fertility. In the 6-week and 13-week repeat-dose toxicity studies, there were no notable effects in the male and female reproductive organs.

Embryo-fetal development toxicity studies were not conducted with penpulimab. Literature data shows that a central function of the PD-1/PD-L1 pathway is to preserve pregnancy by maintaining maternal immune tolerance to the fetus. Blockade of PD-L1 signaling has been shown in murine models of pregnancy to disrupt tolerance to the fetus and to increase fetal loss. There are potential reproductive risks when giving penpulimab during pregnancy, including increased rates of miscarriage or stillbirth. Penpulimab is an IgG1 antibody drug with high affinity to human FcRn, thus it is expected to cross the placenta during third-trimester pregnancy in human, though the exposure of penpulimab in fetus under treatment dose level is undetermined. Based on its mechanism of action, fetal exposure to penpulimab may increase the risk of developing immune-mediated disorders or altering the normal immune response and immune-mediated disorders have been reported in PD-1 knockout mice.

Pre- and postnatal toxicity studies were not conducted with penpulimab. As described in ICH Guidance S9, pre- and postnatal toxicity toxicity study is not warranted for marketing of pharmaceuticals for the treatment of patients with advanced cancer.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA agrees that dedicated reproductive and developmental toxicity studies are not needed to support this BLA submission. To address the potential effects of penpulimab on embryo-fetal development, the Applicant submitted an assessment of the potential reproductive toxicity of penpulimab using a weight-of-evidence approach. FDA agreed in January 2021 that this approach appeared reasonable.

The PD-1/PD-L1 pathway plays a critical role in maintaining maternal immunological tolerance to the fetus during pregnancy (Guleria et al. 2005). PD-L1 is expressed in the human placenta throughout pregnancy and at the maternal-fetal interface in fetal villous syncytiotrophoblasts and extravillous cytotrophoblasts (Petroff et al. 2003). Blockade of PD-L1 signaling in murine models of pregnancy disrupts tolerance to the fetus, resulting in increased fetal loss (Guleria et al. 2005).

Penpulimab is a humanized IgG1 antibody, and human IgG is known to cross the placenta; thus, penpulimab may be transmitted from the mother to the fetus. Based on its mechanism of action and findings from murine models of pregnancy (Guleria et al. 2005), administration of penpulimab is likely to disrupt the maintenance of normal pregnancy. Trial Design

The Applicant’s Description:

AK105-304 is a randomized, double-blind, multi-center phase III clinical study to compare the efficacy and safety of penpulimab combined with chemotherapy and placebo combined with chemotherapy in the first-line treatment of R/M NPC. Approximately 278 subjects were planned to be enrolled in this study. The subjects were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to the 2 treatment groups, stratified by stages of disease (primary metastases vs. recurrent), ECOG (0 vs. 1), and liver metastasis (present vs. absent): Arm A (experimental arm): Penpulimab+ gemcitabine + cisplatin/carboplatin, every 3 weeks (Q3W) for up to 6 cycles, followed by penpulimab monotherapy Q3W. Arm B (control arm): Placebo+ gemcitabine + cisplatin/carboplatin, Q3W for up to 6 cycles, followed by placebo monotherapy Q3W. In each arm, carboplatin was selected as a replacement of cisplatin at the discretion of investigator prior to randomization. After randomization, change of chemotherapy regimen was not allowed during the treatment period. Subjects received up to 6 cycles of penpulimab or placebo combined with chemotherapy, followed by penpulimab or placebo monotherapy Q3W as maintenance treatment until lack of clinical benefit as judged by the investigator (comprehensive judgment by the investigator based on RECIST v1.1 imaging assessment and clinical condition), intolerable toxicity, completion of 24 months of treatment, or other criteria for discontinuation outlined in the protocol, whichever occurred first. The study consisted of a screening period (up to 28 days from the signing of the informed consent form to the first dose of study treatment), a treatment period (including on-treatment visits and an end-of-treatment visit), and a follow-up period (including a safety follow-up visit, a disease progression follow-up visit, and survival follow-up visits). Subjects underwent a screening evaluation within 28 days before the first dose to confirm eligibility. Tumor specimens (tumor tissue samples either archived or freshly obtained) were collected from subjects during the screening period. Regular tumor response assessments were performed for all subjects, with PFS evaluated by BICR based on RECIST v1.1 as the primary response variable. Subjects underwent a tumor assessment every 6 weeks (±7 days) for the first 54 weeks after the first dose of study

treatment, and every 9 weeks (±7 days) thereafter until radiographic progression or loss of clinical benefit (whichever occurs later), or the start of new antitumor therapy, or lost to follow- up, or death, or withdrawal of consent, or study closure, whichever occurred first.
