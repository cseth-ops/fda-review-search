Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

19.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application Crinecerfont is a selective antagonist of CRF1 receptors. Crinecerfont blocks the binding of CRF to CRF1 receptors in the pituitary but not to CRF2 receptors. Studies in the nonclinical program to support crinecerfont clinical studies were conducted with different salt forms of crinecerfont. The primary pharmacology studies were mainly conducted with crinecerfont hydrochloride

(HCl). The initial pharmacokinetic (PK) and toxicology studies were conducted with the tosylate salt form. Because of the low absorption and a large food effect in clinical studies, different formulations of crinecerfont tosylate salt and free base forms were evaluated in absorption (b) (4) studies. The free base formulation was (b) (4) selected for later nonclinical and clinical studies Nonclinical studies adequately bridged the data from studies conducted with tosylate salt to those conducted with the free base, and both sets of studies contributed to characterizing the toxicity profile of crinecerfont.

19.1.1. ADME/PK Crinecerfont absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion (ADME) and PK parameters were characterized in a series of in vitro and in vivo studies. In vivo studies were conducted in rat, dog, mouse, rabbit, and monkeys. The nonclinical PK studies are summarized below.

Absorption Crinecerfont absorption and pharmacokinetics were evaluated in vitro and in nonclinical species using intravenous (IV) and oral administration of crinecerfont. An in vitro study in colorectal adenocarcinoma (Caco)-2-TC7 cells with [14C]-crinecerfont tosylate showed that crinecerfont membrane permeability was low and that crinecerfont was not a substrate for P-glycoprotein (P- gp) transporter. Single-dose in vivo PK studies were conducted with tosylate salt and free-base crinecerfont following oral (PO) or IV administration. Bioavailability from oral dosing was 53% in rats, 14% in rabbits, and 19% in dogs for tosylate salt, and 49% in monkey for free base. (b) (4) Absorption was higher for free-base crinecerfont in formulation and when administered with food. The half-life (t1/2) of crinecerfont following single oral or IV administration was 8 hours in mice, 13 to 24 hours in rat, 8 hours in rabbits, 24 to 90 hours in dogs, and 5 to 13 hours in monkeys. The time to reach maximum observed plasma concentration (Tmax) following a single oral administration of crinecerfont tosylate ranged from 2 to 6 hours. In general, exposure of crinecerfont increased less than dose-proportionally and approached saturation or plateaued as dose increased. In repeated-dose toxicokinetic (TK) analyses in toxicity studies, no accumulation was observed in rats when crinecerfont was administered as the tosylate salt. Modest (b) (4) accumulation was observed for free base administered as at 5, 15, and 50 mg/kg/day doses (less than 3-fold accumulation), with a decrease in exposure observed at the high dose after repeat doses. Exposure was not sex-dependent in rats, but exposure was slightly higher in female dogs compared to males (<2-fold).

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The toxicity of crinecerfont was evaluated in a series of single- and repeat-dose studies in mice, rats, and dogs. The target organs for crinecerfont were liver (mouse, rat, and dog), thyroid gland (rat and dog), gastrointestinal (GI) tract (mouse and rat), and coagulation-associated effects. GI tract findings were not clinically relevant and thyroid gland findings are discussed in the context of carcinogenic potential (below). Clinical trial data identified neutropenia and neuropsychiatric safety as potential clinical risks. Neutropenia was not observed in the pivotal rat or dog toxicity studies and there were no nonclinical signals for immunosuppression or neurobehavioral effects. Mechanistic studies showed crinecerfont is active at the pituitary and can cross the blood-brain barrier, but distribution to the central nervous system was low, with an observed brain to plasma ratio of 0.1. In comparison, crinecerfont was highly distributed to liver (where it is metabolized) as evidenced by a ≥8.5 liver-to-plasma ratio. Initial studies with a crinecerfont tosylate formulation were associated with coagulopathy (prolongation of prothrombin time [PT] and activated partial thromboplastin time [APTT] and/or bleeding) and mortality in repeat-dose rat studies. Subsequently, the tosylate salt formulation was replaced with a free base form of crinecerfont for evaluation in the pivotal chronic toxicology, carcinogenicity, prenatal and postnatal and juvenile nonclinical studies, and for the clinical studies. In chronic rat studies with crinecerfont free base, no mortality or bleeding effects were noted; however, changes in coagulation parameters (prolongation of PT up to 60%, and APTT up to 90%) were observed at 1250 mg/kg/day (6-fold MRHD exposure). Similar findings (prolongation of PT) were observed in the chronic dog study conducted with crinecerfont free (b) (4) base, without any other coagulopathy up to 1000 mg/kg/day (5-fold MRHD exposure). . Drug-related effects on coagulation were routinely monitored in clinical trials with the crinecerfont free base formulation. Crinecerfont is predominantly metabolized by CYP3A4 in the liver in animals and in humans. In rats and dogs, crinecerfont induces drug-metabolizing enzymes, which resulted in increased liver weight and hepatocellular hypertrophy in both species. Mechanistic studies confirmed that induction of metabolizing enzymes contributed to dose- and duration-dependent signs of liver toxicity. In rodents only, higher crinecerfont doses (approximately seven times MRHD exposures) caused a low incidence of more advanced liver injury, including multinucleated hepatocytes and anisokaryosis and minimal hepatocyte necrosis. There were no liver findings of concern at doses corresponding to one- to fourfold clinical exposures, and induction or inhibition of liver metabolizing enzymes is unlikely to occur at clinically relevant doses. Routine monitoring for biomarkers and signs of drug-induced liver injury was conducted throughout the clinical development program.

Carcinogenicity

The oncogenic potential of crinecerfont was evaluated in two-year rat and 6-month transgenic mouse carcinogenicity studies. Crinecerfont did not show oncogenic effects in transgenic mice. In the 2-year rat study, crinecerfont increased the incidence of thyroid gland follicular cell adenoma and carcinoma in females at 100 mg/kg crinecerfont (4-fold MRHD exposure). Thyroid gland follicular cell tumors arise from excessive clearance of thyroid hormone from induction of liver drug-metabolizing enzymes, which results in release of, and continuous stimulation by, thyroid stimulating hormone (TSH) on the thyroid follicular cells. This mechanism of inducing thyroid gland follicular cell hyperplasia and subsequent tumor development in rats is considered not relevant in humans (Wu and Farrelly 2006; Bartsch et al. 2018). Additional evidence from the chronic 6-month rat toxicity study showed increased thyroid gland weights and follicular cell hypertrophy and hyperplasia, with increased TSH measured in the subchronic rat study further supporting thyroid hormone stimulation leading to thyroid gland hypertrophy. Overall, the data from genotoxicity, 6-month transgenic mouse carcinogenicity, and two-year rat carcinogenicity studies show that the potential human oncogenic risk of crinecerfont is low.

13.2. Potential Risks or Safety Concerns Based on Drug Class or Other Drug-Specific Factors Crinecerfont is a first-in-class drug, so there is no marketing experience. Adrenal insufficiency was identified as a potential safety concern because of the drug’s mechanism of action. Crinecerfont decreases pituitary ACTH levels, leading to decreased adrenal androgen levels, and ultimately allowing for reduction in glucocorticoid dosage. Neuropsychiatric safety was identified as another potential concern because crinecerfont is centrally acting and was previously in development as a treatment for major depression. Potential risks of hypersensitivity and neutropenia were identified because of observations in earlier clinical studies. A hypersensitivity event of angioedema occurred in a phase 1 study (Study NBI-74788-1724) of two dose regimens of crinecerfont (100 mg qAM/200 mg qPM, or 100 mg qAM/150 mg qPM) in healthy adult volunteers. According to the Applicant, the 41-year- old male with no relevant past medical history developed a moderate-grade generalized rash after 3 days of crinecerfont treatment with 100/150 mg. He subsequently experienced perioral edema and throat tightness. No symptoms of shortness of breath or difficulty swallowing were reported, and vital signs were stable. These signs and symptoms resolved following treatment with dexamethasone, inhaled epinephrine, and an oral corticosteroid taper. The study drug was discontinued because of the hypersensitivity event. The investigator considered the event possibly related to study drug. Hypersensitivity reactions are discussed further in Section 13.7.2. Neutropenia was observed in a phase 2 study of crinecerfont for major depressive disorder (Study DFI5687). Reproductive and developmental toxicology

19.1.7.4.1. Oral Male and Female Fertility Study in Rats

Key Findings In a fertility study conducted by daily oral administration of crinecerfont tosylate to mature male and female rats at dose of 0, 150, 300 or 1000 mg/kg/day, no remarkable toxicities on fertility were observed in male and female rats up to the dose of 1000 mg/kg/day (2-fold MRHD based on AUC). However, crinecerfont tosylate was related with deaths or preterminal euthanasia in mid- and high-dose males, which were associated with hemorrhage and changes in coagulation parameters (prolongation of PT and APTT, and low platelet counts). The NOAEL for fertility in males and females was 1000 mg/kg/day which corresponded to exposures 2 times the MRHD. The NOAEL for paternal toxicity was the low dose (150 mg/kg/day).
