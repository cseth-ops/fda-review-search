General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The cynomolgus monkey model was used to predict the effects of ofatumumab in patients. The pivotal nonclinical studies tested only two doses: 20 and 100 mg/kg of

ofatumumab. Both of these doses appeared to saturate the receptor. Dose responses were

not apparent for magnitude of effect, but were apparent for duration of responses.

Treatment-related effects generally appeared to

be expected pharmacology, or indirect effects attributable to the expected pharmacology. A no observed effect level (NOEL) was not identified. The primary pharmacotoxic activity is depletion of B-cells from circulation and lymphoid tissues. Recovery by or before 6-months was consistently apparent.

The high dose, 100 mg/kg, is technically considered severely toxic since it caused transient tremors and mortalities in >10% of treated animals in the 7-month study. However, the effects observed in monkeys are considered clinically monitorable and treatable.

Genetic toxicology: No studies to assess potential genetic toxicity were performed. Carcinogenicity: No studies to assess potential carcinogenicity were performed.

An embryofetal study was performed (dosing GD20-GD50, with saturating levels of ofatumumab remaining in maternal circulation until GD100), with Cesarean section on GD100. No teratogenicity was observed. Ofatumumab depleted fetal B-cells (to ~10% of control values); ofatumumab and PAHA (presumably maternal PAHA) were detected in fetal blood, demonstrating that the antibodies crossed the placental barrier. Treatment was associated with decreased placental weights, decreased fetal spleen weight, and decreased fetal thymus weight. The potential biological significance of these changes is unclear.

No stand-alone studies were performed to assess the effects of ofatumumab on fertility parameters. The standard toxicology studies are only of limited utility to assess potential reproductive toxicity: all ofatumumab-treated males were sexually immature, and only 7 sexually mature females (4/12 in the 4-week study, #23542; 3/12 in the 7-month study, #25052), as evidenced by clinical signs of menstruation, were treated with ofatumumab. However, no evidence of treatment-related reproductive toxicity was apparent in these two nonclinical studies, :

ci i : Two studies, a tissue-cross reactivity study in human tissues (#23179) and a screening tissue cross-reactivity study in multiple species (HGMB-3001- 024) were reviewed. Ofatumumab is not pharmacologically active in the mouse, rat, rabbit, dog, or pig. Ofatumumab binds human lymphocytes in multiple organs.

EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

L Recommendations A. Recommendation on approvability

Recommend approval of Biologics Licensing Application (BLA) STN 125326. No basis for objection was identified in the review of the nonclinical pharmacology and toxicology sections of the original BLA submission and amendments. Nonclinical toxicities of ofatumumab are extensions of the pharmacology of the product, were reflected in the clinical studies, and are monitorable in the clinical setting.

B, Recommendation for nonclinical studies

No additional nonclinical studies are recommended to support the treatment of patients with chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL) who have disease refractory to alemtuzumab and fludarabine.

C. Recommendations on labeling

Nonclinical recommendations have been incorporated into the draft label for section 8 (8.1 and 8.3), section 12, and section 13 (13.1 and 13.3). No additional changes to labeling are recommended in this review.

Il. Summary of nonclinical findings

A. Brief overview of nonclinical findings

Ofatumumab is an immunoglobulin class G1 kappa (igG1 x) human monoclonal antibody specific for an epitope on the cell surface protein cluster of differentiation 20 (CD20). The product is intended for treatment, by intravenous infusion, of patients with B-cell chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL).

* CD20 is expressed on nornial B-cells during their differentiation and maturation, from the pre-B cell stage to the memory B-cell stage, Reportedly, it is also expressed on some CD3* T cells. CD20 is expressed by several hematological tumors that originate from hematopoietic stem cells. Ofatumumab binds human CD20 with high affinity and specificity; in vitro and presumably in vivo, binding of ofatumumab causes death of CD20* cells by inducing antibody-dependent cell- mediated cytotoxicity (ADCC) and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC). As a proof-of- concept, ofatumumab was shown to exhibit anti-tumor activity in SCID mice bearing CD20* human tumors.

Ofatumumab bound rhesus monkey and cynomolgus monkey CD20 with the same or slightly higher (2-fold higher) affinity compared to human CD20. Ofatumumab did not bind to CD20 from the other species tested. The pivotal nonclinical in vivo testing was limited to one species, the cynomolgus monkey,

In monkeys, the primary effect of ofatumumab treatment was depletion of B-cells; other effects | were secondary to this primary effect and included histological changes (atrophy of the spleen, lymph nodes, and Peyer’s patches) and increased risk of infection.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Comment: The sponsor reported (m1.9.1 Request for Waiver of Pediatric Studies) that it does not expect persons under 20 to be diagnosed with CLL; the median age at diagnosis is 72 years, and the disease is extremely rare in patients younger than 40 years of age. This information was considered in assessing the need for and the relevance of nonclinical reproductive and developmental toxicology data to support the treatment of patients with CLL.

An embryofetai development toxicity study (study # 2148-010) was performed for ofatumumab. No stand-alone nonclinical fertility assay was performed for ofatumumab. No nonclinical studies to assess early embryonic development or postnatal development have been performed for ofatumumab.

No evidence of treatment-related reproductive toxicity was observed in cynomolgus monkeys treated with ofatumumab in the 4-week study (study #23542), which treated 4 ‘sexually mature females (and 8 immature females, 12 immature males) or in the 7-month study (report # 25052), which treated 3 sexually mature females (and 11 immature females, 14 immature males). Fertility was not assessed; evaluation of reproductive toxicity for these assays is limited to clinical signs (e.g. evidence of menstruation), reproductive organ weight, gross reproductive organ pathology, and reproductive organ histopathology.
