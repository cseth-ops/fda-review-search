Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

Mirvetuximab soravtansine (Elahere) is an antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) consisting of a humanized immunoglobulin G1 antibody (M9346A) directed against folate receptor alpha (FRα), a succinimidyl-4-(pyridine-2-yl) disulfanyl-2-sulfo-butyrate (sulfo-SPDB) linker, and maytansine microtubule inhibitor, DM4. Each antibody is conjugated to an average of 3 to 4 molecules of DM4. Mirvetuximab soravtansine is administered by intravenous (IV) infusion once every 3 weeks at the recommended dose of 6 mg/kg (based on AIBW) for the treatment of adult patients with FRα positive, platinum-resistant epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer, who have received one to three prior systemic treatment regimens. The nonclinical development program for mirvetuximab soravtansine and the cytotoxic payload, DM4, consisted of studies in rats, monkeys, and rabbits to evaluate the primary and safety pharmacology, general toxicity, ocular toxicity, phototoxicity, and genotoxicity. During the nonclinical development, mirvetuximab soravtansine was referred to as IMGN853 or M9346A- sulfo-SPDB-DM4.

Folate receptor alpha (FRα, also known as FOLR1 or FR1) is an N-glycosylated protein anchored to the plasma membrane via glycosyl phosphatidylinositol with a molecular weight of 28 kD (Shen, et al., 1994). FRα is upregulated on the surface of many epithelial-derived tumors, compared to normal tissue. This receptor plays a critical role in cellular uptake of folate, a basic component of cell metabolism and DNA synthesis and repair.

In primary pharmacology studies, mirvetuximab soravtansine bound to FRα expressed on human or Rhesus monkey cells but not endogenous mouse or rat FRα. Binding to surface FRα leads to internalization of the receptor-ADC complex and lysosomal degradation of mirvetuximab soravtansine. This degradation releases the DM4 from the antibody and can result in cell cycle arrest and cell death. In vivo, mirvetuximab soravtansine delayed tumor growth and caused tumor regression in several ovarian cancer xenograft mouse models when mice were administered a single IV dose of mirvetuximab soravtansine containing antibody doses as low as 1.2 mg/kg and DM4 dose of 25 µg/kg. Based on the nonclinical pharmacology studies provided in the BLA submission, the established pharmacological class (EPC) of “folate receptor α-directed antibody and microtubule inhibitor conjugate” is appropriate based on its pharmacological activity, prior EPC for related approved products, and the approved EPC for microtubule inhibitors.

In vitro, DM4 was not stable in rat, human, and monkey plasma but was stable in human, rat, and monkey blood for up to 90 minutes. Metabolite S-methyl-DM4 (DM4-Me) was stable in human plasma either tested alone or in combination with DM4. In addition, S-methyl-DM4 was

stable in human, rat, and monkey blood after incubation for up to 90 minutes. The binding of DM4 and S-methyl-DM4 to human plasma protein in vitro was 99.38% and 99.27%, respectively.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

GLP compliant single and repeated dose toxicity studies of IMGN853 and a non-GLP single dose toxicity study of DM4 in cynomolgus monkeys were reviewed previously under IND 111915 and summarized below.

Study title/ number: 10-week intravenous (slow push) toxicity and toxicokinetic study in cynomolgus monkeys with a 5-week recovery period/ CVT28-001

IMGN853 at doses of 0, 2, 4, and 8 mg/kg/dose was administered intravenously (slow push) to cynomolgus monkeys once every 3 weeks for up to 10 weeks (a total of 4 doses) with a 5- week recovery period (n=6/sex/group). No early mortalities occurred. Drug-related clinical signs of toxicity included broken skin, dry skin, discolored skin, scaly skin, sores, scabs, and/or scars noted mostly in monkeys given ≥ 4 mg/kg/dose.

IMGN853-related ophthalmoscopic findings included keratopathy (faint perilimbal corneal pigment migration into cornea, especially superiorly) of both eyes in 3/6 males and 3/6 females at 8 mg/kg/dose in Week 9. Corneal pigmentation was likely secondary to other corneal changes. At the end of recovery period, similar corneal lesions developed in the superior perilimbal cornea in one previously unaffected female at 8 mg/kg/dose. In addition, the lesions (corneal pigmentation) were unchanged in a second female and more advanced in both eyes in a male at 8 mg/kg/dose. Target organs of toxicity associated with IMGN853 in monkeys were hematopoietic system (decreases ≥ 30% in absolute neutrophils, white blood cell, and reticulocytes when compared to control in both sexes at 8 mg/kg dose), injection sites (at 8 mg/kg/dose showed slight to moderate epithelial ulceration/erosion); skin/subcutis (areas other than injection sites in males at 8 mg/kg/dose showed slight to moderate epithelial ulceration/erosion, acanthosis, hyperkeratosis, and edema); cornea (at ≥ 4 mg/kg/dose showed minimal to marked degeneration/necrosis, pigmentation, and attenuation in the corneal epithelium); and skin of the eyelids (at ≥ 4

mg/kg/dose showed minimal to slight epithelial degeneration/necrosis and inflammation). Hematology and histopathology findings were trending towards recovery at end of 5-week recovery period. Histopathology findings of major target organs of toxicity for male and female rats are presented in Table 4 and Table 5, respectively.

However, there were histopathology findings observed during the recovery period only, including minimal axon degeneration in the sciatic nerve in 1/2 females at 8 mg/kg/dose and minimal to slight fibrosis observed in testis in males at ≥ 2 mg/kg/dose IMGN853 in 1/2 males in each dose group.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

No reproductive or developmental animal toxicity studies were conducted with mirvetuximab soravtansine. The cytotoxic component of ELAHERE, DM4, disrupts microtubule function, is genotoxic, and can be toxic to actively dividing cells, suggesting it has the potential to cause embryotoxicity and teratogenicity.

Study 0417 (SORAYA)

Trial Design

Study 0417 was a phase 3, single arm trial evaluating the safety and efficacy of mirvetuximab soravtansine in patients with platinum-resistant high-grade serous epithelial ovarian cancer, primary peritoneal cancer, or fallopian tube cancer with tumors that expressed a high level of FRα. Patients were appropriate for single-agent therapy as the next line of therapy in the opinion of the investigator.

Clinical safety

Data:

The safety profile summarized in this application consists of data from 3 clinical trials of mirvetuximab soravtansine including 464 patients. All patients received the recommended starting dose of 6mg/kg based on AIBW of mirvetuximab soravtansine. The number of patients in each of the safety analysis groups is described below.

The Study 0417 group includes 106 patients in the safety population, and this was the pivotal trial submitted for this application. All patients had high-grade serous ovarian cancer. All patients had received 1-3 prior lines of systemic treatment and 100% had received bevacizumab. Tumor samples were assessed to have high FRα expression in all patients.

The pooled safety set included all epithelial ovarian cancer (EOC) patients who received mirvetuximab soravtansine 6mg/kg AIBW Q3W on one of 3 studies, including Studies 0401, 0403, 0417. This included EOC patients (expansion cohorts 1, 3, and 5) in Study 0401 (N=113), the Phase 3 portion of Study 0403 (N=245), and all patients in Study 0417 (N=106). Of note, 142 of the total n=464 patients had tumors with FRα high expression according to the criteria used in Study 0417.

The primary evidence of safety is derived from the Study 0417 data, as the subjects represent a more homogeneous patient population than the pooled data. Patients enrolled in Study 0417 had tumors with high FRα expression and received the same protocol-specified mitigation measures and dose modification. Key features of Study 0417 compared to the pooled analysis data sets from Study 0401, 0403 include: • Study 0417 had implementation of the most up to date recommended dose modifications and mitigation strategies for AEs. In particular, this included changes in the ocular toxicity mitigation strategies from prophylactic corticosteroid eye drops for 10 days (Study 0401) to 8 days per cycle (Studies 0403, 0417). • Study 0417 had a more homogeneous population based upon FRα High-expression • The eligibility criteria for Study 0417 included limitations on the number of allowable prior therapies (1 to 3 prior systemic lines of anticancer therapy, including mandatory receipt of prior bevacizumab).

Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment

Ovarian cancer, including fallopian tube and primary peritoneal cancer, is the fifth leading cause of cancer-related death for women in the United States. There will be an estimated 19,880 new cases of ovarian cancer and 12,810 deaths from ovarian cancer in the U.S. in 2022. Unfortunately, there is no recommended screening for ovarian cancer for the general population, and ovarian cancer is often diagnosed at an advanced stage. Five-year overall survival (OS) is only 49%.

Initial treatment for patients with advanced ovarian cancer is typically surgery and (neo)adjuvant chemotherapy with a platinum-based doublet ± bevacizumab. Patients who experience a complete response (CR) or partial response (PR) may be candidates for maintenance treatment with bevacizumab and/or a PARP inhibitor. Although most advanced ovarian cancer is initially platinum sensitive, virtually all patients ultimately develop platinum-resistant disease which is associated with a limited overall survival and few treatment options.

Patients with platinum-resistant ovarian cancer who have received only 1-2 prior lines of systemic treatment and who have not previously received bevacizumab may be candidates to receive bevacizumab in combination with paclitaxel, topotecan, or pegylated liposomal doxorubicin (PLD). If patients cannot receive this regimen, or after progression of disease on this regimen, treatment options are limited to single agent chemotherapy with paclitaxel, topotecan, PLD, gemcitabine, or other options. Historical data suggest that single agent chemotherapies have ORRs ranging from 6-27%. However, these data were collected prior to establishment of a platinum-based doublet ± bevacizumab followed by maintenance treatment with bevacizumab and/or a PARP inhibitor as standard of care for advanced ovarian cancer. More contemporary data suggest available chemotherapies have ORRs of 12-13% and median DORs of 4-7.4 months. In addition, a unique aspect of mirvetuximab soravtansine-gynx is that it targets FRα, and its activity is enhanced in tumors with higher levels of FRα expression. To this end, there are currently no other available therapies for ovarian cancer which target tumors overexpressing FRα.

The efficacy and safety assessment for mirvetuximab soravtansine-gynx was primarily based on results from SORAYA, a single-arm international trial which enrolled 106 patients with FRα-positive, platinum-resistant epithelial ovarian, fallopian tube, or primary peritoneal cancer. Patients had received 1-3 prior lines of therapy and all patients had received

prior bevacizumab. Patients were selected for treatment if their tumors were positive for FRα expression according to the Ventana FOLR1 (FOLR1-2.1) CDx assay. Patients received mirvetuximab soravtansine-gynx 6 mg/kg intravenously every 3 weeks until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. Tumor response assessments occurred every 6 weeks for the first 36 weeks and every 12 weeks thereafter. The primary endpoint was confirmed ORR assessed by investigator according to RECIST v1.1. DOR was a secondary endpoint.
