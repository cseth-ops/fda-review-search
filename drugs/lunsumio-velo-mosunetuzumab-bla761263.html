Accelerated approval confirmatory · approved 2022 · Final Protocol Submission 09/2021 · Trial Completion 06/2025 · Final Report Submission 12/2025

Conduct a randomized clinical trial in patients with relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma, with patients randomized to receive mosunetuzumab in combination with lenalidomide or rituximab in combination with lenalidomide. The primary endpoint should be progression-free survival, with secondary endpoints that include response rate and overall survival. The trial should enroll a sufficiently representative study population to reflect the racial and ethnic diversity of the U.S. patient population with follicular lymphoma and allow for interpretation of the results in these patient populations.

FDA's assessment
For B11 FL RP2D cohort, the compliance rates were >85% until Cycle 8 Day 1 for all three questionnaires and remained ≥75% afterwards. The FDA acknowledges the descriptive improvements for physical function, fatigue, and lymphoma symptoms. However, the FDA disagrees with the statement that the changes were clinically meaningful. For patient-reported disease improvement, a comparator arm along with a prespecified PRO endpoint and analysis plan are necessary for adequate interpretation. Additionally, the single-arm, open-label trial design limits the PRO data interpretation since a patients’ knowledge of the treatment regimen may lead to systematic overestimation or underestimation of the treatment effect. OCP Appendices (Technical documents supporting OCP recommendations)

Summary of Bioanalytical Method

The FDA’s Assessment:

Mosunetuzumab concentrations in human serum were determined using a validated enzyme- linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA).

In the assay, a monoclonal anti-idiotypic antibody against the anti-CD20 arm of mosunetuzumab was used for capture. A biotin-conjugated monoclonal anti-idiotypic antibody against the anti-CD3 arm of mosunetuzumab was applied for detection. Streptavidin (SA)- horseradish peroxidase (HRP) and a peroxidase substrate (tetramethyl benzidine) were added sequentially for color development. The lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) of the assay is 10 ng/mL. This assay was validated at two sites for analyzing samples from Study GO29781. Results for the methods validation, cross-validation and method performance during study sample analysis are provided in Table 52 and Table 53 below.

The FDA’s Assessment:

B-cell Depletion

To assess the magnitude and kinetics of B-cell depletion, a quantitative flow cytometry assay to detect CD19+ B cells was added to the Study Protocol (version 9 [dated 30 January2019]). B-cell depletion was measured in 152 patients from cohort B11. A majority (110/152 patients, 72%) of patients enrolled had B-cell counts below 70 cells/ul (the lower limit of the normal range for peripheral B cell counts) at baseline, and approximately half of patients (71/152, 47%) enrolled had B-cell counts below the level of quantitation (LLOQ) for the assay (5 cells/ul) at baseline.

For patients with paired baseline and on-study samples, i.e., those having measurable B-cells (>=5 cells/ul) prior to mosunetuzumab dosing and at least one additional measurement post- treatment, B-cells were found to be reduced to undetectable levels in most patients within Cohort B11 as early as Cycle 2 [58/63 (92%)], and depletion was sustained at later cycles including at Cycle 4 (58/60, 97%), and Cycle 8 (45/48, 94%)] (Table 58). Furthermore, 100% of these patients had B-cell depletion to <70 cells/ul by cycle 4.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

The Applicant’s Position:

Male and female reproductive assessments were conducted as a part of the 26-week chronic toxicity study in sexually mature cynomolgus monkeys (Study 16-1815). Evaluation included organ weights, and macroscopic and microscopic evaluation of male and female reproductive organs (including qualitative spermatogenesis progression for males and general phases of menstrual cycles for females). There were no mosunetuzumab-related effects identified in the male or female reproductive assessment.

A weight of evidence (WOE)-based risk assessment for potential developmental effects was conducted. Based on low placental transfer of antibodies during the first trimester, the mechanism of action and available data of mosunetuzumab, and the data from other approved anti-CD20 antibodies developed by the Applicant (e.g., rituximab [Rituxan USPI], obinutuzumab [Gazyva USPI], ocrelizumab [Ocrevus USPI]), the risk for teratogenicity is low. Studies with mosunetuzumab in non-pregnant animals have demonstrated that prolonged B-cell depletion in the dam can lead to increased risk of opportunistic infection, which may result in fetal loss, as shown with ocrelizumab [Ocrevus USPI]). Transient CRS associated with mosunetuzumab administration may also be harmful to pregnancy.

A briefing book, including the WOE-based risk assessment and a request to waive a non-human primate embryo-fetal and prenatal/postnatal development study, was submitted to the FDA (dated 3 April 2018; Reference ID: 4277993). In a written communication from the FDA dated 14 June 2018, the Agency agreed to the waiver request. During the End-of-Phase-I Type B meeting on 18 March, 2020, the Agency recommended to remove the list of products for which the Sponsor does not have the right-to-reference, and considered other components used in the WOE-based risk assessment reasonable for submission. The updated risk assessment document is included in Module 4 (Assessment of Developmental Effects).

The FDA’s Assessment:

To address the potential effects of mosunetuzumab on embryo-fetal development, the Applicant submitted an assessment for reproductive and developmental risk using a weight-of- evidence (WOE) approach and with the recommendation in the ICH S6(R1) guideline. Clinical efficacy

GO29781

Trial Design

The Applicant’s Description: The clinical efficacy and safety results supporting this application are from a single ongoing multicenter, open-label, Phase I/II dose-escalation and dose-expansion study GO29781 of mosunetuzumab administered as a single agent (and in combination with atezolizumab) in patients with R/R hematologic malignancies expected to express CD20, including B-cell NHL and CLL.

The initial patients enrolled were treated with escalating doses of single-agent mosunetuzumab administered IV on Day 1 of each 3-weekly (q3w) cycle (Group A fixed dosing schedule). Subsequent protocol amendments were implemented to expand the study to evaluate a different IV dosing schedule (Group B Cycle 1 step-up dosing), include a dose expansion phase to evaluate mosunetuzumab at a recommended expansion dose in specific B-cell NHL histologies, and introduce efficacy as a co-primary objective. Further dose escalation and expansion cohorts were also introduced to evaluate mosunetuzumab IV in combination with atezolizumab (Group E), and mosunetuzumab administered as a single agent by SC injection (Groups D and F). The study was re-classified from Phase I/Ib to Phase I/II to reflect the overall size of the study, and the study objective to investigate the safety, efficacy, and PK/PD of the recommended Phase II dose (RP2D) in expansion cohorts with defined NHL histologies (protocol v12, dated 23 February 2021).

The GO29781 study enrolled adult patients with a history of a hematologic malignancy that is expected to express the CD20 antigen, including FL, MZL, DLBCL, PMBCL, transformed indolent NHL, MCL, Richter's transformation, and CLL/SLL. Confirmation of CD20 expression was not required during eligibility screening prior to enrollment and was evaluated retrospectively based on data from a central lab. Patients included in the pivotal R/R FL expansion cohort had to have histologically-documented Grade 1-3a FL, measurable disease, must have relapsed after or failed to respond to at least two prior lines of systemic therapy and must have received prior treatment of an anti-CD20 directed therapy and an alkylating agent.

Results to support this application are from Groups A and B only, which evaluated mosunetuzumab as a single agent administered by IV infusion.

After completion of the first eight dose escalation cohorts, further enrolment into Group A was stopped beyond 2.8 mg (the highest assessed dose in Group A) to prioritize assessment of other dosing schedules and routes of mosunetuzumab administration. The Group B Cycle 1 step-up dosing schedule evaluated single-agent mosunetuzumab administered IV as incremental doses on C1D1, C1D8, and C1D15 dose levels followed by q3w dosing on Day 1 of subsequent cycles. The dose escalation evaluated 11 dose levels (ranging from 0.4/1/2.8 mg to 1/2/60 mg [B1-B11]), according to a standard 3+3 design. The Group B dose-expansion stage was designed to obtain additional safety, tolerability, PK, and clinical efficacy data with single agent mosunetuzumab administered IV by Cycle 1 step-up dosing in one or more dose expansion cohorts at dose levels at or below the MTD (which was not reached). …

Clinical safety

Data: The safety database included 447 patients in the safety-evaluable population of Study GO29781 defined as all patients in Group A and Group B who received at least one dose of mosunetuzumab (as a single agent by IV administration). The primary safety analysis set comprised of 218 patients in the Group B expansion cohorts treated at the RP2D/intended registration dose and schedule of 1/2/60/30 mg (referred to as B11 RP2D), and includes 90 patients with R/R FL ≥2 prior therapies (referred to as B11 FL RP2D) and other histology- specific cohorts (88 DLBCL/trFL, 25 MCL, and 14 Richter’s transformation; one patient with melanoma who was enrolled in error in the R/R DLBCL/trFL RP2D expansion cohort and included in safety analyses because they received one dose of mosunetuzumab treatment). In addition to the 218 patients treated with the registration dosing regimen, an additional 196 patients treated at other doses in Group B (Cycle 1 step-up) dose escalation and interim expansion cohorts (414 patients in total of mixed NHL histologies; referred as Group B) were included in the safety database to provide a comprehensive overview of the safety profile of single agent mosunetuzumab IV using the Cycle 1 step-up dosing schedule. Safety data from 33 patients with R/R NHL initially treated with escalating doses of mosunetuzumab IV monotherapy administered on a q3w fixed dosing schedule (referred as Group A) were also analyzed to complete the safety evaluation of mosunetuzumab IV monotherapy and to support exposure-response (E-R) modeling analyses and justification of RP2D determination. As the

dosing schedule was different, and patients in Group A received doses of mosunetuzumab per cycle of ≤2.8 mg, the starting dose in the Group B dose-escalation (C1D15 dose in the B1 cohort), Group A was analyzed separately from Group B (see Table 23).

The safety review is of data from Study GO29781 collected up to a clinical cutoff of 27 August 2021, and is an update to the primary analysis based on an earlier CCOD of 15 March 2021.

The FDA’s Assessment: The FDA’s safety review was primarily focused on the 218 patients treated at the RP2D (1/2/60/30 mg) in Study GO29781, which includes the primary follicular lymphoma population of 90 patients, with a data cutoff of August 27, 2021. The safety data from the additional 196 patients with NHL (Group B) treated at different doses or schedules from the RP2D were considered supportive.
