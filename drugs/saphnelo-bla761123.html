Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

The Applicant has conducted a comprehensive program of pharmacology, pharmacokinetic (PK), and toxicology studies with anifrolumab to support clinical development and the BLA submission. All studies submitted to support approval have been reviewed previously under IND 101849. The key findings from the nonclinical studies are cited and summarized in this review to support the approval of BLA 761123. The Applicant has also submitted extractables and leachables studies and safety assessments, which have been reviewed under this BLA (see Section 16.3). The safety assessment of impurities from the drug substance and drug product is reviewed in Section 16.3.

Pharmacology studies demonstrated that anifrolumab bound to IFNAR1 and inhibited the expression of Type-I IFN responsive genes in peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and the expression of differentiation markers and cytokines/chemokines from monocytes. An anifrolumab mouse surrogate was effective in a murine model of scleroderma and an accelerated NZB/W F1 murine model of lupus.

Anifrolumab had no or reduced binding to Fc Gamma Receptors I (Fc RI), Fc RIIA, Fc RIIB, or Fc RIIIA in vitro. No complement dependent cytotoxicity (CDC) or antibody dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) were noted in vitro. In vitro anifrolumab-positive human and cynomolgus monkey tissue binding was consistent with published reports of broad expression of IFNAR in the corresponding tissues.

The effect of anifrolumab on the cardiovascular (CV) system was evaluated in a GLP-compliant 39-week general IV toxicity study in monkeys (Study# 7140-129). In this study, cynomolgus monkeys were given 40 IV infusion (30 minutes) doses of anifrolumab at 5 and 50 mg/kg/week once a week (QW) or 40 subcutaneous (SC) injection doses of anifrolumab at 15 and 60 mg/kg/week QW. Electrocardiography (ECG) parameters and blood pressure (BP) parameters were measured once during the predose phase, on Days 3 and 276 of the dosing phase, and on Day 83 of the recovery phase for these animals after they were anesthetized with ketamine. No drug-related adverse CV findings were noted. In another 4-week IV toxicity study in cynomolgus monkeys with lower systemic exposures (see Section 5.4), ECG parameters and BP were (b) (4) measured within 1 hr. post dosing on Day 22 (weekly dose, Study# 263.01). No drug- related adverse CV findings were noted.

GLP-compliant toxicology studies up to 39 weeks indicated that anifrolumab was well-tolerated in cynomolgus monkeys. No drug-related adverse clinical sign changes were noted. No drug- related CV toxicity was noted. Besides anti-drug antibody (ADA)-induced minimal arteritis in multiple organs in a few male animals given 40 doses of anifrolumab at 50 mg/kg/week (IV) or 60 mg/kg/week (SC), no additional drug-related adverse effect was noted. Trial Designs

Study 1013

Study CD-IA-MEDI-546-1013 (1013) was a 52-week, multi-center, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel group study. Patients were randomized in a 1:1:1 fashion to MEDI- 546 (anifrolumab) 1000mg, MEDI-546 300mg, or placebo (Figure 8). Anifrolumab or placebo was administered as an IV infusion via an infusion pump for approximately 60 minutes every 4 weeks for a total of 13 doses (first dose at Day 1 (week 0) and last dose at Day 337 (week 48)). All patients were to be followed for 85 days after Day 337 through Day 422 (week 60). Randomization was stratified by (SLEDAI-2K) score at screening (< 10 points vs 10 points), Day 1 OCS dose (< 10 mg/day vs 10 mg/day of prednisone or equivalent), and the results of a diagnostic test for type I IFN signature in whole blood at screening using a 4-gene type I IFN test (positive vs negative).

The study design included oral corticosteroid (OCS) tapering targets. Patients who experienced an increase in SLE activity were permitted one burst of OCS or intramuscular corticosteroids between Day 1 and 71, however, were required to taper to Day 1 levels or less within 14 days and to complete by Day 71, otherwise they would be declared non-responders for the primary and disease activity related secondary endpoints. Further, patients who required an increase of OCS above Day 1 dose levels after Day 71 were to be declared non-responders for the primary endpoint and disease activity related secondary endpoints at Day 169 (week 24). In addition, patients who were unable to reduce OCS to < 10 mg/day and Day 1 dose of prednisone or equivalent by Day 85 and unable to maintain OCS doses < 10 mg/day and Day 1 dose of prednisone or equivalent until Day 169 were to be declared non-responders for the primary endpoint at Day 169 (week 24).

Patients were also permitted to receive one additional burst of OCS or intramuscular corticosteroids between Days 169 and 281 and were not to be declared a non-responder for disease activity secondary endpoints at Day 365 (week 52) if they tapered to Day 169 levels or less within 14 days and completed by Day 281. Patients who required an increase of OCS above the minimum of Day 1 dose or Day 169 dose level after Day 281 were to be declared non- responders for disease activity secondary endpoints at Day 365 (week 52). Patients who were unable to reduce OCS to < 10 mg/day and Day 1 dose of prednisone or equivalent by Day 281 and unable to maintain OCS doses < 10 mg/day and Day 1 dose until Day 365 were to be declared non-responders for certain disease activity secondary endpoints at Day 365 (week 52).

Investigators should have tapered OCS based on disease activity in all subjects throughout the study, as permitted between Days 1 and 113 and between Days 169 and 309. Patients who permanently discontinued from receiving investigational product were to be followed for safety. Further, two follow-up visits were scheduled for Day 396 (week 56) and Day 422 (week 60).

Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment To support the proposed indication, the Applicant provided the results from three clinical studies—phase 2 study CD-IA-MEDI-546-1013 (1013) and phase 3 studies D3461C00004 (04) and D3461C00005 (05), that evaluated anifrolumab IV 300 mg as well as two additional doses (150 mg and 1000 mg) in a total of 1,123 adults with active systemic lupus erythematosus (SLE) on stable immunosuppressive background medications. These were international, multicenter, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 52-week studies that evaluated the efficacy, safety, and immunogenicity of the proposed dose of 300 mg IV every 4 weeks in 925 adult subjects with active SLE receiving standard therapy.

Determination of substantial evidence of effectiveness in adult patients was based on efficacy results from one adequate and well-controlled study—phase 3 study (04). This was an appropriately designed, well-conducted study that adequately controlled for multiplicity, to characterize the efficacy and safety of anifrolumab in an adult population with active SLE. In study 04, anifrolumab 300 mg achieved a statistically significant and clinically meaningful higher response rate compared to placebo in the primary endpoint of BICLA at week 52, a composite of multiple measures of SLE improvement—anifrolumab 48% compared to placebo 31%, [risk difference (95% CI): 16.3 (6.3, 26.3)]). Additional supportive evidence for efficacy is provided in this study by the SRI-4 composite 52-week endpoint, where a higher proportion of anifrolumab 300mg IV subjects (56%) achieved response compared to the placebo group (37%)[risk difference (95% CI): 18.2 (8.1, 28.3)].

Confirmatory evidence of effectiveness is provided by study 1013, a phase 2 study designed to provide proof of concept and dose-ranging information, and a second phase 3 study (study 05). While an appropriate method for controlling multiplicity was not pre-specified in study 1013, a higher proportion of anifrolumab 300 mg IV subjects (53.5%) achieved the 52-week BICLA secondary endpoint as compared to the placebo group (25.7%)[risk difference (95% CI): 28.4 (15.3, 41.5)]. Additional supportive evidence for efficacy is provided by the SRI-4 composite 52-week endpoint in study 1013, for which a higher proportion of anifrolumab 300 mg IV subjects (62.6%) achieved response as compared to the placebo group (40.2%)[risk difference (95% CI):24.0 (10.9, 37.2)]. While a second phase 3 study (study 05) did not meet its primary objective and failed on the primary endpoint SRI-4, the results on BICLA and other secondary endpoints are largely consistent with effects observed in study 04 and study 1013 and are viewed as additional confirmatory evidence. In study 05, a higher proportion of anifrolumab 300 mg IV subjects achieved the 52-week BICLA endpoint over placebo. This effect was consistently observed in a post-hoc analysis (using revised restricted medication rules that are more consistent with clinical practice)—a higher proportion of anifrolumab 300 mg IV subjects (47%) achieved the 52-week BICLA endpoint as compared to the placebo group (30%)[risk difference (95% CI): 17.0 (7.2, 26.8)].
