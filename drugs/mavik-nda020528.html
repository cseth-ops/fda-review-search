Carcinogenicity

A brief coversheet for the carcinogenesis study review is shown in Appendix 1A.

The carcinogenicity studies were carried out in France using animals supplied by Charles River Laboratories. The mouse studies were conducted over a period of 78 weeks using the Crl; CD-1 C-CRBR strain, and the rat studies were done using the Sprague Dawley Cri GD(SD)Br strain over a period of 104 weeks. The mean weights of the mice of both sexes were approximately 27 grams, and of the rats approximately 100 grams. The mode of drug and control vehicle administration was by oral gavage, using a 0.5% methylcellulose suspension iispensed via stainless steel dosing needles and plastic syringes. Mice receivec' 10 ml/kg/d and rats 2.5 ml/kg/d of drug suspensions or control vehicle.

Individually housed mice were dosed for 78 weeks according to the schedule shown in Table 1. Rats were housed two animals per cage during the104 weeks of dosing according to the dosing schedule shown in Table 2. The doses were chosen by the conventional method of a sub-chronic range-finding study to determine if a maximum tolerated dose could be defined. Failing that, other surrogate indicators of the selection of rational doses were used. Sponsor described how a 60-day dose-ranging study (doses of 2, 10, and 50 mg/kg/kg/day) was used to choose the final mouse doses of 1, 5, and 25 mg/kg/d, and how a six-month chronic rat study was used to define the final rat doses of 0.25, 2, and 8 mg/kg/day. During the 17th week of the experiment, the original high dose of 16 mg/kg/day was decreased to 8 mg/kg/day because of the histopathological evidence of increased incidence of renal glomerulonephrosis in the high dose animals. Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Fertility and general reproductive performance in rats were not adversely attectsd by doses of trandolapril as great as 100 mg/kg/day, or 1250 times the.maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) on a body weight basis, or 257 times the MRHD on the basis of body surface area. There is some evidence of modified renal growth patterns which result in apparently temporary renal structural abnormalities (pelvic cavitation and dilated ureters) in the 100 mg/kg/day untreated F 1 offspring; these abnormalities disappear as growth rates equilibrate.

In a study of trandolapril's possible effects on organogenesis, the overall incidence of minor visceral defects was significantly increased only in rats receiving the high dose of 1000 mg trandolapriVkg/day which, on the basis of body weight, is 12500 times the MRHD or 2570 times the MRHD on the basis of body surface area. Sponsor reasonably considers that the deferts are of doubtful significance, therefore, there is no evidence of teratogenic potential under the conditions of this experiment in riilts. l

The teratogenic potential of trandolapril was also studied in rabbits orally dosed at 0.2 to 0.8 mg/kg/day, on the basis of body weight, and 2.7 and 10.8 mg/M2, respectively, on the basis of body surface area (1.0 to 4.0 times the MRHD). Maternal toxicity was evidenced by 12/21 deaths in the high dose group compared with 5/21 deaths in the control group as well as clear signs of toxicity during and after treatment. Pre-and post- implantation losses and the incidences of major malformations were balanced by equivalent frequencies of these events in the control groups. Additionaly, it is common experience that rabbits are very sensitive to agents of this type and are thereby poor models for these assays.

Sponsor used rabbits in another test to determine the potential of trandolapril (0.1 to 0.8 mg/kg/day) for maternal toxicity and treatment-related malformations during the period of organogenesis. There were clear signs of maternal toxicity during and after treatment with ' 4 mg/kg/day trandolapril, but there were no treatment-related malformations at and above 0.4 mg/kg/day by body weight or the equivalent of 5.4 mg/M2/day when calculated as surface area.
