Central nervous system (CNS) Irvin tests: Cariprazine was investigated in two Irwin tests in Sprague-Dawley (SD) and Wistar rats after oral administration. Significant gross behavioral and physiological changes, characteristic of the CNS depression, including abnormal gait and decreases in motor activity, startle response, body tone, grip strength and several reflexes were observed during the 24 h post-dose period in SD rats administered single dose of cariprazine at 0.5, 2.5, and 12.5 mg/kg. A NOAEL for the effects was not established under the conditions of this study. However, cariprazine did not induce any CNS effects over the dose range 0.025 – 0.4 mg/kg in Wistar rats. The only dose showing clear, but reversible, effects was 0.8 mg/kg (the highest dose tested), which induced weak CNS depressant effects. Therefore, the NOAEL for the effects on the CNS was 0.4 mg/kg in rats in this study (HED: 3.9 mg/day based on a body surface area, assuming 60 kg human) (see pharmacology/toxicology review dated August 15, 2005).
