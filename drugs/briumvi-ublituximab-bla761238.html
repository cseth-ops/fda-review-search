General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The initial toxicology (single-dose and 4-week) studies in cynomolgus monkey were reviewed by Dr. Stacey Ricci. In the single-dose study, cynomolgus monkeys received IV injections of ublituximab at dose levels of 0, 0.3, 10, or 100 mg/kg followed by a 14-day observation period. Near total depletion of B cell populations in plasma was observed at all dose levels by 24 hours post dosing. Near total B cell depletion was also observed at the MD and HD in cell suspensions from spleen, lymph node, and bone marrow. At the HD, an elevation of fibrinogen (graded moderate) was observed in both sexes. The sponsor did not consider this finding related to the test article, but because it occurred in only the HD group, a relationship cannot be ruled out. No other adverse findings were observed. Anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) were detected in all LD animals.

In the 4-week study, cynomolgus monkeys received ublituximab at weekly IV doses of 0, 10, or 50 mg/kg. The results showed dose-dependent depletion of circulating B lymphocytes and lymphoid depletion in the spleen, thymus, and lymph nodes. Significant body weight loss in males and stagnation of weight in females was observed at the HD. Small (59%) increases in urea and bilirubin (59%) were observed in HD males, fibrinogen levels were increased (graded moderate) in HD males (72%) and females (41%), and a small increase in aPTT was observed in HD males. One unscheduled death of a HD female was reported after reduced food consumption and loss of weight with marked dehydration, hypoactivity, and prostration were observed. At necropsy, findings were dark color of the adrenals, reduced thymus size, and red/purple area on the tongue. Microscopic findings were similar to those observed in other ublituximab-treated animals at the terminal sacrifice. This animal also showed tongue ulcer, leucocytosis of the kidneys, and salivary gland acinar atrophy. No clear cause of death was established but because the animal was in the HD group, the death was considered related to the test article. In the surviving animals at the terminal sacrifice, the following findings were reported:

• Spleen: Increased numbers of neutrophils and/or extramedullary hematopoiesis in red pulp at the HD, fibrinous thrombi at the HD, decreased follicles and germinal centers at the MD and HD. • Lungs: Leucocytosis and fibrinous thrombi at the HD • Bone marrow: Increased myeloid/erythroid ratio. • Liver: single cell necrosis at the MD and HD, green pigment at the MD and HD, fibrinous thrombi at the HD, and extramedullary erythropoiesis at the HD • Adrenals: Extramedullary hematopoiesis and decreased vacuolization of the zona fasciculata at the HD • Pancreas: Acinar atrophy at the HD. • Stomach: One HD animal showed acute erosions of the fundus. • Anti-drug antibody (ADA) development was noted in the LD, MD, and HD groups. ADA levels increased with duration of dosing and, by SD22, significant reductions in AUC were observed in the LD and MD animals. Exposure was maintained through the study for the HD group.

Clinical efficacy

6.1. Study TG1101-RMS301

6.1.1. Study Design

Overview and Objective

Study TG1101-RMS301 (“ULTIMATE I”) was one of two identical randomized controlled trials (RCTs) whose objective was to assess the efficacy and safety of ublituximab-xiiy compared to bioequivalent teriflunomide as treatment for relapsing forms of MS. Studies TG1101-RMS301 and TG1101-RMS302 were conducted under a Special Protocol Agreement.

The primary objective of the study was to determine the Annualized Relapse Rate (ARR) in subjects with RMS after 96 weeks (approximately 2 years with a year equal to 365.25 days) treatment with IV infusion of ublituximab-xiiy/oral placebo compared to 14 mg oral teriflunomide/IV placebo.

Secondary objectives were to examine the effects of ublituximab-xiiy/oral placebo as compared to teriflunomide/IV placebo on the following: 1. MRI parameters, 2. Confirmed Disability Progression, 3. No Evidence of Disease Activity (NEDA), 4. Symbol Digit Modality Test (SDMT), and 5. Safety of ublituximab-xiiy/oral placebo, as determined by adverse events (AEs) and serious adverse event (SAEs), including MS worsening.

Trial Design

Study TG1101-RMS301 was a 120-week, Phase 3, randomized, multicenter, double-blind, double-dummy, active controlled study, designed to evaluate the efficacy and safety of ublituximab-xiiy compared to bioequivalent teriflunomide in patients with relapsing forms of MS per 2010 McDonald Criteria. Patients were required to have at least 2 relapses in the prior 2 years, or 1 relapse in the prior year and/or at least 1 gadolinium-enhancing lesion. Patients were randomized 1:1 to receive treatment with either ublituximab-xiiy or teriflunomide (with oral or IV placebo, respectively), for 96 weeks (last ublituximab-xiiy infusion at Week 72). Double-blind period follow-up continued until 120 weeks. Upon completion of the double-blind treatment period, patients could consent to participation in the 172-week open label extension study, TG1101-RMS303.

The study schematic for Study TG1101-RMS301 is presented in Figure 1. The study consisted of a Screening Period of up to 4 weeks, a 96-week Double-blind Treatment Period, and a Follow- Up Period of 20 weeks.

Benefit-risk

Briumvi (ublituximab-xiiy) is a glycoengineered murine/human chimeric anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody that targets an epitope of the B- lymphocyte antigen CD20 expressed on the cell membranes of lymphocytes. After binding to CD20, ublituximab-xiiy induces B-cell destruction primarily by antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity. Ublituximab-xiiy is proposed as a treatment for relapsing forms of multiple sclerosis (MS). There are at present two anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody therapies approved to treat relapsing forms of MS. Ocrevus (ocrelizumab) is a humanized murine/human chimeric anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody that is approved for the treatment of the relapsing forms of MS and is approved additionally for the treatment of the primary progressive form of MS. Kesimpta (ofatumumab) is a fully-human anti-CD20 monoclonal antibody approved for the treatment of relapsing forms of MS. Rituxan (rituximab) is a chimeric murine/human anti- CD20 monoclonal antibody which is approved to treat several malignancies and rheumatologic autoimmune diseases; rituximab is sometimes used to treat MS but has not been demonstrated to be safe and effective for the treatment of MS.

Relapsing forms of MS, which include clinically isolated syndrome, relapsing-remitting disease, and active secondary progressive disease (secondary progressive MS with relapses), are phenotypes of the chronic and disabling central nervous system disease of apparent autoimmune etiology termed “multiple sclerosis.” In the relapsing forms of MS, subjects experience episodes of focal neurological deficits and disseminated lesions of demyelination within the central nervous system. Symptoms of relapsing forms of MS commonly include recurrent paroxysms of diminished sensory or motor function that can be temporarily or permanently disabling. Many, but not all, subjects with relapsing forms of MS experience some degree of persistent disability that may gradually worsen over years. Over twenty unique therapies have been approved for the treatment of relapsing forms of MS, and all of these approved therapies share a basic common feature of modifying the immune response. The mechanism of action of anti-CD20 therapies in the treatment of relapsing forms of MS is unknown but presumably is linked to depletion of B-cells and immunosuppression.

The applicant presents the results from two adequate and well-controlled Phase 3 clinical trials, Studies TG1101-RMS301 and TG1101-RMS302 (Studies RMS301 and RMS302 hereafter) as the basis of an application to support a claim of safety and efficacy for ublituximab-xiiy in the treatment of relapsing forms of MS. Studies RMS301 and RMS302 were both randomized, double-blind, double-dummy, active-controlled trials that compared intravenous ublituximab to teriflunomide.
