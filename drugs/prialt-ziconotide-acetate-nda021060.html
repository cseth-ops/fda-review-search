Mechanism of Action

Ziconotide binds to N-type calcium channels located on the primary nociceptive (A-8 and C) afferent nerves in the superficial layers (Rexed laminae J and II) of the dorsal horn in the spinal cord. Interaction with opioids

Ziconotide does not bind to opioid receptors and its pharmacological effects are not blocked by opioid antagonists. In animal models ziconotide IT potentiated opioid-induced reduction in gastro-intestinal (GI) motility, but did not potentiate morphine-induced respiratory depression. Inrats receiving IT ziconotide, additive analgesic effects were observed with concurrent administration of “mm

au morphine, baclofen, or clonidine. Concurrent administration of IT ziconotide and morphine did not prevent the development of morphine tolerance in rats.

PHARMACOKINETIC AND BIOLOGICAL DISPOSITION Metabolism

Ziconotide is cleaved by endopeptidases and exopeptidases at multiple sites on the peptide. Following passage from the CSF into the systemic circulation during continuous IT administration, ziconotide is expected to be #™™= — susceptible to proteolytic cleavage by various ubiquitous peptidases/proteases present in most organs (e.g., kidney, liver, lung muscle, etc.), and thus readily degraded to peptide fragments and their individual constituent free amino acids. Human and animal CSF and biood exhibit minimal hydrolytic activity toward ziconotide in vitro. The biological activity of the various expected proteolytic degradation products has not- been assessed.

Neurological effects: No new studies were submitted.
