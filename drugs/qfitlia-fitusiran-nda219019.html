General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Repeat-dose toxicology studies were conducted in healthy rats for up to 26 weeks, healthy monkeys for up to 39 weeks, and in hemophilia mice for up to 26 weeks. Fitusiran is pharmacologically active in mice, rats, monkeys, and dogs because the antithrombin mRNA sequences are highly conserved across species. Animals were dosed once weekly by the SC route. Key findings from these studies are summarized below.

13.2.5.1.1. A 26-Week Subcutaneous Injection Toxicity, Toxicokinetic, and Fertility and Early Embryonic Development (to Implantation) Study in the Rat Followed By a 13-Week Recovery Period (AT3- NCD14-009)

Key Study Findings

Toxicity • Once-weekly SC administration of fitusiran for 26 weeks was well tolerated by female rats up to 1 mg/kg/week and by male rats up to 0.5 mg/kg/week. • Unscheduled deaths occurred in 5/5 males at 1 mg/kg/week. The cause of deaths was excessive clotting (leading to platelet reductions and, paradoxically, systemic hemorrhage), necrosis, and/or thrombosis in various organs and tissues, consistent with the exaggerated pharmacology of fitusiran. • In surviving animals, microscopic findings were observed at the injection site secondary to the experimental procedure (needle trauma associated with the injection procedure), that resolved by the end of the recovery period. • Dose-related decreases in antithrombin protein levels were observed during the dosing period consistent with the pharmacology of fitusiran. • The no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) was the high dose (HD) of 1 mg/kg/week in females, based on the absence of toxicity, and the MD of 0.5 mg/kg/week in males, based on severe clotting. Both doses represent doses below the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 50 mg per month based on BSA comparisons.

Juvenile Toxicity There were no drug-related adverse effects on growth or physical development in juvenile rats given up to 1 mg/kg/week fitusiran by once-weekly SC dosing up to Day 58 (equivalent to week 4 to 8 age). The NOAEL in juvenile rats was 1 mg/kg/week (below the MRHD of 50 mg per month based on BSA comparisons), based on no adverse findings up to 1 mg/kg/week.

Reproductive Performance There were no fitusiran-related impacts on male fertility or early embryonic development up to 1 mg/kg/week. The NOAEL for male reproductive effects was 1 mg/kg/week (below the MRHD of 50 mg per month based on BSA comparisons).

Carcinogenicity

Study No/ Study Title: ALN-AT3SC: A 2-Year Subcutaneous Injection Carcinogenicity Study in Rats Good laboratory practice (GLP) compliance: Yes

Key Study Findings In agreement with doses recommended by CDER’s Executive Carcinogenicity Assessment Committee, Sprague Dawley rats were administered fitusiran at subcutaneous doses of 0, 0.03, 0.1, or 0.3 mg/kg/week to males and 0, 0.1, 0.3, or 1 mg/kg/week to females once weekly for 2 years. The highest doses were based on mortality due to hemorrhagic thrombosis at ≥1 mg/kg in males and at 3 mg/kg in females in a 26-week and a 7-week repeat dose toxicity study, respectively. The survival rate in fitusiran-treated groups was comparable to control groups. There were no dose-dependent or statistically significant fitusiran-related neoplastic findings noted in either male or female rats up to the highest dose tested. All fitusiran dose levels tested in male and female rats were below the MRHD based on BSA comparisons.

Adequacy of Carcinogenicity Study The Committee concluded that the 2-year carcinogenicity study conducted in Sprague-Dawley rats with fitusiran was adequate and there was no evidence of drug-related neoplasms in male or female rats.

Study No/ Study Title: AT3-GLP17-001 / ALN-AT3SC: A 26-Week Subcutaneous Injection Carcinogenicity Study in TgRasH2 Mice Good laboratory practice (GLP) compliance: Yes

Key Study Findings In agreement with doses recommended by CDER’s Executive Carcinogenicity Assessment Committee, Tg.RasH2 mice were administered fitusiran at subcutaneous doses of 0, 0.05, 0.15, or 0.5 mg/kg/week to males and 0, 0.025, 0.08, or 0.25 mg/kg/week to females once weekly for 26 weeks. The highest doses were selected based on body weight gain decrements in a dose range finding study in wild-type littermates. A positive control group of Tg.RasH2 mice was administered a single intraperitoneal dose of N-methyl-N-nitrosourea (MNU) at 75 mg/kg on Day 1. The survival rate in fitusiran-treated animals was comparable to control animals. No statistically significant increases in dose-response relationships or pairwise comparisons were observed for neoplastic findings after exposure to doses of fitusiran up to 0.5 mg/kg. The positive control MNU induced increased incidences of malignant squamous cell carcinoma and papilloma of the stomach in male and female RasH2 mice relative to control.

ECAC Conclusions The Committee concluded that the 26-week carcinogenicity study conducted in Tg.RasH2 mice with fitusiran was adequate and there was no evidence of drug-related neoplasms in male and female mice.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

No reproductive and developmental toxicity studies were conducted with fitusiran in female animals because the intended population is almost exclusively male, and the clinical development program was conducted exclusively in males. Hemophilia A and B are X-linked recessive inherited bleeding disorders predominantly affecting males (99%), and the likelihood of women of childbearing potential receiving fitusiran is very low; therefore, only a male fertility study was integrated into the 26-week repeated dose chronic toxicity study in rats. There were no fitusiran-related effects on male fertility and early embryonic development up to 1 mg/kg/week (below the MRHD based on BSA comparisons). It is not known whether fitusiran can adversely impact the development of embryo/fetus when administered to a pregnant woman. Only male subjects were enrolled in clinical trials with fitusiran. Deficient factor IX levels result in an increased risk for minor and serious bleeding, including life-threatening spontaneous or traumatic bleeding. Disease manifestations of HB are the same as HA. • One of the most serious complications of HA and HB is development of inhibitors to factors, which is associated with the use of factor replacement. Approximately 30-35% of patients with HA and 5-15% of patients with HB develop inhibitors. Patients with inhibitors often experience increased disease morbidity and mortality because the bleeding is more difficult to prevent or treat.
