Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

Oveporexton is an orally available, brain-penetrant OX2R agonist developed for the treatment of NT1. Orexins are neuropeptides that play a role in maintaining wakefulness and sleep (Abad and Guilleminault, 2017). A precursor peptide is cleaved to form two neuropeptide neurotransmitters, orexin-A and orexin-B, that bind to two different subtypes of G-protein coupled receptors, orexin type-1 receptor (OX1R) and OX2R. Both subtypes of orexin receptors are reported to contribute to the regulation of arousal; OX2R plays a role in the maintenance of wakefulness, whereas both OX1R and OX2R are involved in the inhibition of REM sleep. An association between the development of NT1 and loss of orexin-producing neurons in the brain has been reported in published literature (Tsujino and Sakurai, 2013). In vivo pharmacology data demonstrated significant wake-promoting effects through OX2R activity and improvements in cataplexy-like symptoms in a NT1 mouse model. Although oveporexton showed off-target binding to cannabinoid receptor type 1 (CB-1) and progesterone receptor B (PR-B) receptors, with functional antagonism at the CB-1 receptor (functional activity was not assessed for the PR-B receptor), these effects are not expected to result in clinically significant off-target effects in humans at the maximum recommended human dosage (MRHD) of 4 mg/day (2 mg twice daily). Results from GLP-compliant safety pharmacology studies indicated that oveporexton can affect the CNS (increased body temperature at 20 and 100 mg/kg, decreased body temperature at 1000 mg/kg, and hyperactivity at ≥20 mg/kg in rats), the cardiovascular system (increased blood pressure and left ventricular pressure at ≥3 mg/kg in monkeys), and the respiratory system (non-dose- dependent increased respiratory rate at 20 and 100 mg/kg and minute volume at 100 mg/kg in rats). However, these effects are not expected to constitute a significant clinical risk, given the large exposure margins (>100-fold) compared to clinical exposures at the MRHD. The pharmacokinetic profile of oveporexton was studied in vitro and in vivo in mice, rats, rabbits, and monkeys. Oveporexton was rapidly absorbed after a single oral dose in rats and monkeys; absorption was non-dose proportional in rats but approximately dose proportional in monkeys. The major circulating component in all animal species was unchanged [14C]oveporexton, which accounted for 95.6% (mice), 92.5% (rats), and 75.5% (monkeys) of the total radioactivity. [14C]oveporexton was widely distributed into tissues of pigmented and albino rats with the greatest exposure observed in the adrenal cortex, adrenal gland, and liver, with no evidence of binding to melanin-containing tissues. Oveporexton can be transferred into the fetus via the placenta during gestation and is also secreted into milk in lactating rats. Oveporexton is highly bound to plasma proteins in vitro in rat, monkey, and human hepatocytes. The unbound fraction of oveporexton in plasma is similar in rats, monkeys and humans (4.5%, 2.8%, and 3.8%, respectively). Safety pharmacology

The safety pharmacology of oveporexton was evaluated in a series of good laboratory practices (GLP)-compliant in vitro and in vivo studies to assess potential effects in the CNS, cardiovascular, and respiratory systems.

CNS

The effects of oveporexton on the CNS were evaluated in male Sprague-Dawley rats administered single doses of 0, 20, 100, and 1000 mg/kg/day by oral gavage (6 rats/group) (Study No. B200314). Modified Irwin’s multiple observation method was used to examine the effects on general physical condition and behavior, and body temperature was also measured. Observations were conducted at pre-dose and at 1, 2, 4, 8, and 24h post-dose. Body temperature increased at 20 and 100 mg/kg (+1.0°C and+0.5°C, respectively compared to controls) and a decrease occurred at 1000 mg/kg (-0.7°C compared to controls). The changes in body temperature may be related to pharmacological action of oveporexton; based on literature data, orexin-A induces both hypothermia and hyperthermia, and orexin-B induces hyperthermia in rats (Szekely et al., 2002). Restlessness was observed in all animals at 20 and 100 mg/kg and in 3 of 6 animals at 1000 mg/kg at 1 and 2 h post-dose, and slight increases in spontaneous activity were observed in 1 or 2 animals at 20 and 100 mg/kg at 1 h post-dose (Study No. B200314). These findings were also considered to be due to the pharmacological actions of oveporexton, as OX2R agonists have been reported to increase locomotor activity in rats (Samson et al., 2010). The lowest observed effect level (LOEL) for effects on the CNS in male rats was 20 mg/kg, which provides approximately a 127-fold exposure margin based on Cmax at the LOEL of 3350 ng/ml (taken from Day 1 Cmax value in male rats from the 4-week toxicity Study No. B- 8713) compared to the total Cmax,ss of 26.4 ng/ml at the MRHD in humans. The abuse potential of oveporexton was assessed in several in vivo studies in rats. See the Controlled Substance Staff review for a review of the nonclinical and clinical abuse potential studies.

Cardiovascular

In in vitro cardiovascular assessments, oveporexton produced a minimal but statistically significant inhibitory effect on the hERG current at 3 μg/mL (the highest concentration tested) of 9.0% compared to the vehicle control when tested using HEK293 cells stably expressing hERG channels under physiological conditions and temperature. The IC50 value for hERG inhibition was >3 μg/mL, which provides an approximate safety margin of >3000-fold compared with the human Cmax,ss,unbound (0.98 ng/mL) at the MRHD (Study No.B240294). The potential effects of oveporexton on late Nav1.5 and Cav1.2 current amplitudes at physiologic temperatures were tested at 1, 3, 10, 100 μmol/L in HEK293 cells stably expressing Nav1.5 and Cav1.2 channels (Study No. TPI-24-01). Oveporexton did not block late Nav1.5 current but blocked the Cav1.2 current with an IC50 of 85.8 μM (step pulse) and 7.9 μM (ramp pulse). The IC50 of 7.9 μM oveporexton (4112 ng/mL) corresponds to an approximate exposure margin of >4000-fold compared with the human Cmax,ss,unboud of 0.98 ng/mL at the MRHD. Carcinogenicity

Oveporexton was not carcinogenic in two adequately conducted GLP rodent carcinogenicity studies.

26-Week Oral Gavage Carcinogenicity Study in Tg RasH2 Mice (Study B-8968)

Oveporexton was administered once daily for 26 weeks by oral gavage to CByB6F1-Tg (HRAS)2Jic mice (Tg-rasH2 mice) (25/sex/group) at 0 (vehicle control 0.5% [w/v] methylcellulose solution), 6, 20, 60, and 600 mg/kg for males and 0, 3, 30, and 300 mg/kg for females. A positive control group (15/sex) was administered N-methyl-N-nitrosourea (75 mg/kg, IP). Additional satellite groups of nonTg-rasH2 mice (20 /sex/group) were used to evaluate TK parameters of oveporexton in plasma. The dose selection was based on approximately one-third of the respective lethal doses in a 1-week and 4-week toxicity study in nonTg-rasH2 mice for males and females, respectively (Study No. N-2552 and No. B-8817), in agreement with the recommendation of the Executive Carcinogenicity Assessment Committee (ECAC meeting minutes of June 16, 2022). There was no drug-related, or statistically significant, increase in tumors in Tg-rasH2 mice at doses up to 600 and 300 mg/kg in males and females, respectively. The nonclinical statistical reviewer’s (Dr. Hepei Chen) analysis confirmed that “no statistically significant dose response

relationship or pairwise comparisons in any tested tumor were noted in either male or female mice. For both male and female mice, the pairwise comparisons between the positive control group and the vehicle control group showed p-values less than 0.05 for many tumors, confirming animal expressed oncogenes and responded to carcinogenic insult as expected.” The ECAC concluded that the mouse carcinogenicity study was adequate and that there was no evidence of drug-related neoplasms in male and female mice in the 6-month study (ECAC meeting minutes of June 2, 2026). The highest tested doses of 600 and 300 mg/kg in males and females, respectively were associated with a mean Cmax of 23,400 and 12,500 ng/mL, and a mean AUC24h of 148,000 and 64,100 h*ng/mL, respectively, on Week 26, which is approximately 400-and 173-fold the MRHD, for males and females, respectively, based on AUC.

Clinical efficacy

Study TAK-861-3001

8.1.1.1. Study Design

Overview and Objective

Study Title

“A Randomized, Double-Blind, Placebo-Controlled Study to Evaluate the Efficacy and Safety of TAK-861 for the Treatment of Narcolepsy with Cataplexy (Narcolepsy Type 1)”

Primary Objectives

The study’s primary objective was to assess the efficacy of oveporexton compared to placebo with regard to EDS as measured by the MWT.

Secondary Objectives

Secondary objectives included assessment of between-group difference in: • Changes in EDS as measured by the Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) total score • Changes in cataplexy episodes as measured by the weekly cataplexy rate (WCR) • Changes in sustained attention as measured by the psychomotor vigilance test (PVT) • Changes in overall narcolepsy symptoms measured by the Patient Global Impression of Change (PGI-C) scale • Changes in overall severity of narcolepsy symptoms measured by the Narcolepsy Severity Scale for Clinical Trials (NSS-CT) • Changes in functional impacts of narcolepsy as assessed by the Functional Impacts of Narcolepsy Instrument (FINI) • Changes in quality of life of participants, as assessed by the Short Form-36 Survey (SF-36).

Trial Design

Study Design Overview

Study TAK-861-3001 was a phase 3, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 12-week parallel-group trial to assess the safety and efficacy of two oral dosages of oveporexton for the treatment of NT1.

The study began with a screening period of up to 50 days, which could be extended on a case- by-case basis with Sponsor approval. During screening, participants were required to discontinue their narcolepsy medications, including antidepressants and sodium or multisalt oxybate. Starting no later than Day -16, participants completed an electronic diary to track cataplexy episodes. To be eligible, participants had to complete the diary for at least 11 of the 14 days between Day -16 and Day -3 and had to experience at least four partial and/or complete cataplexy episodes per week during this period. Throughout the study, participants were required to stay overnight at the study site on three occasions: Days -2 to 1 (with one mandatory overnight at Day -2 and one optional overnight at Day -1), Days 55 to 56, and Days 83 to 84. After completing the 12-week treatment period, participants had the option to enroll in a long-term extension study under a separate protocol (Study TAK-861-2003). Those who chose not to participate in the extension study completed two follow-up visits at approximately 7 and 28 days after their final dose of study drug and could restart their restricted medications (except those associated with liver function test elevations) 7 days after the last dose. Participants who terminated the trial early were required to complete an early termination visit as soon as possible and a follow-up visit approximately 28 days after the last dose of trial intervention. Clinical safety

This safety review focused primarily on the two phase 3 safety and efficacy studies in the NT1 population that compared oveporexton to placebo at the to-be-marketed doses—TAK-861- 3001 and TAK-861-3002, pooled together, as they were of similar dosing, design, and length. These studies were also used for the efficacy analysis. See Section 8.1 for a detailed description of the study designs and characteristics. The clinical reviewer assessed study reports, AE database, laboratory database, electrocardiogram database, vital sign database, and participants' scores on the C-SSRS for each study separately and pooled together. Based on the mechanism of action and previous AEs associated with drugs in the same class, elevated liver function tests (LFTs), urinary events, blood pressure (BP) and heart rate (HR) increases, and insomnia were considered adverse events of special interest (AESI). Finally, given psychiatric comorbidities that often accompany narcolepsy and the product's effects on the central nervous system (CNS), psychiatric AEs were also examined closely, especially the risk for suicidal ideation and behavior (SI/B). The clinical reviewer also conducted a review of AEs in the long-term safety study TAK-861- 2003, including the updated 90-day safety report. In addition, the clinical reviewer reviewed the Applicant's integrated summary of safety (ISS) database, which includes data from additional narcolepsy trials as well as trials of oveporexton in healthy volunteers, for serious AEs. The most recent Periodic Benefit Risk Evaluation Report was also reviewed.

Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment The Applicant, Takeda Pharmaceuticals, has submitted NDA 220860 for the approval of TAK-861, also known as oveporexton, with the proposed trade name of Orzeyful, for the treatment of narcolepsy type 1 (NT1; narcolepsy with cataplexy, an orphan indication) in patients . Oveporexton is a new molecular entity (NME) and a selective oral orexin receptor 2 (OX2R) agonist that would be (b) (4)

the first in its pharmacological class approved for the treatment of narcolepsy. The Applicant submitted data from two phase 3 randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, 12-week efficacy and safety studies, TAK- 861-3001 (N=168) and TAK-861-3002 (N=105), conducted at multiple global sites. Against a placebo arm, Study 3001 tested two dosages (1 mg twice daily and 2 mg twice daily) and Study 3002 tested 2 mg twice daily in patients with NT1 in patients 16 to 65 years of age. On the primary efficacy endpoint of change from Baseline at Week 12 in mean sleep latency on the Maintenance of Wakefulness Test (MWT) on polysomnography (PSG), both study results were strongly statistically significant for the difference between drug versus placebo. These results were generally consistent across subgroups of region, race, and sex. (In particular, the regional consistency on an objective endpoint signifies likely mechanistic and pathophysiological consistency for the drug’s effects on NT1, so that the overall study results should apply to the U.S. population.) Also, on the prespecified secondary and additional secondary endpoints, results were statistically or nominally significant. The review team analyzed all of these endpoints to determine their viability in representing clinically meaningful results for the pentad of symptoms known to contribute to NT1. We determined that the evidence supports the conclusion that oveporexton has demonstrated effectiveness and clinically meaningful benefit for the treatment of the whole syndrome of NT1. Additional support was provided by the adequate and well-controlled phase 2 Study TAK-861-2001, whose positive efficacy results were consistent with the phase 3 findings across both studies. The safety profile of oveporexton was consistent across the two placebo-controlled studies as well as the entirety of the safety study database, including a long-term open-label extension (LTE) study. The most common adverse events (AEs) include insomnia (56%), pollakiuria (57%), micturition urgency (16%), and salivary hypersecretion (7%). These events are consistent with the expected on-target pharmacological effects of orexin receptor agonism, occurred predominantly within the first 2 days of treatment, and were generally mild- to-moderate and self-limited. Only one death occurred in the development program, during the LTE study, a myocardial infarction in a subject with pre-existing cardiovascular risks that were the likely primary contributing factors. Serious AEs were infrequent (1%) and were not clearly related to the study drug’s effects.
