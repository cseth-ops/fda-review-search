Conduct a randomized, Phase 3 clinical trial that verifies and describes the clinical benefit of umbralisib in patients with relapsed or refractory follicular lymphoma and marginal zone lymphoma. PBPK in place of a clinical study

FDA rejected or required more · approved 2021 · review page 210

Can PBPK analyses predict the effects of umbralisib on the pharmacokinetics of CYP substrates? No, the PBPK analyses are inadequate to predict the complex inhibitory and induction effects on CYP3A, and the effects of umbralisib on the PK of the substrates of CYP2C8 (repaglinide), CYP2C9 (tolbutamide) and CYP2C19 (S-mephenytoin) due to the uncertainties about in vitro to in vivo extrapolation.

PBPK in place of a clinical study

Described · approved 2021 · review page 77

The effect of umbralisib on CYP3A, CYP2C8, CYP2C9 and CYP2C19 substrates could not be confidently predicted using the PBPK modeling approach due to uncertainties about the in vitro to in vivo extrapolation.

Vital Signs

FDA disagreed · Clinical safety · review page 137
The applicant's position
A concentration-QTc analysis based on data from the 32 subjects of Cohort S (umbralisib monotherapy) in Study TGR-1202-102 was conducted to characterize the relationship of the change from baseline QTc interval with umbralisib plasma concentration. The concentration QTc analysis did not reveal a significant relationship or risk of ≥20 ms corrected QT interval by Fredericia (QTcF) prolongation at the time points evaluated. The predicted ΔQTcF was higher at the 4-hour post dose time point than for other time points, reaching 3.6 ms with a 90% CI upper bound of 10.2 ms.
FDA's assessment
The FDA disagrees with the Applicant’s position that there is no risk of QTc prolongation with umbralisib. The Applicant’s exposure-corrected QT interval (QTc) analyses were conducted using steady state exposure (Cmax,ss) data in Study TGR-1202-102. The geometric mean Cmax on Cycle 2 Day 1 was 4012 ng/mL (n=20), which was lower than the therapeutic exposure (7266 ng/mL) obtained from PPK analysis. The 1200 mg QD dose group in Study TGR-1202-102 did not provide higher exposure (4172 ng/mL) as compared to the 800 mg QD dose group. Thus, the Applicant’s QT assessment was not adequate to extrapolate the risk of QTc prolongation to the higher exposure range. The FDA agrees that there were no large mean increases (i.e., 20 msec) with umbralisib treatment. Immunogenicity

Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

Umbralisib (also known as RP-5264, TGR-1202, (b) (4) , and RP-5307) is a small molecule inhibitor of phosphoinositide-3‐kinase delta (PI3Kδ) and casein kinase 1 epsilon (CK1ε). The targets mediate tumor-progressive signaling in a variety of hematologic malignancies.

Umbralisib binds to human PI3Kδ and CK1ε with dissociation constants (Kd) of 6.2 nM and 180 nM, respectively. A screen of 442 kinases incubated with 1 M of umbralisib showed that the phosphorylated Abelson murine leukemia viral oncogene homolog 1 containing the mutation F317I (ABL1 (F317I)-phosphorylated) kinase was inhibited at levels comparable to CK1. In vitro growth-inhibitory activity was assessed in a panel of 13 human leukemia and lymphoma cell lines. Growth inhibitory (GI50) values ranged from 0.6 to 66 M after the cells were treated for 72 or 96 hours with umbralisib. The test article also inhibited cytokine-mediated adhesion and migration in vitro.

The in vivo antitumor activity of umbralisib was evaluated in a MOLT-4 xenograft model. MOLT- 4 tumor-bearing mice were dosed orally with umbralisib at 150 mg/kg/day for 25 days, with the measurement of tumor growth continuing until 41 days after dosing initiation. The test article dosing group had tumors growing at a slower rate than the control group tumors.

Safety pharmacology studies assessed the effects of umbralisib on cardiovascular, central nervous system (CNS), and respiratory function. Umbralisib had no toxicologically-significant effects on cardiovascular function in a cardiovascular study in dogs, and had no effects on neurobehavioral function or temperature in mice. Respiratory function was affected by umbralisib with decreases in tidal volume as much as 24%, respiration rate as much as 38%, and minute volume as much as 46% observed in respiratory studies conducted in mice administered a single umbralisib dose of 50 to 500 mg/kg.

The oral bioavailability of umbralisib in mice and dogs were 47% and 34% respectively for the 2 species. A mass balance study in male mice demonstrated that 89% and 5% of umbralisib is excreted in feces and urine, respectively. Umbralisib metabolism was evaluated in vitro with liver microsomes. After 60 minutes of incubation with mouse, dog, and human liver microsomes, 75%, 77%, and 86% respectively of umbralisib remained.

The repeat-dose studies in mice and dogs referenced above were conducted to assess the general toxicity of umbralisib. The drug was administered once daily for 26 weeks in mice and

39 weeks in dogs, with the doses and durations informed by the experience of the 13-week repeat-dose studies in both species. The studies were conducted using the oral route of administration, which is consistent with the intended clinical route of administration. There were several unscheduled deaths in the mouse study, while dose reductions and dosing holidays were applied to the dog study. Drug-related findings included decreased white blood cells (WBCs) and differentials, increased liver weights and alkaline phosphatase (ALP) levels, and lymphoid depletion.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Umbralisib was well tolerated after a single dose, with a maximum tolerated dose (MTD) of <1000 mg/kg/day in mice and >2000 mg/kg/day in rats. The tolerability, potential toxicities, target organs, and toxicokinetics of umbralisib were evaluated in repeat-dose toxicity studies up to 26 weeks in mice and 39 weeks in dogs. Umbralisib was generally well tolerated through acute, subchronic, and chronic dosing; test article-related mortality occurred at ≥100 mg/kg/day in mice (equivalent to 300 mg/m2 or 584 mg in a 70 kg human), and adverse clinical findings necessitating dose reductions occurred at ≥10 mg/kg/day in dogs (equivalent to 200 mg/m2 or 389 mg/kg in a 70 kg human) when administered chronically.

The liver was identified as the main target organ in mice and the gastrointestinal tract in dogs. Increased liver weights were observed in all studies in mice for the high-dose groups, but these findings did not correlate with increases in liver enzymes, and liver histopathology findings were minimal to mild. Another major finding was damage to the non-glandular stomach mucosa after subchronic and chronic administration, but the clinical relevance of these findings in humans cannot be determined, as humans do not have a non-glandular stomach. Other findings in mice included decreases in leukocytes and lymphocytes and increases in cholesterol that were considered minimal, non-adverse, or related to the pharmacologic action of umbralisib. All findings in mice were reversible upon treatment cessation. None of these findings were observed in dogs upon treatment with umbralisib. In dogs, diarrhea, body weight reductions, and decreased food consumption were noted in all studies and increased in severity and frequency in a dose-dependent manner. Dogs with body weight loss and decreased food consumption were offered canned food to increase appetite and food consumption. Dose holidays and dose reductions were necessitated during chronic administration due to low body condition scores. There were no gross pathology or histopathology findings that correlated to the clinical signs. Clinical pathology showed minor changes in metabolic parameters that were considered secondary to the clinical findings. There were no other adverse findings upon umbralisib treatment in dogs.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

The potential for umbralisib exposure to impact male and female fertility parameters and pregnancy parameters was evaluated in mice at doses up to 300 mg/kg/day. In male mice, umbralisib administration at 300 mg/kg/day (equivalent to 900 mg/m2 or 1707 mg in a 70-kg human) for 4 weeks resulted in reduced testis weights accompanied by reduced sperm count and motility. The magnitude of these changes was small and did not impact overall male reproductive health.

The potential for umbralisib exposure to impact maternal health, pregnancy parameters, and embryonic development was evaluated in embryo-fetal development studies in mice and rabbits. In mice, doses ≥200 mg/kg (equivalent to ≥600 mg/m2 or ≥1138 mg in a 70 kg human) administered during the period of organogenesis resulted in no effects on maternal health but impacted pregnancy parameters (resorptions and post-implantation losses) and the developing fetus (increased incidence of cleft palate, nasopharyngeal fistula, and minor skeletal abnormalities). Treatment-related findings included an increase in the number of litters that exhibited 100% resorptions and post implantation resorption; and external (cleft palate), visceral (nasopharyngeal fistula), and skeletal fetal abnormalities. In rabbits, doses of 300 mg/kg (equivalent to 3600 mg/m2 or 6774 mg in a 70 kg human) administered during the period of organogenesis resulted in maternal toxicity of reduced body weights and food consumption with no other reported findings on pregnancy and fetal development in rabbits.

Clinical efficacy

The efficacy of umbralisib in subjects with MZL was evaluated in a Phase I study, TGR-1202-101, and 2 Phase II single-arm studies, UTX-TGR-205 and TGR-1202-202. The pivotal study to characterize the efficacy of umbralisib in MZL is the ongoing Phase IIb Study UTX-TGR-205; efficacy data of subjects with MZL are summarized as of the cutoff date of 30 November 2019. Efficacy data from Study TGR-1202-101 and Study TGR-1202-202 are used as supportive information.

Study UTX-TGR-205

Trial Design

The Applicant’s Description:

Basic Study Design

Study UTX-TGR-205 is an ongoing, Phase IIb, randomized study to evaluate the efficacy and safety of single agent umbralisib and umbralisib in combination with ublituximab with or without bendamustine in subjects with previously treated iNHL, mantle cell lymphoma (MCL), and diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL). Subjects were enrolled into 1 of 4 cohorts (MZL, follicular lymphoma [FL] and small lymphocytic lymphoma [SLL], MCL, or DLBCL) based on tumor type.

Trial Location

This study is being conducted at 83 sites in the US and 38 sites outside of the US (10 sites in Poland, 6 sites in the United Kingdom, 5 sites each in Spain and South Korea, 4 sites each in Israel and Italy, 3 sites in Australia, and 1 site in Slovakia).

Choice of Control Group

On the basis of the disease types and settings being studied, an open-label Phase II design without an active controlled standard of care arm was selected.

Diagnostic Criteria

Subjects were enrolled with histologically confirmed diagnosis of B-cell NHL with histological subtype MZL (splenic, nodal, or extranodal) based on criteria established by

the World Health Organization 2008 classification of tumors of hematopoietic and lymphoid tissues.

Key Inclusion/Exclusion Criteria

Male or female subjects ≥18 years of age with MZL were required to have prior treatment with 1 or more lines of therapy, including at least 1 anti-CD20-based regimen (either as monotherapy or chemoimmunotherapy). Additional requirements included documented failure to achieve at least partial response (PR) or documented progressive disease (PD) after the most recent systemic treatment regimen and must have documented evidence for requiring treatment (1 or more of the following: bulky disease [>5 cm], high lactate dehydrogenase level, B symptoms, threatened organ function, splenomegaly, cytopenias due to lymphoma, or effusions).

Subjects with MZL also had to have measurable disease , defined as at least 1 measurable disease lesion >1.5 cm in at least 1 diameter by computed tomography (CT), positron emission tomography (PET)-CT, or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) in an area of no prior radiation therapy, or in an area that was previously irradiated that has documented progression.

Subjects were excluded if they were receiving cancer therapy (i.e., chemotherapy, radiation therapy, immunotherapy, biologic therapy, hormonal therapy, surgery, and/or tumor embolization) or any investigational drug within 21 days of Day 1 of Cycle 1 or had prior exposure to drugs that specifically inhibit PI3K.

Clinical safety

Safety data for subjects exposed to single agent umbralisib from the pivotal Study UTX- TGR-205 as of the cutoff date of 01 September 2019 were integrated with data from 4 other studies (Studies TGR-1202-101, TGR-1202-202, UTX-TGR-103, and UTX-TGR-501) to form an integrated database (IDB), which included a total of 371 subjects (82 subjects with MZL, 147 subjects with FL, and 142 subjects with other hematologic malignancies) hereafter referred to as the Umbralisib Safety Population.

Safety data from Study TGR-1202-102, a completed Phase I study in 32 subjects with solid tumor cancers, and safety data from 6 clinical pharmacology studies in healthy subjects (Studies TGR 1202-PK101, TGR 1202-PK102, TGR-1202-901, TGR-1202-902, TGR-1202-904, and TGR-1202-908) were not integrated with the other studies.

Data from the IDB allow for an informed assessment of the safety profile of umbralisib

800 mg QD and an evaluation of the overall benefit-risk in subjects with MZL. This safety population is also considered appropriate for the detection and characterization of common AEs and to provide guidance on toxicity management.
