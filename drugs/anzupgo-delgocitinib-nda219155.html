13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application The nonclinical studies conducted to support delgocitinib were originally submitted to and reviewed under IND 135351. All pertinent studies were also submitted to the present NDA. Delgocitinib was coded as JTE-052 or JTP-105319 in nonclinical study reports.

13.1.1. Pharmacology (Primary and Secondary) Delgocitinib is a JAK inhibitor that targets the activity of all four members of the JAK family of enzymes, consisting of JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, and TYK2. The established pharmacologic class for delgocitinib is JAK inhibitor. Delgocitinib exhibits inhibitory activity against human JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, and TYK2, thereby inhibiting signal transduction in many inflammatory processes. Delgocitinib inhibits the phosphorylation of STATs stimulated by cytokines IL-2, IL-6, GM- CSF, and IL-23 and inhibits IL-13 and TNF-α production. Delgocitinib inhibits human T cell and B cell proliferation. Tabulated summaries of the in vitro and in vivo pharmacology studies in animals conducted in support of delgocitinib are listed below.

Safety pharmacology

Delgocitinib had no effects on cardiovascular, respiratory, or neurological parameters at clinically relevant doses. Delgocitinib had no remarkable inhibitory effect on the human ether-à- go-go-related gene (hERG) current at clinically relevant concentrations. Delgocitinib had an inhibitory effect on gastrointestinal transport (↓27%) in rats at an oral dose of 30 mg/kg.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

No treatment-related changes were observed in a 9-month dermal toxicity study in minipigs treated with delgocitinib ointment. The no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) was 5% (20 mg/kg/day) delgocitinib ointment, the maximum feasible dose. Skin lesions were observed in a 9-month oral toxicity study in dogs at 3.0 mg/kg/day and the dose was lowered to 1.5 mg/kg/day from Week 24. The NOAEL was 0.6 mg/kg/day.

Reproductive and Developmental Toxicology and Juvenile Animal Toxicology Reproductive and developmental toxicology studies included fertility and early embryonic development studies in male and female rats, embryo-fetal development studies in rats and rabbits, and a pre- and postnatal development study in rats. Juvenile animal studies were conducted in rats (oral) and minipigs (dermal). There were no changes suggestive of systemic or target organ toxicity at up to the highest dose level, 30 mg/kg/day, in juvenile rats. In juvenile minipigs, there were no treatment-related findings following 28-day repeated dermal application of delgocitinib ointment 5% (20 mg/kg/day). Carcinogenicity

In the dermal carcinogenicity study in mice, delgocitinib ointment (1%, 3% and 5%) was administered once daily for 104 weeks. No drug-related neoplastic findings were noted at topical doses up to 5% delgocitinib ointment. In the 2-year oral carcinogenicity study in rats, increased incidence of benign thymoma was noted in females at 30 mg/kg/day (see Section 13.2 for detailed information).

Benefit-risk

The Applicant submitted an NDA for delgocitinib cream, 2%, for the proposed indication of moderate-to-severe CHE in adults who have had an inadequate response to, or for whom topical corticosteroids are not advisable. Delgocitinib is a JAK inhibitor, which inhibits the activity of JAK1, JAK2, JAK3, and TYK2. Janus kinases are intracellular enzymes that transmit signals arising from cytokine or growth factor-receptor interactions on the cellular membrane to influence cellular processes of hematopoiesis and immune cell function. Chronic hand eczema is a persistent inflammatory skin condition affecting the hands and wrists with a high prevalence of 9 to 10% annually and 15% lifetime in the general population. The

condition involves skin barrier dysfunction and multiple inflammatory pathways (Th1, Th2, Th17, and JAK pathways), with etiology predominantly related to allergic contact dermatitis, irritant contact dermatitis, or atopic hand dermatitis. CHE significantly impairs quality of life, causing work-related disabilities, psychological burden including anxiety and depression, and social isolation. Current CHE management begins with nonpharmacologic approaches including allergen/irritant identification and avoidance, emollients, and behavioral modifications. Topical corticosteroids, indicated for the relief of the inflammatory and pruritic manifestations of corticosteroid-responsive dermatoses, have been the standard of care for CHE treatment for all age groups (Eichenfield et al. 2014). Other topical agents approved for atopic dermatitis (topical calcineurin inhibitors, phosphodiesterase-4 inhibitors) are used off-label for CHE. For moderate- to-severe CHE with inadequate topical response, systemic therapies include dupilumab (used off- label) and immunosuppressive agents (cyclosporin, methotrexate, mycophenolate mofetil, azathioprine), but these carry significant safety concerns, including potential for rebound, hypersensitivity reactions, and systemic toxicities, that limit long-term use. Therefore, there is an unmet need for treatment options. To establish the safety and effectiveness of delgocitinib cream, 2% for the treatment of moderate- to-severe CHE, the Applicant submitted data from two adequate and well-controlled clinical trials of similar design. Trials 1401 (DELTA 1, enrolled 487 adult subjects) and 1402 (DELTA 2, enrolled 472 adult subjects) were randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trials that evaluated delgocitinib cream, 2% applied BID compared to vehicle for 16 weeks. Trials DELTA 1 and DELTA 2 were followed by an open-label extension period up to Week 52 in Trial DELTA 3. Enrolled subjects were 18 years of age and older with moderate-to-severe CHE (defined as an IGA-CHE score of 3 or 4) with a history of inadequate response to, or for whom topical corticosteroids are not advisable. The primary efficacy endpoint was the proportion of subjects who achieved IGA-CHE TS at Week 16, defined as a score of 0 (clear) or 1 (almost clear) with at least a 2-point improvement from baseline.
