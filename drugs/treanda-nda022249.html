EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

I. Recommendations

A. Recommendation on approvability: The non-clinical studies submitted to this NDA provide sufficient information to support the use of Treanda ® (bendamustine hydrochloride) for the treatment of patients with chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL).

B. Recommendation for nonclinical studies: No additional non-clinical studies are required.

C. Recommendations on labeling: A separate review will be conducted.

I. Summary of nonclinical findings

A. Brief overview of nonclinical findings: Bendamustine hydrochloride [Treanda®, Cytostasan® (Germany), and Ribomustine® (Germany)] belongs to bifunctional nitrogen mustards. Nitrogen mustard and its derivatives are alkylating drugs which dissociate into electrophilic alkyl groups. These groups form covalent bonds with electron-rich nucleophilic moieties. The bifunctional covalent linkage produced can lead to cell death via several pathways. The precise mechanism of action of bendamustine has not been fully characterized.

Pharmacology: The cytotoxic activity of bendamustine, bendamustine impurities, and bendamustine degradation products had been studied in human tumor cell lines. The antineoplastic activity of bendamustine was tested in vivo in xenograft models. In both in vivo and in vitro tests, bendamustine showed cell cycle effects analogous to other alkylating drugs including cyclophosphamide and chlorambucil.

Safety pharmacology: During safety pharmacology testing, bendamustine at 20 and 25 mg/kg affected the urine output, urinary electrolyte (kaliuretic and natriuretic) and glomerular filtration rate (creatinine clearance) in Sprague Dawley rats, suggesting dysfunction of glomerular filtration. Histopathology lesions in the kidney were also found in the pivotal rat and dog studies described below. Bendamustine hydrochloride perfused at concentrations of 1.5 and 7.5 1g/mL in isolated canine Purkinje fibers had no statistically significant effect on action potential parameters. Bendamustine at 20 pM and 200 1M significantly inhibited the hERG tail current amplitude and caused a significant deceleration of the tail current decay time constant in HEK 293 cells stably expressing the potassium channel. No effects on hERG channel were seen at 2 EM. The clinical significant of these findings are not clear. However, cardiotoxicity was also noted upon histopathological evaluation in the rat and dog pivotal toxicity studies. Heart failure was also noted in the clinical trials. This finding may translate into adverse effects that need to be fully explored in future clinical trials. A QT evaluation in patients is planned as part of post-marketing (phase 4 commitments).

2.6.2 PHARMACOLOGY

2.6.2.1 Brief summary: Bendamustine hydrochloride [Treanda®, Cytostasan® (Germany), and Ribomustine® (Germany)] was first synthesized by Wolley GJ Biol Chem 1944; 152:225-32) and evaluated by Hirschberg et al [Cancer Res 1957; 17(9):904-10]. The structure of bendamustine is related to chlorambucil, the benzene of chlorambucil is replaced by 1- methyl-benzimidazole. Bendamustine, chlorambucil, and melphalan are bifunctional nitrogen mustards. Nitrogen mustard and its derivatives are alkylating agents which dissociate into electrophilic alkyl groups. These groups form covalent bonds with electron-rich nucleophilic moieties on all large and small biologic molecules [Goldenberg & Moore. Nitrogen mustards. In: B. Teicher (ed.), Cancer therapeutics; experimental and clinical agents, pp. 3-22, Human Press, 1997]. The bifunctional covalent linkage produced can lead to cell death via several pathways.

Bendamustine has been approved in Germany since 1971 for use in non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma (NHL), chronic lymphocytic leukemia (CLL), and multiple myeloma. Much of the early nonclinical and clinical work was done in the former German Democratic Republic before current GLP regulations were in effect. Bendamustine appears to act primarily as an alkylating agent inducing DNA breaks, which result in inhibition of DNA replication, repair, transcription, and cell cycle arrest. The precise mechanism of action of bendamustine has not been fully characterized.

The cytotoxic activity of bendamustine hydrochloride was determined on different human solid tumor and leukemic cell lines. In the case of solid tumors, breast, lung (non-small- cell, small-cell) and ovarian cancer cell lines were tested. Bendamustine proved to be active at concentrations of between 4 1M and 500 1M depending on the cell line and the condition of the assay. Regarding leukemic cell lines, the IC59 values were found between 10 and 200uM. Bendamustine showed its maximum effect in about one hour. Bendamustine, chlorambucil, and melphalan induced analogous cell cycle alterations in THP-1 and CCRF-CEM cells. The effi icacy of bendamustine may not be limited by the multidrug-resistance-associated protein (MRP).
