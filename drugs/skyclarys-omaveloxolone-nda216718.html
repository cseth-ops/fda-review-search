The nonclinical reviewer for this application was Dr. David Lee, with Dr. Lois Freed performing the secondary review. Please see Dr. Lee’s Pharmacology/Toxicology Review and Dr. Freed’s supervisory memo for a full review of the nonclinical considerations for this application. The key findings are summarized below.

The Applicant conducted the full battery of GLP-compliant nonclinical studies agreed to by the Division, including toxicity studies of up to 6 and 9 months in rat and monkey, respectively, reproductive and developmental toxicology studies, and genetic toxicology studies.

In vitro assays in rodent and human cell lines and in vivo studies in animals to characterize the pharmacological activity of omaveloxolone demonstrated activation of the NrF2 pathway.

Safety pharmacology

Summarized from nonclinical reviews of INDs : (b) (4)

CNS and respiratory safety pharmacology endpoints following single oral doses of RTA 408 up to 30 mg/kg were assessed in SD rats. There were no notable findings. Cardiovascular endpoints were assessed in cynomolgus monkeys administered single oral doses of RTA 408 up to 100 mg/kg. There were no drug effects on body temperature, blood pressure, heart rate, or ECG (PR, RR, QRS, and corrected QT intervals). Additionally, in vitro concentrations of up to 1.2 µM RTA 408 inhibited the hERG current by 21.5%.

5 Pharmacokinetics/ADME/Toxicokinetics 5.1 PK/ADME Standard PK parameters were assessed following oral administration of RTA 408 in mouse, rat, rabbit, and monkey, with t1/2 ranging from 6 to 30 h. Following oral administration to monkeys, [14C]RTA 408 was approximately 3.41% bioavailable and was widely distributed to most tissues, including brain, between 8 and 30 h, with the highest concentrations observed in the gastrointestinal tract. RTA 408 was between 92.7 and 99.6% protein bound in plasma from mouse, rat, dog, rabbit, minipig, and monkey, and was 96.9% protein bound in human plasma. The major circulating human metabolites were M17 and M22 that accounted for 10.9% and 18.6% of total plasma radioactivity, respectively. Following a single oral dose of [14C]RTA 408, M17 was observed at higher plasma levels in monkeys (13.9%) compared to human (10.9%); however, M22 was not observed in monkey plasma. Additional mixed-matrix normalization analytical methodologies were used to assess the metabolite profiles for M17 and M22 after repeated oral administration in monkey, rat, Tg.rasH2 mouse, CD1 mouse, and patients with FA. M17 showed adequate safety coverage (75.1 to 67700% peak area ratios in plasma) in all tested species compared with humans. The ratios for M22 in Tg.rasH2 mouse plasma and CD1 mouse plasma at HD were similar to patients with FA; however, M22 was 25.6%, 16.6%, and 5% in monkey, male rat, and female rat, respectively, of levels in patients. The hepatobiliary route was the primary route of elimination in monkey after oral administration of [14C]RTA 408.

Nonclinical ADME / toxicokinetics

(Sponsor’s Table)

(13-Week, non-GLP; RTA408-P-1111; from nonclinical review of 122349 by Chun-Ting (David) Lee):

Male and female SD rats (12/sex/group) were administered doses of 0 (sesame oil), 1, 3, 10, or 30 mg/kg RTA 408 by daily oral gavage for 13 weeks. Five animals including 1 HMDM, 2 HDM, and 2 HDF were found dead or euthanized moribund between Days 54 and 87. COD was considered to be treatment-related multiple organ degeneration/necrosis. Clinical signs at the HD included audible/shallow breathing, white discoloration of the gums, swelling of the lip/nose/nuzzle, red discoloration of the nose/muzzle lacrimation, and salivation. A dose-dependent decrease in body weight gain was observed in males at all doses except the LD (LMD 3%, HMD 42%, and HD 60%). A decrease in body weight gain was also observed in HMDF (42%) but due to the lack of a dose response, the relationship to treatment is not clear. The decrease in body weight gain was not associated with a decrease in food consumption but, rather, increases in food consumption were observed in HDM (13.5%) and HDF (22.7%). Increases in AST (up to 2.9-fold) and ALT (up to 6.7-fold) were observed at all doses except the LD, relative to controls. Dose-dependent increases in liver weights were observed at all doses except the LD. Dose-dependent minimal to severe panlobular hepatocellular hypertrophy was observed at all doses except the LD, which was associated with minimal to moderate individual hepatocyte necrosis in HDM and minimal individual hepatocyte necrosis in HMDF and HDF. Mild to moderate focal to multifocal necrosis was observed in HDM and HDF. Additionally, dose-dependent minimal to moderate bile duct hyperplasia and kidney tubular degeneration/regeneration were observed in males at ≥ HMD and females at ≥ LMD. TK parameters were evaluated on Days 1 and 92. Increases in Cmax and AUC were generally dose proportional. Based on panlobular hepatocellular hypertrophy with individual hepatocyte necrosis, bile duct hyperplasia, and kidney tubular degeneration/regeneration, the NOAEL of RTA 408 in rats through 13 weeks was determined to be the LD of 1 mg/kg/day in males and females.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

6.1 Single-Dose Toxicity No single-dose toxicity studies were submitted.

6.2 Repeat-Dose Toxicity Rat (28-Day, GLP; RTA408-P-1103; from nonclinical review of 122349 by Chun-Ting (David) Lee):

Male and female SD rats (15/sex/group) were administered doses of 0 (sesame oil), 3, 10, or 30 mg/kg RTA 408 by daily oral gavage for 28 days, with 28 days recovery. A dose-dependent decrease in body weight gain was observed in males at all doses (LD 10%, MD 16%, and HD 44%) with incomplete recovery, which correlated with decreases in food consumption. Increases in AST (up to 2.83-fold) and ALT (up to 6.88- fold) were observed at all doses except the LD, relative to controls, which resolved over the recovery period. Dose-dependent increases in liver weights were observed at all doses, while dose-dependent increases in kidney weights were observed at the MD and HD in females, which did not fully recover during the recovery period. Dose-dependent minimal to severe panlobular hepatocellular hypertrophy was observed at all doses, which was associated with minimal individual hepatocyte necrosis at the MD and HD. Minimal to moderate focal necrosis was observed in all dose groups except LDM. Additionally, minimal to mild bile duct hyperplasia was observed at the MD. All these liver findings showed incomplete recovery. Dose-dependent minimal to mild kidney tubular degeneration/regeneration was observed at the MD and HD with incomplete recovery. TK parameters were evaluated on Days 1 and 28. Increases in Cmax and AUC were generally dose proportional. The NOAEL of RTA 408 in rats through 28 days could not be defined as the liver findings of focal necrosis and bile duct hyperplasia were observed at the LD at the end of the recovery period.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

A standard battery of reproductive and developmental toxicology studies was conducted for omaveloxolone.

To assess effects on fertility and early embryonic development, omaveloxolone (0, 1, 3, and 10 mg/kg/day) was orally administered to male and female Sprague Dawley rats prior to and during mating and continuing in females to gestation day 7. At the highest dose tested, there was an increase in pre- and post-implantation loss and resorptions, resulting in a decrease in viable embryos at the highest dose tested. The no-effect dose (3 mg/kg/day) for adverse effects on fertility and reproductive function was associated with plasma exposures (AUC) approximately 2 times that in humans (1340-1440 ng*hr/mL) at the recommended human dose of 150 mg/day.

The potential for adverse effects on embryofetal development (EFD) was assessed in embryofetal development studies in pregnant Sprague Dawley rat and New Zealand White rabbit.

Doses for the GLP-compliant EFD study in rat were selected based on a dose range finding study in Sprague Dawley rat at oral doses of 0, 1, 3, 10, and 30 mg/kg/day), administered from gestation days (GD) 6 through GD 17. At 30 mg/kg/day, omaveloxolone produced post-implantation loss and resorptions, resulting in a decrease in viable fetuses, and reduced fetal weight.

In the pivotal study in rat, oral administration of omaveloxolone (0, 1, 3, and 10 mg/kg/day) throughout organogenesis (GDs 6 to 17) resulted in no adverse effects on embryofetal development. At 10 mg/kg/day, plasma exposure (AUC) was approximately 5 times that in humans at the recommended human dose (RHD) of 150 mg/day.

In the GLP-compliant EFD study in rabbit, oral administration of omaveloxolone (0, 3, 10, or 30 mg/kg/day) throughout organogenesis (GDs 7 to 19) resulted in increased embryofetal mortality and skeletal variations and reduced fetal weight at the highest dose tested, which was associated with maternal toxicity. At the no-effect dose for adverse effects on embryofetal development (10 mg/kg/day), plasma exposure was less than that in humans at the RHD.

Potential effects of omaveloxolone on pre- and postnatal development were tested in Sprague Dawley rat; omaveloxolone (0, 1, 3, or 10 mg/kg/day) was orally administered throughout pregnancy and lactation (GD 6 through lactation day 20). Omaveloxolone produced an increase in stillbirths at all doses, reduced body weight in offspring at all but the lowest dose tested, and delayed sexual maturation (males), increased postnatal mortality, and impaired reproductive performance in offspring at the highest dose tested.

Integrated nonclinical assessment

Introduction RTA 408 is a Nrf2 activator developed by Reata Pharmaceuticals Inc. for the treatment of Friedreich's Ataxia. The recommended human dose (RHD) is 150 mg/day.

Pharmacology Primary Pharmacology The ability of RTA 408 to activate Nrf2 has been demonstrated in ARE-luciferase reporter assays and a variety of CNS cell types including neurons, astrocytes, and microglia, with activity observed at concentrations in the low nM range. RTA 408 restored mitochondrial function in myoblasts under oxidative stress, suppressed IFNγ- induced nitric oxide (NO) production in mouse macrophages, and inhibited proinflammatory gene expression in LPS-stimulated mouse microglial cells. In in vitro model of Friedreich’s Ataxia (FA), RTA 408 increased mRNA levels of Nrf2 target genes in hFXNI154F MEFs and in FA patient-derived fibroblasts and lymphoblastoid cells. RTA 408 significantly increased GSH levels and mitochondrial function in hFXNI154F MEFs. Daily oral administration of RTA 408 in mouse, rat, and monkey for 14 days increased Nrf2 target gene expression and antioxidant enzyme activity in various tissues including liver, lung, and/or brain. RTA 408 was more potent than its metabolites (including M22, M17, M28, M14, M15, and M23) in suppressing IFNγ-induced NO production in mouse macrophages.

Secondary Pharmacology RTA 408 was evaluated for off-target binding against an in vitro panel of 38 targets as well as 6 enzymes. RTA 408 did not significantly interact with any target.

RTA 408 differently modulated the expression of aminotransferase genes (including ALT1, ALT2, AST1, and AST2) in different types of cells, increased the expression of Fth1(Ferritin Heavy Chain 1) in mouse myoblasts, and dose-dependently increased the expression of FTH1 in monkey liver after oral dosing.

Study Design

Overview and Objectives

Study 408-C1402 was a 2-part study (Part 1 or PT1 and Part 2 or PT2) evaluating the efficacy, safety, PK, and PD of omaveloxolone in the treatment of patients with FA. The Applicant has submitted separate Clinical Study Reports for both parts of the study. PT1 was a placebo- controlled dose-escalating phase evaluating doses 2.5-300 mg omaveloxolone in FA patients. PT2 was a randomized, double-blind placebo-controlled phase that further evaluated 150 mg omaveloxolone for 48 weeks. I will summarize the trial design for all parts of the study but first discuss the results of Study 408-C1402 PT2 as this is the main study in the application in support of effectiveness of omaveloxolone in FA patients, followed by results of Part 1 and the extension phase.

The primary objectives of Part 2 of the study were: • To evaluate the change in the modified FA rating scale (mFARS) score at Week 48 • To evaluate the safety and tolerability of omaveloxolone

Trial Design

Part 1 of this study was a randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind, dose-ranging study to evaluate the safety, efficacy, and pharmacokinetics (PK) and pharmacodynamic (PD) activity of omaveloxolone at 2.5 mg, 5 mg, 10 mg, 20 mg, 40 mg, 80 mg, 160 mg, and 300 mg in patients with FA. A cohort consisted of the next 8 eligible patients randomized 3:1 to omaveloxolone at the cohort-specific dose (n = 6) or placebo (n = 2). Intra-patient dose-escalation was utilized in the first cohort 1 to evaluate omaveloxolone at the first two dose levels (2.5 mg and 5 mg). Patients were escalated to 5 mg dose after 2 weeks unless a dose limiting toxicity was reported. Subsequent to that, patients stayed at that dose up to Week 12. Patients in Cohort 2-9 were treated at their dose levels for 12 weeks.

Benefit-risk

Friedreich’s Ataxia (FA) is a rare, genetic, cumulatively progressive neurodegenerative disorder caused by a defect in the Frataxin (FXN) gene, typically an expansion of the guanine-adenine-adenine (GAA) triplet repeat within the FXN gene, which impairs transcription, leading to deficiency of the frataxin protein. Frataxin is essential for proper functioning of the mitochondria, and deficiency leads to mitochondrial dysfunction, oxidative stress, and neuroinflammation. Patients develop ataxia, balance issues, loss of motor skills, and speech impairment, among other neurological impairments including weakness, visual impairment, and sensory loss. Patients with FA also have diverse non-neurological features such as cardiomyopathy, kyphoscoliosis, and foot deformities, such as pes cavus. Patients typically have loss of ambulation and wheelchair dependence by their mid-20s, leading to significant disability, and a shortened life expectancy, with an average age of death around 37.5 years of age. There are currently no approved drugs for the treatment of FA.

Omaveloxolone is proposed for the treatment of FA. The Applicant postulates that omaveloxolone activates the transcription factor nuclear factor (erythroid-derived 2)-like 2 (Nrf2), which regulates the cellular response to oxidative stress and restores mitochondrial function, thus restoring redox balance and reducing inflammation. The proposed dose is 150 mg by mouth daily on an empty stomach, one hour before eating.

The Applicant has provided data from a single, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled study (Study 1402 Part 2) and an open-label extension study (Study 1402 OLE). In Study 1402 Part 2, a total of 103 patients with FA with or without pes cavus received placebo (N = 52) or omaveloxolone (N = 51) daily for 48 weeks (All Randomized population). A total of 82 FA patients without pes cavus (Full Analysis Population) were included in the Applicant’s prespecified primary analysis population. There was a statistically significant improvement in the modified Friedreich’s Ataxia Rating Scale (mFARS) at Week 48 in the omaveloxolone arm compared to the placebo arm (-2.41 points, p = 0.0138) in the Full Analysis population. Patients randomized to omaveloxolone (N = 42) had a mean improvement from baseline in mFARS with a change from baseline of -1.56 points, and patients randomized to placebo (N = 40) had a mean worsening from baseline of 0.85 points at Week 48. In the prespecified Full Analysis Population, the key secondary endpoints did not reach statistical significance; however, the secondary endpoint of Activities of Daily Living (ADL) scale was nominally significant (p = 0.04). In the All Randomized population, there was also a nominally significant improvement in mFARS of -1.94 compared to placebo (p = 0.0331) and the Patient Global Impression of Change (PGIC) showed nominally significant improvement compared to placebo (p = 0.0282).
