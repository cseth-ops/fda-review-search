Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary Sevabertinib is a reversible oral tyrosine kinase inhibitor that targets human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2), including HER2 exon 20 insertion mutations and HER2 point mutations. It also exhibits activity against wild-type (WT) epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and certain EGFR mutants. The indication is for the treatment of adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic non-squamous non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) whose tumors have HER2 (ERBB2) tyrosine kinase domain activating mutations, as detected by an FDA-approved test, and who have received a prior systemic therapy. The recommended dose and schedule of sevabertinib for patients is 20 mg orally twice daily (BID).

The established pharmacological class for sevabertinib is kinase inhibitor. Predictive X-ray crystallography showed that sevabertinib noncovalently binds to the HER2 kinase domain. In in vitro kinase activity inhibition assays, sevabertinib inhibited the activity of WT HER2 and WT EGFR with IC50 values of 2.94 nM and 0.15 nM, respectively. In cellular assays, sevabertinib inhibited the in vitro proliferation of cells overexpressing WT HER2 (IC50 = 5.30 nM) and a panel of HER2 exon 20 insertion mutations and activating point mutations (IC50 = 0.54 to 13.9 nM) at concentrations below the free Cmax of 88 nM at the recommended dose of 20 mg BID based on ~95% protein binding. Sevabertinib also inhibited phosphorylated HER2 (p-HER2) and downstream signaling in cancer cells harboring an HER2 exon 20 insertion or WT HER2 amplification. In vivo, sevabertinib exhibited antitumor activity against NSCLC patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models harboring the HER2 exon 20 insertion YVMA.

Secondary pharmacology studies showed that sevabertinib exhibited off-target activity in functional in vitro assays at the human adenosine A1 receptor and motilin receptor at concentrations ≥46-fold the free Cmax of 88 nM at the recommended clinical dose. In kinase activity competition assays, sevabertinib inhibited discoidin domain receptor 2 (DDR2) with an IC50 of 53.3 nM, DDR1 with an IC50 of 111 nM, and mitogen-activated protein kinase 14 (MAPK14) with an IC50 of 132 nM, indicating that sevabertinib has the potential to target these kinases at clinically relevant concentrations. To assess sevabertinib’s effects on cardiac function due to the potential off-target activity on human adenosine and motilin receptors, ex vivo rabbit Langendorff heart preparations showed concentration-dependent decrease in left ventricular pressure (LVP), contractility, and coronary flow. Sevabertinib also shortened the QTcF interval and the left ventricular monophasic action potential duration at 90% repolarization (MAPD90) and caused widening of the QRS interval. Effects were noted at doses up to 113-fold higher than the free Cmax of 88 nM.

Safety pharmacology

Effects of sevabertinib on vital organ systems, i.e. cardiovascular, respiratory, and the CNS, were investigated in one in vitro (hERG assay) and in three in vivo studies, according to the ICH S7A and ICH S7B guidelines. The in vivo studies were conducted after single oral administration of sevabertinib (i) in rats at doses of 0 (Ethanol/KolliphorHS15®/Water [10/40/50, v/v/v], pH 4), 5, 15, and 45 mg/kg; and (ii) in telemetered dogs at doses of 0 [ethanol/PEG400/water, 1:5:4, pH ~4, 2 mL/kg], 2, 5, and 15 mg/kg body weight. Concordant with the ICH S9 guideline, the effects of sevabertinib on safety pharmacology endpoints of CNS function (detailed clinical observations following dosing) were assessed within a pivotal 4 week, toxicity study in Wistar rats, with once daily oral administration. The studies were conducted in compliance with the OECD Principles of GLP at certified testing facilities in Germany or the United Kingdom, which are member countries of the OECD Mutual Acceptance of Data Agreement. Sevabertinib, at single oral doses, was devoid of major acute effects (i) on hemodynamic parameters in a dog telemetry study (Module 4.2.1.3, R-14051), (ii) on parameters of CNS function (behavior, locomotor activity, and body temperature) in rats (Module 4.2.1.3, PH- 42619), and (iii) on parameters of respiratory function (respiration rate, and tidal and minute volume) in rats (Module 4.2.1.3, Report PH-42756). Detailed clinical observations as included in the 4-week, repeat-dose, rat toxicity study did not reveal abnormalities in general behavior, posture, and breathing on Days 1 and 2 (Module 4.2.3.2, Report PH-42111). As shown in vitro (hERG K+ assay) and in vivo (ECG, including QT/QTC intervals in telemetry dogs), sevabertinib has a potential for delayed repolarization at clinically relevant plasma concentrations and above. A hERG IC50 value of 2.4 µmol/L was calculated (Module 4.2.1.3, R- 13892), resulting in a no-effect level (defined as 1/30 of the hERG IC50) of 80 nmol/L; this is similar to the human plasma concentration (Cmax,u,ss ~91 nmol/L) at the clinically relevant dose of 20 mg/patient BID. At the high dose in dogs, the QT/QTc interval in the ECG was slightly (~8 ms), but significantly prolonged at about 1.5 h to 4 h post-treatment. This delayed repolarization was observed at concentrations about 12-fold higher than the human plasma concentration (Cmax,u,ss ~91 nmol/L) at the clinically relevant dose of 20 mg/patient BID (Module 4.2.1.3, Report R-14051).

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The nonclinical safety testing strategy for sevabertinib was developed in accordance with the ICH S2(R1), M3(R2), S3A, S5(R3), S7A, and S9 guidelines. All nonclinical studies were conducted in accordance with the best scientific principles. Pivotal nonclinical studies were conducted in conformance with GLP; 21 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR), Part 58 and/or the principles of OECD GLP in countries that are part of the OECD Mutual Acceptance of Data process. Rats and cynomolgus monkeys were used in the toxicology program; both species are considered to be pharmacologically active and have a metabolism of sevabertinib comparable to that of humans. Dogs could not be used because of insufficient exposure due to enzyme induction after repeated dosing. The toxicity, the reversibility of effects, and exposures of sevabertinib were assessed in repeat dose, toxicology studies, up to 13 weeks duration in rats and monkeys. Daily oral administration of sevabertinib to rats and monkeys over 2, 4, or 13 weeks revealed adverse effects characterized by general signs of toxicity, such as reduced food intake, changes in laboratory parameters, diarrhea, and degenerative and inflammatory processes in epithelial and mucosal tissues. The highest doses tested in the 13-week studies correspond to MoEs slightly less than 1 for unbound AUC and greater than 2 for unbound Cmax compared to exposures at the maximum therapeutic dose of 2 x 20 mg per day. Genetic toxicology

The Applicant’s Position: Sevabertinib was not mutagenic in a GLP-compliant bacterial reverse mutation assay (Ames assay) in the presence and absence of Aroclor 1254-induced rat liver S9 fractions at concentrations up to 5,000 μg/plate. The aneugenic and clastogenic potential of sevabertinib was assessed in vitro in a GLP- compliant micronucleus test. Based on toxicity, the limit concentrations for assessment were set at 75 µg/mL (without S9 mix, 4 hours treatment) and 85 µg/mL (with S9 mix, 4 hours treatment). In addition, concentrations up to 30 µg/mL were chosen for assessment of the extended treatment without the S9 mix. Evaluation of the data indicate that sevabertinib is not genotoxic in the micronucleus test in vitro, when tested up to cytotoxic concentrations in the absence or presence of metabolic activation. In addition, there was no evidence of a biologically relevant or statistically significant genotoxic effect of orally administered test item in the peripheral blood of the micronucleus test in the male and female rats when tested in assessable doses up to the MTD in the course of the pivotal 4- week toxicology study.

The FDA’s Assessment:

FDA confirmed the Applicant’s results of the submitted genotoxicity assays and agrees with the conclusions. Standard positive controls confirmed the sensitivity and validity of these GLP- compliant assays. The genotoxic potential for metabolite M1 was not investigated by the Applicant; however, we note that M1 was present following incubation of sevabertinib with hepatocytes from rat and monkey and measured in plasma of animals in the 13-week repeat-dose studies. Additionally, M1 is structurally similar to sevabertinib, which was not genotoxic in in vitro and in vivo assays. Therefore, M1 is not genotoxic based on the totality of data with active pharmaceutical ingredient and metabolite (genotoxicity, ADME, and structure).

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Dedicated fertility and early embryonic development studies were not conducted and are not warranted per ICH S9 guidance for nonclinical safety assessment of pharmaceuticals intended for the treatment of advanced cancer. Reproductive organs were weighed and/or examined microscopically in repeat-dose rat and monkey toxicity studies. A preliminary embryo-fetal development study in rats was conducted. Oral (gavage) administration of 0, 3, 7, or 11 mg/kg/day sevabertinib to pregnant female Wistar rats during organogenesis resulted in test item-related effects at all dose levels, including three mortalities following administration of 11 mg/kg/day. A dose level of 7 or 11 mg/kg/day was considered excessive due to the severity of the test item-related body weight loss, reduced food consumption, adverse clinical observations, and reduced fetal weights, however no fetal malformations or variations were noted. Test item-related effects following administration of 3 mg/kg/day were confined to initial reduced body weight gain and small reductions in food consumption and considered not to represent an adverse effect to the pregnant animals. The marginal reductions in fetal weight were not classified as adverse. A no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) of sevabertinib was established as 3 mg/kg/day as a result. In the pivotal EFD study in rats, sevabertinib-related effects observed after administration of 1.5 or 3 mg/kg/day were limited to marginal reductions in body-weight gain and food consumption. This resulted in slightly lower absolute body weights at GD 21 compared with controls and did not adversely affect maternal health or fetal development. Fetal effects were limited to slightly reduced fetal weights (combined and individual sexes), resulting in a slight reduction in gravid uterine and placental weights; however, mean values were within the historical control data range. A dose level of 6 mg/kg/day was considered excessive due to the severity of the sevabertinib-related body weight changes, reduced food consumption, and reduced fetal and placental weights. In summary it can be stated that no fetal malformations or adverse fetal variations were noted at unbound exposures of sevabertinib at multiples of exposure below or in the range of 1 compared to the therapeutic human unbound exposure. Based on these findings, the NOAEL is 3 mg/kg/day. Rabbit did not qualify as a test species for embryo-fetal development assessment, as no relevant exposure to sevabertinib could be achieved.

Clinical efficacy

Study 21607 (SOHO-01)

The efficacy analyses in support of the proposed indication are based on data from Study 21607 to evaluate sevabertinib in participants with locally advanced or metastatic NSCLC harboring EGFR and/or HER2 activating mutations (see Table 27). Study 21607 consists of Dose Escalation, Backfill, Dose Expansion, and Dose Extension parts, with the latter representing the Phase 2 part of the study (Figure 5). Populations supporting efficacy The efficacy of sevabertinib is presented for the following populations of patients with advanced NSCLC harboring HER2 activating mutations (including ex20ins): • Primary efficacy population is Group D (N=81), which includes participants who have received prior systemic therapy but were naïve to HER2 ex20ins-targeted therapy. Participants who had prior treatment with HER2 ex20ins-targeted therapy for ≤2 months and stopped treatment due to reasons other than progressive disease were also eligible. • Supportive efficacy data are provided by Group E (N=55), which includes participants who have received prior systemic therapy and had documented progression to HER2- targeted ADCs. Prior treatment with HER2 ex20ins-TKIs for ≤2 months was exclusionary unless treatment lasted for ≤2 months and was stopped due to reasons other than progressive disease. These populations include participants from DE/BF, Dose Expansion, and Dose Extension parts of the study who received sevabertinib at the 20 mg BID dose and who met the eligibility criteria for Groups D and E. Group D included 37 participants from the Dose Extension, 34 from Dose Expansion, and 10 from the DE/BF part of the study. In Group E, 21 participants were from Dose Extension, 31 were from the Dose Expansion, and 3 were from the DE/BF part of the study. As follow-up was longer and the data are more mature in the DE/BF and Dose Expansion parts compared to the Dose Extension part, efficacy data (ORR, DOR, DCR, and PFS), based on response assessments by BICR, are also presented by study part: • DE/BF/Expansion (N=44 in Group D and N=34 in Group E). • Extension (N=37 in Group D and N=21 in Group E).

Clinical safety

Data: The main safety analyses to support the proposed indication are based on the data from Study 21607 (SOHO-01), a non-randomized, open-label, single-arm, multicenter, Phase 1/2 study in participants with advanced NSCLC harboring activating mutations in HER2 and/or EGFR. The key safety findings from Study 21607 are presented in the following sections. An integrated analysis of safety was not performed, since no data from other clinical studies in cancer patients are available for this submission. All safety analyses were performed using the SAF, which included all participants who received at least 1 dose of sevabertinib. As of the database cut-off date, a total of 357 participants had started treatment and were included in the safety analyses. For completeness, supportive safety data from 5 clinical pharmacology studies (Studies 22249, 22250, 22251, 22252, and 2253), conducted in 89 healthy participants to evaluate sevabertinib for DDI potential, mass balance, and the effect of food, is provided in the NDA Module 2.7.4.
