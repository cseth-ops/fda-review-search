4 Pharmacology

LX1606 is an inhibitor of L-tryptophan hydroxylase (TPH) which mediates serotonin synthesis in the mucosa of the gastrointestinal (GI) tract. LX1606 is rapidly converted in vivo to its active metabolite, LP778902. Both LX1606 and LP778902 inhibited TPH,

thereby decreasing the peripheral serotonin levels. Carcinoid tumors are mostly derived from enterochromaffin cells of the midgut, and often produce and release large amounts of 5-HT. By reducing the serotonin level, LX1606 would be beneficial for patients with carcinoid syndrome, which is caused by excessive production and release of serotonin from metastatic neuroendocrine tumors.

4.1 Primary Pharmacology In Vitro Studies

Both telotristat ethyl and its active metabolite, LP-778902, inhibited purified human L- tryptophan hydroxylase (TPH1 and TPH2). The active metabolite, LP-778902, was a more potent inhibitor of purified human TPH1 and TPH2 compared to telotristat ethyl. The minor metabolite LP-951757 showed no inhibitory activity against TPH1. The results were summarized in the sponsor’s table below.

In Vivo Studies

Oral administration of telotristat ethyl for 5 days significantly reduced 5-HT levels in the intestine in rats, with an ED50 (daily dose causing 50% depletion of 5-HT) of approximately 50 and 100 mg/kg/day for jejunum and proximal colon, respectively (study # P103-002).

Reduction of 5-HT levels in the intestine was also seen following oral administration of telotristat ethyl in mice and dogs.

Telotristat ethyl also delayed GI transit and gastric emptying following two oral doses in rats (P103-002).

Telotristat ethyl had no effects on brain 5-HT levels at doses up to 300 mg/kg/day, 500 mg/kg/day, and 100 mg/kg/day in mice, rats, and dogs respectively.

4.2 Secondary Pharmacology

The binding of telotristat ethyl and the active metabolite LP-778902 to a variety of receptors, enzymes, and ion channels was assessed in vitro at a concentration of 10 µM. Telotristat ethyl (LP-778914) showed binding activity at a number of these targets. The results are summarized in the sponsor’s table below.

LP-778902 had limited binding affinity for most of these targets. However, it showed significant binding to A3, 5-HT1B, and 5-HT2A receptors (i.e. >50% inhibition of radioligand binding). The results are shown in the sponsor’s table below.

Integrated nonclinical assessment

Xermelo (telotristat ethyl) was developed under IND 78,749 for the treatment of diarrhea associated with carcinoid syndrome in patients that no longer respond to standard care with somatostatin analogues. This development program was designated as a Fast Track development program on May 19, 2008. LX1606 was designated as an orphan drug on March 9, 2012. A pre-NDA meeting was held February 24, 2015. All of the needed study reports are submitted in this NDA.

LX1606 (telotristat ethyl) is an inhibitor of L-tryptophan hydroxylase (TPH) which mediates serotonin synthesis in the mucosa of the gastrointestinal (GI) tract. LX1606 is rapidly converted in vivo to its active metabolite, LP778902 (telotristat). Both LX1606 and LP778902 inhibit TPH, thereby decreasing peripheral serotonin levels. However, the potency of telotristat exceeds that of telotristat ethyl by 29-fold and 38-fold for human TPH1 and TPH2, respectively. Furthermore, the systemic exposure to telotristat markedly exceeds that of the parent drug. Thus, the overall pharmacology and toxicology profile of telotristat ethyl should be attributed to the active metabolite, telotristat.

Carcinoid tumors are mostly derived from enterochromaffin cells of the midgut, and often produce and release large amounts of a variety of chemical signaling molecules and hormones, including 5-HT (serotonin). By reducing the peripheral serotonin levels, LX1606 is expected to be beneficial for patients with carcinoid syndrome, which is caused in part by excessive production and release of serotonin from metastatic carcinoid tumors.

In the present NDA, the sponsor seeks market approval for Xermelo for the (b) (4)

treatment of carcinoid syndrome diarrhea in patients with metastatic neuroendocrine tumors who are receiving somatostatin analogue therapy. The recommended dose is 250 mg three times daily (b) (4) In support of this NDA, the following
