Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

Rearranged during transfection (RET) is a receptor tyrosine kinase that functions in the early development of the human enteric nervous system and genitourinary tract (Mulligan 2014). Activating RET mutations including V804 gatekeeper mutations have been observed in medullary thyroid cancer (MTC) and multiple endocrine neoplasia type 2 (Romei Ciampi et al. 2016); oncogenic RET fusions including KIF5B-RET and CCDC6-RET have also been identified in 1-2% of patients with NSCLC (Lin, Wang et al. 2015). Pralsetinib (BLU-667 or X581238) is a small molecule drug with an established pharmacological class of kinase inhibitor that primarily targets wild-type (WT) RET (IC50s between 0.14 and 0.43 nM) as well as kinase-activating RET mutations (V804L, V804M, and M918T) and the coiled-coiled domain containing 6 (CCDC6)-RET fusion (IC50s between 0.33-0.45 nM) in biochemical activity assays at concentrations below those achievable at the intended clinical pralsetinib dose of 400 mg (free Cmax of approximately 154 nM based on ~97.1% protein binding). In vitro, pralsetinib also inhibited the enzymatic activity of other human kinases including DDR1, JAK1-2, TRKC, FLT3, TRKA, and PDGFRb at IC50 concentrations ≤100 nM, all lower than those achieved clinically at the 400 mg dose. Further, despite variability in some of the in vitro assays suggesting a degree of uncertainty of the effects of pralsetinib for these targets, pralsetinib inhibited fibroblast growth factor receptor 1 (FGFR1) in biochemical inhibition assays and vascular endothelial growth factor receptor 2 (VEGFR2) in both biochemical and cellular assays at clinically relevant concentrations. Typical side effects associated with both FGFR and VEGFR were present in both clinical trials and nonclinical studies providing additional support for these kinases as pralsetinib targets.

In vitro incubation of pralsetinib with Ba/F3 cells expressing fusions of KIF5B with RET or RET variants (V804L, V804M, and V804E) or with cancer cell lines endogenously expressing RET mutations (C634W and M918T) or CCDC6-RET resulted in reduced RET phosphorylation and downstream signaling and/or inhibition of viability. In contrast, pralsetinib did not substantially inhibit the viability of unmanipulated, RET-negative Ba/F3 cells. Pralsetinib exhibited in vivo anti-tumor activity in mice bearing subcutaneously (SC)-implanted tumor xenografts bearing RET activating mutations, including the cabozantinib resistant mutation V804L, or patient- derived NSCLC and colorectal cancer (CRC) xenografts expressing KIF5B-RET and CCDC6-RET, respectively. Further, pralsetinib improved survival in mice implanted intracranially with tumor models expressing KIF5B-RET or CCDC6-RET, suggesting that pralsetinib may have activity against RET-dependent CNS metastases.

Pralsetinib did not inhibit in vitro hERG potassium currents at relevant concentrations or induce QTc prolongation in the 13-week repeat-dose study in cynomolgus monkeys. A stand-alone in vitro safety pharmacology study was conducted to assess the potential for pralsetinib to prolong the QT interval (Report CPB-25-15-010A-0169). All pivotal toxicology studies were conducted in accordance with GLP and ICH S9 guidance using the proposed clinical regimen of daily oral dosing. Repeated-dose toxicology studies in rats and monkeys of 28 days and 13 weeks duration to support registration of pralsetinib are summarized below.

A GLP-compliant 28-Day Once Daily Oral Gavage Toxicity and Toxicokinetic Study of Pralsetinib in Sprague-Dawley Rats with a 14-Day Recovery Period (Report -124570) (b) (4)

In the GLP-compliant 28-day repeated-dose toxicology study, pralsetinib was administered at oral doses of 10, 20, 30, and 75 mg/kg/day to Sprague Dawley rats (15 animals/sex/group). Dose-limiting toxicity and severely toxic dose effects in rats at 75 mg/kg/day (450 mg/m2/day) were attributed to inanition, metabolic perturbations, and contributions by hematologic and tissue effects. The severely toxic dose eliciting ≥ 10% lethality in the rat was 30 mg/kg/day (180 mg/m2/day). Off target, nonseverely toxic pharmacologic effects included thickened physeal cartilage (femur), hyperphosphatemia with corresponding mineralization in the glandular stomach, incisor tooth degeneration, and decreased bone marrow cellularity with corresponding lower erythron parameters. These effects were either resolving or resolved after the 14-day recovery period with the exception of incisor tooth degeneration.

A GLP-compliant 13-Week Once Daily Oral Gavage Toxicity and Toxicokinetic Study of Pralsetinib in Sprague-Dawley Rats (Report 00124770) In the GLP-compliant 13-week repeated-dose toxicology study in rats (10 animals/sex/group), pralsetinib was administered at oral doses of 5, 10, and 20 mg/kg/day. There were no new severely toxic findings, nor was there an increase in the severity of known adverse findings. At the highest dosage administered (20 mg/kg/day), the following new adverse findings were observed: testicular degeneration in males, decreased lymphoid cellularity in the thymus in males and females, and degeneration of the corpus luteum in females. Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Studies on reproductive function, early embryonic development, pre- and postnatal development, and effects in juvenile animals were not conducted in accordance with ICH S9 guideline. Embryofetal developmental toxicity was investigated in rats. Consistent with ICH S9, a confirmatory study in a second species was not conducted because teratogenicity and fetal lethality was observed in the rat embryofetal study.

A GLP-compliant Oral Gavage Enhanced Dose Range-Finding Study of the Effects of Pralsetinib on Embryofetal development with Toxicokinetics in Sprague Dawley Rats (Report 00124766) In the GLP-compliant embryofetal development toxicology study, pralsetinib was administered at oral doses of 5, 10, 20, and 30 mg/kg/day to pregnant Sprague Dawley rats (10 animals/dose group) from Gestation Days 6 to 17; dosage levels were selected based on findings from the 28-day repeated-dose toxicology study in rats. Pralsetinib was tolerated in pregnant rats up to the highest tested dose of 30 mg/kg/day (180 mg/m2/day). Because of this, 30 mg/kg/day was the NOAEL for maternal toxicity. The NOAEL for embryofetal development could not be determined due to adverse effects on fetal morphology and/or intrauterine survival at all dosage levels. At 5 and 10 mg/kg/day (30 and 60 mg/m2/day), multiple visceral (kidney and ureter abnormalities) and skeletal (rib, costal cartilage, and vertebral anomalies) malformations were observed. The renal malformations were attributed to on-target inhibition of RET

signaling, as RET is known to be an essential component of renal organogenesis in utero. Mice homozygous for a mutation in the RET gene display renal agenesis or severe dysgenesis (Schuchardt et al, 1994). Ureteric bud growth from the Wolffian duct into the adjacent mesenchyme depends on sequential expression of the ligand glial cell line derived neurotrophic factor and its receptor, RET (Costantini and Shakya, 2006). Postimplantation losses of 100% were observed at dosage levels ≥ 20 mg/kg/day (≥ 120 mg/m2/day) and were attributed to pralsetinib’s secondary pharmacologic effect of inhibiting VEGFR, an effect that has been observed with other anti-VEGF compounds due to their antiangiogenic effects (Patyna et al, 2008).
