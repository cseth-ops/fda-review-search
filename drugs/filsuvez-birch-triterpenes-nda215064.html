Summary of Regulatory Action

Amryt Pharmaceuticals re-submitted this NDA for Oleogel (tradename Filsuvez) for the treatment of wounds in adult and pediatric patients with dystrophic and junctional epidermolysis bullosa (EB). The previous submission received a complete response action for

insufficient evidence of effectiveness, primarily due to a lack of confirmatory evidence. This conclusion was subsequently overturned during a formal dispute resolution process.

Epidermolysis bullosa is a rare skin fragility disease caused by mutations in genes for adhesion and anchoring complexes in the basement membrane zone of the skin and mucosa, leading to blistering, erosion, and ulceration of the skin and mucosa, and primarily affecting pediatric patients. The Oleogel development program enrolled patients with two of the four subtypes: junctional and dystrophic. Junctional EB is caused, in most cases, by autosomal recessive mutations in the laminin-332 genes, which result in a structural defect of the anchoring filaments located in the lamina lucida and superior lamina densa of the basal membrane zone, leading to a cleavage plane within the lamina lucida of the dermoepidermal junction. Dystrophic EB is caused by either recessive or dominant mutations in the COL7A1 gene, encoding the alpha-1 chain of type VII collagen, which is the main constituent of the anchoring fibrils located below the lamina densa of the epidermal basement membrane zone, leading to a characteristic cleavage plane below the lamina densa.

Safety pharmacology

Neurological effects: In two separate experiments, female CD-1 mice were given a single intraperitoneal dose of triterpene extract (doses: 0 [vehicle: sesame oil], 30, 100, or 300 mg/kg). In the first experiment, 30 minutes after triterpene extract administration, mice received an intravenous dose of hexobarbital (45 mg/kg) to induce sleep and time until regaining righting reflex was recorded. In the second experiment, fine and gross motor activity was automatically recorded for 2 hours post-dose. No triterpene extract-related effects were noted at any dose level in either study.

Cardiovascular effects: In vitro, betulin, the primary component of triterpene extract, did not produce clinically relevant hERG current inhibition. Up to 190 nM betulin produced 23% inhibition of the hERG current.

Renal effects: Female Sprague-Dawley rats were given a single intraperitoneal dose of triterpene extract (doses: 0 [vehicle: sesame oil], 125, 250, or 500 mg/kg) and urine was collected for 24 hours post-dose for analysis. Triterpene extract had no effect on urine volume (diuresis) or saluresis.

Gastrointestinal effects: In the isolated guinea pig ileum test to assess spasmolytic and spasmogenic properties, triterpene extract was neither an agonist or an antagonist. In female CD-1 mice given a single intraperitoneal dose of triterpene extract (doses: 0 [vehicle: sesame oil], 30, 100, or 300 mg/kg), reduced intestinal motility was noted at the high dose; however, this effect is not clinically relevant.

Nonclinical ADME / toxicokinetics

Absorption Following topical treatment of Filsuvez gel once daily, every 2 or 3 days or once weekly with a mean treatment area of 12% BSA or affected wound surface area of 1100 cm2 at baseline, approximately 70% evaluable subjects had betulin venous blood concentrations BLQ of 10 ng/mL during the double-blind phase of Study BEB-13, suggesting a low systemic absorption. The highest concentrations of betulin in adults and pediatrics were 33 ng/mL and 207 ng/mL, respectively. Of note, one subject had a betulin blood concentration of 27 ng/mL on Day 0 before the treatment. It is probably due to dietary uptake as betulin is reportedly present in olives and some fruits. Distribution Plasma protein binding of betulin is >99%, in vitro. Elimination Metabolism The in vitro study indicated that incubation of 1 µmol/L betulin with human hepatocytes generated several unidentified phase I and/or phase II metabolites. In vitro, betulin was mainly metabolized by CYP3A. The relative contribution of CYP3A and phase II metabolizing enzymes in the overall metabolism of betulin has not been fully characterized. Excretion Excretion of betulin has not been fully characterized in humans. Drug-drug interaction In vitro studies suggest that Filsuvez gel is unlikely to have clinically meaningful effect on the PK of drugs metabolized by CYP 1A2, 2B6, 2C8, 2C9, 2C19, 2D6, or 3A. Pediatric subjects Among 49 evaluable pediatric patients with the median age of 10 years (range: 13 months to <18 years), betulin venous blood concentrations in 67% subjects (n=33) were BLQ of 10 ng/mL on Day 90 (EDBP) after once daily, every 2 or 3 days, or once weekly topical treatment of Filsuvez gel for 90 days with a mean treatment area of 13% BSA or affected wound surface area of 1100 cm2 at baseline. The highest concentration of betulin in pediatrics was 207 ng/mL. Betulin systemic exposure in pediatric patients was generally similar to those observed in adults; however, concreate conclusions cannot be made due to high variability in systemic concentrations and variable sampling times among subjects. Also, the frequency of dosing was different among different subjects. Clinical efficacy

8.1.1. Study BEB-13 – Trial Design

Trial Design

Study BEB-13 is a randomized, double-blind, vehicle-controlled efficacy and safety study of Oleogel-S10 in subjects with inherited epidermolysis bullosa (EB). Subjects were enrolled in 26 countries from April 2017 through June 2020. The study enrolled subjects with junctional EB (JEB), dystrophic EB (DEB), or Kindler syndrome. Subjects were to have an EB target wound of 10 to 50 cm2 aged at least 21 days but less than 9 months with the wound located outside the anogenital region. Subjects with EB simplex (EBS) and target wounds older than 9 months were initially eligible for the study, but EBS subjects and target wounds older than 9 months were excluded after the April 20, 2018 amendment. When the study began recruitment it enrolled subjects 4 years of age and older, with the plan to enroll younger subjects if deemed acceptable by the Data Monitoring Committee (DMC) following a safety review. After reviewing the data in February 2019, the DMC recommended allowing subjects younger than 4 years of age to enroll. The youngest enrolled subject was 6 months old.

Subjects applied Oleogel-S10 or control gel to the target wound and all body areas affected by partial thickness EB wounds every 1 to 4 days with dressing changes for 90 days. Wound areas were to be covered with a standard of care non-adhesive wound dressing. Subjects were to attend site visits at screening/baseline and Days 14, 30, 45, 60, and 90. The primary timepoint for efficacy assessment was Day 45. Following the double-blind treatment period, subjects could enter the 24-month open-label period where all subjects were to be treated with Oleogel-S10 for 24 months. During the 24-month open-label period, subjects were to attend site visits at Months 3, 12, and 24.

The initially planned sample size was 192 subjects randomized 1:1 to Oleogel-S10 or control gel. Randomization was stratified by EB subtype (EBS, JEB/Kindler, DEB) and target wound size (10 to <20 cm2, 20 to <30 cm2, 30 to 50 cm2). The EBS stratum was dropped after the protocol was modified to no longer enroll EBS subjects.

An unblinded interim analysis was planned when approximately 50% of subjects completed Day 45 assessments. The interim analysis included a sample size re-estimation using conditional power to either stop for futility, to increase the sample size, or to maintain the initially planned sample size. The sample size re-estimation procedure allowed for the initial target sample size to increase from 192 subjects to a maximum of 338 enrolled subjects. At the interim analysis, the DMC recommended increasing the target sample size to 250 subjects.

Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment

Epidermolysis bullosa (EB) is a rare, heterogeneous group of genetic skin fragility disorders characterized by blistering and erosions of the skin in response to minor trauma or friction. EB is primarily a pediatric disease due to the hereditary nature, and the usual onset occurs at birth or in early childhood. The subtypes of EB are due to defects or absence of specific skin proteins resulting in skin and mucosal surface fragility. Blisters occur upon pressure and trauma, and may be local or general. Mucosal and/or organ involvement, wound superinfection, and life- threatening infections and complications can result in significant morbidity or mortality. This group of genetic immunobullous diseases has no cure. Treatment is primarily supportive with few available treatments; there is an unmet medical need for approved therapy.

Effectiveness

The Applicant provided data of effectiveness from one pivotal trial, NCT03068780 (BEB-13), that evaluated birch triterpenes to control gel in subjects age 4 months and older with different types of EB. For the primary endpoint on Day 45, the overall measured efficacy of birch triterpenes compared to control was 41% to 29% respectively. Efficacy was driven by subjects in the recessive DEB (RDEB) subset. By Day 90, the difference between the arms in the proportion of subjects with their first wound closure was reduced to 51% vs. 44%. The prespecified and exploratory secondary endpoints provided limited support for substantial benefit with Filsuvez.

From Day 0 to Day 90, the trajectory of treatment effects for Filsuvez and control gel appear similar for total body wound burden, body surface area percentage, itch, procedural pain and background pain, suggesting that there were no meaningful differences between treatment arms. While the primary endpoint for Study BEB-13 was met, there are insufficient supporting data to support the conclusion that Filsuvez treatment reliably resulted in accelerated wound healing or reduced total wound burden.

The applicant has submitted one study in subjects with EB that met the statistical significance criteria for the primary endpoint at α=0.05 demonstrating efficacy, but none of the secondary endpoints provided supportive evidence of efficacy. Because the study results from Study BEB-13 were not overwhelmingly convincing and none of the supportive studies were adequate, additional confirmatory evidence is needed to provide substantial evidence of efficacy for Filsuvez in the treatment of EB.
