4.1 Mechanism of Action Midostaurin is a multikinase inhibitor. Based on the reported IC50 being <100 nM and demonstration of inhibitory activity in cellular assays in vitro, Dr. Simpson identified the Cross Discipline Team Leader Review NDA 207997 Rydapt (midostaurin)

CGP62221 and CGP52421, the two major metabolites of midostaurin, also inhibit multiple kinases, although the potency of CGP52421 is about 10-fold lower than that of midostaurin.

4 Pharmacology Midostaurin was primarily developed and characterized as an inhibitor of the oncogenic mutant and wild-type forms of FLT3 and KIT receptor tyrosine kinases, a primary pharmacology that is useful in treating cancers that are driven by constitutively activated receptors. At the same time, due to the conserved arrangement of secondary structure elements that make up kinase proteins in general, midostaurin inhibits numerous other kinases which have been implicated in drug-related toxicities. Screens for secondary pharmacology revealed midostaurin also inhibits cycolooxygenase-1 (COX-1) and phosphodiesterase 4D (PDE4D). Safety pharmacology studies revealed high doses of midostaurin can result in reduced heart rates, mean arterial pressures, and respiration.

Of note, the canonical pathway of signal transduction mediated by FLT3 or KIT includes the receptor auto-phosphorylation after ligand stimulation followed by phosphorylation of secondary substrates like STAT proteins and MAPK (Mitogen-activated protein kinase) pathway activation. Clinical safety

ͺǤͳǤ Safety Review Approach

Safety Populations The primary safety evaluation consists of an integrated analysis of all 142 patients with AdSM who received at least one dose of midostaurin on Study D2201 or Study A2213. All patients had a planned doseͲschedule of 100 mg po BID, dosed continuously until PD or unacceptable toxicity. The safety analysis considered allͲcausality AEs reported during treatment and up to 28 days after the last midostaurin dose, with the exception of preferred terms (PTs) that directly described the underlying malignancy (e.g. disease progression, mastocytosis, AML).

For supportive safety data, the reviewer pooled 85 patients with other hematologic malignancies treated with midostaurin monotherapy, with a planned dose that equaled or approximated the recommended starting dose for AdSM. These trials, listed in Section 5.1 (Table 2), evaluated a planned doseͲschedule of 100 mg BID, its pediatric equivalent (60 mg/m2 BID), or 75 mg TID. Including the latter doseͲschedule is justified because of the similar steadyͲ state midostaurin exposures with 75 mg TID and 100 mg BID dosing (Source: SCS Section 1.3.2). Due to significant differences in exposure, safety analyses in AdSM and in other hematologic malignancies are presented separately.

Approach to AE Analysis The safety analysis considered allͲcausality AEs reported during treatment and up to 28 days after the last midostaurin dose. The analysis did not consider reported attributions because the studies were singleͲarm and midostaurin is an NME. Patients were censored for AE reporting at the time of PD. With the exception of onͲtreatment deaths, the primary safety analysis is based on originally submitted databases, supplemented by responses to IRs.

With the exception of laboratory values, the Sponsor did not collect baseline grade. Therefore, all nonͲlaboratory events, as well as postͲtreatment laboratory abnormalities having an unknown baseline grade, are assumed to be treatmentͲemergent. Notably, the Sponsor also did not collect grade 5 events, such that fatal AEs had a designated maximum grade of 4 and in some cases a grade of 3 (see Section 8.2.3). Accordingly, AEs reported as “grade 3Ͳ4” are here termed “grade ш 3”.

Because laboratory abnormalities were consistently underrepresented in the AE datasets, as compared to the laboratory datasets, the AE tables exclude laboratory terms. TreatmentͲ emergent laboratory abnormalities are instead presented using the dedicated laboratory datasets.

Benefit-risk

1.1 Recommendation on Regulatory Action

This reviewer recommends regular approval of Rydapt (midostaurin) for the following indications:

• For the treatment of adult patients with aggressive systemic mastocytosis (ASM), systemic mastocytosis with associated hematological neoplasm (SM-AHN), or mast cell leukemia (MCL).

• In combination with standard cytarabine and daunorubicin induction and cytarabine consolidation chemotherapy, for the treatment of adult patients with newly diagnosed acute myeloid leukemia (AML) who are FLT3 mutation-positive, as detected by a FDA approved test.

Limitations of Use - Rydapt is not indicated as a single agent therapy for the treatment of patients with clinically overt AML.

A postmarketing requirement for assessment of risks for fetal harm is also recommended.
