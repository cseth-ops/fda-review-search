Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Where appropriate, effect margins are reported relative to the clinical Cmax under three different conditions: following a single dose of 85 mg, following a single dose of 170 mg, or following repeated dosing (i.e., at steady state) at a dose of 170 mg/day.41 As tradipitant is intended for the prevention of vomiting induced by motion, an individual may take a single dose or otherwise use the drug so infrequently that PK parameters from a single dose are most relevant. Alternatively, an individual may use tradipitant frequently, or many days in a row (e.g., daily on a week-long cruise, daily during commute to and from work), such that steady-state PK parameters are most relevant. Thus, multiple safety/effect margins are reported to best represent the wide range of potential scenarios with tradipitant under the proposed conditions of use. While safety margins are reported relative to AUC, when possible, other effect or safety margins in this section are reported relative to HED (assuming a 60 kg adult) at the maximum clinical dose of 170 mg/day, due to the limitations of the nonclinical and clinical pharmacokinetic data with tradipitant and its major human metabolites. The review team notes that tradipitant margins based on HED are likely overestimating safety, based on the known pharmacokinetic properties (i.e., nonlinear exposure such that large increases in dose level result in marginal changes in AUC). Therefore, all HED-based margins should be interpreted with caution.

Special Toxicology The Applicant conducted two special toxicology studies using a “four-organ” MPS comprised of human hepatocytes, cardiomyocytes, proximal tubular epithelial cells, and cortical neurons, with the objective of prospectively evaluating the safety of tradipitant in a human-based system. In the first study, the Applicant stated that no effects of tradipitant were observed on selected biomarkers of function52 for any of the four cell types following 7 days of treatment when administered once per day at concentrations of 3 or 100μM. These concentrations were determined to result in cellular exposure to tradipitant at 500 and 3,500 ng/mL, respectively (approximately 5.9/41-, 4.5/31-, and 1.5/11-fold the clinical Cmax in healthy volunteers following administration of a single dose of 85 mg, a single dose of 170 mg,42 and 170 mg/day at steady state,43 respectively). Importantly, limitations of the assay precluded accurate interpretation of the study findings (see below). In the second study, evidence of potential toxicity with tradipitant was observed with select endpoints under select conditions at select timepoints (up to 14 days) in hepatocytes (substantial cell detachment), cardiomyocytes (increased troponin-I, decreased cardiac force, increased field potential duration), renal proximal tubular epithelial cells (increased kidney injury molecule-1 [KIM-1] release), and cortical neurons (increased firing rate). Carcinogenicity

The carcinogenic potential of tradipitant was evaluated in a 26-week study in CB6F1-TgN (RasH2) transgenic mice and a 104-week study in Han Wistar rats. In transgenic mice, no tumors were attributed to treatment with tradipitant at doses up to 1500 mg/kg/day. In male and female

rats, the incidence of thyroid follicular cell adenomas was significantly higher compared to controls at the mid and high doses (30 and 150 mg/kg/day or 1.7- and 8.5-fold the maximum clinical dose, respectively, based on HED; less than the exposure to tradipitant in males and 2.8- to 3.4-fold the exposure to tradipitant in females at the MRHD, based on AUC). Combined incidences of thyroid follicular cell adenoma and carcinoma were significantly higher compared to controls in male rats at the high dose of 150 mg/kg/day only and in female rats at ≥30 mg/kg/day.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

No effects on fertility, maternal health, or embryo-fetal development were observed in rats treated with tradipitant during the periods of mating and organogenesis at doses up to 1000 mg/kg/day (57-fold the maximum clinical dose, based on HED; 2.2-fold based on AUC). No effects on maternal health or embryo-fetal development were observed in rabbits treated with tradipitant during the period of organogenesis at doses up to 1000 mg/kg/day (114-fold the maximum clinical dose; 1.4-fold based on AUC). In rats treated with tradipitant during gestation and lactation, no maternal toxicity or developmental effects on the offspring were observed at doses up to 1000 mg/kg/day (57-fold the maximum clinical dose; 2.2-fold based on AUC). In a juvenile animal study in Han Wistar rats, tradipitant was orally administered at doses of 0 (vehicle), 15, 50, or 150 mg/kg/day for 11 weeks, from Postnatal Day 10 to 90. No juvenile- specific toxicities were observed. Dose-dependent increases in liver, thyroid, and parathyroid weights were observed compared to the controls. Increased liver weights correlated with elevated liver enzymes and microscopic finding of hepatocellular hypertrophy, while increased thyroid weights correlated with hypertrophy of the thyroid follicular cells. Consistent with the findings in adult animals, changes observed in the liver and thyroid were considered secondary to metabolic enzyme induction.
