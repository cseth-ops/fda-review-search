Conduct a worldwide descriptive study that collects prospective and retrospective data in women exposed to Tryvio (aprocitentan) during pregnancy and/or lactation to assess risk of pregnancy and maternal complications, adverse effects on the developing fetus and neonate, and adverse effects on the infant. Carcinogenicity

The carcinogenicity assessment of aprocitentan was based on carcinogenicity studies conducted with another ERA, macitentan, for which its major metabolite is aprocitentan. No carcinogenicity studies were conducted with aprocitentan alone. Two-year carcinogenicity studies with macitentan did not identify any carcinogenic potential at doses up to 100 mg/kg/day and 250 mg/kg/day in mice and rats, where aprocitentan exposures represented approximately 30-fold and 11-fold, respectively, the clinical aprocitentan exposure at the MRHD based on AUC.

7.2. Potential Risks or Safety Concerns Based on Drug Class or Other Drug-Specific Factors Aprocitentan is an ERA. Risks described in labeling for approved ERAs include embryo-fetal toxicity, hepatotoxicity, pulmonary edema with pulmonary veno-occlusive disease, decreased sperm counts, decreases in hemoglobin and hematocrit, and fluid retention. To date, all ERAs have been approved with a risk evaluation and mitigation strategy (REMS) for embryo-fetal toxicity, and two of the ERAs, bosentan and sparsentan, also include risk mitigation for hepato- toxicity in the REMS.

7.3. Potential Risks or Safety Concerns Identified Through Postmarket Experience Aprocitentan is neither approved nor marketed in the United States or worldwide.

7.4. FDA Approach to the Safety Review The safety review included a review of data quality, as well as adverse events (AEs), laboratory data, and vital sign datasets. AEs were analyzed by the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) [version 24.1] preferred term and by pooling similar AEs (referred to as the MedDRA SMQ or Food and Drug Administration Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities query [FMQ]). The review team did not identify any major data quality or integrity issues that raised concern. No major issues were identified with respect to recording, coding, and categorizing AEs, and the translation of verbatim terms to MedDRA preferred terms for the AEs reported in PRECISION was found to be acceptable. The FDA safety review focused on the data collected in the PRECISION study. PRECISION included three treatment periods (Part 1, 2, and 3) with differing designs and durations, thus, the three treatment periods were analyzed separately. Adverse events were collected up through the

safety follow-up period, which started on the day after the last dose of study treatment and ended with a safety follow-up visit 30–33 days later. Adverse events of special interest (AESIs) included treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) suggestive of anemia, edema/fluid retention, decompensation of heart failure, and hepatic injury. Since the target population has multiple cardio-cerebrovascular risk factors, an additional safety topic of interest included treatment-emergent major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), defined as cardiovascular death, nonfatal myocardial infarction, or nonfatal stroke, and MACE-plus, defined as hospitalization for heart failure, which were adjudicated by an independent central adjudication committee (CAC). Resistant hypertension is defined as blood pressure that remains above goal despite concurrent use of three antihypertensive agents of different classes (including a diuretic) at maximum recommended (or maximally tolerated) doses. The prevalence of true resistant hypertension is not known, and many patients who meet the definition of resistant hypertension have been found to have poor adherence or inadequate treatment regimens.
