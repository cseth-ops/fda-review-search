Safety requirement, 505(o)(3) · approved 2024 · Draft Protocol Submission 08/2024 · Final Protocol Submission 01/2025

Conduct a prospective observational study of sufficient sample size to evaluate the incidence of and characterize anaphylactic reactions to Lumisight in adults with breast cancer. Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

5.1. Executive Summary

This NDA can be approved from a nonclinical perspective. The Applicant conducted nonclinical studies that support the marketing authorization of the optical imaging agent, LUMISIGHT (pegulicianine, LUM015), at the proposed clinical dose of 1 mg/kg for fluorescence imaging in adults with breast cancer. The recommendation to approve the NDA is based on nonclinical findings that established specificity, proof-of-concept, adequate margins of safety (15-fold safety margin for extended single-dose toxicity studies in rats and 4.8-fold safety margin for repeat-dose toxicity studies in rabbits; margins were 52.7-fold and 11.6-fold by area under the curve [AUC] in rats and rabbits, respectively), and absence of important toxicological findings. The pivotal studies include proof-

of-concept studies in mice and dogs, single and repeat dose general toxicity studies with safety pharmacology and toxicokinetic (TK) endpoints (rats, rabbits, and dogs), and in vitro and in vivo genotoxicity studies.

In Vitro and In Vivo Pharmacology Studies

Proof-of-concept studies were conducted in mice and dogs primarily to demonstrate proteolytic activation of the probe and tumor visualization in xenograft tumor models (mice) and naturally occurring tumors (dogs).

Safety pharmacology

There were no significant safety findings identified for pegulicianine for any of the safety pharmacology parameters reported in neurobehavioral (functional observational battery [FOB]) assessments conducted as part of an expanded acute intravenous (IV) toxicity study in Sprague Dawley rats (up to 93 mg/kg), cardiovascular, electrocardiogram (ECG) and respiratory assessments conducted in a 7-day IV toxicity study in Beagle dogs (up to 0.5 mg/kg), and heart rate and body temperature evaluations conducted in a 7-day IV toxicity study in New Zealand White rabbits (up to 15 mg/kg). Signs of hypoactivity, ataxia, and prostration were observed in the single-dose rat study in males treated with a pegulicianine-derived fragment containing the QSY21 non-fluorescent quencher (referred to as Fragment 1) at dose level of 11.24 mg/kg.

Pharmacokinetics/Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion

The plasma pharmacokinetics (PKs) of pegulicianine was determined in good laboratory practice (GLP) toxicology studies conducted in rats, dogs, and rabbits. There were no important sex differences in systemic exposure by AUC0-24 for pegulicianine and Fragment 1 in Sprague Dawley rats. There were no apparent sex differences in systemic exposure for pegulicianine in New Zealand white rabbits and no important accumulation of pegulicianine following daily IV administration for 7 days.

Single- and Repeat-Dose Toxicology

Extended single-dose and repeat-dose toxicity and TK studies of pegulicianine were conducted in Sprague Dawley rats, New Zealand White rabbits, and Beagle dogs. There was blue discoloration of tissues (injection site and lung) and urine that was related to the color of pegulicianine and Fragment 1 and not considered adverse. Based on the findings of the rat study, the administered high dose of 93 mg/kg was determined to be the no-observed-adverse- effect level (NOAEL) with a 15-fold safety margin (by body surface area scaling) based on the proposed clinical dose of 1 mg/kg. There was also blue discoloration observed in repeat-dose studies in New Zealand White rabbits and Beagle dogs that was related to the dark blue color of the test article. Beagle dogs demonstrated dose-limiting clinical observations of hypersensitization (swelling, hypoactivity, salivation) at 1 and 10 mg/kg/day. Based on the

findings of repeat-dose toxicity studies, the NOAELs were 0.5 mg/kg/day and 15 mg/kg/day, respectively in dogs and rabbits. The safety margin based on the repeat-dose rabbit study was 4.8-fold (by body surface area scaling) based on the proposed clinical dose of 1 mg/kg. An adequate safety margin was not demonstrated in the repeat-dose toxicity study in dogs primarily due to important dose-related hypersensitivity reactions which included facial swelling, swelling of the ears and limbs, hypoactivity, and salivation. At the proposed clinical dose of 1 mg/kg, the dose margin was 0.28-fold by body surface area scaling. Signs of hypoactivity, ataxia, and prostration were observed in male rats following intravenous administration of Fragment 1 at 11.24 mg/kg only, and not pegulicianine, which the Applicant attributed to the infusion rate. General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Toxicological evaluation of pegulicianine and/or Fragment 1 (containing QSY21 non-fluorescent quencher) was conducted in mice, rats, rabbits, and dogs. The clinically relevant route of exposure (IV injection) was used for all in vivo toxicology studies which included acute single- dose (rats and rabbits) and repeat-dose toxicology (rabbits and dogs), and genotoxicity studies (in vivo micronucleus assessment of Fragment 1 in mice). An expanded single-dose IV (slow bolus) toxicity study with 14-day recovery and TK assessment was conducted with pegulicianine and Fragment 1 at up to 93 mg/kg and 11.24 mg/kg, respectively, in rats (Study No. 51103-11- 620); a non-GLP single-dose IV toxicity study with 10 mg/kg pegulicianine was also conducted in rabbits (Study No. 51103-14-167). Additional 7-day and 8-day repeat-dose toxicity studies with TK assessment were conducted with pegulicianine at 15 mg/kg in rabbits (Study No. 51103-15- 205) and up to 10 mg/kg in dogs (Study No. 2330-010-001), respectively.

from review page 44 onward · excerpt

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

No development and reproductive toxicology studies for pegulicianine were conducted or identified in the peer-reviewed scientific literature. The Applicant requested to omit the conduct of development and reproductive toxicology studies of pegulicianine. The Agency concluded that this request was justified because pegulicianine is an optical imaging agent with a short half-life (5 to 7 hours) that will be administered as a single or infrequent dose to patients with breast cancer (of whom 70 – 80% are past childbearing age) undergoing tumor resection. Moreover, the Agency considered that there would be comparatively minimal risk to reproductive health of the patient and/or the developing fetus compared to the known pregnancy risks and embryo-fetal toxicity associated with certain adjuvant therapy. The waiver request was granted based on the proposed single-use indication, intended clinical population, and totality of nonclinical data that suggested pegulicianine would pose no risk to the developing fetus.

Clinical efficacy

8.1.Review of Relevant Individual Trials Used to Support Efficacy

8.1.1. CL0007

8.1.1.1. Study Design

Overview and Objectives

CL0007 was a prospective, multicenter study to evaluate the safety and efficacy of the optical imaging agent LUMISIGHT used with the Lumicell DVS to identify and guide the removal of residual cancer in the lumpectomy cavity of patients with breast cancer. The study used a two- arm, randomized, intra-patient control design to reduce the risk of surgical bias and minimize variability due to study-subject and operating-surgeon factors. Surgeons were unaware of the randomization status for a specific patient until the SoC lumpectomy was completed.

Trial Design

CL0007 enrolled adults who had primary invasive breast cancer with or without DCIS or had DCIS only. All patients were required to be scheduled for a lumpectomy whereas, neoadjuvant therapy (chemotherapy, hormonal therapy, or radiotherapy) was not permitted. Patients scheduled for bilateral breast cancer resection, follow up surgery for positive margins from a prior lumpectomy, or a breast cancer surgery involving frozen section analysis were excluded. Patients with a history of ipsilateral reduction mammoplasty within 2 years or any history of ipsilateral breast cancer surgery, reconstruction, or implant were also excluded. Use of any dye for sentinel lymph node mapping prior to imaging with the Lumicell DVS was prohibited due to potential for interference with LUMISIGHT fluorescence detection. Patients with history of allergic reaction to PEG or any oral or IV contrast agents were also excluded. A study design diagram is shown in Figure 15. Patients were randomized at a ratio of 10:1 between device arm and control arm. This randomization was intended to reduce the risk of surgical bias. The control arm was not planned to be large enough to support meaningful comparison of results between the study arms. All clinical staff were blinded to whether the patient was randomized to the device arm or control arm. All enrolled patients, regardless of

randomization, received 1 mg/kg LUMISIGHT intravenously injected over 3 minutes, 2 to 6 hours prior to surgery. Patients then proceeded to their planned SoC BCS. The BCS consisted of removal of the main specimen, optionally followed by cavity shaves. SoC shaves could be selective, intended to remove specific tissues of concern to the surgeon, or comprehensive, namely applied to all orientations of the lumpectomy cavity that could be safely resected regardless of suspicion for residual malignancy. For this study, the lumpectomy cavity was divided into six orientations, anterior, posterior, medial, lateral, superior, and inferior. After removal of the main specimen and documentation of intent to remove SoC shaves, but before the surgeon took any SoC shaves, the cavity walls were imaged using the Lumicell DVS. The cancer detection algorithm of the Lumicell DVS was not active during this imaging. The resulting images were used to determine a fluorescence signal threshold that varied for each patient and would later be used by the cancer detection algorithm to mark portions of images as potentially containing cancer. Clinical safety

Safety data were collected from 8 completed and ongoing studies of LUMISIGHT in patients with breast cancer, soft tissue sarcoma, prostate cancer, and gastrointestinal cancers. In addition, after data lock for the Integrated Summary of Safety, 1 additional patient experienced an anaphylactic reaction and the Applicant incorporated this event into the NDA. Finally, a cardiovascular safety study enrolling healthy volunteers was included. All of these data sources were reviewed. The Applicant excluded data from 10 patients enrolled in 2 additional studies of brain cancer and peritoneal metastasis, but states that none of these patients reported an AE (AE).

8.2.2. Review of the Safety Database

Overall Exposure

In the submitted safety data, 710 patients with breast cancer, 56 patients with other cancers, and 24 healthy volunteers each received a single dose of LUMISIGHT for a total of 790. LUMISIGHT doses of 0.5 mg/kg (n=32), 1 mg/kg (n=732), 1.5 mg/kg (n=14), 2 mg/kg (n=6), or 4 mg/kg (n=6) were administered by IV injection over 3 minutes.

Patients with any cancer who were intended to receive the to-be-marketed dose of 1 mg/kg compose the primary safety analysis population. This population has 726 patients, including 703 (97%) patients with breast cancer, 15 patients with gastrointestinal cancers, 5 patients with sarcoma, and 3 patients with prostate cancer. Healthy volunteers are excluded because they may have less exposure to the cleavage products of LUMISIGHT.

Relevant Characteristics of the Safety Population

As shown in Table 38, the large majority of patients in the safety population are female, which is appropriate for a breast cancer imaging drug. The age of patients is similarly reasonable for purpose. Fewer patients of Black or African American race or of Hispanic or Latino ethnicity are present than expected based on the demographics of the United States population in general. However, we are not aware of evidence to suggest that race or ethnicity will affect likelihood or severity of AEs.
