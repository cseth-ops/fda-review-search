Pediatric — 4296-1

Pediatric (PREA) · approved 2023

Conduct a 24-week, randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled, parallel group study of the safety, efficacy, pharmacokinetics (PK), and pharmacodynamics (PD) of bexagliflozin for the treatment of type 2 diabetes mellitus in pediatric patients ages 10 to 17 years (inclusive), followed by a 28-week double-blind, controlled extension. The bexagliflozin dose will be determined based on the initial PK/PD lead-in portion of the study.

What the review says about it

Approval will include a Pediatric Research Equity Act (PREA) postmarketing requirement (PMR) issued to assess the safety and efficacy of bexagliflozin in pediatric patients ≥10 to <18 years of age.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

26-Week Oral Gavage Chronic Toxicity and Toxicokinetic Study With Bexagliflozin in Rats With a 4-Week Recovery Phase Key Study Findings • Rats were administered bexagliflozin by oral gavage at doses of 1, 3, 10, or 30 mg/kg/day for 26 weeks • Consistent with the intended pharmacology, marked glucosuria and slightly decreased plasma glucose levels were observed in all bexagliflozin treatment groups. • Chronic progressive nephropathy (CPN), particularly in the male treatment groups, was observed at ≥3 mg/kg. The severity of this finding remained increased in the high dose recovery males (slight/moderate). CPN is a common background finding in aging male Sprague Dawley rats and is generally not considered to be relevant to human risk. • A low incidence and severity (minimal to slight) of glandular stomach erosion/ulcer was observed in all male treatment groups, not dose-related, at Week 26 or after 4 weeks of recovery, and in high dose females at Week 26. Similar stomach findings have been observed with other sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors. The clinical relevance of stomach erosion is likely limited considering the low incidence and severity and due to the absence of similar findings in other species (monkeys with bexagliflozin and dogs with other drugs in this class), as well as in humans with other drugs in this class. • The no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) was considered 30 mg/kg (54031 and 112292 ng.h/mL, 43- and 89-fold MRHD, respectively).

104-Week Oral Carcinogenicity Study of Bexagliflozin in Sprague-Dawley Rats Male rats were administered bexagliflozin by oral gavage in 1% medium viscosity carboxymethylcellulose at 3, 10, and 30 mg/kg for 507 days (72.4 weeks) and then at 1, 3, and 10 mg/kg for additional 12, 5, and 2 weeks for the low dose, mid dose, and high dose group, respectively. In the male study, dosing was stopped at Week 75, 78, and 85 and the groups were terminated at Week 82, 85, and 88 at the high, mid, and low dose, respectively. Female rats were administered bexagliflozin at 3, 10 and 30 mg/kg for 100 weeks. Treatment-related findings included higher mortality in males, lower body weight (up to -14%) and higher food consumption (up to 26%) in both males and females. Treatment-related exacerbation of CPN and associated secondary findings (widespread vascular mineralization and diffuse hyperplasia of parathyroid gland), observed primarily in males, were considered to be the cause of death. There was no increase in the incidence of preneoplastic findings or tumors in any treatment group at doses up to 30 mg/kg/day. The study in males was conducted at doses that exceeded those recommended by the Executive Carcinogenicity Assessment Committee (ECAC: 2, 7, and 20 mg/kg/day), which were selected based on adverse kidney findings (increased kidney weight and chronic progressive nephropathy) in a 6-month dose ranging study. ECAC and the Division considered the study in males inadequate to assess the risk of carcinogenicity because the duration of exposure was limited. However, based on the absence of preneoplastic findings and tumors in males exposed for up to 88 weeks at 3 times the MRHD and in females exposed for 100 weeks at doses up to 68 times the MRHD, repeating the carcinogenicity study in male rats was not considered warranted.

104-Week Oral Carcinogenicity Study of Bexagliflozin in CD-1 Mice Mice were administered bexagliflozin by oral gavage at 0 (1% medium viscosity carboxymethylcellulose in water), 15, 50, or 150 mg/kg/day for approximately 2 years (males, 104 weeks; females, 97 weeks). The high dose of 150 mg/kg was selected based on AUC ratio and maximum tolerated dose (MTD) in males based on mortality at 120 to 160 times the clinical AUC. ECAC concurred with the Applicant’s dose selection. No treatment-related non-neoplastic

or neoplastic findings were observed at doses up to 150 mg/kg, corresponding to an AUC of 170550 ng.h/mL (135 times the MRHD of 20 mg/day).
