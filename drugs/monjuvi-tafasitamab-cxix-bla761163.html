Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

Tafasitamab (also known as MOR00208, MOR208, and XmAb5574) is a humanized IgG1/2 hybrid Fc-modified monoclonal antibody that targets CD19, a cell surface protein expressed on B cells and follicular dendritic cells of the hematopoietic system and on several B-cell malignancies including DLBCL 1, 2.

Tafasitamab bound to human CD19 antigen with an average dissociation constant (KD) of approximately 0.4 nM. Comparable binding of tafasitamab was observed to CD19-expressing lymphocytes of human, cynomolgus monkey, and rhesus monkey, and low-affinity binding to dog, rabbit, rat, and mouse lymphocytes. Tafasitamab exhibited similar binding affinities to human and monkey Fc gamma receptor (FcyRs). There was no off-target binding of tafasitamab detected in tissue panels from the human and cynomolgus monkey. Tafasitamab bound to mononuclear leukocytes in the majority of tissues examined, lymphocytes in blood, and hematopoietic precursor cells in the bone marrow in both species. Based on these findings, the cynomolgus monkey was chosen as the relevant toxicology species.

The in vitro activity of tafasitamab was assessed in human B-cell lines, as well as primary cells derived from patients with hematological malignancies. Upon binding to CD19, tafasitamab mediates B-cell lysis of tumor cells through apoptosis, mainly antibody dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC), and antibody-dependent cell-mediated phagocytosis (ADCP). Safety pharmacology

No stand-alone safety pharmacology studies were conducted. The safety pharmacology parameters were assessed within the pivotal 8-week and 13-week repeat-dose toxicity studies in cynomolgus monkey (XC-9-14408, MOR208P008) and did not reveal any unwanted effects on vital organ functions (cardiovascular, respiratory, central nervous system) at doses up to 100 mg/kg.

The neurobehavioral assessments in the 13-week repeat-dose toxicology study in monkeys revealed severe tremors of the whole body observed occasionally in one out five females at the high dose during the tafasitamab 60-minute infusion as well as slight tremors for several animals at the control, low-dose and high-dose. The number of animals affected and the number of incidences of tremors were higher at the high-dose. The tremors resolved following the termination of dosing. In addition, statistically significant differences in body temperature

(100-102°F) were observed in males at 100 mg/kg and females at 30 and 100 mg/kg during dosing, that recovered in the recovery period. These data demonstrate tafasitamab-related adverse effects at 100 mg/kg.

ADME/PK

Following i.v. administration in five separate studies in cynomolgus monkey at dose levels from 0.3 – 100 mg/kg, tafasitamab exhibited a typical 2-compartment PK profile. Tafasitamab showed consistent PK parameters across studies and study groups (volume of distribution at steady state (Vss) 52.6 mL/kg - 124.3 mL/kg, Clearance (CL) 4.31 mL/day/kg - 7.37 mL/day/kg, mean terminal elimination half-life 7.7 days - 14.2 days). Dose linearity was observed between 2-100 mg/kg, and no gender differences were determined. Exposure values (AUC and Cmax) at NOAEL (100mg/kg) were 8-9 fold higher than corresponding values in humans. As expected for a humanized monoclonal antibody in non-human primates, the development of anti-drug antibodies (ADA) was observed, which was associated with increased tafasitamab clearance but not with any other safety relevant laboratory or clinical findings. An additional exploratory PK study in mice was performed confirming the 2-compartment PK profile and demonstrating PK parameters as expected for humanized therapeutic antibodies in mice without TMDD.

General toxicology; additional studies Tafasitamab was also assessed in three non-GLP single-dose studies (study #XC9-05707, #XC9- 07907, #XC0-08007), and a GLP 8-week repeat-dose study (XC9-14408), each in cynomolgus monkeys. The results are summarized below. In general, findings were comparable to those observed in the 13-week toxicology study and expected based on the pharmacology of the product. See additional information below related to the 8-week toxicology, Study XC9-14408:

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The toxicological assessment was performed in cynomolgus monkeys and included pivotal 8-week (XC-9-14408)and 13-week (MOR208P008) repeat-dose IV infusion toxicity studies. In summary, tafasitamab was well tolerated after weekly IV administration to cynomolgus monkeys at doses up to 100 mg/kg for 13 weeks, with no target organ toxicities or mortalities. Administration of tafasitamab was not associated with electrocardiographic evidence of cardiotoxicity, arrhythmogenesis, or effects on blood pressure when evaluated as part of the pivotal toxicity studies. Importantly, no evidence of an increased susceptibility to infections was noted after tafasitamab administration to cynomolgus monkeys for 13 weeks. The B-cell clearance observed in these pivotal toxicity studies represents the desired pharmacologic mechanism of action for the proposed therapeutic intervention in B-cell malignancies.

In general, we agree with the Applicant’s assessment; see additional information provided below for the 13-week and 8-week repeat-dose studies.

Genetic toxicology

No studies were conducted to evaluate the genotoxicity of tafasitamab. According to ICH guideline S6(R1), the range and type of genotoxicity studies routinely conducted for pharmaceuticals are not applicable to biotechnology-derived pharmaceuticals and therefore are not needed for tafasitamab. In addition, it is not expected that tafasitamab would interact directly with DNA or other chromosomal material.

The FDA concurs with the Applicant.

Carcinogenicity studies in animals were not conducted with tafasitamab. Reproductive and developmental toxicology

In accordance with ICH S9, no embryofetal development or (enhanced) pre- and postnatal development studies were conducted, as the drug is given in the intended patient population in combination with a drug known to be teratogenic or causing embryo-fetal lethality (lenalidomide). The approach was agreed upon with FDA. Potential effects of tafasitamab on male and female fertility were examined by standard histopathology evaluation of the reproductive tract and by menstrual cycle assessment in females during the pivotal 13-week, repeat-dose, toxicity study in sexually mature cynomolgus monkeys (MOR208P008); no drug-related changes were observed.

The FDA concurs with the Applicant that no embryo-fetal development (EFD) studies are needed when tafasitamab is given in combination with lenalidomide, a known teratogen and embryo-fetal toxicant.

To address the potential effects of tafasitamab on embryo-fetal development, the Applicant submitted an assessment for reproductive and developmental risk using a weight-of-evidence approach and with the Oncology Pharmaceuticals: Reproductive Toxicity Testing and Labeling Recommendations Guidance for Industry. The relevant information is summarized below and supports that the main reproductive risk for tafasitamab is B-cell depletion in neonates.

The WOE risk assessment include the following information; references cited were provided by the Applicant: 1) As CD19 expression is mainly restricted to cells of the B-cell lineage, and dendritic cells, the main reproductive risk for tafasitamab is immune related. While the impact of tafasitamab on fetal development is unknown; data in CD19 knockout mice and SCID mouse deficient in T and B cells, suggest that CD19 is not essential for developmental processes during embryofetal development 7,8,9,. Tafasitamab is an IgG monoclonal antibody, and as a class, IgG monoclonal antibodies have the potential to cross the placental barrier permitting direct fetal exposure. Based on its mechanism of action and pharmacodynamic effects seen in animals, tafasitamab may cause depletion of B cells and impair the immune response after birth due to fetal exposure to tafasitamab. 2) As described in toxicology studies in monkeys, the main toxicities of tafasitamab treatment were to the immune system with no evidence of off-target toxicity, including effects to the male and female reproductive organs. 3) The dosing regimen for the intended patient population (relapsed or refractory diffuse large B-cell lymphoma) consists of the combination of tafasitamab and lenalidomide. Lenalidomide is a teratogenic drug.

Clinical efficacy

Data supporting the overall efficacy of 12 mg/kg IV tafasitamab in combination with lenalidomide (LEN) in patients with relapsed or refractory (R/R) diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) are based on 3 studies, MOR208C203 (L-MIND), MOR208C206 (RE-MIND), and MOR208C201 (Table 10). MOR208C203 studied the efficacy of tafasitamab in the intended regimen (in combination with LEN), the proposed dosing regimen, the proposed indication (adult patients with R/R DLBCL, including DLBCL arising from low grade lymphoma, and who are (b) (4) not eligible for, autologous stem cell transplant) and utilizes the intended commercial drug formulation. The contribution of each of the single agents in the combination was determined via 1) MOR208C201, which demonstrated the activity of tafasitamab monotherapy in DLBCL patients; and 2) MOR208C206, in which the efficacy of LEN monotherapy was statistically assessed in a matching observational cohort to the combination in MOR208C203 study
