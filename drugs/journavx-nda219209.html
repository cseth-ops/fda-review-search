Safety pharmacology

SUZ and M6-SUZ were evaluated in good laboratory practice (GLP) in vitro human ether-à-go- go-related gene (hERG) assays, and they had no effect on hERG channel activity (IC50 greater than 10µM). SUZ was tested for effects on the central nervous system (CNS), cardiovascular system, and respiratory system in a GLP single-dose safety pharmacology study and a GLP 28-day repeat- dose toxicity study in monkeys. SUZ and M6-SUZ had no effects on CNS, cardiovascular, and

respiratory parameters at doses up to 100 mg/kg. Of note, M6-SUZ, which is a major human metabolite, is also a major metabolite in monkeys. Based on toxicokinetic data of this metabolite in the 28-day repeat-dose toxicity study in monkeys, there are approximately ≥2× safety margin for the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of suzetrigine (i.e., 100 mg loading dose and 50 mg maintenance dose every 12 hours).

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Systemic safety of SUZ and M6-SUZ were evaluated in 28-day repeat-dose toxicity studies in rats and monkeys. In addition, the safety of SUZ and impurities/degradants in the drug substance/drug product were evaluated in 91-day repeat-dose toxicity studies in rats and monkeys. In the 28-day rat study, weights of the liver and ovaries increased in the high-dose groups. However, there were no associated histopathological changes in the liver or ovaries. In the 28- day monkey study, increased incidences of soft/watery feces were observed in treated groups. The mean organ weight of the thyroid/parathyroid was increased in females without statistical significance. The no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) was determined to be 1,000 mg/kg/day (male) and 300 mg/kg/day (female) for rats and 100 mg/kg/day for both sexes in monkeys.

In a 91-day rat study, males were dosed at 0, 200, 600, and 1,000 mg/kg/day and females at 0, 10, 100, and 1,000 mg/kg/day via oral gavage. Ploughing, salivation, and wet fur at the mouth were observed in test article-treated animals at ≥100 mg/kg/day. Minimal-to-moderate ovarian follicular cysts were observed in female rats treated with the test article at ≥100 mg/kg/day. Higher incidences of minimal-to-mild decreased number of corpora lutea, minimal-to-mild distalis hyperplasia in the pituitary gland, moderate-to-marked dilatation in the mammary gland were observed in females treated at 100 mg/kg compared to the concurrent and historical controls. Minimal-to-mild cortical hypertrophy in the adrenal gland was observed in females treated at 1,000 mg/kg/day, which could be a result of stress responses. The NOAELs for males and females were 1,000 mg/kg/day and 10 mg/kg/day, respectively. In a 13-week monkey study, animals were treated with SUZ at 0, 20, 50, and 150 mg/kg via oral gavage. Of note, no recovery group was included in this study. Test article-treated animals showed increased soft/liquid feces and decreased thymus weight, which is associated with decreased thymic cellularity, which could be related to stress responses. In addition, reduced weights of testis, epididymis, and prostate gland were observed in SUZ treated males; however, there were no adverse microscopic findings while all animals including the control group showed immaturity in the testis. There were no test article-related changes in body weights, ophthalmoscopy/electrocardiogram (ECG)/ hematology/clinical chemistry/urinalysis parameters, and thyroid biomarkers. The NOAEL for both sexes was determined to be 150 mg/kg.

Genetic toxicology

SUZ was evaluated for genotoxicity potential in a full battery of genotoxicity studies, including an in vitro Ames assay, an in vitro micronucleus assay, and in vivo micronucleus assay in rats. SUZ was not considered mutagenic or clastogenic in these assays. M6-SUZ (a major metabolite in human and monkeys) was also evaluated for genotoxicity potential. An Ames study that tested M6-SUZ demonstrated the metabolite was not mutagenic. An in vitro chromosomal aberration test was not conducted with M6-SUZ. However, the chromosomal aberration potential of M6-SUZ was assessed through the in vivo micronucleus assay that tested the parent SUZ, which was negative for genotoxicity. Although M6-SUZ is a

minor metabolite in rats, the systemic exposure levels of M6-SUZ achieved in rats after SUZ oral administration in this study exceeded the steady-state levels in humans at the maximum recommended daily dose. Together with the negative Ames result of M6-SUZ, negative genetic toxicology battery of SUZ, and in silico quantitative structure-activity relationship (QSAR) data with M6-SUZ, the weight of evidence supported minimal concern for clastogenicity.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Reproductive and developmental toxicities of SUZ were assessed in male and female fertility and early embryonic development studies in rats, embryo-fetal development (EFD) studies in rats/rabbits, and a pre- and postnatal development (PPND) study in rats. SUZ doses up to 1000 mg/kg did not affect male reproductive performance when administered at least 28 days before mating and throughout mating. In a female fertility study in rats, increased preimplantation loss was observed at oral SUZ doses of 15 mg/kg when administered prior to mating and through gestation day (GD) 7. These effects on fertility in female rats appear to be reversible as pre-implantation loss was not observed when female rats were mated following discontinuation of SUZ for 4 weeks. 7.3. Potential Risks or Safety Concerns Identified Through Postmarket Experience SUZ has not been approved in any country or region for marketing. Therefore, no postmarketing experience is available at the time of this review.

7.4. FDA Approach to the Safety Review The primary sources of safety data are the two controlled phase 3 studies (Studies 104 and 105) and an open-label, uncontrolled phase 3 study (Study 107). Additional data are available from two phase 2 studies (Studies 101 and 102). Studies 101 and 104 were conducted in subjects after bunionectomy, while Studies 102 and 105 were conducted in subjects after abdominoplasty. Study 107 was an open-label evaluation of SUZ in subjects with acute pain resulting from a variety of surgical or nonsurgical conditions. In addition, 13 phase 1 studies evaluated SUZ in healthy subjects and special populations (subjects with hepatic and renal impairment). The Applicant performed analyses for the individual studies and two pooled populations. These pooled populations consisted of the pooled phase 3 controlled studies (Studies 104 and 105) and the pooled phase 2 and 3 controlled studies (Studies 101, 102, 104, and 105). The Applicant states that the latter pool is a supportive data pool. Data from the phase 1 and open-label phase 3 studies were not pooled given significant differences in their study designs and/or populations. The review team did not identify any major data quality or integrity issues that precluded performing a thorough safety review. No major issues were identified with respect to coding and categorizing adverse events. This review focused primarily on comparisons between the treatment arms in the phase 3 controlled studies. The major differences between these two studies were those relating to the acute pain model studied and procedure-specific subject selection criteria and anesthetic techniques. Pooling of the safety data from these studies is acceptable considering the otherwise similar study designs and selection criteria. The use of descriptive analyses of safety was predefined in the protocol for summarizing the safety outcomes. Separate analyses of the individual phase 3 controlled studies are described in Section 17.2 of this review.

Fertility and early embryonic development

In a male fertility study, male rats were treated with 0, 200, 600, or 1,000 mg/kg of SUZ from at least 28 days before mating and throughout mating with untreated female rats, and there were no adverse effects on male reproductive performance or sperm parameters. Thus, the NOAEL for male reproductive toxicity was 1,000 mg/kg. In a female fertility study, female rats were treated with 0, 5, 10, or 15 mg/kg of SUZ from a minimum of 14 days prior to mating, throughout mating with untreated male rats, and continuing until gestational day (GD) 7. There were higher mean preimplantation losses in the 15 mg/kg group. Thus, the NOAEL for female fertility toxicity was 10 mg/kg based on increased preimplantation loss.

Embryo-fetal development

In dose range-finding studies for rat embryo-fetal development (EFD), all pregnant female rats that received ≥25 mg/kg (GDs 6 through 17) had 100% postimplantation loss, which was a basis for the dose selection for the definitive EFD (dEFD) study in rats. In a rat dEFD study, pregnant rats were dosed at 0, 5, 10, and 15 mg/kg of SUZ during GDs 6 through 17. The high-dose group (15 mg/kg) showed higher postimplantation loss and a lower number of live fetuses. However, there was no SUZ-related effect on malformations or variations in the fetuses. The maternal NOAEL was 15 mg/kg and the developmental NOAEL was 10 mg/kg based on increased postimplantation loss and a lower number of live fetuses. In a rabbit dEFD study, pregnant rabbits received 0, 50, 100, and 200 mg/kg of SUZ during GDs 7 through 20. The high-dose group (200 mg/kg) showed body weight loss and lower food consumption during dosing period, lower mean gravid uterine weight, lower adjusted body weight gain, higher postimplantation loss, and lower fetal body weights. There was no SUZ– related effect on malformations or variations in fetuses. The maternal NOAEL was 100 mg/kg/day based on evidence of maternal toxicity, including body weight loss and lower food consumption during treatment period and lower adjusted body weight gain. The developmental NOAEL was 100 mg/kg/day based on postimplantation loss and lower fetal body weights.

Prenatal and postnatal development

In a pre- and postnatal development (PPND) study, pregnant rats received 0, 5, 10, and 15 mg/kg of SUZ once daily via oral gavage from GD 6 to lactation day 20. Two out of 22 F0 females dosed at 10 mg/kg/day (9% dam) and 12 out of 30 F0 females dosed at 15 mg/kg (40% dam) showed fully resorbed litters. F0 females dosed at 15 mg/kg also showed lower body weights (- 36%) during the gestation period (GD 6 to GD 20), lower food consumption during gestation/lactation period, fewer live newborn pups, and a shorter mean gestation length. F1 pups from dams dosed at 10 mg/kg and 15 mg/kg showed a lower viability index from birth to postnatal day 4 (84% and 34% per litter, respectively, versus 98% per litter in the control), lower birth weights (13 to 33%), and lower mean body weights (8 to 20%) on postnatal day 21. F1 pups from dams dosed at 15 mg/kg also showed late pinna detachment (4.6 days) compared to the control (3.2 days). There was no effect on eye opening, pupillary reflex, balanopreputial separation, vaginal opening, auditory startle response, motor activity, and Biel Maze swimming trials in F1 pups. There was no effect on reproductive parameters (precoital interval, mating index, fertility index, or pregnancy index) in the F1 generation. The maternal NOAEL was 10 mg/kg based on lower food consumption during the gestation and lactation periods. The developmental NOAEL was 5 mg/kg based on lower body weight gains during preweaning at 15 mg/kg, total resorptions of litters, shorter gestation length, decreased postnatal survival, and lower pup body weight at 10 mg/kg and 15 mg/kg.
