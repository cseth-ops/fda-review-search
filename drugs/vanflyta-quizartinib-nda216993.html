Executive Summary

Quizartinib (AC220) is a receptor tyrosine kinase (RTK) inhibitor that binds to Fms Related Receptor Tyrosine Kinase 3 (FLT3) and several other RTKs. Kinase inhibition is mediated through competition with the adenosine triphosphate (ATP) binding pocket of the kinase. Quizartinib binding is relatively specific for FLT3 (Kd = 1.3 nM) and KIT (Kd = 4.9 nM), as is the active metabolite AC886, with Kd values of 0.54 nM for FLT3 and 0.97 nM for KIT. In binding assays, quizartinib and AC886 showed approximately 10-fold lower affinity towards the FLT3 internal tandem duplication (FLT3-ITD) mutation (Kd= 9.4 nM and 5.8 nM for quizartinib and AC886, respectively) compared to FLT3 (Kd= 1.3 nM and 0.54 nM for quizartinib and AC886, respectively).

Quizartinib pharmacology was characterized in vitro and/or in vivo in MV4-11 cells and RS4;11 cells. MV4-11 cells are a human leukemia cell line that expresses FLT3 with an internal tandem duplication (ITD) activating mutation. The RS4;11 cell line is a leukemia cell line that expresses wild-type FLT3 and is not dependent on FLT3 activity for proliferation. Western blot analysis showed quizartinib induces concentration-dependent inhibition of autophosphorylation of FLT protein in MV4-11 cells in vitro. Quizartinib inhibited proliferation of cultured MV4-11 cells with an IC50 of 0.3 nM, whereas it had more than 1000-fold weaker activity against the RS4;11 control cell line (IC50 = 990 nM). Using the MV4-11 cell line, quizartinib also displayed in vivo anti-tumor activity in a mouse tumor xenograft model and increased the lifespan of animals.

Quizartinib was tested for inhibitory activity against a panel of 118 non-kinase enzymes, receptors, channels, and transporters. Quizartinib produced greater than 50% inhibition in five of the 118 targets, but was more than 1000-fold less potent against those targets than for FLT3.

Standalone in vitro and in vivo safety pharmacology studies were conducted to assess the cardiovascular risks of quizartinib and AC886, whereas endpoints to assess the effects of quizartinib on the central nervous and respiratory systems were incorporated into repeat-dose general toxicology studies. To assess the cardiovascular risks, in vitro studies examined the effects of quizartinib and its active metabolite on cloned human ether-a-go-go-related gene (hERG) potassium, L-type calcium, hKvLQT1/mink potassium, and early and late sodium channels. dedicated study in telemetered monkeys. Overall, these studies demonstrated quizartinib exposure was associated with inhibition of potassium channel currents, prolongation of QT intervals, and transient increases in blood pressure.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Single-dose and repeated-dose toxicity studies for up to 13 weeks of daily quizartinib administration have been conducted in rats, dogs, and monkeys. At the early phase of development, the dog was used to characterize the toxicity of quizartinib as a nonrodent species. However, the dog showed a unique major circulating metabolite (morpholino oxidation product) that has not been detected in human, rat, and monkey, despite achieving higher exposure of those species than the dog to quizartinib, and may possibly contribute to a dog- specific liver toxicity due to birefringent crystal deposition. Based on pharmacokinetic (PK) and toxicology data, the cynomolgus monkey was selected as a potential alternate nonrodent model for evaluating the toxicity profile of quizartinib to predict human safety risk.

In all animal species studied, the principal target organs of toxicity were the bone marrow and lymphoid organs. In addition, toxicological effects were observed in the kidney, liver, ovary, vagina, and testis. Target-organ toxicity appeared to be dose and time dependent, and most toxicological findings were reversible following a 28-day or 30-day recovery period; however, slight decreases in red blood cell (RBC) and white blood cell (WBC) parameters, minimal bone marrow hypocellularity, and liver crystal deposition (only in dogs) persisted through the post recovery period. In 13-week studies, toxicological findings were present among rats and dogs at 10 mg/kg/day and 15 mg/kg/day, respectively, and these findings included decreases in hematology parameters, increased liver enzymes, and microscopic changes in bone marrow and lymphoid organs. In a 13-week study in monkeys, mortality and adverse clinical signs were reported at 10/6 mg/kg/day, with the findings consistent with those reported in the other species. NOAELs in the 13-week repeated dose toxicity studies were 3 mg/kg/day in rats, 5 mg/kg/day in dogs, and 3 mg/kg/day in monkeys.

Clinical efficacy

The clinical development program of quizartinib in AML subjects consists of 13 clinical studies, 10 of which supported efficacy. Five Phase 1 studies and three Phase 2 studies examining the efficacy of quizartinib at different dose levels in subjects with FLT3-ITD (+) have been completed. Two large pivotal Phase 3 studies were conducted: Study AC220-007 in patients with R/R FLT3-ITD (+) AML and Study AC220-A-U302 in patients with newly diagnosed FLT3-ITD (+) AML.

The efficacy of quizartinib in the treatment of patients with newly diagnosed FLT3-ITD (+) AML is based on the data from a single pivotal Phase 3 study AC220-A-U302 (N = 539) in newly diagnosed AML subjects, with supplemental data from the three Phase 1 studies, 2689-CL-0005 (N = 18; newly diagnosed AML), AC220-A-J102 (N = 7; newly diagnosed AML), and 2689-CL-0011 (N = 13; post-HSCT AML), for the safety and preliminary efficacy of quizartinib in this indication.

In addition to the studies mentioned above, the results from Studies AC220-007, AC220-002, 2689-CL-2004, CP0001, AC220-A-J101, and AC220-A-J201 provide additional supporting data pertinent to the use of quizartinib in patients with AML and are described in the sections below.
