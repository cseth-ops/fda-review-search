Other clinical — 4631-5

Safety requirement, 505(o)(3) · approved 2024 · Draft Protocol Submission 12/2024 · Final Protocol Submission 06/2025 · Trial Completion 12/2030 · Final Report Submission 06/2031

To potentially minimize the serious risks of imetelstat treatment including neutropenia and thrombocytopenia and other serious adverse events and improve tolerability, conduct a randomized trial comparing at least 2 dosages of imetelstat in patients with low or intermediate-1 risk myelodysplastic syndromes (LR-MDS) to evaluate alternative dosing regimens such as different dose levels, dosing frequencies, and duration of treatment. The study should include sufficient clinical pharmacokinetic sampling to analyze the exposure-response (ER) relationships. Update the current population pharmacokinetics (PopPK) and ER analysis with this clinical trial data. The trial should enroll sufficient representation of U.S. racial and ethnic minorities to ensure that the results are reflective of the U.S. population. Additionally, the study should follow subjects for a sufficient time after end of dosing to establish the time required to return to baseline grade for neutrophil and platelet count, in the absence of disease progression or subsequent therapy.

Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

Imetelstat is a 13-mer thio-phosphoramidate oligonucleotide telomerase inhibitor indicated for the treatment of adult patients with low- to intermediate 1 risk myelodysplastic syndromes (MDS) with transfusion-dependent anemia requiring 4 or more red blood cell units over 8 weeks who have not responded to or have lost response to or are ineligible for erythropoiesis- stimulating agents (ESA). It binds to the template region of the RNA component of human telomerase (hTR), which lies in the catalytic site of human telomerase reverse transcriptase (hTERT), resulting in competitive inhibition of hTERT enzymatic activity. The nonclinical development program for imetelstat included various in vitro and in vivo studies to evaluate the pharmacology, pharmacokinetics, general toxicology, reproductive and developmental effects, and the genotoxic potential of imetelstat.

In vitro studies have shown that imetelstat inhibited telomerase activity in the HT-3 human cervical carcinoma cell line in a concentration-dependent manner with an IC50 of 0.056 µM. The in vivo activity of imetelstat was assessed using multiple mouse models. A single or repeated administration of imetelstat in mice resulted in telomerase inhibition in the liver tissues collected for activity analysis. The anti-tumor activity of imetelstat was evaluated in the OVCAR- 5 human ovarian cancer xenograft model. The animals were administered imetelstat intraperitoneally (IP) at 15 or 30 mg/kg three times per week (TIW) for 50 days. The results showed tumor growth inhibition with an average reduction in tumor volume of 34% and 53%, respectively, at 15 and 30 mg/kg. No xenograft studies were conducted in MDS cells lines, and this was described by the Applicant as being due to the lack of MDS specific cell lines and mouse xenograft models.

In the safety pharmacology study conducted in monkeys (Study No. GS04-072), 4 male animals were assigned to a single treatment group. The animals were treated with vehicle control, 5 mg/kg, 10 mg/kg, and 15 mg/kg of imetelstat from Days 1-28 via IV infusion. Each animal received a dose of vehicle control on Day 1 and increasing doses of imetelstat on Days 2, 9, 21 and 28. There were no test article-related changes observed in the cardiovascular, central nervous or respiratory systems. Consistent with these findings, the electrocardiogram (ECG) assessments evaluated in the 9-month toxicology study in monkeys were considered normal and no test article-related changes were observed.

The plasma exposures of imetelstat increased in a dose-dependent manner in mice without showing a sex-difference. In monkeys, increases in plasma exposure were slightly greater than

dose proportional and higher exposures in male compared to female monkeys were observed. Distribution studies demonstrated high (> 90%) plasma protein binding of imetelstat across species (human, monkey, rat, and mouse). The highest concentration of imetelstat was seen in the liver and kidney, followed by the bone marrow and spleen. General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The Applicant’s Position: A comprehensive program of nonclinical toxicology studies was conducted with imetelstat that fulfilled all relevant International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH) guidelines for the development of a drug to treat a life- threating condition, including guidelines S9, S1A, and S4 (ICH S1A, 1995; ICH S4, 1998; ICH S9, 2009). In addition, the imetelstat development program is consistent with the industry standards for the development of oligonucleotide (ODN) drugs (Berman, 2014; Cavagnaro, 2014). Pivotal studies were conducted according to Good Laboratory Practice (GLP) (Organisation for Economic Cooperation and Development Principles of GLP).

The mouse and monkey were used for the single- and repeat-dose toxicity studies, and mouse and rabbit were used for the EFD toxicity studies. These species were selected based on sequence homology to the template region of the RNA component of human telomerase and expected relevant pharmacologic action of imetelstat across species, from human to monkey to rabbit to rodent, as well as species-specific sensitivities to known ODN class effects.

The general toxicities observed in rodents and/or non-rodents with imetelstat dosing were cytopenias (most notably thrombocytopenia), complement activation, and coagulation abnormalities. Complement activation and coagulation abnormalities were related to the plasma concentration of imetelstat and reflect the well-characterized toxicity profile of the related phosphorothioate ODN class. Such effects were most pronounced in the initial toxicity studies that tested very high doses of imetelstat ranging from 5 to 1230 mg/kg in mice and from 5 to 130 mg/kg in monkeys (by bolus IV injection and IV infusion) and demonstrated that the high IV doses (ie, ≥ 73.8 mg/kg) produced excessive imetelstat exposures that were poorly tolerated. As demonstrated in the 9-month toxicity study in monkeys (002-TOX-2008), the degree and potential adverse sequelae of complement activation and coagulation abnormalities can be mitigated by administering imetelstat over a 2-hour IV infusion, even at doses (15 mg/kg/week) that produced exposures (Cmax) in animals that are 6-7 x higher than the Cmax in MDS patients at the proposed clinical dose. Mild to moderate decreases in platelet counts were seen at 10 and 15 mg/kg/week doses in the 9-month toxicity study in monkeys.

Additional imetelstat toxicities observed in the chronic pivotal toxicity studies, ie, the 6-month study in mice (003-TOX-2008) and the 9-month study in monkeys, included microscopic findings in the liver and kidney (described in Applicant Table 5 and Applicant Table 6 below). Importantly, there were no degenerative changes or alterations in clinical pathology parameters indicative of hepatic dysfunction or injury (ie, no substantial elevations in serum

transaminases, bilirubin, or alkaline phosphatase), nor were there any remarkable changes in parameters of kidney function.

Carcinogenicity

The Applicant’s Position: In alignment with ICH S9 guidance, carcinogenicity studies have not been conducted with imetelstat based on its intended clinical use for treatment of transfusion-dependent anemic patients with MDS who have a limited life-expectancy.

The FDA disagrees with the Applicant that no carcinogenicity risk assessment is warranted for the intended patient population, which includes patients with low-risk MDS. The need for carcinogenicity studies depends on the life expectancy of the patients for the proposed indications. FDA asked the Applicant to provide justification for not conducting carcinogenicity studies based on the life expectancy of low-risk MDS. Based on the literature provided by the Applicant and discussion with the clinical team, the life expectancy for patients with lower risk MDS (defined variously, but usually as low or intermediate-1 risk disease by IPSS) and ESA failure or transfusion dependency can be > 5 years and carcinogenicity risk assessment is warranted for the intended patient population. To address the carcinogenicity risk assessment of imetelstat, there will be separate Post Marketing Requirements (PMRs) for carcinogenicity assessments in the mouse and rat species.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

The Applicant’s Position: Fertility, Pre- and Post-Natal Development Consistent with ICH S9 guidelines, standalone fertility, pre- and post-natal development studies were not conducted based on imetelstat’s intended clinical use for treatment of transfusion- dependent anemic patients with MDS with limited life-expectancy. This finding was reversible after a 14-week recovery period. Part 2 (hereafter referred to as MDS3001-Phase 3 study) is a Phase 3 double-blind, randomized study to compare the efficacy of imetelstat with placebo (Applicant Figure 5) and provides the pivotal efficacy and safety data for this NDA submission.
