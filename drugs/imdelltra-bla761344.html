Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

5.1. Executive Summary

Tarlatamab (AMG 757) is a bispecific T cell engager that binds to delta-like ligand 3 (DLL3) and CD3. DLL3 is a Notch ligand that is expressed during fetal development but has limited expression on the cell surface in healthy adult tissues. DLL3 has also been reported to be expressed on the cell surface of some cancers, including small cell lung cancer (SCLC). CD3 is expressed on T cells, and upon simultaneously binding to the surface of DLL3-expressing target cells and CD3 on T cells, tarlatamab promotes T cell activation and T cell-dependent lysis of DLL3-expressing cells, including SCLC cells. The Applicant proposes to use tarlatamab at 1 mg on Day 1, then 10 mg on Days 8, 15, and every 2 weeks thereafter. The proposed indication is for the treatment of adult patients with SCLC with disease progression on or after platinum-based chemotherapy.

As assessed by surface plasmon resonance (SPR), tarlatamab bound human and monkey CD3 with KDs of 14.9 nM and 12 nM, respectively. Tarlatamab bound CHO cells expressing human or monkey DLL3 with KDs of 0.64 nM and 0.5 nM, respectively. Tarlatamab also bound human and monkey T cells at a concentration of 50 ng/mL but did not bind T cells from mice, rats, rabbits, dogs, or minipigs. The cynomolgus monkey was selected for toxicology testing given that tarlatamab similarly bound human and monkey CD3 and DLL3 but did not bind CD3 from other species. However, the monkey may not be pharmacologically relevant considering DLL3 is expressed intracellularly in normal cells and tissues. It is unknown if other conditions or disease states can lead to expression of DLL3 on the surface of some cells.

The ability of tarlatamab to activate T cells and mediate T cell cytotoxicity was evaluated in in vitro assays. In co-cultures with human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and DLL3- expressing human small cell lung cancer cell lines, tarlatamab resulted in upregulation of T cell activation markers and cytokine release, as well as cytotoxicity of DLL3-postive target cells. Tarlatamab mediated T cell lysis of several human SCLC cell lines with EC50s of 32 pM to 161 pM. Additionally, the ability of tarlatamab to mediate cytotoxicity of DLL3-postive target cells positively correlated with levels of DLL3 expressed on the surface of target cells. Tarlatamab also demonstrated ability to activate CD4 and CD8 T cells in co-cultures with DLL3-expressing human cancer cell lines as assessed by increased expression of T cell activation markers, such as CD25 and CD69, and cytotoxicity markers, perforin and granzyme B, as well as release of cytokines, including the proinflammatory cytokines IL-2, IL-6, TNF-α, and IFNγ. Tarlatamab did not mediate cytokine release from human PBMCs when co-cultured with target cells lacking expression of DLL3.

In the presence of dexamethasone, a glucocorticoid used clinically to antagonize severe inflammatory reactions, the ability of tarlatamab to mediate T cell activation and cytotoxicity of DLL3-expressing human SCLC cell lines was reduced approximately 3-fold. Genetic toxicology

The Applicant’s Position:

No studies evaluating the genotoxic or mutagenic potential of tarlatamab have been conducted. Tarlatamab is a recombinant BiTE molecule made up entirely of naturally-occurring amino acids and contains no inorganic or synthetic organic linkers or other non-protein portions. Therefore, no genotoxicity studies have been conducted or are planned, consistent with ICH Harmonised Tripartite Guideline S6, “Preclinical Safety Evaluation of Biotechnology- Derived Pharmaceuticals” (ICH S6[R1], 2011).

The Applicant’s Description:
