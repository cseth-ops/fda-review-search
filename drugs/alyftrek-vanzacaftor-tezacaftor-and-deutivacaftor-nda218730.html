Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application The Applicant has conducted a comprehensive program of pharmacology, pharmacokinetic, and toxicology studies to support clinical development and the NDA submission of VNZ/TEZ/D- IVA. VNZ is the only component of this triple combination (TC) for which a complete nonclinical program was needed. All pivotal toxicology studies of VNZ have been submitted and reviewed previously under IND 142001. The Applicant performed a bridging study between D- IVA and IVA to leverage existing IVA nonclinical data. The Applicant has also provided combination toxicology studies for the new TC. The impurities studies have been submitted and reviewed under this application. The key findings from the nonclinical studies are cited and summarized in this review to support the approval of NDA 218730.

13.1.1. Pharmacology Primary pharmacology studies using human bronchial epithelial (HBE) cells from CF donors demonstrated that VNZ was able to improve CFTR glycosylation which would be expected to improve CFTR trafficking to the membrane. VNZ alone did not greatly improve CFTR function as evaluated by chloride (Cl) transport. However, VNZ in combination with TEZ and IVA greatly enhanced CFTR activity. VNZ did not appear to act as a potentiator for CFTR. Secondary pharmacology studies found that VNZ inhibits human prostanoid EP4 (Ki 0.64 μM), IP (PGI2) (Ki 0.83 μM), prostanoid EP3 (Ki 1.27 μM), prostanoid EP2 (Ki 3.23 μM), rat adrenergic α1A (Ki 3.48 μM), and rat adrenergic α1B (Ki 7.46 μM).

Safety pharmacology

Safety pharmacology studies indicated that VNZ does not affect the CNS or respiratory systems in rats at doses up to 10 mg/kg. In a good laboratory practice (GLP) human ether-a-go-go related gene assay, VNZ inhibited the channel with a half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) of 0.7 μM. The safety pharmacology study evaluating cardiovascular toxicity in dogs did not note any treatment-related effects. Additionally, the 4-week and 39-week repeat-dose toxicology studies in dogs evaluated electrocardiogram (ECG) parameters and did not find any treatment-related effects. Therefore, the results of the human ether-a-go-go related gene assay were not considered clinically meaningful.

Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, Excretion/PK Metabolite profiling using liver microsomes and cryopreserved hepatocytes of human, rat, dog, and monkey origin did not identify any human-specific metabolites. There were also no major metabolites identified in rats, dogs, or monkeys. Cytochrome P450 (CYP)3A4/5 appeared to be the major metabolic enzymes responsible for VNZ metabolism via oxidative metabolism. VNZ does not appear to partition into rat, dog, monkey, or human red blood cells. Plasma binding was high (>99.9% bound) across rats, dogs, monkeys, and humans. Tissue distribution study using

male Sprague-Dawley rats given single oral doses of VNZ (3, 10, and 30 mg/kg), found little drug distribution to the brain, lung, heart, and kidney (tissue to plasma ratios ranged from 0.115- 1.18 and did not increase with dose). Liver to plasma ratios were higher than all other tissues and decreased with increasing dose (17.3, 11.53, and 3.16, respectively).

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Repeat Dose Toxicology Studies In Rats

4-Week Rat Study (VRT-1725010-TX-006) In a GLP study, Sprague Dawley rats (10 animals/sex/group) were administered VNZ by oral gavage once daily at 0 (vehicle), 1, 5, and 20 mg/kg/day in males and at 0, 0.5, 2.5, and 10 mg/kg/day in females for 4 weeks. The sex-specific dosing was due to preliminary studies showing that female rats had twice the exposure of male rats receiving the same dose. There was one low dose female that was euthanized on Day 7 due to shallow, difficult breathing. The cause of death was likely from technical error with the gavage delivery. Hematology noted a dose- dependent decrease in basophil counts in both males and females. Monocyte counts increased in a dose-dependent manner for both sexes. No potential target organs of toxicity were identified. The no observed adverse effect level (NOAELs) were identified as 20 mg/kg (AUC: 418 hr∙μg/mL) for males and 10 mg/kg (AUC: 655 hr∙μg/mL) for females. 26-Week Rat Study (VX-121-TX-007) In a GLP study, Sprague Dawley rats (15 animals/sex/group) were administered VNZ by oral gavage once daily at 0 (vehicle), 5, 12, 25, and 125 mg/kg/day in males and 0, 2.5, 6, 12.5, and 30 mg/kg/day in females for 26 weeks with additional animals included in the control and 125(male)/30(female) mg/kg/day groups for a 6-week recovery period. All male rats administered 125 mg/kg/day VNZ were found dead or euthanized by Day 14 of the study due to declining clinical condition. Most of the animals had erosions or ulcers in the nonglandular or glandular stomach. Four female rats in the main and toxicokinetic groups administered 30 mg/kg/day VNZ were euthanized or found dead on Day 98, 136 (2), and 183. A cause of death was not identified for all of the female rats but several had findings in the stomach. Target organs of toxicity were the liver (hepatocellular vacuolar degeneration for males at 25 and 125 mg/kg/day and multifocal hepatocellular necrosis for females at the 30 mg/kg/day), glandular stomach (erosion(s) and severe ulceration for males at 125 mg/kg/day and females at 30 mg/kg/day), nonglandular stomach (ulceration for males at 125 mg/kg/day, hyperplasia and/or hyperkeratosis in males at 125 mg/kg/day and females at 30 mg/kg/day), adrenal gland (hypertrophy for males ≥25 mg/kg/day and females ≥12.5 mg/kg/day), and decreases in lymphocytes in the spleen, thymus, and lymph nodes for males ≥25 mg/kg/day and females ≥12.5 mg/kg/day. The NOAELs were identified as 12 mg/kg/day (AUC: 390 μg∙hr/mL) for males and 12.5 mg/kg/day (AUC: 1120 μg∙hr/mL) for females due to mortality, reduced body weight, multifocal hepatocellular necrosis, ulcers and/or erosions in the glandular and nonglandular stomach for males at 125 mg/kg/day and females at 30 mg/kg/day, and hepatocellular vacuolar degeneration for males at 25 and 125 mg/kg/day.

Carcinogenicity

In the 6-month carcinogenicity study, transgenic rasH2 mice were administered 0 (vehicle), 3, 10, and 30 mg/kg/day vanzacaftor by oral gavage. The Applicant did have concurrence from the Executive Carcinogenicity Assessment Committee for dose selection. There were no treatment- related effects on mortality rates and body weights. There were no statistically significant treatment-related tumor findings in male or female mice. All animals in the positive control group (urethane) were observed with neoplastic findings. Non-neoplastic target organs of toxicity were kidney (multifocal unilateral mononuclear cell infiltration) and thymus (minimal hemorrhage).

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Fertility Studies In a GLP fertility study (Study VX-121-TX-009), VNZ was administrated to rats at oral doses of 0 (vehicle), 2.5, 5, and 12.5 (Male, M)/10 (Female, F) mg/kg/day (3, 9, and 19 (M)/30 (F) times the exposure at the maximum recommended human dose [MRHD], respectively). Males were dosed for 28 days prior to mating, throughout mating, and continuing until the day prior to euthanasia. Females were dosed for 14 days prior to mating, throughout mating, and continuing until Gestation Day 7. VNZ had no effects on male and female fertility and reproductive performance indices up to 12.5 (M)/10 (F) mg/kg/day (19 (M)/30 (F) times the exposure at the

MRHD). In conclusion, the NOAEL for fertility in this study was determined to be 12.5 mg/kg in males and 10 mg/kg in females associated with AUC0­24h of 361 and 565 µg·hr/mL, respectively.

Embryofetal Development Studies In a GLP embryo-fetal development study (Study VX-121-TX-005), pregnant rats were administered VNZ at oral doses of 0 (vehicle), 2.5, 5, and 10 mg/kg/day (4, 10, and 30 times the exposure at the MRHD, respectively) during the period of organogenesis from gestation days 6 to 17. VNZ did not cause adverse effects to the fetus at exposures up to 30 times the MRHD. In conclusion, the NOAEL for maternal and embryo/fetal toxicities was determined to be 10 mg/kg/day (AUC0­24h, 565 µg·hr/mL) in rats. In a GLP embryo-fetal development study (Study VX-121-TX-006), pregnant rabbits were administered VNZ at oral doses of 0 (vehicle), 10, 40, and 70 mg/kg/day (3, 22, and 71 times the exposure at the MRHD, respectively) during the period of organogenesis from gestation Days 7 to 20. VNZ did not cause adverse effects to the fetus at maternal exposures up to 22 times the MRHD. The high dose of 70 mg/kg/day (71 times the exposure at the MRHD) produced maternal toxicity (i.e., mortality, abortion, decreased mean body weight or body weight gains) and was associated with findings of increased postimplantation loss, decreased live fetuses, decreased fetal body weight, increased kidney malformations, and increased incidences of skeletal variations. The effects observed at the high dose were related to maternal toxicity and so were not considered relevant to the human risk. In conclusion, the NOAEL for maternal and embryo/fetal toxicities was determined to be 40 mg/kg/day (AUC0­24h, 410 µg·hr/mL) in rabbits.

Pre- and Postnatal Development Study In a GLP pre- and postnatal development study (Study VX-121-TX-017), pregnant rats were administered VNZ at oral doses of 0 (vehicle), 2.5, 5, and 10 mg/kg/day (approximately 2, 7, and 18 times the exposure at the MRHD, respectively) from gestation Day 7 to lactation/postpartum Day 20. No adverse effects were observed in the offspring at maternal doses up to approximately 18 times the MRHD. On PND 14, AUC values of VNZ in dams were 10.0 to 28.6 times greater than that in pups. In conclusion, the NOAEL for maternal and developmental toxicities was determined to be 10 mg/kg/day (AUC0­24h, 339 µg·hr/mL) in rats.
