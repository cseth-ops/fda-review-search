Lactation — 4487-1

Safety requirement, 505(o)(3) · approved 2023 · Draft Protocol Submission 05/2024 · Final Protocol Submission 03/2025

Conduct a worldwide descriptive study that collects prospective and retrospective data in women exposed to zilucoplan during pregnancy and/or lactation to assess risk of pregnancy and maternal complications, adverse effects on the developing fetus and neonate, and adverse effects on the infant. Infant outcomes will be assessed through at least the first year of life. The minimum number of patients will be specified in the protocol.

Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application The in vitro metabolic profiling of zilucoplan indicated that in addition to parent, 6 metabolites were produced in monkey and human hepatocytes. No human unique metabolite was observed. In vivo, two major metabolic pathways, hydrolysis, and terminal ω-oxidation, resulted in the formation of RA102758 and RA103488, respectively, in similar proportions in monkey and human plasma. These metabolites are covered in the monkey toxicity studies; however, according to the FDA clinical pharmacology reviewer, there are no major human metabolites. Based on the pharmacologic and metabolic similarity to humans, the cynomolgus macaque was considered the most relevant species for toxicity testing; therefore, nonclinical safety assessment in two species was not required by the Division. Repeat-dose toxicity studies consisted of 4- week SC studies in the rat and cynomolgus monkey and 13- and 39-week SC studies in the monkey. In the 4-week rat study, adverse effects were limited to injection site reactions at the HD (40 mg/kg/day). In the 4-week monkey study (0, 1, 2, or 4 mg/kg/day), elevated bile acids and lipase and peribiliary mixed cell inflammation in the liver were observed at the MD and HD. Widespread inflammatory effects and liver and pancreas histopathology were highlighted in the reviews of the 13-week monkey study (0, 0.25, 1, 2, or 10 mg/kg/day). In the (b) (4) pharmacology/toxicology review under (Rama Guidi, Ph.D., December 29, 2016), the histopathological changes observed in the 13-week study included: ...marked inflammation (vasculitis), mild pancreatic acinar degeneration, mild ductular hyperplasia, and degeneration of the pancreas in one male monkey administered 10 mg/kg/day, and minimal pancreatic acinar degeneration at ≥ 1 mg/kg/day in females. Moderate mucosal vesicular degeneration of the tongue was present in two 10 mg/kg/day males, one of which had minimal neutrophilic inflammation of the tongue mucosa in an area of vesiculation. Minimal vascular/perivascular mononuclear cell infiltration of the tongue and urinary bladder was observed at 0.25 mg/kg/day. Vascular/perivascular mononuclear cell infiltration showed partial recovery, however microscopic changes in the pancreas and liver at 10 mg/kg/day did not fully resolve after 4 weeks of observation. (b) (4) According to the pharmacology/toxicology review under (Wei Sun, Ph.D., April 22, 2019):

There were RA101495-related clinical signs for males in the high dose [10 mg/kg/day] group that included reddened and dry face, bilateral ocular discharge with periorbital redness and dry face, slight facial jaundice (icterus), and abdominal pain. Increased ALT, ASP, ALP, GGT, creatine kinase, total bilirubin, lipase, and bile acids were observed in males from the 10 mg/kg/day group. These findings, with particular reference to bilirubin, appeared to correlate with findings of slight facial jaundice (icterus). Organ weights/Gross pathology There were no drug-related gross observations or organ weight differences on Days 274 (termination) or 330 (recovery).

Histopathology Treatment-related histopathology findings at terminal necropsy consisted predominantly of inflammatory cell infiltrates of various organs at all doses, with generally a dose-related incidence and severity. Mononuclear and/or mixed cell interface infiltrates and epithelial vesicular degeneration/erosions/necrosis were prominent in the tongue, cervix, vagina, esophagus, stomach, and skin. Other findings included salivary gland duct degeneration/regeneration, uterine endometrial degeneration, increased cellularity of the thymus, and epithelial vesicular degeneration in the cervix accompanied by increased incidence and severity of squamous metaplasia and epithelial erosions (Table 39). In the 3 animals sacrificed prematurely (LD animal 2501 with colitis, MHD and HD animals 5003 and 6503 with skin ulcerations and erosions), histopathology findings included liver fibrosis with biliary duct degeneration and lung edema (5003) and moderate pancreatic acinar degeneration (all 3 animals) or pancreatic duct degeneration/regeneration with acinar atrophy (2501). One MD animal (4003) had pancreatic acinar cell degeneration with fibrosis/fibroplasia, similar to that noted in animals sacrificed prematurely. After the recovery period, inflammatory cell infiltrates of most organs and the administration sites were considered completely recovered; however, mucosal infiltrates of the esophagus, squamous metaplasia of the cervical mucosa, increased medullary cellularity in the thymus, and

lymphoid follicles or aggregates in the bone marrow remained present although decreased in incidence and/or severity (Table 40).
