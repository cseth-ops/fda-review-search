Carcinogenicity study not conducted / waived

FDA accepted · approved 2023 · review page 162

Based on a weight of evidence assessment, conduct of carcinogenicity studies were deemed not necessary for UCB7665. 13.1.3.4.

Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application

13.1.1. Pharmacology (Primary and Secondary) Pharmacology UCB7665 is a humanized immunoglobulin G (IgG) monoclonal antibody intended to suppress FcRn-mediated IgG recycling. In vitro studies using plasmon surface resonance and transfected Madin-Darby canine kidney (MDCK) cells indicated picomolar affinity of UCB7665 for both human and monkey FcRn. In vivo studies in transgenic mice that express human FcRn (i.e., hFcRNTg) indicated increased clearance of previously administered human IgG following single intravenous (IV) doses of 0, 10, 30, and 100 mg/kg UCB7665. Additionally, circulating IgG levels were reduced in male and female cynomolgus monkeys in the general toxicity studies in which subcutaneous (SC) doses of up to 150 mg/kg UCB7665 were administered every 3 days for up to 26 weeks.

Secondary Pharmacology The potential for UCB7665 to stimulate cytokine release was investigated in in vitro studies using whole blood, human peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMC), human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVEC), whole blood with HUVECs, and PBMCs with HUVECs. Four assay designs were used that assessed soluble UCB7665, UCB7665 immobilized on plates, UCB7665 cross-linked with an anti-human Fc F(ab’)2, and UCB7665 immobilized on beads. All assays, conducted using appropriate positive and negative controls, did not indicate the potential for stimulation of UCB7665-induced cytokine release.

Pharmacokinetics Pharmacokinetic parameters were assessed following intravenous and SC administration of UCB7665 in the 4- and 13-week toxicology studies in cynomolgus monkeys. In both studies, anti-drug antibody (ADA) development in most animals was observed. The ADA response was generally correlated with reduced pharmacodynamic (PD) effect (i.e., a reduction in IgG levels) and reduced exposure in animals with higher ADA titers.

14. Clinical Pharmacology

14.1. In Vitro Studies Rozanolixizumab is a human IgG4 monoclonal antibody to target and bind to FcRn receptor with increased affinity. It is neither subject to CYP450 metabolism nor expected to interfere with cytokine levels. Further, rozanolixizumab is not expected to be affected by transporters. Therefore, no in vitro transporter or CYP450-mediated drug interaction studies were conducted.

14.2. In Vivo Studies Study UP0018/UK: First-in-Human, Phase 1, Single-Ascending Dose Study in Healthy Volunteers

Study Design UP0018 was a first-in-human, randomized, participant- and investigator-blind, placebo- controlled, single dose-escalating study conducted in healthy study subjects. Each study participant was randomized to 1 of 6 cohorts and received a single dose of placebo (n=2 per cohort) or rozanolixizumab at a dose of ≈1 mg/kg, ≈4 mg/kg, or v7 mg/kg (n=6 per cohort) administered intravenously (IV) or SC as a 1-hour infusion. The study objectives were to assess safety, tolerability, PK, PD, and immunogenicity. A total of 49 subjects were randomized to 6 dosing cohorts and were included in the full analysis set and the pharmacodynamic per-protocol set. Additionally, all 36 subjects who received rozanolixizumab were included in the pharmacokinetic per-protocol set. The plasma concentration-time profiles for rozanolixizumab administered by the IV route showed similar profiles for the ≈4 mg/kg and ≈7 mg/kg doses, with nonlinear increases in rozanolixizumab plasma concentrations consistent with target-mediated disposition. Rozanolixizumab administered by the SC route was detectable only at the highest concentration of ≈7 mg/kg. There was no effect of ADA on the plasma concentrations of rozanolixizumab.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

General toxicity studies of 4-, 13- and 26-weeks’ duration were conducted in cynomolgus monkey administered up to 150 mg/kg UCB7665 by SC injection every 3 days. There was no serious toxicity. Drug-related findings consisted of reductions in globulin levels due to reduced IgG levels, which are expected based on the pharmacology of the drug. Accompanying the dose- related reductions in IgG were decreases in total protein and increased A/G ratio. In addition, a trend toward slightly reduced albumin was observed. Full recovery was observed by the end of the 8-week recovery period. Immunophenotyping was conducted and no test article-related changes in immune cell subtypes were observed. An assessment of T-cell-dependent antibody response was conducted during the dosing period in the 13-week study. Although there was no effect on immunoglobin M response, administration of UCB7665 resulted in inhibition of the IgG response to keyhole limpet hemocyanin (KLH); recovery was not assessed. Neutralizing anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) were detected in all dosed groups and ADA was greater in groups that received UCB7665 by SC injection.

Carcinogenicity

Based on a weight of evidence assessment, conduct of carcinogenicity studies were deemed not necessary for UCB7665.

13.1.3.4. Special Toxicology

Tissue Cross-Reactivity Good Laboratory Practice-compliant tissue cross-reactivity studies were conducted in human and cynomolgus monkey tissues. The staining patterns were generally similar between species; however, the study report did not specify whether specific binding was membranous or cytoplasmic. Therefore, relevance of the binding in specific tissues for assessing safety is difficult to determine. However, the similarity of staining patterns between species supported use of the cynomolgus monkey for nonclinical testing.

13.2. Individual Reviews of Studies Submitted With the New Drug Application

13.2.1. Introduction An enhanced pre- and postnatal development study in cynomolgus monkey was the only nonclinical study of UCB7665 submitted only to the NDA.

13.2.2. Key Findings Subcutaneous administration of 0, 50, or 150 mg/kg UCB7665 every 3 days to pregnant female cynomolgus monkeys from gestational day (GD) 20 through parturition caused embryonic losses in 3 of 19 females at both doses. Throughout the postnatal period, infant body weights in females were reduced at both doses. The immune function in infants was impaired at both doses. There were no test article effects on maturation, neurobehavioral endpoints, or postmortem findings in offspring from postnatal day (PND) 7 through PND 180.
