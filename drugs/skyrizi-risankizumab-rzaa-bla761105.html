5.4.1. Pharmacokinetics and immunogenicity of BI 655066 in Cynomolgus monkeys receiving a single intravenous or subcutaneous dose of BI 655066 (RD161476)

Two groups of 3 female monkeys received a single intravenous dose of 1.0 mg/kg or subcutaneous dose of 1.5 mg/kg risankizumab. Blood samples for pharmacokinetic analysis were collected at pre-dosing and at 0.167 (IV only), 0.5, 2, 8, 24, 48, 72, 96, 120, 168, 240, 336, 504, 672, 840, and 1008 hr post-dosing. Following intravenous treatment, the serum concentration-time profiles of risankizumab was characterized by a short distribution phase of approximately 1 - 2 days followed by a longer terminal phase. The terminal half-life (t ½ ) averaged 173 hr. Clearance (CL) of risankizumab averaged 0.243 mL/h/kg and steady-state volume of distribution (Vss) averaged 40.7 mL/kg. Following subcutaneous treatment, maximum serum concentrations of risankizumab occurred at 48 - 72 hr and the terminal half-life averaged 184 hr. The bioavailability of BI 655066 following subcutaneous dosing was 71.8%.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

5.5.1.1. BI 655066: A 26-Week subcutaneous toxicity and toxicokinetics study in Cynomolgus monkeys with an 8-week recovery period (RD161444/12R003)

In this 6-month repeat-dose study with a 2-month recovery period, four groups of up to 6 male (4.1 to 6.4 years old) and up to 6 female (2.5 to 3.8 years old) Cynomolgus (b) (4) (b) (4) monkeys were dosed subcuatenously with 0 (vehicle, succinate, (b) (4) (b) (4) sorbitol, Polysorbate 20, pH ), 5, 20, or 50 mg/kg risankizumab (BI 655066) once weekly. There were no treatment-related effects on mortalities, clinical signs, neurobehavioral assessments, ophthalmology, body weights, body temperature, ECG, blood pressure, responses to KLH immunization (IgM and IgG antibody responses), clinical pathology including peripheral blood lymphocyte phenotyping, and macroscopic findings. Hematology analysis showed a slight dose-dependent change in reticulocytes that did not reverse. Males developed a decrease in the weight of the prostate (LD - 24%, MD -18%, HD -25%, normalized to brain), which did not reverse after the recovery period (HD -37%, normalized to brain). There was also a decrease in the weight of the epididymis (-27%) and testes (-35%) weight at the HD, with reversal after the recovery period. The change in testicular weight was associated with bilateral, multifocal hypospermatogenesis, which developed in a dose dependent manner [one low-dose male (Grade 1), one mid-dose male (Grade 2) and two high-dose males (Grade 2)], based on both incidence and severity. The testicular finding was reversible. The NOAEL of risankizumab was determined to be 5 mg/kg in males (AUC 12,300 μg·h/mL) and 50 mg/kg in females (AUC 77,200 μg·h/mL).

A Pathology Working Group (PWG) composed of veterinary pathologists experienced in the evaluation of the male cynomolgus monkey reproductive system was convened to review the testicular findings from this 26-week toxicology study. The PWG considered the hypospermatogenesis unrelated to treatment with risankizumab, but rather reflected normal testicular development in young Cynomolgus monkeys. Because of the testicular findings in this 26-week toxicity study in monkeys, a follow-up study using sexually mature male monkeys was performed.

Carcinogenicity

Conventional rodent carcinogenicity studies were not conducted for the evaluation of the carcinogenic potential of risankizumab, as rodents are not a pharmacologically relevant species. A carcinogenicity study with long-term antagonism of IL23p19 in rodents will not be informative because rodents are not a pharmacologically relevant species. Concurrence has been received from the Executive Carcinogenicity Assessment Committee of CDER that a 2-year rodent carcinogenicity study is not needed. The applicant provided a weight-of-evidence analysis of available information to address the carcinogenic potential of risankizumab. In general, immunosuppressive drugs tend to be associated with increased cancer risk. However, no tumors or histopathological evidence of pre-neoplastic changes were

observed in organs or tissues examined following subcutaneous administration of risankizumab to monkeys at dose levels up to 50 mg/kg once weekly for 26 weeks followed by a 8-week post-dosing observation period. The immune function tests in the monkey toxicity studies showed that risankizumab did not cause immunosuppression [(as assessed by T-dependent antibody response (TDAR)] or deplete T-cells. Animal data from available literature suggests that a risk for malignancy may be reduced in the case of IL-23 inhibition but also reduced in the case of exogenous administration of IL- 23. Overall the risk for malignancy associated with long-term inhibition of IL-23 following administration of risankizumab to humans is considered to be low. Therefore, no additional nonclinical studies are recommended to evaluate the carcinogenic potential of risankizumab. Adequate labeling based on animal data from the sponsor and from literature as well as post-marketing patient monitoring of malignancy will be sufficient at this time.

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment

Psoriasis is a chronic, inflammatory disease that primarily affects the skin and is characterized by erythematous, scaly plaques and substantial impairment of quality of life . Skyrizi (risankizumab) injection, for subcutaneous use is proposed for the treatment of adults with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis who are candidates for systemic therapy or phototherapy. Skyrizi (risankizumab) injection for subcutaneous use is a humanized IgG1 monoclonal antibody that specifically binds to the p19 protein subunit of the IL-23 cytokine and inhibits its interaction with the IL-23 receptor. IL-23 is a naturally occurring cytokine, composed of 2 subunits (IL-23p19 and IL-12/23p40), that is involved in inflammatory and immune responses. Risankizumab is available as 75 mg PFS to be administered as a 150 mg dose (two injections).

For the treatment of moderate to severe plaque psoriasis, current therapeutic options include phototherapy and photochemotherapy with methoxsalen, systemic small molecule drugs (acitretin, apremilast, cyclosporine, methotrexate), and biologic products (adalimumab, etanercept, infliximab, ustekinumab, secukinumab, ixekizumab, brodalumab, guselkumab, and tildrakizumab). Although the efficacy varies, no product produces a response in all patients or provides a permanent cure. Phototherapy and photochemotherapy may be impractical due to office- based administration requirements. All the systemic products may have one or more serious adverse reactions, including malignancy, serious infections, teratogenicity, depression, nephrotoxicity, hepatotoxicity, and bone marrow suppression. Because of these limitations, there is a recognizable need for additional therapeutic options despite the number of available therapies.

Substantial efficacy was demonstrated in two pivotal Phase 3 trials (M15-995 [1311.28], M16-008 [1311.3]). In these trials, 997 adult subjects with moderate to severe plaque psoriasis who were eligible for systemic therapy or phototherapy were treated with risankizumab 150 mg (N=598), placebo (N=200) or EU-sourced ustekinumab (N=199).
