Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

This NDA should be approved from a nonclinical perspective.

ExEm Foam is prepared from a clear gel and sterile purified water. The gel is composed of (b) (4) (b) (4) (b) (4) glycerol 85% , HEC , and purified water USP/EP . Glycerol, a clear, viscous, water soluble polyhydric alcohol is present in ultrasound gel, vaginal moisturizers and lubricants, food additives, cosmetics, and personal health care products. HEC is a water soluble, modified cellulose polymer containing hydroxyethyl side chains commonly used in personal vaginal moisturizers and lubricants, lotions, oils, skin and hair care products. The safety of glycerol and HEC is well established by their widespread and long-term clinical use, which is well documented in the literature and made available by the Applicant to support this NDA.

The Applicant conducted biocompatibility safety studies on glycerol and HEC components of this product and no safety concern was reported. Based on available nonclinical pharmacology, pharmacokinetics (PK), toxicity, genotoxicity, reproductive and developmental toxicity, local tolerance, skin sensitization, and compatibility data on glycerol and HEC, there is no nonclinical safety concern with the use of ExEm Foam for the proposed indication and the new route of administration. However, because of lack of nonclinical safety studies involving intrauterine exposure of glycerol, HEC, or ExEm Foam, it was difficult to determine the risk of inflammatory reactions and the potential for scarring and adhesions in the uterus, tubes, and around the ovaries. The safety assessment of ExEm Foam following intrauterine exposure was therefore conducted using the following tools: 1. Search on FDA database for products containing glycerol or HEC for safety comparison to ExEm Foam 2. Applicant’s literature search on possible ExEm Foam effects such as embolization, inflammation, scarring, and adhesion following intrauterine or intravaginal use 3. Search on available CDRH data on the clear gel and other products containing glycerol and HEC 4. Relevant CMC data including osmolality and pH of ExEm Foam

The information obtained from the FDA database on products containing glycerol and HEC yielded no safety signals and supported the safety information in the NDA. The analysis demonstrates that there are no safety concerns with the intended route of administration for ExEm Foam.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Glycerol is extensively used in cosmetics and personal care products, food additives, and noncosmetic applications (Becker et al. 2015). HEC is used as humectant, binder, emulsion stabilizer, and film former in lotions, oils, bath products, eye make-up, skin care products, hair conditioners, hair dyes, and hair colors (Bergfeld et al. 2009). The Applicant conducted no toxicity studies on the safety of glycerol and HEC. However, safety data of glycerol and HEC available in published literature are provided to support the nonclinical safety of these compounds. The data are mostly from studies conducted before standardization of good laboratory practices and generally are not consistent with the standards described in current International Conference on Harmonization (ICH) or Organization for Economic Co-operation and Development guidance documents. Available safety data include single-dose, repeated- dose, genotoxicity, carcinogenicity, reproductive toxicity, local tolerability, skin irritation, and sensitization studies.

Carcinogenicity

No carcinogenicity studies have been conducted with glycerol. Literature data from a 2-year study in Long Evans rats given up to 20% glycerol in the diet showed no treatment-related increase in tumor incidence (Hine et al. 1953). A tumor-promoting activity was reported in male ddY mice administered 5% glycerol in drinking water following a single subcutaneous dose of 10

mg/kg 4-nitroquinolone 1-oxide (4NQO) at durations ranging from 4 weeks to 25 weeks (Inayama 1986; Inayama et al. 1986; Nagahara 1987). However, no tumors were reported with glycerol itself in studies involving 25-week dosing period using this model. Similarly, no carcinogenicity studies have been reported with HEC.

No treatment-related increase in tumors was found in a 2-year HEC general toxicity repeated- dose study in rats (Smyth et al. 1947). Furthermore, no treatment-related tumor effects were reported following longer-term administration studies involving three structurally related, modified cellulose analogs: methyl ethyl cellulose, carboxymethyl cellulose (McElligott and Hurst 1968), and hydroxypropyl methyl cellulose (HPMC) (McCollister et al. 1973). These data suggest a lack of carcinogenicity in this class of compounds. Thus, available literature evidence shows that glycerol is not carcinogenic though it could demonstrate minimal carcinogenicity potential when administered with other compounds. No evidence of any carcinogenicity has been attributed to HEC and its analogs in studies involving longer-term administration of 2 years duration.

Clinical efficacy

Review of Relevant Individual Studies Used to Support Efficacy

Riganelli et al. (2018)

8.1.1.1. Study Design

“Ultrasonography Reappraisal of Tubal Patency in Assisted Reproduction Technology Patients: Comparison Between 2D- and 3D-Sonohysterosalpingography. A Pilot Study” (Riganelli et al. 2018) was a prospective study of 50 infertile women, previously evaluated with laparoscopy, who were randomly assigned to either 2D-HyFoSy (Group 1) or 3D-HyFoSy (Group 2) imaging evaluation. Results of HyFoSy were compared to laparoscopy with dye as the reference standard.

Ultrasound Imaging Readers/Performers

The ultrasound operator performing and interpreting HyFoSy was the same for all procedures and the individual was blinded to the laparoscopy results.

ExEm Foam Instillation

20 mL of ExEm Foam was prepared by mixing 10 mL of clear gel and 10 mL of sterile purified water as per product instructions. These volumes are twice those of the product proposed for marketing in the United States. However, the ratio of gel to water (1:1) and the mixing process to create the foam are the same. The catheter used for instillation was not specified.

Ultrasound Imaging Technique

In Group 2, 3D evaluation was performed twice: real-time during foam instillation and postprocedure using 3D reconstructions.

8.1.1.2. Study Endpoints

The study reported sensitivity and specificity of HyFoSy for each imaging test (2D-HyFoSy or 3D- HyFoSy). The publication also reported the false positive rate, false negative rate, positive predictive value, and negative predictive value.

Definition of Patent Tube on HyFoSy

Passage of contrast along the length of the tube until its (peritoneal) spread at adnexal level.

Definition of Occluded Tube on HyFoSy

No (peritoneal) spread of the contrast medium and contrast is seen within the course of the tube only partially, even after repeated instillation of foam.

The above definitions were used to define negative and positive imaging results on a tube-level, and when compared to the results of the reference standard, were used for the calculation of sensitivity and specificity. The length of the procedure was also reported for each group.

8.1.1.3. Statistical Analysis

Data from the two HyFoSy imaging groups were compared using the Student’s t-test for continuous data analysis while the Fisher’s Exact Test was used for categorical data. A p-value less than 0.1 was considered statistically significant. Comment: In general, a p-value less than 0.05 in a two-sided test is the threshold for concluding statistical significance.

8.1.1.4. Study Results

Compliance With Good Clinical Practice

This study was performed at Sapienza University of Rome, Italy. The Hospital Ethical Committee approved the study and written informed consent was obtained from all patients prior to enrollment.

Financial Disclosure

The publication states that the authors “certify that there is no conflict of interest with any financial organization regarding the material discussed in the manuscript.” See Section 13.2 for the Applicant’s financial disclosure statement.

Clinical safety

The following sources of clinical evidence of safety were evaluated: • Published studies reporting ExEm Foam use • The pharmacovigilance database from the foreign postmarketing experience • Comparisons to the approved contrast agents for HSG • Safety profile of cleared devices for HyCoSy using saline and air • Safety profile of (clear gel) in the context of use as a coupling agent

Review of the Safety Database

Overall Exposure

Based on the literature searches for safety and/or efficacy, 1,616 patients in 14 studies were evaluated with ExEm Foam (see Table 36). In 12 out of the 14 studies (1,434 patients), a foam that was equal parts clear gel and sterile purified water was used. The two other studies, one evaluating 50 patients (Zizolfi et al. 2018) and one evaluating 132 patients (Exacoustos et al. 2017), were included in the integrated summary of safety. The investigators from these two studies reported using diluted foam in their investigations to achieve easy passage of the foam through 5-Fr catheters.

Of the 12 studies using the proposed formulation, two evaluated HyFoSy in the context of proximal tubal occlusion via Essure device (Dreyer et al. 2015), (Rosic et al. 2018), as described above. These two studies evaluated a total of 112 patients. The remaining 10 studies evaluated 1,322 patients for tubal patency in patients with history of infertility and used the proposed formulation of ExEm Foam.

Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment Pathology leading to fallopian tube occlusion is a major cause of infertility in the United States. As a noninvasive, nonionizing imaging modality available at the point of care, ultrasound plays an important role in the diagnostic armamentarium for guiding infertile couples toward appropriate therapy. Yet fallopian tube patency is poorly evaluated on ultrasound without the use of contrast agents, and none currently marketed are approved for fallopian tube assessment. In this multidisciplinary review and evaluation authorized under Section 505(b)(2) of Federal Food, Drug, and Cosmetic Act, the review team relied on two reports published in the scientific literature to find that substantial evidence supports the effectiveness of ExEm Foam for assessment of fallopian tube patency in women with known or suspected infertility. Both reports demonstrate that in these patients, a high proportion of tubes were correctly identified as patent or occluded, using sonohysterosalpingography with ExEm Foam or equivalent and measured against laparoscopy with chromopertubation. For assessing safety, the review team also relied on data from FDA databases, reports on animal and other preclinical studies published in the scientific literature or owned by the Applicant, and the Applicant’s reported experience marketing ExEm Foam outside the United States. Subsequent to labeling revisions, the review team finds that the benefit-risk of the product is favorable and recommends approval.
