Pediatric (PREA) · approved 2023 · Study Completion 08/2029 · Final Report Submission 02/2030

Conduct a trial to evaluate the pharmacokinetics, pharmacodynamics and safety of Jesduvroq for the treatment of anemia associated with chronic kidney disease in children and adolescents aged 3 months to under 18 years requiring dialysis. Submit datasets at the time of the final clinical study report submission.

Renal impairment — 4400-2

Safety requirement, 505(o)(3) · approved 2023 · Draft Protocol Submission 08/2023 · Final Protocol Submission 02/2024 · Interim Report Submission 07/2028 · Study Completion 07/2031 · Final Report Submission 07/2032

Conduct a prospective observational study in the United States to characterize the long-term safety (at least 5 years follow up) of Jesduvroq in adults with dialysis-dependent chronic kidney disease treated with the approved dosing regimen of Jesduvroq versus an ESA comparator arm. Specific safety outcomes of interest include: the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) defined as all-cause mortality, non-fatal myocardial infarction and non-fatal stroke; thrombotic vascular events to include vascular access thrombosis; hospitalization for heart failure; serious gastrointestinal bleeds, eye disorders (proliferative retinopathy, macular edema, choroidal neovascularization), and hepatic injury. The study population should include adults previously treated with erythropoiesis-stimulating agents (ESAs) and adults naïve to ESAs. The effect of baseline and maximum achieved hemoglobin on the specified safety outcomes should be evaluated.

13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application

13.1.1. Primary pharmacology Daprodustat is a reversible inhibitor of hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF)-PH1, PH2, and PH3 which results in the stabilization and nuclear accumulation of HIF-1α and HIF-2α transcription factors, leading to increased transcription of the HIF-responsive genes, including erythropoietin. Treatment of daprodustat in vitro and in vivo pharmacology studies showed that daprodustat increases erythropoietin (EPO) levels (mRNA and protein), which results in an increase of red blood cell (RBC) parameters. Tabulated summary of the in vitro and in vivo pharmacology studies in animals conducted in support of daprodustat’s mechanism of action are listed below. Daprodustat was coded as GSK1278863A in the pharmacology study reports.

Safety pharmacology

• Daprodustat had no effect on hERG channel activity (up to 47.66µM), neurobehavioral, and respiratory activity (up to 20 mg/kg). • Mild cardiovascular effects of daprodustat including small magnitude of QT and QRS prolongation was observed in ex vivo study with rabbit left ventricular wedge at concentrations ≥10µM. • Mild reversible increase in heart rate was observed in rats after repeated oral dosing of daprodustat for 5 days at doses of ≥30 mg/kg and did not alter the mean arterial pressure.

13.1.4. ADME/PK Absorption Pharmacokinetics of daprodustat (coded as GSK1278863A or GSK1278863 in the pharmacokinetic study reports) and its predominant human metabolites were evaluated in mice, rats, dogs, and monkeys. For the species that do not generate the metabolites (mice and rats), the animals were treated with major human metabolites of daprodustat [GSK2391220 (M2), GSK2506104 (M3), and GSK2531401(M13)]. • Single dose studies were conducted with daprodustat and its metabolites in mouse, rat, dog, and monkeys for characterization of pharmacokinetics of daprodustat.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

General toxicology studies of 4- and 13-weeks duration were conducted in Sprague Dawley rats and Beagle Dogs with rats receiving daily oral doses by gavage and dogs by capsule. A 13-week toxicity study was also conducted in the cynomolgus monkey with daily dosing by oral gavage. Toxicities in rats were overall consistent with toxicities observed in the 26-week rat study and were mainly due to exaggerated pharmacology (see Section 13.2). Higher doses were tolerated in the 4- and 13-week rat studies compared to the 26-week rat study. The 100 mg/kg/day dose administered to rats in the 13-week study toxicity resulted in severe toxicities to the heart (valvulopathy, atrial thrombus, valvular thrombus, endocardial hyperplasia, and cardiomyopathy), lung (chronic passive congestion), kidney (infarct, thrombus, cortical tubular pigment and tubular basophilia), brain (focal degeneration) and stomach (glandular erosion/ulceration, submucosal inflammatory cell infiltrate, hemorrhage, and vascular medial hypertrophy). Multifocal erosions, ulcers, hemorrhage, edema, and/or inflammation in the glandular region of the stomach were observed at doses as low as 7 mg/kg/day in the 4-week study. Toxicities in dogs were due to the exaggerated pharmacology of daprodustat related to polycythemia and hyperviscosity of the blood leading to poor tissue perfusion and anoxia. In monkeys, there were fewer organs of toxicity (only bone marrow [erythroid hypercellulartity], lung [alveolar hemorrhage], thymus [lymphoid atrophy], and stomach [hemorrhage and effects [To complete this review, detailed instructions are available in the Integrated Review Template How-to Guide.]
