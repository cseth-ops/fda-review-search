Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application The pivotal and supporting pharmacology (primary and safety), absorption and distribution, and toxicology (repeat-dose toxicity, genotoxicity, carcinogenicity, and reproductive and developmental toxicity) studies submitted with both IND 142564 and NDA 219407 have been reviewed under either the IND or NDA and are summarized here.

13.1.1. Pharmacology

13.1.1.1. Primary Pharmacodynamics Donidalorsen is a triantennary GalNAc3-conjugated ASO that causes ribonuclease H1 (RNase H1)-mediated degradation of PKK messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA) through binding to PKK mRNA, which results in reduced production of PKK protein. PKK is a pro-enzyme for plasma kallikrein, which results in the release of bradykinin, a potent vasodilator causing swelling and pain in HAE. In patients with HAE, C1-INH deficiency or dysfunction leads to excessive plasma kallikrein activity, bradykinin generation, and angioedema attacks. Donidalorsen lowers PKK concentration, which is intended to prevent excessive bradykinin production in patients with HAE. The primary pharmacodynamics of donidalorsen have been evaluated in both in vitro and in vivo primary pharmacology studies.

In Vitro Primary Pharmacology Studies As described by the Applicant, over 1300 ASOs were designed to target various sites within the human PKK gene and were evaluated for their ability to inhibit the level of PKK mRNA in vitro in the human hepatocellular carcinoma cell line HepaRG. Of those ASOs, ISIS 546254, an unconjugated 2'-O-methoxyethyl (2′-MOE) gapmer ASO with a uniform phosphorothioate (PS) backbone was identified as a lead candidate. Donidalorsen was derived from ISIS 546254 as a 2′- MOE gapmer ASO with the same nucleobase sequence as ISIS 546254 but with a mixed PS and phosphodiester (PO) backbone and conjugated to a trivalent GalNAc3.

ISIS 546254 is perfectly matched to only PKK mRNA in the human transcriptome (targets site free of meaningful sequence variation). Based on the in silico Basic Local Alignment Search Tool analysis, there were no identified potential off-target genes with a 0-, 1-, or 2-base mismatch or a 3-base mismatch with 17 matched bases in a row. Nonclinical ADME / toxicokinetics

Unconjugated donidalorsen was measurable in the liver and kidney after SC administration in mice and monkeys. Donidalorsen was highly bound (≥96.81%) to whole blood plasma proteins in human, monkey, and mouse. As determined by the Applicant, the primary route of elimination of donidalorsen is initial rapid hydrolysis of the GalNAc conjugate in tissues, followed by slow nuclease-mediated metabolism of the unconjugated oligonucleotide, and subsequent excretion of chain-shortened metabolites, as well as full-length oligonucleotides, albeit minor in urine and feces. There were no unique metabolites found in humans compared to animal species. For donidalorsen linker-related metabolites, the GalNAc moiety is hydrolyzed intracellularly following uptake into tissues and the THA linker undergoes extensive oxidative metabolism, and is nearly completely eliminated via biliary excretion, and to a lesser extent, renal excretion, within 24 hours after administration.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Donidalorsen-related toxicity findings in the 26-week mouse study and/or the13-week interim/39-week monkey study included effects on thrombocytopenia and hematology (monkeys), coagulation (monkeys), liver (mice and monkeys), kidney (monkeys), and injection site (mice and monkeys).

Thrombocytopenia, Hematology, and Coagulation In monkeys, severe thrombocytopenia was noted in one female and one male at 12 and 30 mg/kg/dose donidalorsen, respectively. Thrombocytopenia was not seen in either animal during the recovery period. Platelet decreases were accompanied by mild to marked decreases in red cell mass with mild to moderate increases in reticulocyte counts and/or increased polychromasia. The female had mild SC tissue necrosis with fibrin and hemorrhage at one of the injection sites, and both the female and male had red nasal discharge, red skin discoloration, bruising, and/or petechial hemorrhage at various body sites, which may have accounted for the decrease in red cell mass and the increase in reticulocyte counts. Complement Bb levels (marker of alternative pathway activation) were slightly elevated in males at 12 mg/kg/dose donidalorsen and in females at 12 and 30 mg/kg/dose donidalorsen. Minimal prolongations in mean activated partial thromboplastin time (aPTT) time were seen in males and females at 30 mg/kg/dose donidalorsen but were not evident by 24 hours postdose.

Liver In mice, liver histopathology findings included minimal or slight centrilobular hepatocellular hypertrophy in males (1, 4, and 12 mg/kg/dose donidalorsen) and one female (12 mg/kg/dose donidalorsen), which correlated with increased mean liver weights (4 and 12 mg/kg/dose donidalorsen) and liver enzymes (AST, ALT, and/or ALP). There was also an increase in the severity (slight to marked) of hepatocyte vacuolation in males (4 and 12 mg/kg/dose donidalorsen), which correlated with gross pathology findings of liver discoloration at all dose levels. These findings were consistent with uptake of donidalorsen. In monkeys, liver histopathology findings included hepatocyte hypertrophy (≥6 mg/kg/dose donidalorsen terminal), accumulation of basophilic granules in hepatocytes (≥2 mg/kg/dose donidalorsen terminal and ≥6 mg/kg/dose donidalorsen interim) and Kupffer cells (≥6 mg/kg/dose donidalorsen interim and terminal), and sporadic perivascular/vascular inflammation in one male (at 12 mg/kg/dose donidalorsen interim).

Kidney In monkeys, kidney histopathology findings included accumulation of basophilic granules in tubular epithelial cells (≥2 mg/kg/dose donidalorsen interim and terminal).

Carcinogenicity

In a 6-month carcinogenicity study in transgenic (Tg.rasH2) mice, SC administration of donidalorsen (5, 10, or 20 mg/kg for males and 10, 20, or 60 mg/kg for females) or mouse- specific surrogate ISIS 722059 (10 mg/kg) once every 2 weeks (Q2W) did not result in a statistically significant increase in tumors compared to the vehicle control in male mice. The carcinogenicity study was inadequate for female mice because a maximum tolerated dose (MTD) was not achieved and no other valid basis to support the high dose was discernable. Of note, at the time of the special protocol assessment (SPA) for the 6-month carcinogenicity protocol, the Executive Carcinogenicity Assessment Committee could not recommend doses for female mice for the proposed study, as no acceptable basis for dose selection could be identified in the non- GLP-compliant 14-week dose range finding (DRF) study in Tg.rasH2 mice. A 2-year carcinogenicity study in rats is ongoing. At the time of the pre-NDA meeting, the Division agreed that it would be acceptable to submit a summary of the in-life data for the 2-year carcinogenicity study in rats at the time of the NDA submission and to submit the final report as a postmarketing requirement (PMR).

7.2. Potential Risks or Safety Concerns Based on Drug Class or Other Drug-Specific Factors Donidalorsen is a prekallikrein-directed GalNAc-conjugated ASO. The potential safety concerns for donidalorsen are based on both the molecule and the target.

ASOs FDA-approved ASOs for intravenous or subcutaneous administration include mipomersen, inotersen, eteplirsen, golodirsen, vilpolarsen, casimersen, tofersen, defibrotide, and nusinersen. Safety risks associated with systemically administered ASOs have included injection site reactions, liver toxicity (Boxed Warning for mipomersen), thrombocytopenia (Boxed Warning

for inotersen), coagulation abnormalities, and kidney toxicity (Boxed Warning for inotersen). Hypersensitivity reactions have also been reported. Approved GalNAc conjugated ASOs include eplontersen and olezarsen. GalNAc conjugation is thought to enhance the safety of ASOs by targeting delivery to hepatocytes which improves the activity of ASO, allowing for lower dosages and potentially improved safety. It is not known if liver-targeted ASOs accumulate in the liver over time. The known class effects of ASOs (liver toxicity, thrombocytopenia, and glomerulonephritis) were not identified as risks for eplontersen. Olezarsen is labeled for hypersensitivity reactions, decreased platelet counts, and increased liver enzymes.

Kallikrein Inhibitors Approved HAE products which target prekallikrein include lanadelumab and berotralstat for prophylaxis, and sebetrolstat for acute treatment of HAE attacks. Safety risks include coagulation abnormalities and hypersensitivity reactions.

7.3. Potential Risks or Safety Concerns Identified Through Postmarket Experience Not applicable. This drug is not yet commercially available in any country.
