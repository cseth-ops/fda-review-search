Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Summary of Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application The nonclinical safety profile of IB1001 (also referred as N-acetyl-L-leucine, NALL, and levacetylleucine) supporting this NDA was evaluated in: (1) repeated dose toxicity studies in rats (up to 6 months) and dogs (up to 9 months); (2) a standard battery of in vitro and in vivo genotoxicity studies; and (3) dose-ranging reproductive and developmental toxicity studies including embryo-fetal developmental (EFD) studies in rats and rabbits. Additional nonclinical studies, including fertility studies in male and female rats, peri- and postnatal developmental study in rats, carcinogenicity studies in transgenic mice (6 months) and rats (2-year bioassay). Juvenile toxicology studies in rats were not included in this submission. Given the imminently life-threatening nature of NPC and the high unmet medical need, these studies will be requested as postmarketing requirements (PMRs).

The following study was previously reviewed under the IND:

Study Number 36243: 6-Week Subchronic Toxicity Study of IB1001 by Repeated Oral Administration to Beagle Dogs Purpose: Evaluate oral toxicity following daily dosing and reversibility of effects in dogs. Design: Daily oral dosing in Beagle dogs (3M/3F per dose level + 2M/2F in the high dose and control groups) dosed at 0, 225, 750, or 2500 mg/kg/day for 6 weeks followed by a recovery period of 2 weeks. Body weight, feed consumption, electrocardiography (ECG), blood pressure, ophthalmological and auditory examinations, clinical pathology, histopathology, and toxicokinetics (TKs) were evaluated. Key Findings: There were no adverse effects at up to 2500 mg/kg/day. The maximum plasma concentration (Cmax) and area under the concentration-time curve (AUC) levels of levacetylleucine were dose-related after Days 1 and 42 of dosing. The combined male and female Cmax was 252.8 µg/mL and AUC0-∞ was 598 µg.hr/mL at the no-observed-adverse- effect level (NOAEL) on Day 42. Conclusion: The NOAEL for levacetylleucine was 2500 mg/kg/day in a 6-week oral gavage toxicity study in Beagle dogs. The NOAEL dose in dog provides a 19-fold safety margin based on exposures in humans (31.6 µg·hr/mL) at a daily maximum recommended human dose of 4 g/day.

Fertility and early embryonic development

A fertility and early embryonic development study in rats has not been conducted with levacetylleucine. PMRs for evaluating effects of levacetylleucine on fertility and early embryonic development will be issued because there are adult-onset forms of NPC, and treatment with levacetylleucine for early onset NPC may continue into reproductive age; therefore, some patients of reproductive age are likely to be exposed. Additionally, levacetylleucine is a modified amino acid and appears to have widespread distribution; therefore, placental and lactational transfer are expected.

Prenatal and postnatal development

The prenatal and postnatal development study in rats has not been conducted with levacetylleucine. PMRs for evaluating effects of levacetylleucine on pre- and postnatal development will be issued because onset of NPC ranges from infancy into adulthood, and treatment with levacetylleucine may range from infancy into late adulthood. Levacetylleucine was found to cause increases in post-implantation loss and malformation in skeletal bones in a dose-range finding (DRF) EFD study in rabbits. Leucine, a metabolite of levacetylleucine, injection as a single 0.2 mL of a 1% solution in the amnionic sac of a 9-day-old embryo of chicken is reported to cause morphological anomalies of the legs (including inward flexion of the toes, leg extension or flexion at hip joint), defects in ossification of the skull, torsion of the neck and depression of the brain waves (Bergstrom et al. 1967). Therefore, exposure to levacetylleucine has potential to cause adverse effects on growth and development (neurological, physical, and sexual) from prenatal and into postnatal stage of life and requires investigation. Additionally, levacetylleucine is a modified amino acid and appears to have widespread distribution; therefore, placental and lactational transfer are expected.
