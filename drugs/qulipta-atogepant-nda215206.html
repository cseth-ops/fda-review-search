Safety pharmacology

A complete battery of safety pharmacology (CNS, cardiovascular, and respiratory) endpoints was adequately assessed and showed no safety concerns.

13.1.3. PK/ADME In rats and monkeys following IV or PO administration, the half-life was between 1 and 4 hours, oral bioavailability was 27%, and plasma protein binding was 92%. 14C-atogepant (total radioactivity) was eliminated in the urine (~9%), bile (~55%), and feces (~18%). Peak tissue concentrations in rat were as follows (highest to lowest concentration): GI tract > liver > bile duct > salivary gland (submaxillary). Oxidative metabolism primarily occurred by CYP3A4. Atogepant was a substrate for membrane-bound efflux transporters P-gp and BCRP, hepatic uptake transporters OATP1B1 and OATP1B3, and the renal uptake transporter, OAT1. There were no major or unique circulating metabolites in humans.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The toxicologic and toxicokinetic profile of orally administered atogepant was assessed in a series of single and repeat dose studies in rats and monkeys up to 6- and 9-months’ duration, respectively. At the high dose (HD) in the 14-day (250 mg/kg) and 3-month (200 mg/kg) toxicology studies in rat, there were body weight decreases of 23 and 16%, respectively, and vacuolation in the small intestine. In the 3-month rat (10/sex/group) study (0, 5, 20, or 200 mg/kg), the no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) was the mid dose (MD) based on a 16% decrease in body weight gain at the HD. In the pivotal 6-month study in rat (15/sex/group; 0, 10, 30, or 100 mg/kg), there were non-adverse findings consisting of decreases in body weight of up to 10% at the HD, which correlated with a decrease in food consumption. These findings partially resolved during the recovery period. There was also minimum to marked vacuolation in

the villous epithelium of the small intestine in MD and HD animals, which resolved during the recovery period. The HD was justified based on decreases in body weight gain at ≥200 mg/kg in the 14-day and 3-month studies. The safety margin based on plasma AUC at the NOAEL of 100 mg/kg was 27 times relative to that at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 60 mg. Plasma AUC0­t generally increased dose proportionally. In the single dose (up to 1000 mg/kg) studies in monkey, vomiting was observed at 150 and 300 mg/kg and liquid feces was observed at 300 mg/kg. In the pivotal 9-month oral toxicology study in monkey (4/sex/group, 0, 15, 40, or 300 mg/kg) there were no drug-related findings. Plasma Cmax and AUC0­24 h increased dose proportionally from the low dose (LD) to MD and less than dose proportionally from the MD to HD. The NOAEL was the HD. The study was considered adequate based on the vomiting observed at the 150 and 300 mg/kg doses in the single dose studies. At the HD (300 mg/kg) in the 9-month study, the safety margin was 10-fold compared to that at the MRHD based on plasma AUC at the NOAEL.

Carcinogenicity

Based on the FDA statistical analysis of the 2-year mouse and rat carcinogenicity studies, atogepant was not carcinogenic. The study in female mice was not adequate because the HD was not adequately justified; it was not demonstrated to be a maximum tolerated dose or maximum feasible dose and did not achieve plasma exposures at least 25-fold that at in humans at the MRHD. Reproductive and developmental toxicology

A fertility study in male and female rats (20/sex/group) administered oral doses of 0, 5, 20, or 125 mg/kg (QD) for 15 days prior to cohabitation, showed no effects on the testes, sperm motility or number, pregnancy status, corpora lutea, uterine implants, live or dead fetuses, or resorptions. The HD was justified based on decreases in body weight gain at ≥200 mg/kg in the 14-day and 3-month studies. No toxicokinetic data were collected. (The exposure data from the rat embryofetal development study was used to calculate margins.) At the high dose, plasma exposure (AUC) was approximately 15 times that in humans at the maximum recommended human dose (MRHD) of 60 mg/day. In a preliminary oral embryo-fetal development study in rat (12/group), animals were orally administered doses of 0, 5, 20, 250, or 750 mg/kg (QD) on gestation days (GDs) 6 through 20. Findings consisted of decreased fetal body weight (11-14%) at 250 and 750 mg/kg. There were

no effects on fetal viability, weight, sex ratio, and external or visceral morphology. In the pivotal study, doses of 0, 5, 15, 125, or 750 mg/kg (PO) was administered QD (20/group) on GDs 6 through 20. There were no adverse findings in the dam. The fetal NOAEL was 15 mg/kg based on increases in total resorptions and post-implantation loss (125 and 750 mg/kg) and incomplete ossification (125 and 750 mg/kg). Exposure increased greater than dose proportionally between 5 and 125 mg/kg and decreased between 125 and 750 mg/kg. At the no-effect dose (15 mg/kg), plasma exposure (AUC) was approximately 4 times that at the MRHD. In the preliminary embryofetal development study in rabbits (12/group), animals were orally administered doses of 0, 15, 45, 100, or 325 mg/kg (QD) on GDs 7 through 20. There was a 6% decrease in maternal body weight gain at the high dose. At necropsy (GD 28) corpora lutea, gross morphology, fetal viability and weight, sex, and fetal external and visceral morphology were assessed. At the HD, there were decreases in male and female fetal weight of 10 and 14%, respectively, compared to control. In the pivotal study, oral doses of 0, 30, 90, or 130 mg/kg (23/group) were administered daily on GDs 7 through 20 and the necropsy was conducted on GD 28. Maternal body weight loss (18%) was observed at the HD. The fetal NOAEL was the MD based on an increase in skeletal and visceral variations at the HD. Plasma exposure (AUC) at the NOAEL for adverse development effects was approximately 3 times that in humans at the MRHD. In the pre and postnatal development study, rats (21/group) were orally administered doses of 0, 15, 45 or 125 mg/kg (QD) from GD 6 through Lactation Day 20. The NOAEL was at the MD based on a decrease (8-13%) in pup body weight that persisted into adulthood. Toxicokinetic data were not collected. (The data from the rat embryofetal development study were used to estimate plasma exposure at 45 mg/kg.) Plasma exposure (AUC) at the NOAEL for adverse effects on pre- and postnatal development was approximately 5 times that in humans at the MRHD.
