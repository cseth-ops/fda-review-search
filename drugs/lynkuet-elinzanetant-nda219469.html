Review versus label

Where the approved label's nonclinical text does not match what the reviewer reported: a margin stated differently at the same dose, or a tumour finding the review flags that the label does not name. These are leads to check, not errors: labeling can deliberately recompute margins, and some flagged tumours were judged not drug-related elsewhere in the review.

NOAEL and Multiples of exposure

Agreed with caveats · Nonclinical · review page 598
Reviewer comment
This reviewer agrees with the NOAEL set at 15 mg/kg/day for F1 maturation and reproductive function. However, based on maternal findings of body weight loss, clinical signs of not attending to offspring, and general poor health observed in 3 F0 dams at 5 mg/kg/day and 3 F0 dams at 15 mg/kg/day, the maternal toxicity NOAEL should be set at the low dose of 1.5 mg/kg/day. • The Applicant set the NOAEL for developmental toxicity at the low dose or 1.5 mg/kg/day (corresponding to maternal BAY 3427080 Cmax of 134 μg/L and AUC(0-24h) of 2000 μg*h/L on GD 20), based on the increase in litter loss and clinical observation of missing milk bands.

Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application Selected nonclinical studies were originally submitted and reviewed under IND 129492. All nonclinical safety studies conducted in support of elinzanetant were also submitted to the present NDA, and Section 13.2 highlights key findings of those studies. All nonclinical studies submitted by the Applicant under the NDA and IND are captured in the nonclinical review archived in DARRTS dated July 11, 2025 (DARRTS°ID:°5623053 2025).

13.2. Individual Reviews of Studies Submitted With the New Drug Application The Applicant refers to elinzanetant in the nonclinical studies by the code name BAY 3427080. Previous code names (NT-814 and GSK1144814A) are still noted in earlier study reports and hence also occur in this review in some tables and figures. Three forms of elinzanetant (free base [BAY 3427080], [HCl] salt [BAY 3453656], and tosylate salt [BAY 3453651]) were used in nonclinical studies. However, all doses and concentrations in the tables, figures, and text descriptions are expressed in terms of free base.

13.2.1. Pharmacology 13.2.1.1. Primary Pharmacology Elinzanetant is an NME that acts as a noncompetitive antagonist of both NK1R and NK3R. In binding assays, elinzanetant displayed high affinity for human NK1R (pKi values of 8.71 to 10.15 [mean pKi of 9.43]) and NK3R (pKi values of 8.03 to 8.8 [mean pKi of 8.42]). Elinzanetant has ~10-fold lower affinity towards human NK3R compared to human NK1R. Additionally, elinzanetant has higher affinity for human NK1R and NK3R than for human NK2 receptors (pKi values of approximately 6.0). In functional in vitro assays, elinzanetant inhibited the activity of human NK1R and NK3R, with calculated pKB values of 9.02 to 9.1 for human NK1R and 8.17 to 8.5 for human NK3R.

Drug Activity Related to Proposed Indication In the context of VMS due to menopause, NK1R and NK3R antagonists are thought to act by inhibiting the activity of KNDy neurons, which project to the vasomotor center in the median preoptic nucleus to modulate neuronal activity involved in thermoregulation. This inhibition of KNDy neurons, which are thought to be in a hyperactivated state because of estrogen decline in menopause, is proposed to result in improvement of menopause-associated VMS (Figure 1). KNDy neurons also synapse onto hypothalamic neurons that secrete GnRH, and, by suppressing the activity of NK1R/NK3R in KNDy neurons, elinzanetant may suppress hypothalamic GnRH secretion and, consequently, reduce the release of gonadotropins (LH and FSH) from the pituitary (Figure 1). Although the relationship between gonadotropin levels and VMS is poorly understood, studies have reported synchrony between hot flushes and LH secretion (Sassarini and Anderson 2024). It is unknown whether suppression of gonadotropin levels contributes to the effectiveness of elinzanetant to mitigate menopause-associated VMS. Integrated nonclinical assessment

Bayer submitted NDA 219469 for elinzanetant (BAY 3427080) in soft gel capsule dosage form for the indication to treat moderate to severe VMS associated with menopause. The recommended oral dose of elinzanetant is 120 mg (two 60 mg capsules) taken orally once daily at bedtime. Bayer has submitted a full nonclinical program for elinzanetant to support their NDA under the 505 (b)(1) pathway as an NME (New Molecular Entity).

Pharmacology

Elinzanetant is an NK1R/NK3R antagonist. Inhibition of NK1R/NK3R on KNDy neurons can decrease neuronal activity involved in thermoregulation, which is thought to contribute to the VMS associated with menopause.

Elinzanetant has ~10-fold higher affinity to NK1R compared to NK3R, with mean pKi values of 9.43 and 8.42 for NK1R and NK3R, respectively. Elinzanetant has three principal metabolites M27, M30/34, and M18/21, which also act as noncompetitive antagonists at NK1R and NK3R.

In-vitro selectivity assays/ (b) (4) screen including a wide range of receptors, ions channels, transporters, and enzymes to identify potential off-target effects of elinzanetant yielded only a few notable findings. Elinzanetant and its three principal metabolites may have the potential to inhibit estrogen and progesterone receptors (ER and PR, respectively). Radioligand binding and functional assays with elinzanetant produced equivocal results. In study R-13674, elinzanetant had no effect on radioligand binding to ER and PR, and in study R-14344, elinzanetant (up to 30 μM) had no effect on the activity of recombinant human PR-B. However, in study 14343, elinzanetant inhibited radiolabeled estradiol and progesterone binding to recombinant human ERβ and to recombinant human PR-B, respectively; the IC50s were 22.5 μM and 0.64 μM, respectively. The metabolites M27, M30/M34 (major metabolite), and M18/21 inhibited radiolabeled progesterone binding to recombinant human PR-B with IC50s of 1.34 μM, 1.20 μM, and 1.69 μM, respectively.
