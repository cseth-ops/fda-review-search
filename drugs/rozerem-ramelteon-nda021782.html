Safety pharmacology

Examination of TAK-375 in safety pharmacology studies revealed TAK-375 administered at high doses was capable of producing a profound CNS depression which could be lethal and was preceded by prone postures and respiratory abnormalities. TAK-375 did not reveal an ability to acutely disrupt cardiovascular, pulmonary or gastrointestinal function. No alterations in ECG waveforms were noted when tested in the conscious dog, no block of the hERG channel potassium current, and no prolongation of the action potential duration in sheep purkinje fibers were noted.

Pharmacokinetics:

TAK-375 is rapidly and passively absorbed from the GI tract but shows low oral bioavailability in all species tested ranging from 0.3% in the dog to 6.3% in the rat (human oral bioavailability is approximately 1.8%) due to extensive first pass hepatic metabolism. TAK-375 half-life is short in nonclinical species, <1 hr. TAK-375 has low plasma protein- binding and radiolabel studies demonstrate the radiolabeled drug or metabolites are distributed rapidly throughout the body with highest concentrations outside of the GI tract being in the liver, kidney, adrenal gland, and pituitary gland. Maximal concentrations in most tissues were achieved within 30 minutes following oral administration with levels declining rapidly 2 hr after oral administration and radioactivity was nearly absent in most tissues by 48 hr though radioactivity was retained longer in epididymal fat (tissue 7, estimated at 60 hr versus <24 hr in all other tissues). In general, no evidence suggesting significant tissue accumulation or retention was demonstrated. Both TAK-375 and M-II rapidly penetrate the blood-brain-barrier when assessed in rats and enter the brain with levels that approximate the prevailing plasma concentration. TAK-375 and TAK-375 metabolites were found to cross the placenta and enter breast milk of rats as well.

TAK-375 is extensively metabolized by the liver as determined in in vivo studies in rats and monkeys, in sit studies in rats and in vitro studies with hepatic microsomes. Metabolites identified in the plasma and excreta of the rat were M-I, M-IT, M-III, M-IV, M- V, M-VI, M-VII, and M-VIII. M-IX was detected in the bile of the rat. Studies in monkey demonstrated a similar metabolite profile, except M-V and M-IX were not found and M-VI and M-VIII were only detected in the urine. The only metabolite formed in human that was not observed in any nonclinical species was M-VIII-glucuronide. The major pathways of metabolism involved the hydroxylation of the propionamide side chain, forming M-II, and the oxidation of the ring hydroxyl group to generate a carbonyl! group and form M-IV.

Another major metabolic pathway involved cleavage of the dihydrofuran ring of TAK-375 to form M-L. Other pathways typically involved ring or side chain oxidation. All metabolites other than M-II are believed inactive. Jn vitro study of the metabolism of TAK-375 by hepatic microsomal preparations from rat, dog, monkey and human liver indicate the predominant CYP involved is CYP1A2 which metabolizes TAK-375 to M-II with lesser involvement of CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 in the generation of subsequent metabolites. Summary & Conclusions

Oral administration of TAK-375 to rats for 2 weeks with dosing up to 2000 mg/kg/day produced excessive mortality, clinical signs involving ataxic gait and chromaturia with dosing > 600 mg/kg/day and reductions in bodyweight gain in males at the highest dose and females in all dose groups, partly explained by reductions in food consumption. Mild hematologic effects were observed at 2000 mg/kg/day but clinical chemistry parameters were significantly altered A/G ratio, and plasma glucose. Dose dependent increase in cholesterol] was observed. Increases were also found in triglycerides, BUN, creatinine, total bilirubin and ALT at TAK-375 dose increased or at the 2000 mg/kg/day level only. The liver appeared to be the most sensitive organ for toxicity with increased liver weight observed with dosing > 200 mg/kg/day which correlated with the microscopic findings of centrilobular hypertrophy. Examination by electron microscopy revealed an increase in smooth endoplasmic reticulum which supports an explanation of a drug-related enzyme induction though inclusion bodies were considered to be a sign of toxicity. Focal necrosis of liver hepatocytes, described as minimal in severity, was noted in two male rats at the 2000 mg/kg/day TAK-375 level. Increased adrenal gland weight was noted in both males and females and correlates with adrenal cortex vacuolation found on histologic analysis. Decreased thymus weights observed with 2000 mg/kg/day dosing in both males and females was on microscopic assessment found to represent lymphocytic necrosis and is a probable response to stress encountered at this dose.

Thus, target organs of TAK-375 in a 2-week rat study appear to be the CNS, liver, adrenal gland, thyroid gland and thymus with liver demonstrating greatest sensitivity. The greater toxic responses observed in females appears to be explainable by the nearly 2-fold increase in exposure to TAK-375 found in the toxicokinetic analysis of plasma in satellite groups. A NOAEL would appear to exist at 200 mg/kg/day with a NOEL of 30 mg/kg/day.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The nonclinical toxicology program consisted of repeat-dose toxicity studies of up to 13 weeks duration in mice, 26 weeks in rats and 39-weeks in monkey. Dogs were not utilized due to the near complete first pass effect and other differences in metabolism when compared to humans. The Sponsor additionally evaluated TAK-375 in genetic toxicology assays, 2-year carcinogenicity bioassays in mice and rats, and a full reproductive toxicology assessment using rat and rabbit as models.

Chronic treatment with TAK-375 produced evidence of CNS toxicity in all species when given at a relatively high dose. In the mouse, decreased spontaneous locomotor activity was noted at a dose level > 300 mg/kg/day while oligopnea was observed in males at and above this dose though in females it was seen only at 1000 mg/kg/day. Mice also exhibited prone or lateral position at a dose level > 300 mg/kg/day in males and at 1000 mg/kg/day in females. These responses fully resolved within several days of treatment. In the rat, decreased spontaneous locomotor activity was also noted at doses > 600 mg/kg/day in both Sprague-Dawley and Wistar strains. Salivation was observed with doses > 40 mg/kg/day but this was considered to be due to the bitterness of the test article and therefore not a manifestation of toxicity. Administration of TAK-375 to the monkey produced a reduction in spontancous locomotion when measured | hr post administration of dose levels of > 12 mg/kg/day and which were observed throughout the 39-week study. An increased incidence and severity of emesis was also commonly observed in monkey treated with doses 2 50 mg/kg/day. Convulsions were seen in 2 male monkeys at the 200 mg/kg/day dose level on a single occasion and were preceded by observations of decreased muscle tone and oligopnea. These animals did not exhibit convulsive behavior subsequently.

Hematologic changes that were notable or common between species included an increase in platelets observed across species. In mice this occurred with dosing > 1000 mg/kg/day while in rat an increase was noted with TAK-375 doses > 150 mg/kg/day in females in the chronic study and at > 600 mg/kg/day in both sexes in a shorter 13-week study. Although no statistically significant changes in platelets were found when analyzed by group in monkeys, 2/4 high dose (200 mg/kg/day) males had platelet counts that were above the historical data range. Female cynomolgus monkeys did not show this effect at the highest dose studied. Mild reductions (<5%) in red blood cell parameters (RBC, hemoglobin

and hematocrit) were noted in rats in the chronic study at the maximum dose level of 150 mg/kg/day and were indicative of a mild anemia with consequent histopathologic finding of splenic cell proliferation and increased spleen weight. This observation was not noted in either mouse or monkey animal models. In the chronic 39-week study of TAK-375 in the monkey, a dose-related but mild decrease in APTT was statistically significant at the 200 mg/kg/day level. Although dismissed by the Sponsor, 4/8 animals at this dose level were reported to have values below the historical control range.

Genetic toxicology

A standard battery of genetic toxicity tests comprising a bacterial reverse mutation assay, in vitro chromosomal aberration assay in CHL cells, and in vivo micronucleus assays in mice and rats were conducted with TAK-375. Several additional tests including an in vitro cell gene mutation test and an in vivo, in vitro unscheduled DNA synthesis assay in rat hepatocytes were subsequently conducted for further informational support. The Sponsor also performed follow-up studies to demonstrate assay exposure to TAK-375 metabolites when the parent compound was incubated with the S9 liver fraction containing rat hepatic microsomes. All studies with the exception of the in vitro chromosomal aberration were judged to be negative for evidence of genotoxicity. The in vitro chromosomal aberration study conducted in CHL cells demonstrated an increase in structural and numerical! aberrations with exposure to TAK-375 in direct assays of 24 and 48 hr as well as a +S9 activation condition. This assay failed to provide an adequate evaluation of the clastogenic potential of TAK-375 in the —S9 activation condition due to the inappropriate use of a positive control which is known to require metabolic activation (i.e. +S9 condition). Thus, the lack of clastogenicity demonstrated by TAK-375-exposed cells in this condition cannot be assured due to the failure of the positive control. Although the weight of evidence suggests TAK-375 is not genotoxic, the Sponsor did not provide adequate data to explain the positive result obtained. As such, a genotoxic effect cannot be ruled out and should be included in the label as such unless a convincing explanation is brought forward by the Sponsor or the study is repeated without demonstration of clastogenic effects.

Carcinogenicity

The tumorigenic potential of TAK-375 was assessed in B6C3F, mice as well as Sprague-Dawley rats in 2 year bioassays. TAK-375 doses used in the mouse study were 0, 30, 100, 300 or 1000 mg/kg/day by oral gavage. Dosage levels in the rat study were 0, 15, 60, 250 or 1000 mg/kg/day by oral gavage. Plasma concentrations of TAK-375 and metabolites M-I through M-IV were assessed on Day 1, Week 13 and Week 52.

In the mouse study, excessive mortality was observed at the 1000 mg/kg/day level which necessitated the carly termination of this group at Week 94. The presumptive cause of death at this dose was the devclopment of hepatic tumors. In surviving animals hepatic tumors were found in males and females treated with doses of > 300 mg/kg/day. These

tumors consisted of hepatic adenomas, carcinomas and blastomas in males while in females adenoma was demonstrated with TAK-375 dose levels of > 300 mg/kg/day and carcinoma was observed only in the 1000 mg/kg/day female group. Combining adenoma and carcinoma in males reduced the level at which a significant difference in tumors was found to 100 mg/kg/day, relative to control group animals. The NOAEL dose in male mice was 30 mg/kg/day and was associated with a plasma concentration which provides a 103-fold safety margin for TAK-375 compared with the expected human clinical exposure while for the active metabolite M-II the safety margin is 3-fold over the human exposure and the margin for M-IV is 64-fold. The NOAEL dose for tumors in female mice was 100 mg/kg/day which provides a safety margin of 827-fold for TAK-375 and 12-fold for M-II over the expected human therapeutic exposure while M-IV has a 105-fold margin. Evidence for hepatic enzyme induction was suggested in both species with marked reductions in plasma exposure with dose levels of > 100 mg/kg/day in both males and females.

Administration of TAK-375 to rats for up to 104 weeks at dose levels of 0, 15, 60, 250 or 1000 mg/kg/day was associated with excessive mortality in the 1000 mg/kg/day group females which required termination of this group during Week 94. Early mortality with dose levels < 250 mg/kg/day were ascribed to the development of pituitary tumors, while at 1000 mg/kg/day mortality was considered to be due to TAK-375-induced hepatic tumors or intoxication. The latter explanation was given for deaths early in the study period and occurring shortly after administration of the test article. TAK-375-induced hepatic tumors were found in males treated with > 250 mg/kg/day (adenomas at > 250 mg/kg/day, carcinomas at 1000 mg/kg/day) and in females treated with > 60 mg/kg/day (adenomas > 60 mg/kg/day, carcinomas at 1000 mg/kg/day). Reproductive and developmental toxicology

The effects of TAK-375 on reproductive potential as well as embryofetal development and pre- and postnatal development was studied in the Sprague-Dawley rat and Japanese White rabbit (the latter in the embryofetal study only).

Fertility and early embryonic development: Administration of TAK-375 by oral gavage at dosc levels of 0, 6, 60, or 600 mg/kg/day to males 4 weeks prior to mating through mating and in females from 2 weeks prior to mating through Day 6 of pregnancy. Mortality was observed at the high dose which was unexpected due to previous experience with this dose in repeat-dose toxicology studies.
