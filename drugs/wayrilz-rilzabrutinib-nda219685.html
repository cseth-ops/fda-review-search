Safety pharmacology

A core battery of safety pharmacology studies evaluated the potential effects of rilzabrutinib on cardiovascular, respiratory, and neurologic endpoints. These studies included an in vitro human ether-à-go-go related gene (hERG) assay, an in vivo assessment of the cardiovascular system in dogs, and in vivo assessments of the CNS and respiratory system in Sprague Dawley rats. Nonclinical ADME / toxicokinetics

13.1.4.1. Nonclinical Pharmacokinetic Parameters of Rilzabrutinib Rilzabrutinib is a small molecule combination of E (up to 90%) and Z isomers and R/S enantiomers (predominantly R). The Z isomer is mostly converted to E isomer following oral dosing in rats and humans. Both E and Z isomers have comparable pharmacological activity. The R enantiomer has greater durability of pharmacological potency, but there is negligible conversion of R to S in vivo. In addition, physiochemical properties of rilzabrutinib include high hydrophobicity and pH-dependent solubility, which appear to dictate its absorption, distribution, metabolism, and excretion (ADME) profile in both animals and humans, resulting in minimal renal clearance. The pharmacokinetic and toxicokinetic properties of rilzabrutinib were addressed primarily in rats, dogs, mice, rabbits, and monkeys. Single oral/IV dose PK studies found rilzabrutinib to have a rapid rate of absorption, metabolism, and clearance rate and variable bioavailability in all test species. The half-life of rilzabrutinib in single dose studies ranged from 0.6 to 2.6 hrs. (Table 41). The shortest t1/2 in all test species occurred following IV and low oral doses and t1/2 appeared to be longer after larger oral doses. In single dose studies, a 5-fold increase in dose was associated with a 10-fold increase in exposure (20 mg/kg versus 100 mg/kg) in rats. In contrast, the increase in exposure was dose proportional in repeat dose studies. Rilzabrutinib bioavailability in rats increased from 12.5% at 20 mg/kg to 52.7% at 100 mg/kg in single dose

PK studies. Similar findings were also seen in dogs where the bioavailability increased from 7.5% at 20 mg/kg to 28.2% at 100 mg/kg (Table 41).

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

Study 1281-16101: Six-Month Oral GLP Toxicity Study of Rilzabrutinib in Rats

Key Study Findings • Target organs of toxicity included brain and gastrointestinal tract. • Rilzabrutinib-related mortality was noted in five females and 1 male at high dose (HD) but cause of death was not determined except for a HD female that was linked to brain inflammation at exposures 12x higher than the MRHD exposure. • Microscopic findings included: – Brain: Minimal to moderate mixed cell inflammation in meninges, neutrophilic inflammation in neuropil, increased periventricular IBA-1 positivity (microgliosis) and GFAP positivity (astrocyte increase) indicative of neurotoxicity that were partially resolved at the end of recovery. Similar findings were observed in the 13-week toxicology study in rats. – Gastrointestinal system: Hemorrhage/inflammation in stomach, duodenum, and esophagus (inflammation only at HD in two females) occurred at exposures that were approximately 9 to 12 times higher than the MRHD exposure. • Overall, administration of rilzabrutinib shows dose-dependent effects across multiple organ systems. Brain findings suggest neurotoxicity with partial recovery, consistent with previous 13-week study in rats. Inflammatory changes (neutrophil infiltration, microglial activation) in brains occurred without evidence of neuronal damage or degeneration. The occurrence of microgliosis in the absence a direct effect on neurons is consistent with the reported BTK expression pattern in the brain (higher microglial expression, lower neuronal expression). – Thyroid changes indicate potential endocrine disruption, with some sex-specific effects. Liver findings suggest adaptive responses even at lower doses. Gastrointestinal effects, observed at high exposures, imply potential for mucosal irritation and/or damage.

• The no observed adverse effect level (NOAEL) was defined at 50 mg/kg/day based on mortality, inflammation of meninges/neuropil and hemorrhage/inflammation of stomach at higher doses with corresponding AUC0-24 2060 and 5760 ng*h/mL in males and females. Exposures at the NOAEL approximated the clinical exposures at the MRHD of 400 mg BID, based on AUC.

Good Laboratory Practice Compliance Yes.

Carcinogenicity

Study 1281-20014: Twenty-Six Week Oral GLP Carcinogenicity Study of Rilzabrutinib in Tg-RasH2 Mice

Key Study Findings • Tg-RasH2 mice (25/sex/group) were administered rilzabrutinib at 0, 30, 100, or 300 mg/kg/day by oral gavage in 0.5% aqueous methylcellulose (400 cps) in deionized water for up to 6 months. • There was no rilzabrutinib-related statistically significant dose response in mortality or increased incidence of neoplastic lesions in male and female Tg-RasH2 mice at doses up to 300 mg/kg/day. • FDA statistical analysis did not identify imbalances in tumor incidence that were statistically significant by both trend and pairwise comparison. • The positive control of urethane produced expected increases in tumors.

Good Laboratory Practice Compliance Yes.

Executive Carcinogenicity Assessment Committee Conclusions • The Committee concluded that the carcinogenicity study was adequate. • The Committee concluded that there was no evidence of drug-related neoplasms in male and female mice in the 6-month study in Tg-rasH2 mice.
