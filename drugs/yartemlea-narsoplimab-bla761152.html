Pediatric — 4938-1

Safety requirement, 505(o)(3) · approved 2025 · Draft Protocol Submission 06/2026 · Final Protocol Submission 12/2026 · Study/Trial Completion 12/2033 · Final Report Submission 12/2034

Establish a registry to characterize the long-term safety of narsoplimab- wuug in at least 50 adult and pediatric patients with transplant-associated thrombotic microangiopathy (TA-TMA), with at least 1 year of follow-up. Nonclinical ADME / toxicokinetics

Systemic exposure to narsoplimab, as measured by Cmax and AUC(0­72), generally increased with increasing dose from 50 to 300 mg/kg SC but the increases were less than dose-proportional and exposure values decreased on GD 15 compared to GD 6. Narsoplimab exposure after 300 mg/kg IV administration was at least 7-fold increase compared to the SC administration. There were no signs of accumulation after four doses.

GLP compliance: Yes

Reviewer’s Comment

A staggered dosing design was employed to accommodate a robust antidrug antibody (ADA) response identified in preliminary studies. Lectin inhibition by narsoplimab was confirmed in the course of the definitive study. Subcutaneous administration provided the more relevant data as it utilized the clinical route of administration and achieved an ~8-fold multiple of clinical AUC exposure. The intravenous route did not result in clear maternal toxicity but was potentially associated with an increase in postimplantation loss associated with a very high Cmax and AUC relative to clinical exposure.

Key study findings:

Subcutaneous administration:

• There were no clear drug-related adverse findings in dams or fetuses with subcutaneous administration. Transient reductions in maternal weight gain were not considered as indicative of maternal toxicity by the reviewer. • The maternal and fetal NOAEL at 150 mg/kg SC provided ~8-times the exposure at the MRHD, narsoplimab based on extrapolated AUC0­168h. Intravenous administration:

• Increased postimplantation loss occurred on GD 7/10 (+88%) and GD 11/14 (+41%) but not on GD 15/18 at ~91- times the exposure at the MRHD (AUC). This finding is consistent with a drug action during early gestation.

• TA-TMA is a challenging complication of HSCT associated with TA-TMA is a rare, serious and life-threatening significant morbidity and mortality. The complex pathophysiology condition. It is a major cause of morbidity and and multiple contributing factors converge on a final pathway mortality post allogenic HSCT in both adult and involving endothelial cell injury and complement activation. TA-TMA pediatric populations. is characterized by microangiopathic hemolytic anemia, thrombocytopenia, elevated serum lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) and presence of schistocytes on peripheral blood smear. The gold standard for diagnosis is based on characteristic histological tissue findings; however, the high risk for procedural complications and bleeding limits the ability to obtain a tissue diagnosis. • The incidence of TA-TMA varies among studies due to different Analysis of criteria used for diagnosis. There are several risk factors described Condition in the literature for the development of TA-TMA include older age, female sex, advanced primary cancer disease, unrelated donor transplants, high-dose conditioning regimen, human leukocyte antigen (HLA) mismatch, nonmyeloablative transplants, total body irradiation, calcineurin use, acute graft-versus host disease and infection. • The clinical presentation ranges from mild, self-limited disease to more serious disease involving multi-organ involvement (kidneys, gastrointestinal tract, central nervous system, heart, lung, and serosal surfaces), with severe disease leading to multi-organ failure and death.
