Reviewer comment
the sponsor’s characterization of maternal toxicity as “Dose-related” exceeds the effects observed. There were no significant drug-related effects characteristic of maternal toxicity at the 10-mg/kg dose. There was evidence of maternal toxicity at the 30-mg/kg dose, e.g., significant decrease in body wt gain. The sponsor’s revision suggesting an association between maternal toxicity and some of the developmental effects observed in rats is not supported by the data. 4. The purpose of the study was to determine the effects of repeated administration of aripiprazole on 5HT\, and SHT2, receptors in rat brain. For comparison, the effects of repeat-dose administration of risperidone on these receptors were also assessed.

Methods: aripiprazole [0, 10, and 30 mg/kg] and risperidone [0.3 mg/kg] were administered to male Wistar rats [6/grp] by gavage [vehicle: 5% gum arabic in 0.9% saline; vol: 5 mL/kg] for 21 days. The HD of aripiprazole was selected on the basis of Crux. Previous data indicated that the C,., achieved at 30 mg/kg was similar to the C,y, associated with the maximum recommended human dose [30 mg/day].

Animals were sacrificed 3 days after the last dose of aripiprazole or risperidone. Hippocampus and frontal cortex were dissected from each brain and maintained at -80° C until analyzed. Binding to SHT,, and 5HT2, were determined in hippocampus and frontal cortex, respectively.

Results: aripiprazole had no effect on the Byux or Ky for either SHT,,4 or SHT24. Pharmacology summary and conclusions

The sponsor submitted (1) a pharmacology study assessing the effects of repeated doses of aripiprazole on 5HT,, and SHT3, receptors in rat brain and (2) proposed revisions to labeling.

In the pharmacology study, aripiprazole had no significant effect on the Bua, or Ky for either the SHT,4 or 5HT2, receptor. Risperidone significantly increased the Bmax for the SHT, receptor [in hippocampus], but had no effect on the SHT2, receptor.

The sponsor had one comment on the Pharmacodynamics section of labeling [Revision No. 4]. The sponsor discussed the possible mechanism(s) of action of underlying the antipsychotic effects of aripiprazole, and noted that “Aripiprazole appears to exert its effects through a partial agonist action at D, ..and 5-HT,,.. receptors and an antagonist action at 5-HT2, receptors”.

Reproductive and developmental toxicology

The only issue is one of labeling. The sponsor made 4 comments to this section of labeling. 1. Revision No. 25

The sponsor provided relevant historical control data for fused sternabrae in New Zealand White rabbits from studies conducted in 1988-1994 [84 litters, 667 fetuses] in the same laboratory in which the embryofetal development study in rabbits was conducted. The overall incidence of fused sternabrae was 0.75%. The highest fetal and litter incidences were 1.98 and 15.38%, respectively. The sponsor also provided historical control data in NZW rabbits collected by MARTA [Middle Atlantic Reproduction and Teratology Association] and MTA [Midwest Teratology Association]. According to the MARTA/MTA database [data were not provided for each source separately], the incidence of fused sternabrae was 0.674 + 0.92% [mean + SD] for fetuses and 4.635 + 5.94% (mean + SD) for litters. The maximum incidence was 5 and 23.5% for affected fetuses and litters, respectively.

The sponsor noted that “Both fetal and litter incidences of fused sternabrae at 30 mg/kg/day of _ aripiprazole (3.7 and 23.5%, respectively) in the Segment II study in rabbits are within the upper limit of the historical control range (as reported by MARTA/MTA) for this fetal skeletal altemationin NZW = rabbits”. .

Reviewer comment: the fetal and litter incidences of fused sternabrae observed at the mid- [30 mg/kg] and high-dose [100 mg/kg] of aripiprazole exceed the concurrent control and the laboratory’s own HC incidences; therefore, labeling should be revised to indicate the occurrence of fused sternabrae at both doses. The sponsor provided interspecies comparisons based on plasma AUC data [for parent compound]. The AUC data were obtained from a 13-day TK study conducted at doses of 10, 30, and 100 mg/kg p.o. [(AUC@.24m) = 16442, 21150, and 85961 ngehr/mL, respectively]. Whether or not this was due to high variability at the lower exposure is not clear.
