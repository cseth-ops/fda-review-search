Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

This NDA should be approved from a nonclinical perspective. The Applicant did not conduct any nonclinical studies to support the application. The initial nonclinical submission for approval of “Fluorodopa F 18 Injection” was reviewed by Dr. Sally Hargus. Nonclinical recommended the approval of the NDA based upon: • Structural similarities between 18F-FDOPA and levodopa. • The availability of extensive nonclinical and clinical databases on levodopa. • The availability of a nonclinical pharmacology data on 18F-FDOPA. • The safety of 18F-FDOPA demonstrated by historical use of levodopa for treatment of Parkinson’s disease at prescribed doses of up to 2000 mg per day for chronic use (b) (4) (Carbidopa/Levodopa Monograph 2013), which is more than -fold the one-time 18 (b) intravenously-administered F-FDOPA mass dose of (4) mg or less.

Moreover, in 2000, FDA conducted a multidisciplinary review and evaluation of 18F-FDOPA injection in preparation for a public meeting with the clinical PET community. NDA [18F]Fluoro-L-DOPA DMIRDP

Overall Summary:

This review has relied heavily on the similarity between L-DOPA and FDOPA metabolism, and the way both are handled by the body. That FDOPA acts as a substrate for AAAD, and has been used as a biochemical marker of the dopaminergic system is well documented. What is less clear is the detailed clinical interpretation of the results meaning. Although the number of both preclinical and clinical publications on FDOPA is impressive, results interpretation is confounded by our lack of how disease conditions, technology and methodology affect the fundamental mechanistic assumptions governing the use of FDOPA.

From a regulatory perspective, the issue of whether pharmacology/toxicology studies typically conducted and considered critical to support the safety of an NDA have been reported in the literature was examined. No study specifically addressing the toxicology, carcinogenicity, genotoxicity, reproductive toxicology or immunotoxicology of FDOPA were identified in the literature. However, extensive clinical experience without reports of safety related concerns, similarity in the metabolism of LDOPA and FDOPA, safety data base for LDOPA, together with the fact that FDOPA is administered in low mass dose, ameliorate lack of data for the time being. Therefore the need for typical pharmacology and toxicology studies can be waived.

Integrated nonclinical assessment

Fluorodopa F 18 Injection is a metabolic tracer for dopaminergic activity, based on the biochemistry and physiology of striatal function. The receptor biology and biochemical transformations of Fluorodopa F 18 Injection that occur in striatal neurons form the basis of the mechanism of action of [18F]FDOPA in the brain. Prior to the submission of NDA 200655, typical pharmacology and toxicology studies required for an NDA application were waived, based on the Agency’s review of the

NDA #200655 Sally Hargus, PhD, Reviewer

publicly available nonclinical information, which was conducted by Dr. Adebayo Laniyonu (1). The available information was found to be acceptable in lieu of conventional Pharmacology and Toxicology studies for Fluorodopa F 18 Injection based on the following factors: 1) Clinical experience with Fluorodopa F 18 Injection, without reports of safety concerns; 2) Comprehensive Levodopa historical database and 30+ years experience in chronic clinical use at daily doses more than 1000-fold the one-time [18F]FDOPA (b) (4) dose of mg; 3) Similarity in the metabolism and disposition of Levodopa and [18F]FDOPA; and 4) Low mass dose of [18F]FDOPA administered in PET studies. Two literature-based reports and a non-GLP toxicity study were reviewed in this document. The information in the reports provided additional, albeit minimal, evidence that Fluorodopa F 18 Injection is safe for the maximum recommended intravenous mass (b) (4) dose of mg and radiologic dose of 5 mCi. Based on the factors listed above, I recommend that NDA 200655 may be approved from the Pharmacology and Toxicology perspectives.

Overview and Objective

The protocol for Study #18 was submitted by the Applicant in response to the 9/15/16 CR letter issued by the FDA following the review of the re-submitted NDA 200655 containing Study #17. Study #18 was designed to address deficiencies cited in the CR letter and planned to recruit a minimum of 50 patients with possible PS. The primary objective of the study was to assess the imaging performance 18F-FDOPA PET in 3 blinded readers relative to a clinical reference standard generated through at least 6 months of patient follow-up.

Trial Design

Study #18 was a prospective, single center study. Binary interpretations of 18F-FDOPA scans by 3 independent readers were analyzed for agreement with a clinical reference standard of PS or non-PS, as established by a minimum of 6 months of patient follow-up.

Clinical safety

8.2.1. Review of the Safety Database

Study #18 added 68 patients to the prior experience in 53 patients who were administered F-FDOPA in Study #17. Patients from Study #17 are included in the safety database because deficiencies only impacted efficacy assessment rather than safety assessment in that study. In total, Study #17 and Study #18 provided a combined experience in 121 patients who had safety assessment procedures prespecified in study protocols and safety data recorded on detailed case report forms. In prior review cycles, the Applicant submitted a retrospective chart review from their own institution (Study #15). In Study #15, an additional 196 subjects were injected with 18F-FDOPA, including 185 patients and 11 normal control subjects. The Applicant reported no adverse reactions in Study #15. However, since there was no prespecified safety assessment protocol, this limited safety information from Study #15 is only considered as supportive. In addition to the safety data from the Applicant’s studies, potential safety issues from published literature were assessed.

8.2.2. Safety Results

In Study #17 and #18, all patients were observed for any physical discomfort during 18F-FDOPA PET visits and adverse events were collected. After the PET scans, patients were contacted by phone within 24 hours to collect information regarding any additional adverse events.

Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment

Parkinsonism is a general clinical term used to describe any combination of bradykinesia, rest tremor, rigidity, and postural instability. Movement disorders featuring Parkinsonism can be divided into those that are associated with degeneration of dopaminergic neurons (referred to as Parkinsonian syndromes (PS)) and those that are not (referred to as non-Parkinsonian syndromes (non-PS)).

PS includes progressive and serious disorders such as Parkinson’s disease, multiple system atrophy (MSA), progressive supranuclear palsy (PSP), and corticobasal degeneration (CBD). Non-PS includes essential tremor, dystonic tremor, psychogenic Parkinsonism, drug-induced Parkinsonism, vascular Parkinsonism, and other conditions.

Diagnostic distinction between PS and non-PS is clinically important because of respective differences in patient management. Due to the infeasibility of histopathological confirmation, differentiation of patients with PS from those with non-PS continues to be based on clinical evaluation. Unfortunately, overlap in clinical features between PS and non-PS often makes distinguishing between them challenging. Strategies to address such diagnostic uncertainty are limited and not ideal, such as trial of levodopa therapy and continued long-term clinical follow-up.

F-FDOPA PET provides an adjunctive non-invasive approach to evaluation of this patient population. As a radiolabeled analog of levodopa, 18F-FDOPA uptake in the striatum can be imaged by PET to assess dopaminergic neurons. In the setting of PS, 18F-FDOPA PET imaging should show decreased striatal signal. In the setting of non-PS, 18F-FDOPA should show normal striatal signal.

In this resubmission, the Applicant provided results from an adequate and well-controlled prospective trial (Study #18) that addressed deficiencies noted by the FDA in prior review cycles. A clinical reference standard was established in 56 patients with suspected PS through at least 6 months of clinical follow-up by a movement disorder specialist who remained blinded to F-FDOPA PET results. Visual interpretation of 18F-FDOPA PET imaging with a binary reading method demonstrated a high negative percent agreement (with a 95% confidence interval as low as 61% and as high as 99%) in all 3 blinded, independent readers relative to the clinical reference standard but lower positive percent agreement (with a 95% confidence interval as low as 31% and as high as 87%) among these same 3 readers. The review team concluded that these results adequately support the effectiveness of 18F-FDOPA PET for use an adjunct to other diagnostic evaluations in patients with suspected PS.
