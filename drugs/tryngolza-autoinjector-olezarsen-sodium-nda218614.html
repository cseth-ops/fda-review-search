General (repeat-dose) toxicology

The general toxicity of olezarsen has been characterized in repeat-dose toxicity studies in CD-1 mice and cynomolgus monkeys in studies of up to 26- and 39-weeks duration, respectively, with once-weekly SC injections. These studies included 13- and 26-week recovery periods in the mouse and monkey, respectively. The no observed adverse effect levels (NOAELs), basis for the NOAELs, and the safety margins based on body surface area (BSA) are captured in Table 95 below. The summary table is followed by more detailed reviews of the pivotal toxicology studies conducted with olezarsen that support this application. Drug-related effects consistent with the expected toxicities of an ASO with a phosphorothioate backbone were evident in the liver, kidney, and spleen. Tissue effects at the injection site were also observed. The most notable effect was thrombocytopenia, observed in both the mouse and NHP.

Carcinogenicity

Carcinogenicity studies were not conducted for olezarsen. However, carcinogenicity studies were conducted with the active unconjugated ASO volanesorsen. Two-year carcinogenicity studies were conducted with volanesorsen in CD-1 mice (study no. 304801-AS15) and Sprague-Dawley rats (study no. 304801-AS16) to assess its carcinogenic potential. Results from these studies are summarized below. In the 2-year mouse study with volanesorsen, dosages of 6, 25, and 40 mg/kg/wk were administered SC. There was reduced survival attributed to cardiomyocyte degeneration associated with inflammatory cell infiltrates at dosages >25 mg/kg/wk leading to early termination of these groups (Day 625, males at 25 mg/kg/wk; Day 617, females at 25 mg/kg/wk; Day 507, males at 40 mg/kg/wk and Day 487, females 40 mg/kg/wk). Drug-related neoplastic observations were noted at all dosages evaluated. In the liver of male mice, hepatocellular adenomas/carcinomas were increased at dosages >25 mg/kg/wk, and multicentric hemangiomas/hemangiosarcomas were increased at all doses. In female mice, multicentric histiocytic sarcomas were increased at all doses (including in the group dosed with the mouse- specific oligonucleotide), and pituitary gland adenomas were increased at 25 mg/kg/wk. The liver adenomas, histiocytic sarcomas, and hemangiosarcomas likely reflect the pro-inflammatory properties of the phosphorothioate ASO drug class and the greater sensitivity of rodents to these effects. Pituitary tumors are most likely a reflection of the high background rate of hyperplasia in this species, which is also a relatively common background lesion in aging mice. Thus, although the increased incidences of these tumors are drug-related, the relevance to the clinical population is likely to be limited. As a result of the reduction in survival at the mid dose/high dose (HD),

dose-related increased incidences of cardiovascular effects, and neoplastic effects at all dosages, a NOAEL for the induction of neoplasia was not identified. In the 2-year rat study with volanesorsen, dosages of 0.2, 1, and 5 mg/kg/wk were administered. Survival was reduced in males at 5 mg/kg/wk due to either masses at injection sites or chronic progressive nephropathy. Drug-related neoplasms observed were increases in the incidences of malignant fibrous histiocytomas (MFH) in the skin/subcutis of the injection sites at dosages >1 mg/kg/wk in both sexes. The injection site MFH neoplasms with volanesorsen treatment in rats may be attributable to chronic inflammatory reactions associated with repeated SC injections into a limited number of sites and is not likely to be clinically relevant. The NOAEL for the induction of neoplasms in the 2-year rat study is considered the low dose (LD) of 0.2 mg/kg/wk (0.1x the maximum recommended human dose based on BSA comparison of administered dose) based on the increased incidence of MFH at ≥1 mg/kg/wk. In summary, the carcinogenicity studies identified increased incidences of neoplasms in both mice and rats. Reproductive and developmental toxicology

A GLP-compliant fertility and early embryonic developmental study was conducted for olezarsen in CD-1 mice (study no. 678354-AS08) because of decreased prostate/seminal vesicle weight and decreased sperm count from the combined fertility and embryo-fetal development mouse study with volanesorsen. Studies evaluating embryofetal development and pre-/postnatal development were not conducted with olezarsen as there were no effects on parameters evaluated in these studies conducted with the unconjugated ASO volanesorsen.
