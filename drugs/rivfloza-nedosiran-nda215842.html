Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

13.1. Summary Review of Studies Submitted With the Investigational New Drug Application

13.1.1. Pharmacology

13.1.1.1. Primary Pharmacology

• Nedosiran is a dicer-substrate small interfering ribonucleic acid (siRNA) oligonucleotide conjugated to N-acetyl-D-galactosamine (GalNAc) to enable uptake by the asialoglycoprotein receptor, which is highly expressed in the hepatocytes. It is designed to silence the expression of lactate dehydrogenase A (LDHA) messenger ribonucleic acid (mRNA), and thereby reduce production of oxalate in the liver. • The target sequence of nedosiran in LDHA mRNA is conserved in humans and nonhuman primates, but not in mice, and thus, nedosiran is fully active in cynomolgus monkeys but inactive in mice. • Administration of nedosiran (1, 3, 6 mg/kg) to a knock-in mouse model expressing human LDHA mRNA resulted in dose-dependent knockdown of LDHA mRNA in the liver that was sustained for 4 weeks postdose. • A single subcutaneous (SC) dose of nedosiran (1, 3 mg/kg) in cynomolgus monkeys resulted in reduction of LDHA mRNA by 28% and 72%, indicating that a single low dose of 1 mg/kg (human equivalent dose 0.3 mg/kg) is a pharmacologically active dose. • In a mouse model of PH1 induced by knockout of the alanine-glyoxylate aminotransferase 1 gene, a single SC dose of 0.5, 1, or 5 mg/kg DCR-m355 (the mouse active analog of nedosiran) reduced urinary oxalate concentration (normalized to creatinine); the reduction was sustained for 4 weeks, indicating that suppression of LDHA mRNA correlates with reduction of oxalate to near normal levels. • In a mouse model of PH2 generated using siRNA-mediated knockdown of the Grhpr gene, urinary oxalate concentration was reduced to baseline levels after three weekly doses of DCR-m355 (10 mg/kg), indicating that inhibition of hepatic LDHA mRNA expression reduces oxalate production.

• In a mouse model of PH3 generated by drinking 1% hydroxyproline, SC administration of DCR-m355 at a dose level of 5 mg/kg weekly for 3 weeks prevented a hydroxyproline- induced elevation of creatinine-normalized urinary oxalate concentration, demonstrating the potential of LDHA mRNA knockdown for the treatment of PH. • In CD-1 mice subjected to running on a treadmill, administration of DCR-m355 (5 mg/kg) once per week for 4 weeks resulted in significant LDHA mRNA knockdown (>95%) in the liver, with no effect on skeletal muscle LDHA mRNA expression or changes in circulating lactate, pyruvate and creatine kinase levels, demonstrating selective inhibition of LDHA mRNA in the liver without alterations of skeletal muscle LDHA isoenzyme activity.

General (repeat-dose) toxicology

General toxicity studies were conducted for durations up to 26 weeks in juvenile mice and 39 weeks in juvenile and adult monkeys. The ages of the animals in the nonclinical program were appropriate to support the safety evaluation for the indicated clinical population. Key findings in the pivotal toxicology studies are discussed below.

Liver Histological changes in the liver (Kupffer cells), which included minimal to moderate macrophage basophilic hypertrophy, were observed following administration of nedosiran at a dose level of 300 mg/kg administered every 4 weeks for up to 9 months in juvenile monkey. Additional findings included minimal to mild hepatocyte basophilia at doses ≥100 mg/kg, and minimal hypertrophy and mixed inflammatory cell infiltrates in the liver at 300 mg/kg. Electron- microscopic evaluations of the liver revealed an increase in the number and size of clear vacuoles and lysosomes within the hepatocytes, but there was no degeneration or necrosis. During the 8-week recovery period, the findings in the liver were still evident at a dose of 300 mg/kg. The hypertrophic changes in the Kupffer cell/macrophage in the liver may be due to accumulation of the test article and are considered a class effect of oligonucleotides.

Lymph Nodes and Testes Microscopic findings of minimal to moderate macrophage basophilic hypertrophy were observed in the mesenteric lymph nodes at doses ≥30 mg/kg, as well as in the mandibular lymph nodes and testes at a dose level of 300 mg/kg in the juvenile monkeys, indicating hybridization- independent proinflammatory effects. During the 8-week recovery period, the findings in the lymph nodes and testes were still evident at the high dose of 300 mg/kg, which could be due to accumulation of oligonucleotides in cells with macrophagic lineage. Given nedosiran’s long tissue half-life, several weeks to months may be needed for full recovery.

Injection Site Reactions At the injection sites, minimal to mild macrophage basophilic hypertrophy was observed in the subcutaneous tissues in the juvenile mice and monkeys, which resolved following an 8-week recovery period, indicating hybridization-independent effects.

Findings in Young Adult Monkeys In the young adult cynomolgus monkey study, nedosiran was tested at one dose level of 300 mg/kg once every 4 weeks for 9 months. Key findings, including Kupffer cell basophilic hypertrophy and hepatocyte basophilia in the liver, and macrophage basophilic hypertrophy in the lymph nodes (mesenteric, mandibular), testes, and injection sites, were similar to those observed in the juvenile monkeys. There were no apparent differences in the incidence and/or severity of the findings between the juvenile and young adult monkeys.

Conclusion for General Toxicity Studies Overall, the findings discussed above reflect cellular uptake and accumulation of oligonucleotide and were evident at high dose levels. Carcinogenicity

The carcinogenic potential of nedosiran has not been assessed. The Agency agrees that carcinogenicity studies can be conducted postmarketing. The available data do not raise concern for a risk of carcinogenicity that should delay approval given the serious nature of the disease and limited therapeutic options.
