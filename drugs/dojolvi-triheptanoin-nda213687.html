Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Triheptanoin is not genotoxic and has no effect on fertility. Nonclinical findings of tumorigenicity in the gastrointestinal tract of rats are described.

CLINICAL STUDIES

As described in Section II.6.2.1, Gillingham (2017) (referred to as study 3 in the PI), was considered an adequate and well-controlled study to support use of triheptanoin as a source of calories and fatty acids and is the only clinical data described in this section.

. Descriptive results from Gillingham (2017) considered acceptable to include were the number of events of rhabdomyolysis. Metabolic markers of metabolism (e.g., glucose, insulin, lactate) were described in terms of no differences between the treatment groups. Cardiovascular indices of left ventricular ejection fraction and wall mass on echocardiography and maximal heart rate on treadmill ergometry were described as similar. The product studied in Gillingham (2017) was not the to-be-marketed formulation of DOJOLVI; therefore, the product is referred to by the nonproprietary name of triheptanoin in this section.

HOW SUPPLIED/STORAGE AND HANDLING

The incompatibility of triheptanoin with various materials is emphasized by instructions not to dose or store it in polystyrene or PVC containers and to alert the pharmacist to dispense it only in glass or HDPE bottles.

22. Postmarketing Requirements and Commitments The following PMRs apply to triheptanoin. PMRs • PMR #1 - Conduct a worldwide, single-arm, pregnancy safety study in women exposed to DOJOLVI during pregnancy to assess risk of pregnancy and maternal complications, adverse effects on the developing fetus and neonate, and adverse effects on the infant. Infant outcomes will be assessed through at least the first year of life. Results will be analyzed and reported descriptively. – Rationale: The ability to identify patients with long-chain fatty acid oxidation disorders (LC-FOADs) has improved. Standard of care has relied upon dietary modifications to include higher dosing of naturally occurring medium-chain fat. With improved diagnosis and management of LC-FAOD, there is now a greater possibility that women with LC-FAOD may become pregnant. Ingestion of dietary fat is needed for normal fetal development. As triheptanoin is a synthetic fat not found naturally, it is important to understand the risk of fetal anomalies due to use in pregnancy. Pregnant patient. – As LC-FAODs are rare, this information is unlikely to be obtained in a clinical trial. Therefore, a 10-year (of up to 40 pregnancies) assessment for fetal anomalies is

proposed. This would provide reasonable time and number to identify a high risk for specific patterns of teratogenesis.

Nonclinical ADME / toxicokinetics

Blood samples were collected from the toxicokinetic (TK) animals on days 1, 10, and 270 and analyzed for triheptanoin and its metabolites (heptanoic acid, C4 and C5 ketone bodies). Additionally, trough samples were collected monthly at 30, 60, 90, 120, 150, 180, 210, 240, 270, and 298 days from recovery animals. Systemic exposure to triheptanoin showed a dose-related increase in heptanoic acid, its primary metabolite, as well as a dose-related increase in C5 ketone bodies. The levels of triheptanoin at 10%, 30%, and 50% DCI, as well as the C4 ketone bodies, were not appreciably different from those of controls. The AUC values showed that only heptanoic acid and C5 ketone bodies increased in a dose-related manner similar with the percentage of triheptanoin in the diet. On study day 10, the heptanoic acid AUC values were 4,515 and 4,277 min·µM for the males and

females treated with 10% DCI of triheptanoin, respectively. The levels in the 30% dose group were 48,427 and 66,179 min·µM, and those in the 50% dose group were 288,736 and 285,239 min·µM in males and females, respectively. A similar trend was seen on day 270. The monthly trough plasma concentration values for triheptanoin and the five metabolites in the recovery animals were not substantially above control values or were slightly above in the case of heptanoic acid. Although the metabolites levels increased in a dose-related manner, no evidence of accumulation was detected after 9 months of dosing or after the 28-day recovery period. Table 37.
