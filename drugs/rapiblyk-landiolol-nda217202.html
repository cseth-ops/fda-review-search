Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

The pharmacology/toxicology review was authored by Feleke Eshete, PhD (supervisor/team leader David Carlson, PhD). A maximum clinical dose of landiolol 40 μg/kg/min, equivalent to 57.6 mg/kg/day, is proposed for marketing. The nonclinical pharmacology/toxicology safety evaluation included a rat single dose study of 24 hours of continuous intravenous infusion of doses of 250, 500, & 1,000 mg/kg, and a 4-week dog study of 24 hours-a-day of continuous infusion at 0.04 mg/kg/min (58 mg/day), 0.2 mg/kg/min (288 mg/day), and 1 mg/kg/min (1,440 mg/day). The animal exposures at the NOAEL doses were compared with the exposure in patients with tachycardic AF or AFL [protocol# LDLL600.201] who received the recommended clinical dosing regimen. There was a 2-fold safety margin with the maximum recommended clinical dose compared to the tolerated infusion in rats, and a very high safety margin (136-fold) relative to the NOAEL exposure in the 4-week, 24-hr-a-day infusion study in dogs that simulated prolonged exposure to the proposed dosing regimen.

Safety pharmacology

Transient decrease in spontaneous motor activity and bradypnea were noted at 100 mg/kg of ONO-1101 in mice, and a 300 mg/kg dose was lethal. The NOEL in Irwin test was 30 mg/kg (2.4 mg/kg HED). The test-article did not affect the autonomic function in isolated ileum from Guinea pigs and rabbits at concentrations up to 300 µM (~73-times the Cmax at MRHD). The potential effects of LAN and the M-1 & M-2 metabolites on hERG and other cardiac ion channels were investigated by patch clamping of cells expressing the channels in a study sponsored by the applicant. IC50 of >300 µM (>21µM free) was predicted for LAN, representing 5-fold the Cmax at MRHD. IC50 of >1,000 µM was predicted for the metabolites M-1 and M-2.

Multiple studies were conducted to evaluate the potential effects of ONO-1101 on HR & mean blood pressure (MBP) in conscious rats and ECG parameters in anesthetized/conscious dogs. A 300 mg/kg (162.2 mg/kg/day HED) dose decreased HR and MBP in rats and the animals died within 5 minutes. The NOEL for MBP after infusion was 30 mg/kg/min (HED of 4.7 mg/kg/min), while the NOEL for the bolus dose was 100 mg/kg (HED 16.21 mg/kg). Significant prolongation of the PR interval was reported at ≥0.1 mg/kg with no significant changes in QTc in anesthetized dogs. Transient effects were noted in respiratory rate at the high dose. No effect was observed on HR, ECG parameters and respiratory rate in conscious dogs. Additionally, ONO-1011 (40 µg/kg/min) did not have effect on acetylcholine-induced ova-sensitized Guinea pigs on anesthesia when increase in total pulmonary resistance and decrease in lung compliance were assessed as endpoints.

The effects of ONO-1011 on GI motility was evaluated in mice, and gastric secretion volume, acidity, and pH effects were assessed in rats. Bolus i.v. injections of up to 100 mg/kg to mice had no effect on gastric motility. In rats with ligated pyloric region of the stomach, neither ONO-1011 administered by i.v. bolus injection (30 mg/kg) nor by continuous infusion (up to 10 mg/kg/min) affected gastric secretion volume, acidity, and pH.

The effect of ONO-1011 on urine volume, and urine Na+, K+ Cl- and pH was evaluated after i.v. bolus injection of 30 mg/kg, or a continuous infusion of up to 10 mg/kg/min for 5 hrs. Bolus injection had no effect, but ONO-1011 caused a significant increase in urine volume and Na+, K+ and Cl- after infusion. Urine pH decreased significantly at all doses and there was positive occult blood reaction that was caused by hemolysis at 10 mg/kg/min. Genetic toxicology

AOP Pharmaceuticals sponsored a battery of genotoxicity assays that have been validated during this review. LAN did not show genotoxic potential under the conditions of the assays (details in section 7 Genetic Toxicology).

Reproductive toxicity

The applicant has cited a fertility and early embryonic development study that was conducted in male and female rats at i.v. doses of 25, 50 or 100 mg/kg/day ONO-1101 administered to the male 64 days prior to mating and during mating and to females 15- days before mating and during mating (Nishimura et al, 1997a). Parental death occurred at the high dose and hypoactivity, clonic convulsions, bradypnea/apnea and reddish lacrimation were observed in both sexes at the same dose. Body weight and food consumption were decreased in females during premating and mating periods at the high dose. Treatment did not affect the estrous cycle, mating, and other fertility indices; skeletal or visceral changes were absent in the fetuses. A NOAEL of 50 mg/kg/day for general toxicity in parents, and 100 mg/kg/day for the reproductive performance in parents and for the development of fetuses were identified.

The same doses of ONO-1101 were administered intravenously to pregnant rats and rabbits during the time of organogenesis (Nishimura et al, 1997b). The effect on lactation was also assessed from the day of delivery to Day 21 in rats. Two rats and three rabbits receiving the 100 mg/kg dose, and another rabbit at 50 mg/kg died during the study. Maternal rats and rabbits presented clinical signs of bradypnea, clonic convulsions, and hypoactivity; chromodacryorrhea was reported in rats.

Prenatal and postnatal development

In a study summarized by the applicant (Nishimura, 1997d), intravenous doses of 25, 50 and 100 mg/kg/day ONO-1101 were evaluated from GD17 to PND20 of parturition to examine the effects on pregnancy, delivery, lactation and the effects on postnatal growth and development of the offspring. Pregnant animals in each group delivered naturally and gestation length, birth rate, delivery rate, live birth rate (# live-born pups/implantation sites x 100) were calculated.

Lactation status was inspected from delivery (Day 0) until lactation Day21 after which dams were necropsied. The weaning rate was calculated after culling of pups on postpartum Day4. In addition, the presence of milk spots visible through the abdominal wall of the offspring was taken as an index of the milk secretion ability. External development was examined by checking for separation of auricles on postpartum Day4, eruption of incisors and appearance of abdominal hair on postpartum day 14, separation of eyelids on postpartum Day17, descent of testes on postpartum Day28, and opening of vagina on postpartum Day42. General behavior of animals was evaluated in Irwin test and the test animals were necropsied and organ weights were measured, and the skeletons examined for malformations and ossification. Sensory function and open field tests were assessed on postpartum Day28.

Five animals in the 100 mg/kg group died immediately after administration (2 animals on GD17 and GD21, and 3 animals on lactation day 0 to 5). At necropsy, lungs of animals that died in the 100 mg/kg dose group during the gestation or lactation period were dark red. Hypoactivity, reddish lacrimation, clonic convulsion and bradypnea/apnea were observed after administration of the high dose. Body weight and food consumption at weaning were also lower than the control group. Reddish lacrimation was noted in animals receiving the 50 mg/kg.

One animal each in the 25 and 50 mg/kg dose groups had not given birth by GD24. In both dams, implantation sites were present and total resorption had occurred. Relative kidney weight was increased in the 100 mg/kg dose group and increased relative lung weight and decreased absolute liver weight in the 25 mg/kg dose group.

Viability of offspring on day 4 after birth decreased in the 100 mg/kg/day group, and body weight gain was low during suckling but recovered after weaning. The day 4 survival rate decreased in the 100 mg/kg dose group compared to the control group. The weaning rate and growth rate did not differ between the control group and treatment groups.

Clinical efficacy

Kato et al. 1997.

Study Design

Overview and Objective

Citation: Kato K, Hayakawa H, Atarashi H, et al. Clinical effect of intravenous infusion of landiolol hydrochloride (ONO-1101) on paroxysmal atrial fibrillation and atrial flutter – A phase III double-blind study in comparison with placebo. Rinsho Iyaku (Jpn. J. Clin. Therap. Med.) 1997b;13(19):4903(83)-4924(104).

A randomized placebo-controlled double-blind multi-center parallel clinical trial of landiolol hydrochloride as ONO-1101, in patients with non-perioperative paroxysmal AF and AFL, to assess safety and effectiveness of landiolol for control of heart rate (HR) over 11 minutes of administration and 15 minutes of post-administration monitoring.

Trial Design

Kato et al. 1997 is a randomized placebo-controlled double-blind multi-center parallel clinical superiority trial. Comparison with contemporaneous placebo is appropriate for the assessment of landiolol safety and effectiveness.

Kato et al. 1997 was performed at 57 centers in Japan from 1994 through 1997. Although completed entirely outside of the US, the study is felt to be applicable to the US population as the conditions paroxysmal AF and AFL are common in the US population and potential undocumented differences in the populations are believed to be unlikely to affect the mechanism of action nor the safety or effectiveness of the study drug.

Kato et al. 1997 enrolled consented patients with AF and AFL diagnosed within the prior 2 week and sustained for ≥5 minutes, and a HR >100 bpm, with age 16-75 years, and weight <90 kg. A central ECG assessment committee comprised of 2 physicians adjudicated the ECG diagnosis and type of arrhythmia in all cases. Patients were excluded if they had a myocardial infarction within 1 month, NYHA class III or IV heart failure, known second or third-degree atrioventricular heart block, sick sinus syndrome, blood pressure ≤100/70 mmHg, or tachycardia that was felt by the treating clinician to be due to non-cardiac and non-thyroid disease such as anemia or infection. Patients who were pregnant or lactating, and those that had ‘severe’ liver, kidney, or blood disorders were also excluded. Patients were continuously monitored for the 5 minutes prior, during, and for the 15 minutes after drug administration.

Although the diagnostic criteria for AF and AFL are not documented in the submitted trial materials, these are clinically common and have typical non-ambiguous electrocardiographic findings that can be reasonably expected to have been made by the clinical teams treating these patients. The time frame of diagnosis described in the trial report establishes what is meant by ‘paroxysmal,’ or occurring for less than 2 weeks and for at least 5 minutes. Although this definition is not aligned with the definition of paroxysmal in the US guidelines, in which paroxysmal is defined as “AF that terminates spontaneously or with intervention within 7 days of onset,” whereas persistent AF is defined as “continuous AF that is sustained >7 days,” the enrolled trial population and duration of AF and AFL is sufficiently well-defined. 16 Exclusion of

The overall safety review of landiolol relies on the submitted but potentially incompletely ascertained adverse clinical events reported for each randomized controlled clinical trial of landiolol. These data are supplemented by the clinical and regulatory experience with multiple beta-adrenergic receptor antagonists for the purpose of HR control and management of other cardiovascular conditions, including metoprolol and propranolol reported in Table 1. Summary of Pharmacologic Treatments for Supraventricular Tachycardia (SVT). The utilized randomized controlled trials are listed in Table 2. Selected Clinical Trials Relevant to the Landiolol NDA. To formulate estimates of adverse events (AE) with landiolol, safety data for each submitted clinical trial was individually interrogated for the purpose of the safety review. The relevant AEs associated with the drug landiolol were then pooled to create a reasonable estimate of the AE rates for patients treated with landiolol. The primary known safety concern with all of these medications, including those approved for the short-term management of HR in SVT, is hypotension, as described in Table 1. Benefit-risk

Based on the submitted randomized controlled clinical trial data from 8 published trials, intravenous infusion of landiolol hydrochloride appears to have a consistent and predictable treatment effect to lower heart rate (HR) in the short-term for hospitalized patients with supraventricular tachycardia (SVT) including atrial fibrillation (AF) and atrial flutter (AFL). SVT is a common condition in hospitalized patients with and without known cardiovascular disease, and it is generally presumed that HR lowering improves outcomes for patients with tachyarrhythmias within and outside of the perioperative setting. Landiolol appears to mediate the effect on HR by beta-adrenergic receptor antagonism, a well-understood mechanism of HR slowing. Although no individual-patient level data is available for patients treated with landiolol, placebo, or active controls utilized in the clinical trials to demonstrate safety and effectiveness of landiolol, because all the submitted clinical data comes from the published literature, the treatment effect size appears consistent and the adverse event rate predictable. The surrogate endpoint of short-term HR reduction in the hospitalized setting for the management of SVT was established as a clinically approvable endpoint during the Cardiovascular and Renal Drugs Advisory Committee in September of 1986. Varied dosing regimens were used in the published clinical trials, but the proposed staring dose of 10 mcg/kg/min for patients without heart failure, titrated according to HR response in 10 mcg/kg/min increments to a maximal dose of 40 mcg/kg/min, is reasonable in the context of the dosing regimens tested in clinical trials. However, there is no evidence of therapeutic benefit compared to other typically utilized and approved agents indicated for short-term HR slowing. Clinically relevant hypotension was the principal adverse event identified, which was clinically monitorable, and given the relatively rapid onset with initiation and resolution of the landiolol effect on HR and blood pressure (BP) with discontinuation, landiolol administration can be adjusted accordingly. A lower starting dose of 1 mcg/kg/min, titrated in 1 mcg/kg/min increments according to HR response to (b) (4)

mcg/kg/min, for patients with heart failure and reduced left ventricular ejection fraction appears sufficient for safe use in that population. As patients with hepatic impairment were not adequately studied, landiolol use should be avoided in patients with Child-Pugh Class B or C hepatic dysfunction. Otherwise, restricted use of landiolol to the hospitalized setting where continuous monitoring of HR and BP can be performed appears sufficient for its safe and effective use in the intended population of patients with SVT, including AF and AFL, for short-term control of HR. Overall, the available clinical trial data support regulatory approval of landiolol for time-limited use in a monitored hospitalized setting for select patients with tachycardia, as it has a similar benefit and risk profile as other approved agents for the same indication.
