Immunogenicity — 3950-1

Accelerated approval confirmatory · approved 2020 · Draft Protocol Submission 06/2021 · Final Protocol Submission 12/2021 · Trial Completion 03/2027 · Final Report Submission 09/2027

Submit the final report, including datasets, from an ongoing multicenter clinical trial to verify and further characterize the clinical benefit of naxitamab for the treatment of patients with relapsed or refractory neuroblastoma in combination with granulocyte-macrophage colony- stimulating factor (GM-CSF) in bone or bone marrow who have demonstrated a partial response, minor response, or stable disease to prior therapy; and to provide a more precise estimation of overall response rate and duration of response (according to the revised International Neuroblastoma Response Criteria by blinded independent review). Enroll a minimum of 80 patients with evaluable disease and systematically follow all responding patients for at least 12 months from the onset of response, or until disease progression, whichever comes first. Include subgroup analyses of overall response rate and duration of response by prior anti- GD2 exposure, disease status (refractory vs. relapsed disease), anti-drug antibody (ADA) status (neutralizing antibody [nAb] positive versus nAb negative, ADA positive versus ADA negative), and area of disease involvement (bone marrow vs. bone) in the final report reflecting a sufficient number of patients in relevant subgroups. Derive the clinical data from a minimum of six study sites, with at least five patients enrolled per site in at least five sites.

What the review says about it

FDA's assessment
The FDA disagrees with the Applicant’s position that no dose adjustments are required based on demographics or for special populations. FDA recommends a dosing cap (150 mg per day and 450 mg per cycle) for patients with BW >50 kg as described above in Section 6.3.2.2. Executive Summary

The proposed dosing regimen for naxitamab is 3.0 mg/kg/day on Days 1, 3, and 5 of each 28­ day cycle, for a total of 9 mg/kg/cycle, in combination with GM-CSF 250 µg/m2/day on Days -4 to 0 and 500 µg/m2/day on Days 1 to 5 of each 28-day cycle. The primary efficacy endpoint (ORR) of this dosing regimen was evaluated in Study 12-230, Study 201, and Study 2PR01. The safety of this dosing regimen was supported by data from Study 12-230, Study 201, Study 2PR01, Study 11-009, and Study 12-116. Of these studies, 12-230, 11-009 and 12-116 were conducted by Memorial Sloan Kettering Cancer Centers (MSKCC), while 201 and 2PR01 were conducted by the Applicant.

FDA recommends a dose cap for patients with a body weight (BW) > 50 kg, with a maximum naxitamab dose of 450 mg/cycle. The dose cap is based on the pharmacokinetic characteristics of naxitamab and the relationships between naxitamab exposure and efficacy/safety. Naxitamab exposure is predicted to increase with increasing BW using per kg dosing. The exposure-response relationship for safety suggests higher safety risks (bronchospasm, hypotension, and hypertension) for patients with higher naxitamab exposures, while for efficacy, no significant relationships between naxitamab exposure and ORR/complete response. The exposure-response analysis for efficacy is based on the limited data from the trials and in small number of patients with potential confounding factors (e.g., ADA effect).

The immunogenicity data from studies 12-230, 11-009 and 12-116 conducted by MSKCC are deemed unreliable as the anti-drug-antibody (ADA) assays used in these trials were not validated. Clinical efficacy

The efficacy evaluation for the BLA is based on an interim analysis of all efficacy data from the pivotal Trial 12-230 at the data cutoff date. Trial 201 and the retrospective Study 2PR01 provide supporting efficacy data.

• Subjects could enroll to Trial 12-230 more than once and could have different disease profiles at different enrollments. Therefore, they are counted by enrollment for the efficacy analysis, and the term “enrollments” is used in place of “subjects.” • Trial 201 provides interim data for enrollments with at least 1 follow-up for assessment of efficacy endpoints. Subjects could only enroll once in this trial. • The retrospective Study 2PR01 provides medical record data from patients treated with naxitamab in combination with GM-CSF under compassionate use in Spain.

Clinical safety

This safety evaluation is based on data from 273 unique subjects spanning 298 enrollments in four clinical trials and a retrospective study with data collected from subjects treated in a compassionate-use program. Of the 273 unique subjects, 256 subjects were diagnosed with neuroblastoma and 17 subjects were diagnosed with other GD-2-positive malignancies.

• Trial 12-230 (naxitamab + GM-CSF) includes safety data from 117 subjects, spanning 131 enrollments. • Trial 201 (naxitamab + GM-CSF) includes safety data from 25 subjects. • Trial 11-009 (naxitamab) includes safety data from 67 subjects (8 of them with other GD2-positive malignancies). • Trial 12-116 (naxitamab + interleukin) includes safety data from 12 subjects (9 of them with other GD2-positive malignancies). • Retrospective Study 2PR01 (naxitamab + GM-CSF) includes safety data from 52 patients, spanning 63 enrollments.

All clinical trials are single-arm with no comparator, because of the rarity of the disease and the high-risk population involved.

The main focus is on safety data for subjects with neuroblastoma exposed to naxitamab in combination with GM-CSF from Trial 12-230 followed by Trial 201 and 2PR01, then further supplemented by pooled safety data from Trial 12-230 and Trial 11-009 .

Data from these trials allow for an informed assessment of the safety profile of naxitamab in combination with GM-CSF and an evaluation of the overall benefit-risk in subjects with high-risk refractory or relapsed neuroblastoma in bone and/or bone marrow. This safety population is also considered appropriate for the detection and characterization of common AEs and to provide guidance on toxicity management.

FDA considered the analysis of safety to be supported primarily by patients in Trial 12­ 230 who received the recommended phase 2 dose of naxitamab (n=72) and Trial 201 (n=25). Analyses were also performed for the overall population of patients in Trial 12­ 230 (n=117) who received any dose of naxitamab, given that some rare infusion-related adverse reactions such as anaphylaxis may not be dose-dependent. Safety data from studies 11-009, 12-116 and 2PR01 was considered supportive and were not included in the primary safety population for FDA’s review of the BLA.
