Nonclinical pharmacology / toxicology

Executive Summary

Primary Pharmacology: Erdafitinib (JNJ-42756493, BALVERSA) is an oral fibroblast growth factor receptor (FGFR) tyrosine kinase inhibitor being developed for the treatment of adult patients with locally advanced or metastatic urothelial carcinoma whose tumors have particular FGFR2 and FGFR3 genetic alterations. In an enzymatic assay, erdafitinib inhibited the tyrosine kinase activity of FGFR1-4 with an IC50 of 1.2, 2.5, 3, 5.7 nM, respectively. The binding affinity of erdafitinib was tested in a panel of 451 kinases; erdafitinib bound to all 4 members of the FGFR family (1-4) at a Kd of 0.24, 1.1, 1.4, and 2.2 nM, respectively, and to other kinases with less than or similar affinity. In the in vitro binding assay, erdafitinib also bound to RET, CSF1R, PDGFRA, PDGFRB, FLT4, KIT, and VEGFR2 at clinically relevant concentrations. Erdafitinib inhibited growth of BaF3 cells expressing FGFR1, 3 or 4 (IC50 of 11 to 24 nM), however showed considerably weaker inhibition against BaF3 cells expressing VEGFR2 and other kinases (IC50 of 1190 to 8560 nM). Additionally, erdafitinib demonstrated antiproliferative activity in selected cancer cell lines from various tissues, including bladder cells, with FGFR1-4 alterations (amplification, mutation or fusion) at IC50 values ranging from 0.1 to 129.2 nM. Erdafitinib inhibited autophosphorylation of FGFR proteins in cancer cell lines displaying activated FGFR1, 2, 3 and 4 and phosphorylation of key downstream signaling components of the FGFR signaling pathway in NCI-H1581 cells (i.e. pFRS2 -dǇƌϰϯϲ͕ W>ɶ ϭ͕ Z< ϭͬϮ͕ ĂŶĚ Ɖ^ϲ<). Similarly, erdafitinib demonstrated antiproliferative activity (IC50 < 1 nM) in RK3E (rat kidney epithelial) cells that were transfected with constructs for FGFR2 (BICC1, AFF3, CASP7, CCDC6, OFD1) and FGFR3 (TACC3v1, TACC3v3, BAIP2L1) fusion genes and was shown to inhibit FGFR phosphorylation and phosphorylation of key downstream FGFR signaling pathway components (MAPK and S6). In vivo, erdafitinib demonstrated antitumor activity in immune-deficient rodents bearing tumors derived from multiple human cancer cell lines with FGFR1, 2 and 3 alterations (fusion or amplification) including the bladder, lung, GI and liver. Other Toxicology Studies

Phototoxicity The Applicant evaluated the effect of JNJ-42756493 on phototoxicity in in vitro and in vivo studies (Study #TOX1235; non-GLP; TOX10684, GLP).

The in vitro phototoxic potential of JNJ- 42756493-AAA was assessed using Balb/c 3T3 mouse fibroblasts assay. JNJ-42756493-AAA was considered phototoxic in the in vitro study (Study #TOX1235; non-GLP), while in a follow-up GLP-compliant toxicology study in female Long-Evans pigmented rats, JNJ-42756493-AAA (orally 3 qd at 4, 8, and 16 mg/kg) was not considered to have phototoxic effects on the skin. Systemic exposure increased in a dose-dependent manner. AUC values were 10.1, 79.9 and 544 ng.h/mL for 4, 8 and 16 mg/kg/day, respectively. (Study # TOX10684, GLP)

Skin Sensitization A murine local lymph node assay (LLNA) was conducted to assess the skin sensitization potential of topically applied JNJ-42756493-AAA. Trial Design

BLC2001 was a multi-center, open-label, Phase 2 clinical study of erdafitinib in patients with metastatic or surgically unresectable urothelial cancer harboring select FGFR genetic alterations.

Key Eligibility Criteria 1. Metastatic or surgically unresectable urothelial cancer (cT4b, N+, M+). A minor component of patients (<50% overall) with variant histologies (glandular or squamous differentiation, or evolution to more aggressive phenotypes such as sarcomatoid or micropapillary change) were eligible for enrolment 2. Disease progression following: a. Chemotherapy for metastatic disease b. Progression within 12 months of receiving chemotherapy in the neoadjuvant or adjuvant setting c. Patients who were ineligible for cisplatin-based chemotherapy were also eligible for enrolment. Platinum ineligibility was based upon: i. Impaired renal function defined as a glomerular filtration rate (GFR) <60 mL/min/1.73 m2 by 24-hour urine measurement or calculated by Cockcroft-Gault ii. Grade 2 or higher peripheral neuropathy by CTCAE version 4.0 3. All patients must have had FGFR mutation or fusion: a. FGFR3 gene mutations: R248C, S249C, G370C, and Y373C, b. FGFR fusions: FGFR2-BICC1, FGFR2-CASP7, FGFR3-TACC3, and FGFR3-BAIAP2L1. 4. ECOG performance status score 0, 1, or 2 5. Adequate bone marrow, liver, and renal function 6. Absence or persistent phosphate level > upper limit of normal during screening

Reviewer Comments: Although cisplatin-ineligible patients were enrolled on BLC2001, they were not ultimately included in the indication population for this approval due to insufficient numbers enrolled in the indicated dosing regimen (Regimen 3, see section below on Study Treatment) to adequately assess the efficacy of erdafitinib in these patients (n=12).

Study Treatment There were 3 dosing regimens evaluated in patients on BLC2001; • Regimen 1: 10 mg once daily, 7 days on/ 7 days off • Regimen 2: 6 mg once daily, every day

• Regimen 3: 8 mg once daily, with pharmacologic-guided dose increase to 9 mg based on tolerance and serum phosphate levels

At the initiation of the study, patients were randomized to Regimen 1 or Regimen 2. At Interim Analysis 1 (IA1), the Data Review Committee (DRC) recommended the selection of Regimen 2 for continued development and also recommended additional safety and efficacy analyses. Based on these additional analyses and PK/PD modeling, the DRC recommended that BLC2001 use Regimen 3 for continued development. These changes were implemented in Amendment 3 (August 9, 2016).

Clinical safety

The erdafitinib clinical safety database contains pooled data from a total of 416 patients in two phase 1 dose escalation trials in subjects with advanced solid tumors (EDI1001, and GAC1001) and one phase 2 trial (BLC2001) in patients with advanced or metastatic urothelial cancers. The proposed dose and regimen for approval is 8 mg daily with pharmacodynamically guided up- titration to 9 mg daily. A total of 99 subjects were treated with this dosing regimen in BLC2001. This includes the 87 patients who form the primary efficacy population for this NDA, as well as 12 patients who were treated in the first-line metastatic setting due to their cisplatin-ineligible status. Additionally, to expand the safety database of this dosing regimen, data from subjects in EDI1001 treated with 9-mg daily (no up-titration; n = 65) was also reviewed, for a total of 164 subjects treated with what is referred to as the 8/9 mg daily regimen. The Applicant coded verbatim adverse event (AE) terms for all three trials using MedDRA version 20.0.

Reviewer’s comment: Because the intended patient population in this submission was relatively small (N = 87) the clinical review team examined the 8/9 mg daily population (N = 164) for a more in-depth review. The strategy of pooling of safety data for the 8/9 mg daily regimen from EDI1001 and BLC2001 is considered scientifically sound because the safety profile of erdafitinib is not thought to be markedly indication-dependent. This population forms the basis of the analyses for much of this safety review. For labeling purposes, the 87 patients with advanced urothelial cancer who form the primary efficacy (i.e. the indication) population for erdafitinib were used to inform safety analyses for section 6. The 87 patients were chosen for labeling purposes for clarity, to align with the efficacy population, and because the safety profile of erdafitinib in these patients was generally representative of the overall safety profile of the pooled analyses in the pooled 8/9 mg dosing cohort (n=164).

Benefit-risk

Benefit-Risk Summary and Assessment

Patients with locally advanced or metastatic urothelial cancer have a poor prognosis despite the high response rate seen with standard platinum/gemcitabine chemotherapy. While pembrolizumab has received regular approval in the second-line setting, patients whose tumors harbor FGFR alterations represent a molecularly-defined subset of patients with urothelial carcinoma. This subset may have a lower response rate to PD-L1/PD-1 inhibitors and represents a population of patients for which an unmet medical need exists.

Erdafitinib is a breakthrough-therapy designated kinase inhibitor targeting susceptible FGFR genetic alterations, that is under review for accelerated approval. Erdafitinib was evaluated in the single-arm phase 2 study BLC2001which enrolled patients with locally advanced or metastatic urothelial cancer whose tumors were found to harbor specific FGFR3 and FGFR2 alterations. Efficacy and safety for the labeled indication was assessed via independently-determined confirmed response rate and reported adverse events in a cohort of 87 patients in BLC2001 who received prior chemotherapy, 97% of which was platinum-based. These patients received erdafitinib at a starting dose 8mg with a dose increase to 9mg based on serum phosphate levels and tolerability, which is the proposed dose for this approval.

The BICR-assessed response rate was 32% with a median duration of 5.4 months. Responses appeared to be higher in those patients with FGFR3 mutations (n=64, ORR=40.6%), compared to those with FGFR3 fusions (n=17, ORR=11.1%) and FGFR2 fusions (n=6, ORR 0%). A companion diagnostic will be approved with erdafitinib.

The safety profile of erdafitinib is acceptable in this setting. Deaths due to an adverse event occurred in 1.1% of patients while permanent treatment discontinuation occurred in 12.7%. Grade 3-4 adverse events occurred in 59% of patients and the most common (> 15%) all grade adverse events were hyperphosphatemia, stomatitis, dry mouth, decreased appetite, dry skin, alopecia, palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia syndrome, dry eye, onycholysis, paronychia, nail dystrophy, diarrhea, constipation, nausea, fatigue, urinary tract infection, dysgeusia, musculoskeletal pain, weight decreased, and anemia. Ocular toxicity, hyperphosphatemia, and embryo-fetal toxicity are labeled as warnings and precautions for erdafitinib.

Accelerated approval of erdafitinib is recommended by all disciplines. The Applicant will be asked to submit the results of the randomized phase 3 trial BLC3001 that is underway to confirm the clinical benefit of erdafitinib.
